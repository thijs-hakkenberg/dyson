# TRL assessment T14: Propellant storage, feed and refuelling transfer at hundreds of tonnes per tug

## Scope note

The March 2026 entry `solar-electric-propulsion` rates the **thruster and power chain** (HERMeS/AEPS, NEXT). It contains no evidence about storing, feeding or transferring propellant at the required scale. T14 should therefore be carried as a **separate technology**. The thruster rating does not transfer to it. I rate T14 here as a new line, `propellant-storage-and-feed`, and do not touch the thruster TRL.

## Required scale

**Derived from the revised architecture.** The mass-flow check below is my arithmetic, not evidence.

- **Feed rate.** Thrust power to the jet is η·P = ½·ṁ·vₑ². Assuming η ≈ 0.6:
  - 1.0 MWe at 3,000 s gives about 5 kg/h.
  - 2.5 MWe at 2,000 s gives about 28 kg/h.
  - The stated 7–18 kg/h is the central band. Feed hardware should be sized for **5–28 kg/h, continuous for months**.
- **Lifetime throughput.** 20,000–40,000 h at 7–18 kg/h gives **145–720 t per tug**. For 10–18 tugs that is **1.5–13 kt per fleet lifetime**.
- **Per-fill load.** This is my estimate; the architecture does not state it.
  - Assumptions: tug dry mass 30–60 t, payload 150–300 t, about 3–8 km/s per round trip, Isp 2,500 s.
  - Result: **roughly 20–100 t per fill**, so refuelling happens **once per round trip**.
  - Assumption: neither iodine nor argon is produced at an NEA in Phase 0. Propellant therefore comes from Earth and is transferred at SEL1, by fluid transfer or tank swap.
- **Environment.** Vacuum and microgravity under milli-g thrust, thermal cycling at 0.9–1.3 AU, 1.5–5 yr per round trip, and a 20,000–40,000 h feed duty.

**Physical sizing per 100 t stored.** These figures are my estimates.

| Option | Density | Volume per 100 t | Main issue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Iodine, solid | ~4.9 g/cm³ | ~20 m³ | Heat delivery through a low-conductivity solid (~0.45 W/m·K); recondensation in lines; corrosion; **transfer of a solid** |
| Iodine, liquid (>114 °C) | ~3.9 g/cm³ | ~26 m³ | Heated tank and lines held above the melting point; corrosion at temperature |
| Argon, ~300 bar, ambient | ~0.5 g/cm³ | ~200 m³ | Composite-overwrapped pressure vessel (COPV) mass ~20–35 % of propellant mass; large volume |
| Argon, liquid at 87 K | ~1.4 g/cm³ | ~70 m³ | Multi-year zero-boil-off; cryocooler power ~1–3 kW, which is negligible against MWe |

- storing more than ~1 t of iodine;
- iodine feed above ~0.1 kg/h for more than ~10² h;
- any transfer of iodine, solid or liquid, in space;

- argon storage above ~1 t on one vehicle;
- argon transfer in space.

## 2. Current TRL: 2–4, overall best estimate 3

The propellant is not selected (D-05 is only proposed), so I rate each option and take the system band.

| Option | Storage + feed | Refuelling transfer | Combined | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Iodine, solid sublimation (reference) | 3 | 2 | **2–3** | Sublimation physics and small-scale feed validated (E1, E3). Tonne-scale solid heat management not even breadboarded. Solid transfer is concept only. |
| Iodine, liquid store/transfer | 2–3 | 2 | **2–3** | No breadboard of liquid-iodine tankage or transfer that I can cite |
| Argon, high pressure | 4–5 | 3 | **3–4** | COPV and regulator hardware are mature (E4, E5), but nothing near tens of tonnes on one vehicle. Gas transfer only at small scale. |
| Argon, 87 K cryogenic | 4 | 3–4 | **3–4** | LOX-analogue zero-boil-off ground data (E9) and short in-flight cryo transfer (E8). No multi-year space storage. No argon-specific data. |

**Lower bound (TRL 2).** The iodine reference, with refuelling, has a documented concept that addresses feasibility and benefit (TRL 2 exit), but no experimental result validating key parameters at relevant flow or mass.

The TRL 3 exit is unmet for iodine transfer. There are no documented results validating predicted:
- sublimation or melt behaviour of a multi-tonne mass;
- transfer rate of about 1 t/h;
- void and recondensation behaviour.

**Upper bound (TRL 4).** Argon cryogenic storage components meet TRL 4 by analogy:
- lab zero-boil-off data agree with thermal models at 87–90 K-class temperatures;
- the relevant environment can be defined.

The TRL 5 exit is unmet. There is no breadboard of argon tankage plus feed tested in a relevant environment at a documented, scalable size, and no scaling requirements have been documented for 20–100 t loads.

**Unmet exit criterion for the next level, system-wide.** No candidate has a laboratory breadboard storing ≥1 t and feeding at 5–28 kg/h with documented performance against prediction. Nor has any candidate documented the relevant environment: milli-g settling, 1.5–5 yr thermal cycling, and transfer in microgravity. That is the TRL 4 exit for the reference propellant.

## 3. Target and difficulty

**Target: TRL 6 at KDP-0B for the selected propellant, including transfer.**
- Interim TRL 4 by the 0A mid-term review, to support the D-05 down-select.
- I classify the risk as architecture-change, not project-ending, so D-02 strictly allows a lower target. I still recommend TRL 6 because the decision is not reversible: the tug fleet's tankage, thermal design and refuelling interfaces are committed at KDP-0B.
- TRL 6 for the transfer element almost certainly needs a **flight demonstration**. Microgravity fluid and solid behaviour and multi-month thermal cycling cannot be fully reproduced on the ground; drop towers and parabolic flights only cover seconds.
- The pathfinder demonstrator is the natural carrier.
- **Assumption:** KDP-0B falls about 6–9 yr after KDP-0A, roughly 2032–2035. The phase brief does not state the date.

**AD² for the step from current to TRL 6: 5 overall** ("new development; similar development has not been done, but the path is clear", ≈70 %).

| Option | AD² | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Argon cryogenic | 4 | Close LOX analogue. CFM and zero-boil-off development has been done; it was never flown for years. |
| Argon high pressure | 3–4 | Within the experience base. The penalty is mass and volume, not feasibility. |
| Iodine, solid feed and refuel | 5–6 | Nobody has subliming-fed from a multi-tonne solid, refuelled a solid, or run iodine-wetted valves for 20,000–40,000 h. Liquid iodine transfer is a plausible but unproven path. |

If the down-select slips past the 0A mid-term review, the honest rating becomes **AD² 6** ("several competing approaches; path not clear"). That covers both the propellant choice and the choice between fluid transfer and tank-module swap.

## 5. Risk and fallback

**Risk class: architecture-change.** Failure does not end the project technically, but it changes the tug and logistics design:
- smaller per-fill loads and more refuelling events;
- swappable Earth-filled tank modules instead of fluid transfer;
- or a change of propellant.

**Gate interaction.** If transfer cannot be made routine, propellant logistics may push delivered $/kg past the D-02 **economics gate**. In that case it becomes de facto project-ending for 0B, through the pre-committed "0A only, Earth supply" fallback.

**Fallback.** Use Earth-filled, swappable tank modules of argon or krypton high-pressure propellant, berthed robotically at SEL1. This avoids in-space fluid transfer and relies on flown storage technology, at a cost in tank mass, volume and refuelling cadence.

## 6. Where others might disagree

1. **Iodine as reference (D-05).** Proponents point to density (~4.9 g/cm³) and unpressurised storage, which favour iodine for *storage*. My rating penalises it for *feed at scale and refuelling*, which have essentially no evidence. Argon at 87 K borrows real LOX cryogenic heritage. A reviewer weighting tank mass over transfer risk would rank the options the other way.
2. **The upper bound of 4.** Someone could argue argon high pressure is already TRL 5–6 because Starlink flies it by the thousand. I hold that per-vehicle storage is ≳10³ below requirement and that transfer is undemonstrated. Fleet statistics validate small-tank reliability, not scale.
3. **Whether a 1–5 t flight transfer earns TRL 6** for a 20–100 t requirement, a ratio of ~10–100. That depends on whether P1/P5 produce credible, validated scaling laws. A strict reviewer may hold TRL 6 until a ≥10 t transfer.
4. **Risk class.** Through the economics gate this could be argued as project-ending for 0B. If so, D-02 makes TRL 6 mandatory with no waivers, which I recommend anyway.
5. **Supply.** Supply is outside TRL. A fleet need of 1.5–13 kt of iodine over its life is small against ~30 kt/yr world output, but it is not negligible for price exposure. Some will say this alone should make argon the reference.
6. **Uncertain citations.** E2, E3, E9 specifics and E10 are uncertain from memory and must be checked before this rating is used at a review.
