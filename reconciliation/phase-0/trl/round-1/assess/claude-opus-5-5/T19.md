# T19 — Microgravity bulk-solids handling and comminution: TRL assessment

## Scope and assumptions

The two requirement statements describe the same mass balance with different scope.

- **GPT's version:** 2–8 kt/yr concentrate plus 13–47 kt/yr tailings, i.e. 15–55 kt/yr total.
- **Claude's version:** the same 15–55 kt/yr feed, plus crushing and milling to sub-mm and a ≥5-yr wear life.

I rate the union of both statements. The sub-functions are:

1. Metering and feeding without gravity.
2. Enclosed conveying and transfer.
3. Buffering and storage, including bridging and arching.
4. Charging and discharging of process vessels.
5. Comminution to sub-mm.
6. Size classification.
7. Tailings compaction and containment.
8. Upset recovery from jams, plugs and wall build-up.
9. Wear over 5 years.

**Derived rate**

- 15–55 kt/yr equals 1.7–6.3 t/h if run continuously.
- At an assumed 70% plant availability, design rate is roughly **2.4–9 t/h**.
- Excavation is batchy (robot duty cycle 9–25%), so buffering is required, not optional.

**Containment implication of D-03**

- The interim escape ceiling is ≤1 t/yr per site.
- Against 13–47 kt/yr of tailings, that is an escape fraction of about 2×10⁻⁵ to 8×10⁻⁵.
- That means roughly **99.998% retention summed over every transfer point**, before considering excavation ejecta.
- This makes enclosed handling the main debris control, not just a process convenience.

**Environment**

- Vacuum, at or near microgravity (10⁻⁴–10⁻⁶ m/s² at the NEA), or 0.05–0.15 g inside a rotating module (Gemini route).
- Abrasive, tribo- and photo-charged regolith: fines from friable C-type material, metal-bearing clasts from S/M-type material.
- 0.9–1.3 AU thermal cycling.
- 5-yr life.

**Routes**

The metallurgy route changes the handling problem, so I rate it per route (below). One point applies to all routes: excavation and transfer to the plant are microgravity handling in every case, even if processing is spun.

**On the supplied literature reviews**

- The rq-0-6 and rq-0-9 reviews contain arXiv-ID citations and quantitative claims I cannot verify, for example "10s of W per kg/h" for bucket wheels.
- I do not use them as evidence. Where I cite a paper below, it is one I am reasonably confident exists.
- The rq-0-6 background figures (20 robots, 1,000+ t/robot/yr) are superseded by the revised architecture.

### Terrestrial heritage at TRL 9, mostly gravity-reliant

- **Jenike-method hopper and flow design** (Jenike 1964, Utah Eng. Exp. Station Bulletin 123). Confident.
  - Critical outlet dimension for cohesive arching scales roughly as σ_c/(ρg).
  - This is directly relevant to the rotating route: at 0.1 g, cohesive-arch outlets are ~10× larger than at 1 g.
  - In true microgravity the method gives no answer.
- **Force-driven, nominally orientation-independent equipment** exists at t/h scale and above:
  - screw and auger feeders, rotary-valve and piston metering;
  - dense-phase pneumatic conveying (needs a carrier gas, so a closed gas loop in vacuum);
  - high-pressure grinding rolls;
  - stirred-media and vibratory mills.
  - None has been qualified for operation without gravity-assisted feed or discharge. Industrial mills feed and discharge by gravity.
- **Ball, rod, AG and SAG mills** depend on gravity cascading. They are not transferable to microgravity. They are partly transferable to a 0.1 g centrifuge with large redesign.

### What does not exist

I am aware of none of the following:

- an in-space demonstration of continuous bulk-solids transfer above the gram–kilogram scale;
- any in-space comminution;
- any in-space size classification;
- vacuum-plus-microgravity wear data for handling equipment.

I am not certain no small ISS powder-handling experiment exists. I know of none relevant to abrasive regolith at process scale.

## 2. Current TRL: **2–3**

Ratings by sub-function in microgravity, which is the no-melt and native-metal path:

| Sub-function | TRL | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Positive-displacement metering and conveying (screw, piston) | 3 | Mechanism is force-driven. Analysis is credible, and E3/E4 give constitutive inputs. No validated prediction exists for cohesive, charged fines without body force. |
| Closed-loop pneumatic transfer | 2–3 | E1 and E7 show gas mobilisation at grams. No loop-closure or gas-recovery data in vacuum. |
| Buffering, bridging, charging and discharge of vessels | 2 | No validated flow theory for zero-body-force storage. Bennu-like fines with high cohesion to weight are worst-case. |
| Comminution to sub-mm | 2 | Concept formulated (HPGR or stirred mill, force-fed and force-discharged). No proof-of-concept of feed and discharge without gravity. |
| Size classification | 2 | Screens rely on gravity or vibration. Alternatives proposed (centrifugal classification, gas classification) are unvalidated for this feed. |
| Tailings compaction and containment at 99.998% | 2 | Concept only. E1 is a direct counter-example: a single particle wedge defeated a seal. |
| Upset recovery | 2 | No data. |
| 5-yr wear | 2–3 | Terrestrial abrasion indices and E8/E9 bound the mechanisms. No relevant-environment life data. |

Rotating route (0.05–0.15 g):

- **2–3** for in-module handling. Jenike-type design applies in principle, but cohesive arching worsens by ~1/g, Coriolis effects distort chute and hopper flow, and no partial-g handling data exist below lunar g.
- **2** for transferring solids from a non-rotating to a rotating frame across a sealed interface in vacuum. This interface is not needed by the other routes and has no heritage I know of.

**Overall rating**

- **Lower bound, TRL 2 met.** Documented application concepts exist: the round-2 mass balances and the industrial equipment classes listed above.
- **Upper bound, TRL 3 partly met.** Only for force-driven conveying, supported by analysis plus E3/E4 constitutive data.
- **Unmet for TRL 3 overall.** There are no documented experimental results validating predictions of the key parameters for cohesive, charged, abrasive regolith with no or low body force:
  - feeder mass-flow versus speed;
  - plug and bridge onset;
  - wall build-up rate;
  - mill throughput and product size with forced discharge.
- **Also unmet for TRL 4.** There is no documented definition of the relevant environment for the handling train, specifically:
  - Bond-number range;
  - charge state;
  - particle size distribution and clast fraction by target type.

## 3. Target and difficulty

### Is it project-ending for 0B?

Yes. Co-located processing that ships product rather than ore needs every sub-function except possibly comminution. Tailings containment is also tied to the D-02 licence gate through D-03.

### Targets under D-02

- **0A mid-term review:** TRL 4, as an internal 0A milestone.
  - Ground breadboards of each sub-function, run orientation-independent in vacuum with flow-characterised simulants.
- **KDP-0B:** TRL 6 in a relevant environment, no waivers.
  - The KDP-0B date is not given in the material supplied. I state it as a milestone, not a year.
  - TRL 6 cannot be earned on the ground. Sustained microgravity is the defining condition, and parabolic flight (~20 s) or suborbital flight (~3 min) cannot show steady-state flow, wall build-up or upset recovery.
  - It therefore needs a flight subsystem prototype (free-flyer, or a 0A pathfinder payload) running for months.
  - It also needs a documented scaling argument from that subscale up to 2.4–9 t/h.

### AD² = 6

"Several competing approaches; the path is not clear."

- Metering and conveying alone would be **AD² 5**: the path is clear, the steps are new development.
- Bridging-free buffering, forced-discharge comminution, 99.998% containment and autonomous upset recovery have competing approaches with no basis to choose yet:
  - screw versus closed-loop pneumatic versus spin-assisted versus vibratory or electrostatic transport;
  - HPGR versus stirred mill versus none.
- The flight-test step itself is new.
- I would move to **AD² 7** if ground breadboards show that cohesive Bennu-like fines wall-plate continuously in vacuum regardless of transport method.

### Years to target

**6–12 years** from programme start:

- 2–3 years to TRL 4;
- 2–3 years more to TRL 5;
- 3–6 years for a flight prototype, including build, launch and months of operation.

## 5. Risk and fallback

### Risk class: project-ending for 0B

There are graded partial outcomes short of full failure:

- **Comminution fails, transfer works.** Architecture change: restrict to targets and products that need only coarse liberation, such as native Fe-Ni clasts or coarse metal from S/M-type material, and accept a lower concentration ratio. This risks the 3:1 falsifier in rq-0-51.
- **Microgravity handling fails, spun handling works.** Architecture change to Gemini's rotating module, which adds the rotating-interface risk.
- **Containment cannot be shown at ≤1 t/yr.** This is a licence-gate failure, which D-02 treats as project-ending for 0B.
- **All fail.** Pre-committed D-02 fallback: 0A only, with Earth supply.

Reopening ore shipping would reverse the co-location decision. It would be an architecture change requiring panel reconciliation, not a fallback.

## 6. Where others might disagree

1. **Whether the upper bound is TRL 3.** Someone could credit TRL 3 to the force-driven sub-functions on the strength of terrestrial physics plus E3/E4. I do not credit it for the system, because the hard functions (bridging, wall plating, forced mill discharge) lack any validated prediction. Equally, someone could argue TRL 1–2 for comminution, since there is no peer-reviewed work specific to microgravity milling that I can cite.
2. **Whether a 10–100 kg/h flight loop can earn TRL 6 for a 2.4–9 t/h plant.** That is a 25–900× scale-up. I accept it only with a DEM scaling law validated across P2–P3. A strict reading of "no waivers" might demand a larger flight article, which would push past the 0A budget.
3. **Whether ground testing can substitute for microgravity.** Advocates of orientation-independent equipment may argue that inverted 1 g tests plus cohesive simulants suffice for TRL 5–6. I disagree for steady-state storage and wall build-up. Cohesion-to-weight can be raised on the ground, but the absence of any preferred body-force direction cannot be reproduced.
4. **Gemini's rotating route.** The likely counter-argument is that 0.1 g makes conventional chutes and hoppers work. My objection is that cohesive arching and outlet sizing scale ~1/g, Coriolis effects distort flow, and the static-to-rotating solids interface is unprecedented. The route may still win, but it is not lower-risk on handling.
5. **Whether sub-mm comminution is required at all.** GPT's narrow native-metal scope may avoid it. Claude's no-melt carbonyl/H₂ route probably needs it. This is unresolved and depends on target sampling under the D-02 target gate.
6. **Wear.** I treat 5-yr wear as demonstrable only by accelerated ground test plus in-flight trend data. Others may say no pre-KDP-0B evidence can close it, and that it should be carried as a cost/schedule risk with replaceable liners rather than gating TRL.
