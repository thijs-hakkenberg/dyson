# T08: Particle containment, escape verification and tailings management. TRL assessment

**Bottom line.** T08 is at **TRL 2–3** (concept formulated) against a requirement of **TRL 6 at KDP-0B**. Containment capture efficiency in vacuum and microgravity has never been tested, and no published work on stabilising kiloton-scale tailings exists to my knowledge. AD² is **6**, the risk class is **project-ending for 0B**, and TRL 6 probably requires the 0A pathfinder to carry an instrumented containment experiment.

## 0. What the requirement actually demands

Rating at required scale means turning D-03 into capture efficiencies. The arithmetic is mine, from the architecture figures.

- **Mass handled.** Ore is about 15–50 kt/yr per site, and tailings 13–47 kt/yr. The robot figures give 0.5–5 t/h × 9–25% duty × 8,766 h × 6–20 robots ≈ 2.4–220 kt/yr. The tailings figure sits in the lower-middle of that, and the upper bound of the robot range is not credible against the tailings figure.
- **Handling steps.** Each tonne is handled roughly 4–5 times: excavation, robot-to-plant transfer, beneficiation, tailings transfer, and stockpile or emplacement.
- **Allowed escape per handling at the 1 t/yr interim ceiling.** About 4×10⁻⁶ to 1.7×10⁻⁵. That means 99.998–99.9996% capture at every step, every year, including abnormal events.
- **At the 10 kg/yr aspiration.** About 4×10⁻⁸ to 1.7×10⁻⁷ per handling.
- **Comparison with rq-0-41.** The 99% figure rq-0-41 started from is 3–5 orders of magnitude short. Gemini's 99.9% for grains above 5 µm is 2–3 orders short on mass unless the escaping mass is dominated by grains below 5 µm. It is not; mass is dominated by the coarse tail.
- **Verification by mass balance is impossible.** Terrestrial bulk weighing achieves about 0.25–0.5%, and I know of no microgravity bulk mass gauging that does better. A mass balance can therefore only bound escape to roughly 40–250 t/yr. Escape must be verified by direct flux measurement, which is a separate technology.
- **Abnormal events will probably dominate.** A single breached tailings container, jammed transfer gate or furnace upset can release more than a year's budget in one event. GPT's emphasis is correct: the 1 t/yr budget is more a release-frequency × release-mass problem than a seal-leakage problem.
- **Chronic loss from the stockpile.** After five years the tailings pile holds about 65–235 kt.
  - Losing the full 1 t/yr budget from that pile would take a loss fraction of only about 4×10⁻⁶ to 1.5×10⁻⁵ per year.
  - Fines on a body with escape speed of about 0.05–0.5 m/s are subject to loss by solar radiation pressure, electrostatic lofting, thermal cracking and micrometeoroid impact. The mechanisms are documented, but I know of no published loss rate for a disturbed pile.
  - I have not seen this term quantified anywhere. It may be the largest single uncertainty in T08.
- **"Escaped" is undefined.** There is no agreed definition of whether escape means exceeding escape speed at release, failing to re-impact within N days, or reaching a heliocentric orbit. Without one, the relevant environment cannot be documented, which is a TRL 4 exit item.

## 2. Current TRL

**Overall: TRL 2 (min) – 3 (max).** T08 is rated at its weakest critical sub-function.

| Sub-function | TRL | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Ejecta and escape dynamics modelling, including heliocentric fate | 3–4 (analysis capability) | Validated against E1–E4 and E12. Weakened by E4 (Bennu strength mispredicted) |
| Excavation source containment (housed bucket wheel) | 2 | No vacuum or microgravity measurement of capture efficiency for any enclosed excavator |
| Transfer and processing enclosure, **no-melt** (magnetic beneficiation → carbonyl/H₂) | 2–3 | Gas-tight reactors contain fines inherently (E13). The open questions are vacuum-side feed locks and magnetic separators |
| Transfer and processing enclosure, **rotating 0.05–0.15 g furnace** | 2 | Adds a tangential-release failure mode (rim speed sets ejection velocity, well above escape speed). Melt and slag handling in rotation is untested |
| Transfer and processing enclosure, **narrow native-metal** | 2 | Open magnetic or electrostatic separation of a fines stream in vacuum is a lofting source. Simplest metallurgy but not the simplest containment |
| Tailings stabilisation, 13–47 kt/yr | 2 (fines long-term retention physics 1–2) | Concepts (bagging, compaction, sintering, burial) exist only as studies. Lunar sintering lab work addresses material properties, not retention |
| In-situ escape verification | 2–3 | Instruments flown (E3, E11). No source-budget closure demonstrated. Calibration at cm/s–m/s, µm–cm grains is non-standard; dust accelerators work at much higher speeds |
| Array and radiator contamination control | 3 | E9 shows lunar settled-dust removal. Ballistic charged flux in microgravity onto MW-class arrays and radiators (absorptance growth) is untested |
| Abnormal-event bounding (excavation, transfer, processing, storage, docking) | 2 | No event catalogue, no release-mass data. E5–E7 are the only real-world analogues |

**Exit criteria:**
- **Met at the lower bound (TRL 2):** a documented concept addressing feasibility and benefit (rq-0-41, rq-0-26, D-03).
- **Unmet for TRL 3:** documented experimental results validating predictions of the key parameter. Here that parameter is the escape fraction per handling step, by size bin, for an enclosed excavation or transfer interface in vacuum with charged simulant. No such data exist.
- **Also missing for TRL 4:** a documented relevant-environment definition, including a fate-based, size-binned definition of "escaped".

## 3. Target and difficulty

**Target.** TRL 6 at KDP-0B, no waivers. T08 is the technical basis of the D-02 licence gate, and 0B cannot be licensed without it.

**Internal 0A milestones (dates are my assumption; no KDP-0B date is in force):**
- TRL 4 for source containment, tailings stabilisation and the verification method at the 0A mid-term review (≈2029–2030).
- TRL 5 before CDR of the pathfinder containment payload (≈2030–2031).
- TRL 6 at KDP-0B (≈2033–2036).

**What "relevant environment" means for TRL 6 here:**
- Vacuum, NEA microgravity of about 10⁻⁵–10⁻⁴ g, charged regolith, thermal cycling at 0.9–1.3 AU, and representative feed for hundreds of hours.
- Ground tests cannot reproduce microgravity ballistics for longer than seconds.
- **TRL 6 therefore almost certainly needs the 0A pathfinder** to run a subscale containment and escape-measurement experiment at an NEA. A 1 g vacuum test at full feed can reach TRL 5 at most.

**AD² = 6** (range 5–7): several competing approaches; path not clear.
- **Containment:** similar enclosures exist terrestrially, but none at about 10⁻⁶ loss per handling without gas-mediated filtration. Vacuum offers no airflow to capture with, so capture must be ballistic, electrostatic or mechanical.
- **Tailings:** at least four competing approaches (bagging, compaction or sintering, burial or redeposition, controlled disposal), with no data to choose between them.
- **Fines retention:** may need fundamental work, which pushes toward AD² 7.
- **Verification:** the path is clear, so on its own it would be AD² 5.

## 5. Risk and fallback

**Risk class: project-ending for 0B.**
- Under D-02 the licence gate is discriminating, and T08 is its technical evidence.
- If TRL 6 is not shown, 0B does not proceed.
- The pre-committed fallback is "0A only, Earth supply".

**Intermediate architecture-change fallbacks, before 0B is abandoned:**
- Lower throughput (fewer handlings, fewer tonnes).
- A larger target with higher escape speed.
- Sealed-container or sintered tailings in place of loose piles.
- Prefer routes with gas-tight processing (no-melt) over the rotating furnace on containment grounds alone.
- Negotiate a size- and fate-weighted licence limit, provided the licensing state accepts it.

## 6. Where others might disagree

1. **TRL 3 already met?**
   - **Their argument:** ejecta physics is flight-validated (E1–E4), dust detectors are TRL 9 and EDS has flown, so T08 is TRL 3–4.
   - **My position:** the critical function is capture and retention efficiency at about 10⁻⁶ loss per handling. Nobody has measured that for anything. Validated ejecta models describe what escapes, not what an enclosure captures.
2. **Risk class.**
   - **Their argument:** this is architecture-change, not project-ending. Throughput, target size and tailings method are all trade knobs.
   - **My position:** that is true technically. Under D-02's no-waiver rule, though, an unmet licence-gate technology stops 0B. I keep the disagreement visible rather than splitting the difference.
3. **Can ground tests reach TRL 6?**
   - **Their argument:** seal leakage is driven by adhesion and electrostatics, not gravity, so a 1 g vacuum test is "relevant".
   - **My position:** in 1 g, gravity returns released grains and holds material in buckets. That masks exactly the loss paths that matter at 10⁻⁵ g, so I cap ground testing at TRL 5.
4. **Is chronic pile loss real?**
   - **Their argument (likely GPT and Gemini):** a gravity-bound tailings pile is benign.
   - **My position:** a loss fraction of only about 10⁻⁵ per year from a multi-hundred-kt pile uses the whole budget. Radiation pressure and electrostatic lofting of fines are documented mechanisms. This is unquantified, not negligible. I may be overweighting it, and P6 resolves it.
5. **The metric itself.** A flat mass limit penalises harmless coarse grains that re-impact and under-weights µm grains that form long-lived streams. A size- and fate-weighted limit may be more defensible. That is a licence-gate value choice, as Claude says, not something this rating can settle.
6. **Route ranking.**
   - **My view:** no-melt scores best for containment.
   - **Gemini's likely reply:** furnace slag is easier to stabilise than fine tailings.
   - Both claims are untested.
