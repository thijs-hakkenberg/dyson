# TRL assessment: T04 Asteroid excavation with anchoring

**Re-rate of `asteroid-mining-bucket-wheel` against the revised Phase 0 architecture**

## 0. What is being rated, and a problem with the requirement

The technology is **anchored, tethered, autonomous excavation** of NEA regolith or rubble feedstock for co-located Fe-Ni beneficiation. It covers:

- tool–regolith interaction,
- reaction-load management (anchoring, reaction control, counter-rotation),
- material capture and containment, and
- durability over 5 years.

The bucket wheel is now one candidate mechanism, not the definition of the technology. The metallurgy route (no-melt, rotating furnace, or narrow native-metal) does not change this rating. It sits downstream, although the native-metal route opens a low-force alternative (see §5).

**Required scale, worked from the architecture.** The two stated requirements are not equivalent. Only part of the Gemini/GPT envelope closes the mass balance.

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Excavating hours per machine per year (9–25% duty) | 790–2,190 h |
| Annual excavation per machine (0.5–5 t/h) | ~0.4–11 kt/yr |
| Cumulative excavation per machine over 5 yr | ~2–55 kt over ~3,900–11,000 h of tool contact |
| Fleet-average rate from the Gemini/GPT envelope (6–20 machines) | 0.27–25 t/h |
| Ore implied by tailings (13–47 kt/yr) plus product (0.6–2.5 kt/yr) | ~14–50 kt/yr, i.e. **1.6–5.7 t/h fleet average** |

The ore figure is consistent with Claude's 1.7–6.3 t/h.

**Consequence.** The low corner of the Gemini/GPT envelope (6 machines, 0.5 t/h, 9% duty) delivers about 2.4 kt/yr, which is 6–20× short. At a mid-fleet of 12 machines and 17% duty, each machine must achieve **≈0.8–2.8 t/h**. With 6 machines at 9% duty it would need 3–10 t/h, which exceeds the envelope. I rate against **0.8–5 t/h per machine** and record the inconsistency in §6 rather than averaging it away.

**Reaction loads implied by the requirement.** These are not quoted anywhere in the RQs and are the crux of the rating.

- Excavation power is disputed: 2–15 kWe (Claude) against 40–100 kWe (GPT).
- At a bucket-wheel tip speed of about 0.5 m/s, even 1–2 kW of cutting power is **~2–4 kN** of tool force. At the GPT figures it is tens of kN.
- A 3 t robot on a 0.3–3 km NEA weighs **~0.03–1 N**. The anchor therefore reacts **10³–10⁵ × robot weight**.
- In regolith with near-zero cohesion (Bennu, Walsh et al. 2022), frictional anchor capacity scales with overburden, which is roughly 10⁻⁴–10⁻⁵ of its value on Earth.
- Cohesive capacity is at most tens to ~100 Pa per m² of failure surface, i.e. ≤10² N.

So kN-class anchoring must bite into coherent boulders or bedrock, or the tool must be **self-reacting** for both force and torque. Counter-rotation cancels torque but not net translational force, as GPT notes in rq-0-26.

The literature review's force table (0.5–30 N cutting force, 100 kg excavator) describes a machine three to four orders of magnitude smaller than the one specified.

### Corrections to the RQ literature reviews

These do not change the rating, but the register should not carry them forward.

- **"Ballouz et al. 2020, TAG bearing capacity 0.2–20 Pa, ~1 N contact."** I cannot confirm this attribution or these numbers. The TAG geotechnical results I am confident of are Lauretta et al. 2022 and Walsh et al. 2022.
- **Philae "bounced at ~0.38 m/s, above escape velocity".** As I recall, 67P's escape velocity is roughly 1 m/s, so a 0.38 m/s rebound was likely *below* escape.
- **Gecko "ISS grappling experiments (DARPA/JPL)".** This is unverified.

### Environment definition

All asteroid regolith geotechnical data comes from C-types (Bennu, Ryugu) plus Itokawa grains. A native Fe-Ni product points to S-type, LL, or M-type targets, for which there are **no** NEA geotechnical data. The Psyche mission (launched 2023, arrival ~2029) will characterise a main-belt M-type remotely only. The TRL 4 exit criterion requires a "documented definition of the relevant environment", and that criterion is not met for the likely target class.

## 2. Current TRL: **2–3**

**Lower bound, TRL 2: met.** The application is documented, with feasibility and benefit addressed in Nallapu 2017, the round-2 reconciliation, and rq-0-26/39/42.

**Upper bound, TRL 3: partially met at sub-function level only.** Separate experimental results exist for:

- granular rheology in reduced gravity (E10),
- microspine grip on rock in reduced gravity (E8),
- counter-rotating drum reaction cancellation at 1 g (E11), and
- very weak near-subsurface response to a spacecraft (E1).

**Unmet for TRL 3.** There are no documented experimental results that validate *predicted* excavation force and torque and anchor holding capacity for the **critical function**:

> cutting and capturing cohesive or near-cohesionless regolith with clasts, while reacting the resulting kN-class loads through an anchor or a self-reacting tool, at low confining stress.

The predictions themselves span two orders of magnitude (2–15 kWe against 40–100 kWe). That spread is a direct sign that key parameters have not been validated.

**Unmet for TRL 4 (if TRL 3 is granted).** The following do not yet exist:

- a lab breadboard of integrated anchor plus excavator with test data agreeing with predictions, and
- a documented relevant-environment definition. Target-class cohesion, clast distribution and subsurface strength are undefined (rq-0-39).

I lower the March 2026 rating from 3 to 2–3, because its stated basis was consensus.

## 3. Target and difficulty

### Target TRL

**TRL 6 at KDP-0B, no waivers.**

- Without excavation at rate there is no in-space feedstock, so 0B cannot proceed. Under D-02 that makes the *function* project-ending for 0B.
- Internal 0A milestones:
  - TRL 3 by the 0A mid-term review, to select the tool and anchor families;
  - TRL 4 (with selected components at TRL 5) before pathfinder CDR.

**Needed by:** KDP-0B. Assuming 0A starts in 2027, KDP-0B falls around 2034–2038.

**Relevant environment for TRL 6.** Hours of microgravity contact with real regolith while reacting loads against a real body cannot be simulated on the ground, in parabolic flight (~20 s), or in a LEO free-flyer, which has no body to anchor to.

- In practice TRL 6 requires the **0A pathfinder demonstrating anchored excavation at the target NEA**. Formally that is a TRL 7-type environment.
- The 5-year durability requirement cannot be flown before KDP-0B. It must be closed with ground accelerated-life tests in vacuum, with abrasive and charged simulant, at ≥1.5× contact passes. Flight data then anchors the correlation.

### AD²: **7**

Range 6–8. "Requires new approaches; fundamental engineering research; ≈50%."

- **Why not 5:** no similar development has been done. No device has reacted kN-class excavation loads on any small body, the only small-body anchoring attempt failed (E4), and a reaction-limited regolith tool failed on Mars (E5).
- **Why not 6:** "competing approaches" exist on paper (microspines, drill anchors, harpoons, counter-rotation, reaction thrusters). However, anchor capacity in near-cohesionless regolith at 10⁻⁴–10⁻⁵ m/s² sits 1–3 orders of magnitude below the loads, so the path may need a new approach altogether. Candidates are boulder-gripping, fully self-reacting tools, very low cut-force-per-pass operation, or enclosure-and-bag of whole rubble.
- **Why not 8:** the governing physics (cohesion, friction, low confining stress) is understood in principle. TAG showed it is poorly predicted, which is the argument for 8.

### Years to target

**8–14.** The estimate is bounded by pathfinder development of 3–5 years, cruise of 1–3 years, and 0.5–1 year of surface operations.

## 5. Risk and fallback

**Risk if it fails to reach target: project-ending for 0B.** Under D-02 the pre-committed outcome is "0A only, Earth supply". Any *specific* mechanism (bucket wheel, microspine anchor) is only architecture-change, because alternatives exist. None of those alternatives is above TRL 2 either.

**The March 2026 fallback no longer applies.** Thermal extraction of volatiles is irrelevant to an Fe-Ni product and still needs material handling.

**Credible fallbacks, all architecture changes needing their own TRL path:**

1. **Low-force magnetic or rake harvesting** of loose native-metal grains from S-type or LL regolith. This removes most cut force and anchoring and fits GPT's narrow native-metal route, at lower and uncertain rate.
2. **Enclose-and-process** of boulders or loose rubble (bag or capture, ARM-style), which needs no anchored cutting.
3. Fully self-reacting tools that cancel both force and torque, as in RASSOR-style counter-rotating drums, operated at very low per-pass cut depth.

## 6. Where others might disagree

1. **TRL 3 versus 2.** Gemini may argue that the sub-function experiments (E8, E10, E11) satisfy TRL 3. I hold that TRL 3 requires validating the critical function's key parameters, and those predictions currently disagree by ~10×.
2. **The requirement itself.** The low end of the Gemini/GPT per-machine envelope does not close the tailings mass balance. If the panel holds 0.5 t/h at 9% duty with 6 machines, the product and tailings figures are wrong instead. This must be reconciled, not averaged.
3. **The anchoring physics.** My claim that regolith anchoring cannot react kN loads assumes cutting power on the order of kW and Bennu-like near-zero cohesion. If target S-type regolith is a thin layer over coherent boulders, or if real cut forces are near Nallapu's J/kg values, the problem shrinks and AD² could be 5–6. Conversely, TAG-like surprises argue for 8.
4. **The TRL 6 venue.** Some will argue P5 plus P7 constitutes a relevant environment and grants TRL 6 without an NEA pathfinder. I disagree: neither reacts loads against a real body. Conceding this would cut 3–5 years.
5. **Counter-rotation.** Gemini treats it as adopted, Claude as strengthened but to be re-run, and GPT as a candidate only. None of these positions is evidence. It addresses torque but not translation.
6. **Risk class.** The function is project-ending; the mechanism is architecture-change. The YAML records the function.
