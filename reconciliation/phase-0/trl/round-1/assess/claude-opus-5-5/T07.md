# T07: Partial-gravity melting and slag/metal separation in a rotating module (TRL re-assessment)

**Scope.** This entry rates the melt route only: Gemini's baseline, and GPT's "experimentally gated hypothesis" version of it. Claude's no-melt route (magnetic beneficiation followed by carbonyl or H₂ reduction) is a separate technology and belongs in rq-0-56. Here it appears only as the fallback.

**Product scope.** Under the revised architecture the melt is a Fe-Ni native-metal concentrate, possibly whole-rock chondritic ore, with flux. Zone refining, thin-film deposition and the "microgravity zone" elements in the March entry are out of scope, because solar-grade Si is deferred.

## 0. Requirement check: radius, rpm and what they imply

Effective gravity is g_eff = ω²r. The table pairs radius with rotation rate:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 2.99 | 4.23 | **5.18** | 20 % | 0.90 |
| 10 | 2.11 | 2.99 | 3.66 | 10 % | 0.64 |
| 20 | 1.50 | 2.11 | 2.59 | 5 % | 0.45 |
| 50 (rq-0-11 deliberation: ~1.4 rpm gives ~0.11 g) | 0.95 | 1.34 | 1.64 | 2 % | 0.29 |

**Inconsistency in Gemini's stated envelope.**
- The envelope "5–20 m at 1.5–4 rpm" spans 0.013–0.36 g.
- It cannot deliver 0.15 g at 5 m without exceeding 4 rpm (5.2 rpm is needed).
- The consistent requirement for 0.05–0.15 g within ≤4 rpm is roughly r ≥ 8 m. I use **10–20 m at 1.5–3.7 rpm** as the reference.

**Physics consequences.** These are first-order estimates for sizing the experiments; they are not evidence.

- **Separation kinetics scale with 1/g.**
  - Assumptions: Stokes settling of metal droplets through slag, Δρ ≈ 4,000 kg/m³, slag viscosity 0.3 Pa·s (the plausible range is about 0.05–3 Pa·s, a factor of ~50).
  - A 100 µm droplet settles at about 15 µm/s at 0.05 g. Crossing a 0.3 m slag layer then takes about 6 h, against about 17 min at 1 g.
  - A 30 µm droplet takes about 60 h at 0.05 g.
  - Metal-grain size in the feed is therefore a first-order design input. Ordinary-chondrite metal ranges from tens of µm to mm, and Itokawa (Hayabusa, 2010) is LL-chondrite-like. The question is whether grains coalesce in the melt before tapping.
  - Foam drainage and gas disengagement also slow roughly as 1/g. This matters for gas evolution from flux and sulfide reactions.
- **Bulk stratification is not the problem.**
  - The capillary length at 0.05 g is about 2 cm (σ_metal/slag ≈ 1 N/m, an assumption), against about 5 mm at 1 g.
  - A 1–10 t pool with a characteristic size of about 0.5 m therefore has a Bond number of several hundred. The pool will layer.
  - The open questions are kinetics, entrainment and coalescence.
- **Rotation dominates melt convection.**
  - For U ≈ 1 cm/s and L ≈ 0.5 m, the Rossby number is about 0.03 at 10 m and 3 rpm.
  - Taylor–Proudman constraint is expected. It may help stratification, but it suppresses the vertical mixing that the deliberation counted on for "thermal homogeneity". That deliberation claim is not supported.
- **Tapping, launders and pouring are Coriolis-dominated.** For a 1 m/s stream, Coriolis acceleration is 30–90 % of g_eff (last column of the table). Terrestrial tapping practice does not transfer.
- **Free-surface curvature.** The equipotential surface is a cylinder of radius r. A hearth 2 m wide tangentially sags about w²/8r: 0.1 m at r = 5 m and 0.025 m at 20 m. That is comparable to the slag-layer thickness, so the hearth should be elongated along the spin axis.
- **Mass moving radially changes spin rate and balance.** Placing a 10 t batch at 20 m adds about 4,000 t·m² of moment of inertia. Charging and tapping therefore need momentum and balance compensation, such as moving counter-mass.

## 2. Current TRL: **2–3**

**The lower bound, TRL 2, is met.** The concept and application are documented with a feasibility and benefit argument. Basic principles are established by E1 and by standard Stokes and Bond-number physics.

**The upper bound, TRL 3, is arguable but not cleanly met.**
- *For TRL 3:* the critical function, density-driven stratification and settling, is experimentally characterised at 1 g (E1) and at g > 1 (E3). The scaling law is validated physics. Bulk-pool Bond numbers at 0.05 g are high.
- *Against TRL 3:* the key parameters for this application have not been measured. These are separation kinetics, coalescence, entrainment and metal recovery for asteroid-representative melts. The g-scaling has not been validated *below* 1 g, and rotating-frame effects have not been validated at all.
- The TRL 3 exit criterion is "documented results validating predictions of key parameters". It is met only if a 1 g / hypergravity extrapolation counts as validation. I do not think it does, for the coalescence- and Coriolis-dominated parts.

**Unmet for TRL 3 (and TRL 4):**
- No documented experimental results validate predicted separation efficiency and time against g, viscosity and metal grain size for chondritic or Fe-Ni-concentrate melts.
- No breadboard exists, and no documented definition of the relevant environment exists, for:
  - g-level, gradient and Coriolis;
  - vacuum or cover gas;
  - heat count and refractory life;
  - spun/despun transfer.

**Change from March.** The rating drops from 3–4 to 2–3. The March upper bound rested on microgravity heritage and deliberation output, neither of which qualifies.

## 3. Target and difficulty

**Target under D-02.**
- *If 0B selects the melt route:* T07 is project-ending for 0B, so it needs **TRL 6 at KDP-0B, in a relevant environment, with no waivers**.
- *Internal 0A milestone:* TRL 4 at the 0A mid-term review, to make the melt vs no-melt route decision against rq-0-56.

**Critical structural point.** Sustained 0.05–0.15 g cannot be produced on the ground.
- TRL 5 (relevant-environment breadboard) and TRL 6 therefore **require flight**: a rotating free-flyer or the 0A pathfinder.
- If the 0A pathfinder does not carry a partial-g melt experiment, the melt route **cannot be baselined at KDP-0B** under D-02 as written. That should be decided at KDP-0A, not discovered at 0B.

**Needed by.** KDP-0B, which is not dated in the material. I assume about 2033–2036, i.e. 7–10 years after the 2026-10 KDP-0A, but that is not stated anywhere. The TRL 4 route-decision milestone is needed by the 0A mid-term review, which I assume is about 2029–2030.

**AD² = 6** (range 5–7). The step is from TRL 2–3 to TRL 6.
- *Why not 5:* the separation physics path is clear. But several architectural approaches compete, none has precedent, and the choice among them changes the problem:
  - furnace on a spun arm with a rotary joint, versus the whole vehicle spinning, versus a tether and counterweight;
  - refractory lining versus cold-wall skull;
  - continuous feed versus batch transfer at the hub.
- *Why not 7:* no new science is required. However, the coupling of rotation-dominated convection, coalescence kinetics at low g, vacuum refractory chemistry and uncrewed hot-material transfer has not been studied together. Someone could defensibly argue 7.
- *Refractory life.* MgO-C refractories degrade under vacuum (MgO + C → Mg(g) + CO). FeO-rich slags attack oxide linings. Terrestrial furnaces survive by robotic or crewed gunning and relining, which an uncrewed module cannot do. A cold-wall skull avoids lining wear, but moves the load into coolant-loop heat rejection inside the rotating frame.

**Years to TRL 6:** 8–12, consistent with the March estimate. The March target was TRL 7. I expect this to be **later than KDP-0B on most plausible schedules** unless the flight experiment starts in 0A.

**On the March fallback ("raise to 0.3–0.5 g").** It is a valid *parameter* mitigation, because settling time scales with 1/g and the Coriolis fraction scales with 1/√(g·r). It does not address:
- spun/despun transfer;
- uncrewed refractory life;
- vacuum chemistry.

It also raises structural loads and rpm (0.5 g at 20 m needs 4.7 rpm). It is a mitigation, not a fallback.

## 6. Where others might disagree

1. **TRL 3 vs 2 (and vs 3–4).**
   - *Gemini's likely view:* separation obviously works with any density difference and gravity, and E1 and E3 bracket the physics, so TRL 3 or higher is met.
   - *My view:* the bulk-layering argument is sound (high Bond number). Coalescence, entrainment and Coriolis-dominated tapping at 0.05 g are untested, and a 1 g extrapolation runs in the non-conservative direction.
2. **Whether 0.05–0.15 g "transforms the problem".** The deliberation claims convection restores thermal homogeneity. A Rossby number of about 0.03 implies rotation-constrained convection, which partly undermines that claim. This is analysis, not data, so both positions are untested.
3. **Whether subscale flight can satisfy TRL 6.** GPT might accept P6 (kg-scale) plus validated models as TRL 6. I require ≥0.1 t, because tapping, refractory and transfer behaviour does not scale down cleanly.
4. **Phase need and risk class.**
   - *Claude* would tag this as deferred or phase-1, with pilot-scale centrifugal melting only.
   - *Gemini* would tag it 0B and project-ending.
   - I tag it 0B and architecture-change, conditional on the no-melt route maturing. That is a judgement about the alternative, not about T07 itself.
5. **The scale-ratio framing.**
   - I side with GPT that annual-vs-batch comparison is invalid.
   - I give a mass ratio of about 10⁶–10⁷ against ISS-EML, which is somewhat larger than Claude's 4–6 orders.
   - I argue the decisive gap is the absence of sustained partial-g data, not mass.
6. **Feasibility of the radius and rpm envelope.** Gemini's 5 m lower radius is inconsistent with 0.15 g at ≤4 rpm, and gives a 20 % gravity gradient across a 1 m pool. Anyone holding to 5 m will need either more than 5 rpm or a lower g ceiling.
