# TRL assessment: T23, native-metal production and consolidation in rotating modules (GPT route)

**Assessor's scope note.** This rates the GPT route only. That route has three parts:

- Magnetic beneficiation of the native Fe-Ni already present in chondritic or metallic NEA material.
- Consolidation of that concentrate, by melting with minor slag or by solid-state processing, in a module spun to 0.05–0.15 g.
- An explicit experimental gate on whether rotation is needed at all.

It does not rate oxide reduction (Gemini's smelting route) or the carbonyl/H₂ no-melt route (Claude). The March 2026 entry (TRL 3–4) described a different technology: industrial-scale hybrid-gravity smelting plus Si zone refining at a 50 kt/yr central station. Its evidence does not transfer.

**Required scale, derived:**

- **Throughput.** 0.6–2.5 kt/yr of product is about 70–285 kg/h at 100% availability, or about 90–570 kg/h at 50–80% availability.
- **Batch size.** I assume 0.1–1 t per batch for a batch furnace or consolidation press. This is my assumption, not an architecture figure.
- **Ore throughput.** The ore is roughly 10–50× the product mass, from native-metal fractions of about 2–20 wt% in ordinary chondrites. That is consistent with the 13–47 kt/yr tailings figure.
- **Environment.** Sustained 0.05–0.15 g in vacuum, at the NEA site, at 0.9–1.3 AU, with abrasive charged fines.
- **Duration.** Multi-year continuous operation at roughly 0.1–1 t per batch.

I keep batch size and production rate as separate ratios. GPT's point is correct that comparing a batch mass with an annual tonnage is dimensionally invalid.

## 2. Current TRL: 2 to 3

**Lower bound, TRL 2: met.** The application is documented with its feasibility and benefit argument: round-2 GPT position, rq-0-11 and rq-0-13. The physics supports the concept on analysis alone:

- **Silicate rise rate.** Stokes rise of a 100 µm silicate droplet in molten Fe at 0.1 g is of order 1 m/h, taking Δρ about 4,000 kg/m³ and µ about 6 mPa·s. That is a plausible separation time for 0.1–1 t melts.
- **Feedstock and form.** The native metal is already reduced (E1), and native Fe-Ni is workable (E6).

**Upper bound, TRL 3: only if one accepts 1-g evidence plus Stokes/Bond scaling as the critical-function proof.** I do not accept this as met. The hypothesis this route exists to test is precisely the gravity dependence.

**Unmet exit criterion for TRL 3:** documented experimental results that validate predictions of the key parameters. Those parameters are:

- metal recovery;
- product S, P, Si and O content;
- silicate and troilite entrainment;
- consolidated density.

They are needed as a function of g, for chondrite-derived concentrate, with at least short-duration reduced-g data bracketing 0.05–0.15 g.

**Why this is lower than the March entry (3–4).** That rating leaned on ISS-EML, which is gram-scale, containerless and at 0 g, plus design-study consensus. Neither is evidence of partial-gravity consolidation. The required environment has no data.

## 3. Target and difficulty

**Phase need: 0B.** Gating experiments must run in 0A.

**Targets under D-02:**

- **TRL 4 at the 0A mid-term review.** This is an internal 0A milestone. A route down-select against rq-0-56 needs a ground breadboard with defined relevant-environment and similitude parameters.
- **TRL 6 at KDP-0B, if and only if this route is selected as the 0B baseline.** It would then be the sole Fe-Ni consolidation path and therefore project-ending for 0B. No waivers.
  - Relevant environment for TRL 6: sustained 0.05–0.15 g, vacuum, chondrite-derived or sampled-target concentrate, batches of at least 100 kg. Rq-0-11 asks for at least 1 t.
  - Duration: at least weeks of repeated cycles.

**Dates are my assumption.** The architecture gives none. I assume 0A starts 2026–27, the 0A mid-term is about 2029–30, and KDP-0B is about 2032–35.

**AD² for TRL 2–3 → 6: 6**, with a range of 5–7.

- **Why not 5.** The physics path is fairly clear, but the route is one of three competing approaches. There is also a genuine fork inside it: melt with slag separation versus solid-state consolidation, where gravity matters mainly for powder handling. The path to TRL 6 needs a sustained partial-gravity space test platform that does not exist.
- **Why it could be 5.** If ground tests show solid-state consolidation of concentrate meets Phase 1 specification, the gravity requirement shrinks to powder and bulk-solids handling. That is a better-bounded problem.
- **Why it could be 7.** It could rise if:
  - fine metal–troilite intergrowths defeat magnetic beneficiation and force a sulfide-removal melt; or
  - Coriolis and gravity-gradient effects at short radius do not scale to a 40–60 m production module.

  The second concern arises because a 3 m radius at 0.1 g is about 5.5 rpm, while the production module runs at about 1.3 rpm.

**Years to TRL 6:** 6–11.

## 5. Risk and fallback

**Risk class if TRL 6 is not reached by KDP-0B: architecture-change.**

The other Fe-Ni routes remain. In order of preference:

1. Ship beneficiated concentrate, or solid-state compacts consolidated without rotation, if Phase 1 accepts them.
2. Switch to the rq-0-56 no-melt route (H₂ reduction or carbonyl).
3. Raise spin to 0.3–0.5 g. This carries larger structural and Coriolis penalties.

**Escalation.** If all Fe-Ni consolidation routes fail, 0B becomes project-ending. The pre-committed D-02 fallback then applies: "0A only, Earth supply".

## 6. Where others might disagree

**Gemini would rate higher (3–4).** Its argument is that terrestrial smelting heritage applies "with parametric adjustment" at 0.05–0.15 g. My objection is that there is zero data between microgravity and 1 g. The parametric adjustment is the untested hypothesis, so heritage at 1 g cannot validate it.

**Claude would challenge the rotating module's existence, not its TRL.** The argument is that native metal never needs melting. If correct, this technology should be tagged deferred, not 0B. I keep it at 0B only because the P2 outcome is unknown.

**The TRL 6 scale threshold is contestable.** Rq-0-11 asks for at least 1 t batches; I accept at least 100 kg. Taking 1 t would add about 1–3 years and lift P7 cost toward the top of its range.

**Is short-radius test data relevant?** A 3–5 m centrifuge reproduces g but not the Coriolis or gravity gradient of a 50 m module. Some reviewers will say P6 cannot count as relevant environment for TRL 5 without an explicit, validated scaling argument. I require that argument as part of P6's exit.

**Feedstock dependence.** At LL-type targets (about 2–5 wt% metal) or C-types (little native metal), the route may fail the D-02 target gate before any TRL question arises. That is a target risk, not a technology risk, but it can strand the technology.

**Citation confidence.** E7 and E8 are unverified by me. Removing them does not change the rating.
