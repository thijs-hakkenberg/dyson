# TRL assessment: T16 Anchoring, hold-down and surface mobility on low-cohesion bodies

**Re-rating of `asteroid-mining-bucket-wheel`.** This assessment splits off the anchoring and hold-down element and re-scopes it.

## Summary

- **Current TRL:** 2–3. The rubble-pile variant is at 2. The monolith variant is at 2–3.
- **Target:** TRL 6 at KDP-0B.
- **AD²:** 7.
- **Risk if it fails:** project-ending for 0B as currently architected.
- **Main finding:** TRL 5–6 for this technology cannot be closed on the ground. The in-situ demonstration on a target-class NEA is the critical path, and the scouts and pathfinder must carry it.
- **Change from the March 2026 entry:** its TRL 3 rested on "multi-model consensus" and terrestrial analogues. Neither is evidence under the rating rules, so that rating is withdrawn. Its fallback, thermal volatile extraction, no longer applies: the product is now native Fe-Ni, and thermal extraction still needs contact, handling and hold-down.

### Physics point that drives the rating

Frictional anchors (flukes, screws, piles) get their capacity from overburden stress, ρgz.

- Near the surface of a 500 m rubble pile, ρgz ≈ 1,300 × 10⁻⁴ × 1 m ≈ 0.1 Pa.
- Even the central pressure is only of order 10 Pa: (2π/3)Gρ²R² ≈ 13 Pa at ρ = 1,200 kg/m³ and R = 250 m.

Holding kN loads in such a body therefore cannot rely on friction. It must come from one of three sources:

- **Rock contact:** gripping boulders or bedrock.
- **Artificial cohesion:** grouting, sintering, or bagging.
- **Geometry:** a body-encircling girdle or net.

None of these has been tested against a prediction in a medium that is scaled to Pa-level cohesion.

- **Met at the lower bound (TRL 2):** documented application concepts addressing feasibility. TRL 1 is clearly met by the published small-body regolith science.
- **Unmet for TRL 3 (rubble-pile, mobility, tailings):** "Documented analytical or experimental results validating predictions of key parameters." No pull-out, cyclic-load or retention test has been run in Pa-cohesion or cohesion-scaled media against a prior prediction.
- **Unmet for TRL 4 (monolith, the upper bound):** two things are missing.
  - A documented definition of the relevant environment: target boulder or bedrock strength is only thermally inferred.
  - A breadboard test at the load class (kN, 10⁶+ cycles) agreeing with prediction.

**Composite rating.** The requirement covers both variants, and the target is not yet selected under the D-02 target gate. The composite is therefore governed by the weaker variant: min 2, max 3.

## 3. Target and difficulty

### Target TRL

- **KDP-0B:** TRL 6 in a relevant environment, with no waivers.
  - Without hold-down there is no surface excavation.
  - Without tailings retention there is no licence under the D-02 licence gate (D-03 ceiling).
- **Internal 0A milestones:**
  - TRL 4 by the 0A mid-term review.
  - TRL 5 before the pathfinder design freeze.

### When it is needed

KDP-0B. Its date is not stated in the brief. **I assume no earlier than about 2034–2037**, given scout cruise times.

The 5-yr duration cannot physically be demonstrated in situ before KDP-0B. TRL 6 therefore has to rest on three things:

- At least 6–12 months of in-situ anchored operation.
- Accelerated cyclic and thermal ground testing.
- Validated degradation models.

If the board reads "no waivers" as requiring 5 yr in situ, KDP-0B slips by about 5 yr. That must be decided now, not at KDP-0B.

### AD² = 7

"Requires new approaches; fundamental engineering research," about 50% success.

**Rubble-pile variant (drives the 7):**

- Conventional anchor physics fails at about 0.1–10 Pa overburden.
- The candidate approaches (artificial cohesion, girdles, boulder grip) are untested.
- Multi-year ratcheting and creep of anchors in µg granular media under 10⁴–10⁵ thermal cycles is unknown.
- No ground facility reproduces the environment for longer than seconds.

AD² 8 is defensible here: subsurface strength and cohesion distributions are a scientific unknown that only scouts can resolve.

**Monolith variant:** 5 on its own. The path is clear: microspine bootstrap, then drilled bolts. It has simply not been done.

**Mobility and tailings:** 6–7.

## 5. Risk and fallback

### Risk class: project-ending for 0B as currently architected

Failure means two things:

- No licensable surface operation (licence gate).
- No reaction for excavation.

### Fallback

1. **Enclosed capture-and-ingest.** Bag small boulders or a whole small (<10–20 m) body and process inside an enclosure. No surface hold-down is needed. This changes the target class and throughput, and is itself only TRL 2.
2. **Otherwise,** the D-02 pre-committed "0A only, Earth supply."

Choosing a monolith target reduces difficulty to AD² 5 for anchoring. It does not remove the tailings problem.

## 6. Where others might disagree

1. **TRL 3 now.** Some will credit TRL 3 from OSIRIS-REx TAG, Hayabusa2 SCI and low-g penetration experiments. I do not: those data validate regolith properties, not anchor function at the required loads.
2. **Risk class.** "Architecture-change" is defensible if enclosed capture is accepted as a credible alternative. I rate it project-ending because that alternative is no more mature.
3. **No frictional anchoring in rubble piles.** My argument is an order-of-magnitude one. Boulder interlock or compacted interiors could give more capacity than ρgz suggests. Only in-situ pull-out data will settle it.
4. **TRL 6 without 5 yr in situ.** This is a governance call, not a technical one. It must be decided at 0A start.
5. **Counter-rotation.** Gemini treats it as solving the reaction problem. GPT and I note that it cancels torque, not net cutting force. The anchor still carries kN-class translation loads.
