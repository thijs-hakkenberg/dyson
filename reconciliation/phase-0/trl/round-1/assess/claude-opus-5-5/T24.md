# TRL assessment: T24 Dust-tolerant long-life mechanisms and seals

## Scope and required performance

The revised architecture needs mechanisms and seals that survive at an uncrewed NEA processing site. The required scale, environment and duration are derived below. They are my reading of the architecture, not stated requirements.

**Mining robots (6–20, each 2–3.5 t)**
- Each robot has excavation drives, mobility or anchoring actuators, and a tether reel with a 1–3 kV rotary electrical joint.
- At a 9–25% duty cycle over 5 years, each robot operates about **4,000–11,000 h**.
- At 0.5–5 t/h, each robot handles about **2,000–55,000 t** of regolith. Fleet totals are consistent with 13–47 kt/yr of tailings.

**Processing plant (co-located)**
- Feed locks, valves and rotary feeders carry dusty solids across a gas-pressure boundary.
- The no-melt route needs H₂ or CO seals. Fe(CO)₅ formation is typically run at elevated pressure (order 10–100+ bar; my assumption from terrestrial practice).
- The rotating-furnace route needs a large bearing or spin/despin interface, rotary unions for power, coolant and gas, and tolerance of high temperature.
- Conveyors and magnetic separators are also needed.

**Interfaces**
- Product-transfer and docking connectors to the tugs.
- Mate cycles are low (tens over 5 years), but the connectors spend years in a dusty environment between mates.

**Environment**
- Hard vacuum.
- Milligravity (NEA surface gravity roughly 10⁻⁵–10⁻⁴ g), where cohesion and electrostatics dominate how dust moves.
- Photoelectrically and plasma-charged fines.
- Angular silicate grains plus **free Fe-Ni metal grains** for an S- or M-type target. These are magnetic and can bond to clean surfaces in vacuum.
- Thermal cycling at 0.9–1.3 AU.
- Dust self-generated by excavation, which adds to the native environment.

**Duration:** at least 5 years without repair, except by robotic module swap.

**Summary of the evidence.** There is no demonstration of any mechanism or seal in abrasive, charged regolith in vacuum beyond about 1% of the required operating hours at the required duty.

The strongest space evidence, the lunar rovers and Yutu-2, covers **mobility at very low duty**. It does not cover excavation, gas-tight seals on a dusty process boundary, or kV rotary joints.

Several of the most relevant heritage items are failures: Apollo seals, Lunokhod 2, the OSIRIS-REx flap, and the SARJ.

## 2. Current TRL

T24 is a family of technologies, and the elements are at very different levels. I rate them separately and then give the governing range.

| Element | TRL | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Low-duty mobility actuators, dust-shrouded | 4–5 | Lunar flight survival exists (Yutu-2, Lunokhod), but not at required hours or in milligravity |
| Excavation drives and bearings (bucket drum or wheel), 4,000–11,000 h | 3 | Lab and bin tests; no vacuum endurance data I can cite |
| Tether reel with 1–3 kV slip ring/rotary joint in dust | 2–3 | SADA slip rings at ~100 V class in clean vacuum. kV across dusty brush tracks raises arcing and Paschen-regime concerns from outgassing and charged fines. No test known |
| Dusty-feed gas-tight locks/valves (no-melt: H₂ or CO, elevated pressure) | 2–3 | Terrestrial lock hoppers at high TRL in air and 1 g. No vacuum or milligravity seal-life data. Dust-laden gas sealing is a known terrestrial difficulty |
| Rotating-furnace bearing/spin-despin interface plus rotary unions (Gemini route) | 2 | Large clean-vacuum bearings have a failure history (SARJ). Adding dust, heat and material transfer across a spin interface has no precedent |
| Native-metal-only, minimal-mechanism route (GPT) | 3 | Fewer seal types, but excavation and tether elements still apply |
| Dock and transfer connectors (low mate count) | 3–4 | Dust-cap and EDS concepts exist; lab tests only, as far as I know |

**Governing rating for T24: TRL 3 (minimum 2, maximum 4).** The rating is set by the weakest element on each route:
- No-melt: TRL 2–3, driven by feed seals and the kV reel.
- Rotating furnace: TRL 2.
- Native-metal: TRL 3.

**Met at TRL 3 (lower bound for the family).** There are documented experimental results on key parameters for the concepts:
- Dust adhesion and EDS clearing in vacuum.
- Seal and bearing degradation by simulant.
- Apollo and mission failure analyses that validate the damage mechanisms.

The kV dusty rotary joint and the rotating-furnace interface arguably do not meet TRL 3. I have no critical-function test evidence for either, so they sit at 2.

**Unmet for TRL 4.** Two things are missing:
1. Documented laboratory test performance of the actual mechanism classes over a meaningful fraction of life, agreeing with analytical wear and leak predictions.
2. A **documented definition of the relevant environment**.

The environment cannot yet be defined properly. There is:
- no validated simulant for metal-rich NEA regolith;
- no measured charging state or levitation flux at the operating site;
- no quantified ejecta and dust environment self-generated by 0.5–5 t/h excavation in milligravity.

**Assumptions:**
- The target is S- or M-type with free Fe-Ni. A C-type target changes the abrasive and cohesive character but not the rating.
- Robotic module swap is permitted. In-situ lubrication replenishment is not assumed.

## 3. Target and difficulty

**Target TRL**
- **TRL 5 at the 0A mid-term review.** Component level, in a thermal-vacuum chamber with charged, metal-bearing simulant.
- **TRL 6 at KDP-0B.** Subsystem prototypes: excavator head, tether reel, and feed lock for the selected metallurgy route.

**Why I hold this to TRL 6** even though the risk class (section 5) is architecture-change rather than project-ending:
- The KDP-0B economics gate depends on mechanism life. Spares mass, availability and makeup gas all enter delivered $/kg.
- Life data is the long pole and cannot be produced quickly after KDP-0B.

**When it is needed:** KDP-0B. Its date is not stated in the inputs; I assume about 2032–2034. Endurance testing must start by about 2028–2029 to fit.

**Life compression arithmetic.** Duty can be compressed from 9–25% to about 100%. A 5-year requirement of 4,000–11,000 h, with a 1.5× life factor, then becomes **0.7–1.9 years of continuous dusty-vacuum testing per article**. This is feasible but has no slack for redesign iterations.

Two degradation modes cannot be accelerated by duty compression:
- calendar-driven degradation (thermal cycling, lubricant creep and outgassing, dust accumulation between operations);
- milligravity dust transport.

These need separate tests and modelling.

**AD²: 6** (several competing approaches; the path is not clear).

The competing approaches are:
- labyrinth/shroud plus EDS;
- hermetic bellows or magnetically coupled feedthroughs (no sliding seal);
- flexure and non-contact designs;
- sacrificial, swap-able wear modules.

It is not clear which works for feed locks under dusty gas pressure or for kV rotary transfer. There is an element-specific wrinkle: magnetic couplings and ferrofluid seals, the obvious vacuum options, **attract free Fe-Ni fines**.

| Element | AD² |
|---|---|
| Shrouded actuators | 4 |
| Excavation bearings | 5 |
| kV reel | 5–6 |
| Dusty-feed gas seals | 6 |
| Rotating-furnace interface | 7 |

The rotating-furnace interface needs new engineering approaches unless the whole module spins and transfer is done without a bearing, which moves the problem to the dock and transfer interface.

**Years to target:** 5–9.

**On step 7.** A full 5-year life in the true environment cannot be demonstrated before KDP-0B. TRL 6 at KDP-0B will therefore rest on ground endurance testing plus flight correlation over at least 20% of life. The KDP-0B board should accept or reject that basis explicitly.

## 5. Risk and fallback

**Risk class: architecture-change.** If T24 misses TRL 6, the architecture can shift to the following, at a cost in spares mass, availability and makeup gas:
- swap-able wear modules;
- more robots at lower duty each;
- hermetic designs only, with no sliding seals;
- fewer rotating or pressurised interfaces, favouring the native-metal route.

The class escalates to **project-ending for 0B** if demonstrated mean time between replacement of the excavation or feed-seal elements falls below about 6–12 months. That threshold is my estimate. Below it, spares logistics on 1.5–5-year tug round trips and the resulting availability loss are likely to fail the economics gate.

For the rotating-furnace route, failure of the spin interface is effectively route-ending.

**Fallback:** Select the minimal-mechanism, native-metal route with swap-able sacrificial wear modules and hermetic (bellows or flexure) seals. If KDP-0B life evidence still fails, fall back under D-02 to "0A only, Earth supply".

## 6. Where others might disagree

1. **"Heritage puts it higher (TRL 5–6)."** One argument cites Yutu-2's more than 5 years, Opportunity's 14 years and terrestrial mining. My view is that these match on calendar time but miss on operating hours by orders of magnitude, on regolith handled by about 10⁶×, and on environment (Mars atmosphere; lunar 1/6 g vs milligravity). They also include no excavation or dusty pressure seals. I consider this the most likely upward challenge, and I reject it under the scale rule.
2. **"Milligravity is benign."** In milligravity, dust does not settle into seals and normal loads are low, so abrasion could be milder than on the Moon. The counter-argument is that cohesion, electrostatic adhesion and excavation-generated ejecta dominate, and that clean Fe-Ni grains in vacuum may adhere or gall. This is genuinely uncertain. Step 6 is designed to resolve it.
3. **Lumping vs splitting.** One could rate only the weakest element per route, making the rotating-furnace route TRL 2 / AD² 7 and the native-metal route TRL 3 / AD² 5. My single family rating of 3 (range 2–4) hides that spread. The per-element table is the operative rating.
4. **Risk class.** A reviewer focused on the economics gate may call this project-ending. A reviewer focused on design flexibility may call it cost-increase. I keep architecture-change, with the explicit escalation threshold above.
5. **Citations I could not pin down.** I am confident specific NASA KSC/GRC dust-tolerant connector, seal and RASSOR test reports exist, but I cannot cite them. If those reports contain vacuum endurance data at 10³ h or more, the excavation and connector elements might move to TRL 4. That literature check (part of step 1) should precede any upgrade.
