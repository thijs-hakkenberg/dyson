# TRL assessment: T20 Distributed energy storage (and microgrid power management)

## 0. What is being rated, and two problems with the inputs

**Scope disagreement.** Claude frames T20 as storage hardware. GPT adds "microgrid power management". I rate the two separately because they sit at different TRLs:

- **T20-S:** site storage, 0.5–15 MWh usable.
- **T20-R:** robot packs, 10–50 kWh.
- **T20-P:** the MW-class 1–3 kV DC microgrid that ties together storage, site arrays, plant loads and tether-fed robots.

The headline range below covers all three. Folding T20-P in lowers the floor by about one level. That choice belongs to the panel.

**Stale related RQs.** rq-0-21 and rq-0-22 concern a 100 MW, $500M III-V array at L4/L5 feeding a central Processing Station. The revised architecture (D-01) abandoned that design. Neither RQ, nor the attached literature review, is evidence for T20. I would not reuse that review's numbers without checking them. For example, its "$100–300/cm²" cell cost looks roughly an order of magnitude high against per-watt space-cell pricing, though I have not verified current prices. Only rq-0-60 (tether insulation) bears on T20, through the robot power interface.

**The requirement is under-defined, which caps the rating.** Three things are still TBD: discharge power, recharge profile and outage duration (GPT). Claude's 9,000–33,000 cycles assume surface-sited arrays. Those cycle counts are consistent with an NEA rotation period of about 4–10 h over 10–15 yr, i.e. about 900–2,200 cycles/yr. I have not seen the derivation.

Night length is roughly half the rotation period, about 1–5 h for P = 2–10 h. The arithmetic follows:

| Case | Load | Night | Energy needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Keep-alive (BMS, thermal, comms, robot standby) | 0.1–0.3 MW | 1–5 h | 0.1–1.5 MWh |
| Full process ride-through | 2.5–8 MW | 1–5 h | 2.5–40 MWh |

So 15 MWh covers full ride-through only for fast rotators and low plant power. The 0.5–15 MWh range therefore spans two different operating philosophies: nightly curtailment versus partial ride-through. Neither has been chosen.

**Mass consequence.** Installed capacity equals usable capacity divided by depth of discharge (DoD). Assumptions: pack-level specific energy 100–150 Wh/kg (my estimate for space-grade packs, not verified), and DoD that falls as cycle count rises (about 60% at ~9k cycles, about 25% at ~33k cycles).

- Low end: 0.5 MWh at 60% DoD and 150 Wh/kg gives about 6 t.
- High end: 15 MWh at 25% DoD and 100 Wh/kg gives about 600 t, and roughly 700 t with enclosures and thermal control.

The high end exceeds one tug payload (150–300 t). That is why the 200–500 MWh bank was abandoned, and the same logic bites at 15 MWh if arrays are surface-sited and the plant runs through the night.

**The requirement depends on the metallurgy route.**

| Route | Effect on storage |
|---|---|
| Claude (no-melt: magnetic beneficiation → carbonyl or H₂ reduction) | Lower process temperatures; probably tolerates nightly curtailment. Pushes storage toward the low end. |
| Gemini (rotating smelting furnace) | A furnace cannot be cheaply thermally cycled every 1–5 h. Needs hot standby or ride-through. Pushes toward the high end, or toward thermal rather than electrical storage. |
| GPT (gated native-metal production) | Intermediate; depends on which experiment passes. |

**Headline: TRL 3–5.**
- The floor is set by T20-P and by the missing environment and requirements definition. This is a requirements gap, not a physics gap.
- The ceiling is set by the cell and module heritage.
- I do not rate T20-S at 6 on the grounds that "cells are flight-proven and the rest is paralleling". Three things are new and not parallel:
  - thermal control on an NEA surface with ~150–400 K swings and dust on radiators;
  - fault propagation at 10–100× ISS energy;
  - BMS and protection at kV.

## 3. Target and difficulty

**Risk classification.** T20 is not project-ending if a fallback exists (see §5), so D-02 does not mandate TRL 6 at KDP-0B.

**Targets.**
- **0A internal milestone (0A mid-term review):** TRL 4 for all three elements. This includes a documented relevant environment and a sizing case per metallurgy route.
- **KDP-0B:** TRL 5 for T20-S and T20-P; TRL 6 for T20-R, which the pathfinder can demonstrate.
- **Before 0B deployment / PDR:** TRL 6 for T20-S and T20-P.
- **Timing assumption:** the inputs give no KDP-0B date. I assume about 5–7 years after D-01, i.e. roughly 2031–2033.
- **Condition:** if the panel adopts surface-sited arrays plus Gemini's continuous furnace as the reference, the classification moves toward project-ending and the KDP-0B target should become 6.

**AD² = 4** (new development; similar development has been done, ≈80%).
- Storage chemistry, cycle life and BMS are within the experience base (AD² 3 on their own).
- Integration at MWh and 1–3 kV in vacuum, with tether-fed robots and dusty radiators, is similar in kind to ISS EPS and terrestrial MVDC but has not been done in this environment. That lifts the rating to 4.
- I would rate 5 if the high-end case (15 MWh at ≥30k cycles) becomes the reference, because the mass closure is then unproven.

## 5. Risk and fallback

**Risk class: architecture-change.** If storage at the needed scale and cycle life does not close in mass, the architecture changes along one of these lines:

- Move arrays off the surface (stand-off or co-orbiting). Cycle count then falls to contingency events, and the storage drops to fault and outage ride-through. This creates a new transmission technology to rate.
- Accept nightly curtailment. This favours Claude's no-melt route and penalises Gemini's furnace.
- Restrict target selection to fast rotators.
- Substitute thermal storage for process heat.

If stand-off siting is already the reference, the class falls to **cost-increase**.

**Fallback:** size storage to keep-alive only (≤1.5 MWh), curtail processing every night, and select targets and metallurgy routes that tolerate curtailment. If that fails the KDP-0B economics gate, use D-02's pre-committed "0A only, Earth supply".

## 6. Where others might disagree

1. **Scope.** GPT's inclusion of microgrid power management sets the floor at TRL 3. A storage-only rating would be TRL 4–5. Both views should stay on record.
2. **"It's TRL 6 already."** One can argue that Li-ion is TRL 9 and MWh is just paralleling. I reject this on scale ratio (~2–100×), the NEA-surface environment and fault propagation. A reasonable assessor might rate T20-S at 5.
3. **Risk class.** Gemini's continuous-furnace route, with surface arrays on a slow rotator, makes storage close to project-ending. My architecture-change rating assumes curtailment or stand-off is acceptable.
4. **The cycle requirement.** Claude's 9k–33k figure is conditional on surface siting. If the panel assumes stand-off arrays, the binding requirement becomes outage energy and power, not cycles, and the high-end mass problem largely disappears.
5. **The mass figures.** These are my arithmetic, not data. The 100–150 Wh/kg space pack range and the DoD-versus-cycles assumption are both contestable by roughly ±30–50%.
