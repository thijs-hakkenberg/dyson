# TRL assessment: T01, MW-class SEP thruster and PPU cluster

## 0. Scope, assumptions and the electrical interface

**What is rated.** The rating covers the thruster modules, PPUs (or direct-drive units), propellant feed, cathodes and cluster integration that turn 1.0–2.5 MWe of array power into thrust. The array, its deployment and the radiators are separate entries. Their interfaces with the thruster cluster are defined here because GPT asked for that.

**Assumptions:**

- **A1. Duty means per-thruster operating hours.** I read the 20,000–40,000 h duty as the life each thruster must deliver, assuming no in-flight swap. If robotic thruster replacement at the NEA or SEL1 is adopted, the per-thruster requirement drops. Section 6 covers this.
- **A2. Module size.** Modules are 100–250 kW (Claude's proposal). A tug therefore carries 4–25 operating modules plus spares.
- **A3. KDP-0B date.** The date is not stated anywhere I have. I assume KDP-0B falls about 2033–2036. If it is earlier, the schedule conclusion in section 3 gets worse.
- **A4. Krypton.** It is not one of the three required branches. It is the best-documented high-power alternative to xenon (see the X3 tests below), so dropping it should be a recorded decision rather than an omission.

**Derived per-thruster throughput.** The figure below uses ṁ = 2ηP/(g₀Isp)², with η = 0.55–0.65.

| Case | Throughput per thruster |
|---|---|
| 100 kW, 3,000 s, 20,000 h | ≈ 11 t |
| 250 kW, 3,000 s, 40,000 h | ≈ 54 t |
| 250 kW, 2,000 s, 40,000 h | ≈ 110 t |

The required throughput is therefore **~10–110 t per thruster**. This number, more than power, is what makes the step hard.

**Proposed electrical interface (array to PPU).** This is my proposal, for the panel to accept or amend.

| Parameter | Proposed value | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Bus architecture | One independent array string per thruster module, 100–250 kW each, with cross-strapping only at the fault-isolation level | Contains faults and simplifies cluster throttling |
| Input voltage | 300–1,000 V high-voltage bus. Two options: (a) direct-drive, where array voltage ≈ discharge voltage (≈300–800 V for Xe/I₂ at 2,000–3,000 s) and a small PPU supplies only magnets, cathode and keeper; (b) regulated PPU from the HV bus | At 250 kW, a 120–160 V bus means kA-class currents and prohibitive harness mass |
| PPU efficiency and loss | ≥ 0.95. Loss is 5–12.5 kWth per 250 kW unit, rejected by the bus radiator | Claude's "PPU losses only" heat case |
| Thruster loss to bus | To be measured. At η≈0.6, roughly 0.4–1.0 MWth of thruster loss per tug; the fraction conducted to the bus is unknown | GPT's position. The 50–800 kWth range in the architecture spans both cases |
| Dynamic interface | Discharge filter sized for Hall breathing-mode oscillations (≈10–30 kHz). Current-ripple and arc-fault specification on HV strings | Plume plasma near HV arrays raises arcing risk |
| Telemetry and command | Per-module throttle (power and flow), magnet current, cathode health, cumulative throughput counter | Supports total-impulse accounting |

**Corrections to the March 2026 entry:**

- **AEPS has not flown.** It is not TRL 8–9.
- **3,570 h is not life validation.** It is a wear test that revealed a new failure mode, pole erosion.
- **NEXT and Starlink data are at 4–7 kW.** They do not show anything about a 100–250 kW module.
- **The "30,000 h" figure in the rq-0-54 re-assessment is unsourced.** I cannot identify a 30,000 h *demonstrated* magnetically shielded Hall test. It may be a projected life or a requirement. It should be sourced or struck.

## 2. Current TRL: 2–4 (point estimate 3)

The system is rated by its weakest required element at the reference propellant. Branches are kept separate, per GPT.

| Element / branch | TRL | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Xe Hall module at 100–250 kW | 3–4 | ~100 kW lab operation (X3, possibly XR-100), but no MS design, no life data, no relevant-environment definition at this scale |
| Ar Hall module at 100–250 kW | 2–3 (up to 4 if VASIMR is counted) | Flight heritage only at ~4 kW. I know of no 100 kW-class argon Hall test. VASIMR is a different device |
| I₂ Hall module at 100–250 kW (reference) | 2–3 | Lab data ≤ ~10 kW, flight ~W. Cathode/insert compatibility and feed-line condensation are unresolved at high current |
| PPU / direct-drive at 100–250 kW | 2–3 | No PPU validated above ~13 kW that I can cite |
| MW cluster integration (4–25 modules) | 2 | Documented concept only. Never operated, and probably not testable at full power in any existing facility |
| Life (10–110 t per thruster) | 2–3 for every branch | Best throughput demonstrated is ~0.9 t (NEXT, 7 kW, Xe) |

**Lower bound (2) is met.** Documented concepts addressing feasibility and benefit exist for MW-class Hall/SEP cargo vehicles and for iodine propellant.

**Upper bound (4) is reached only for the Xe module, and only for performance.** It rests on short-duration ~100 kW laboratory operation.

**Unmet exit criterion.**

- *For TRL 4 at the system level and for the I₂ reference:* there is no documented laboratory breadboard test of a 100–250 kW module with its PPU, in the reference propellant, showing performance *and wear rate* that agree with analytical predictions. There is also no documented definition of the relevant environment: the duty cycle, throughput per thruster, cluster interaction, plume deposition and bus heat path.
- *For TRL 5 on the Xe branch:* no wear data at ≥100 kW, and no documented scaling requirements from module to cluster.

## 3. Target and difficulty

**Target: TRL 6 at KDP-0B, no waivers** (D-02). rq-0-54 classes tug life and thermal design as project-ending for the 0B transport chain. Internal 0A milestones:

- **TRL 4:** module breadboard per branch at 0A mid-term.
- **TRL 5:** wear-validated module, needed before the propellant down-select (D-05) closes.

**Defining TRL 6 for this technology** (my proposal):

1. A prototype 100–250 kW module with its flight-like PPU is operated in vacuum. The test runs for ≥ 25–50% of required throughput, with a wear model validated against measured erosion, including pole erosion and cathode wear.
2. Facility effects are bounded by background-pressure sweeps.
3. A multi-module cluster of ≥2–4 modules demonstrates thruster-to-thruster and PPU interactions, with documented scaling to the full cluster.
4. Thermal-vacuum measurement of heat conducted into the bus is completed.
5. Plume deposition on array coupons is measured for the selected propellant.

Full 1.5× total-impulse qualification is a TRL 8 activity. That means 30,000–60,000 h, or 3.4–6.8 years of continuous running.

**Schedule clash.** Under A3, KDP-0B is about 7–10 years from programme start. TRL 6 is achievable in that time only if the facility and module work start in the first year of 0A. The I₂ reference branch is the likeliest to miss.

**AD² = 6** (several competing approaches; the path is not clear; ≈60% success). Reasons:

- The propellant is unselected.
- Iodine cathode and materials compatibility at high current is an open problem.
- Pole erosion replaced channel erosion as the life limiter and has not been characterised at high power.
- Ground facilities at 100–250 kW continuous, especially for iodine (corrosion, cryopump loading), may not exist.
- A 1–2.5 MW cluster may never be testable on the ground.

AD² by branch:

| Branch | AD² | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Xe | 4–5 | Similar development has been done at lower power |
| Ar | 5 | Path is clear but efficiency is lower at 2,000–3,000 s |
| I₂ | 6–7 | Several unknowns listed above |

## 5. Risk and fallback

**Risk class: project-ending for 0B.** No propellant branch may reach TRL 6 at ≥100 kW with validated life by KDP-0B. In that case the 0B transport chain does not close, and D-02 triggers the pre-committed fallback: "0A only, Earth supply."

Partial failures are architecture changes, not project-ending:

| Failure | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Iodine fails | Switch to argon or xenon. Xenon brings the supply question (rq-0-53) |
| Life falls short of 20,000–40,000 h | Robotic thruster swap at the NEA or SEL1, with spares carried |
| 250 kW modules fail | More 50–100 kW modules, with heavier PPU/harness and a larger cluster |

**Fallback:** Down-select to whichever propellant branch reaches wear-validated TRL 5 first, carry swappable spare modules to cover the life shortfall. If no branch reaches TRL 6 by KDP-0B, take the D-02 "0A only, Earth supply" exit.

## 6. Where others might disagree

- **The life requirement drives the AD².** Claude (≥10,000 h) and Gemini (5,000 h at ≥100 kWe) would accept shorter per-thruster life with replacement. Under those assumptions, throughput per thruster falls to ~3–30 t and AD² arguably drops to 5. GPT's total-impulse framing, which I adopt, is the stricter case.
- **Is a ground vacuum chamber a relevant environment at 100–250 kW?** NASA practice accepts ground life tests for EP at TRL 6. However, facility effects on Hall thrusters, such as pressure-dependent performance and pole erosion, grow with flow rate. Some will insist on an in-space module test before KDP-0B, which would make that test TRL 7 in practice.
- **Upper bound of 4.** One could argue the Xe module is still TRL 3, because X3 is a nested-channel design, not magnetically shielded, and was not run at MS conditions. One could also argue argon deserves 4 on VASIMR evidence. I keep both inside the range rather than average them.
- **Risk class.** Because the fallbacks are real, some will class this as architecture-change rather than project-ending. That holds only for single-branch failure. It is project-ending only if all branches fail.
- **Iodine as reference.** Given the evidence, iodine is the least mature branch. Making it the reference while xenon and krypton have the high-power heritage is a supply-driven choice, not a maturity-driven one. That choice is contestable while rq-0-53 remains open.
