# TRL assessment: T25, target-local and SEL1 cargo-node navigation and control

## Scope and assumptions

The requirement is mostly TBD, so I fixed working values from the revised architecture. Each one is open to challenge.

| Parameter | Working value | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Payload per transfer | 150–300 t | Architecture |
| Stack mass at the node (payload + tug + residual propellant) | ~200–450 t | Assumes tug dry mass 10–75 t at 10–30 kg/kWe. This is my estimate, not project data. |
| Array power and area | 1–2.5 MWe. Roughly 3,000 m² (1 MWe at 1 AU) up to ~12,000 m² (2.5 MWe at 1.3 AU), at ~200–330 W/m² | My calculation |
| EP thrust and acceleration | ~35–170 N, giving ~7×10⁻⁵ to 7×10⁻⁴ m/s² | Thrust = 2ηP/(g₀·Isp) with η = 0.5–0.65 at 2,000–3,000 s |
| Traffic per node | ~2–17 arrivals/yr | 0.6–2.5 kt/yr ÷ 150–300 t, consistent with 10–18 tugs on 1.5–5 yr round trips |
| Lifetime traffic | ~30–180 proximity campaigns per node over 10–15 yr | Derived from the line above |
| Light time | SEL1 ~5 s one-way. NEA at 0.9–1.3 AU heliocentric gives up to ~20 min one-way, plus solar-conjunction outages. | Geometry |

**What T25 includes:**
- relative navigation;
- proximity guidance, navigation and control (GNC) of the massive, flexible stack;
- stationkeeping and hovering;
- collision avoidance, including against the site's own debris;
- capture, contact, mate and separation dynamics for payload hand-off.

**What T25 excludes:**
- lifting product from the NEA surface to the tug;
- the propulsion string (EP thrusters and power).

### Errors in the related RQs that this rating does not inherit

- **rq-0-17 acceleration.** It sizes attachment loads at 0.01–0.1 m/s². The stated power and Isp give ~10⁻⁴ m/s², which is 15–1,500× lower. Sustained cargo loads under EP are therefore tens of newtons, not 2–20 kN. The design-driving loads are contact and RCS transients, not thrust.
- **rq-0-17 array area.** It assumes 300–500 m². A 1–2.5 MWe array is roughly 6–40× larger. Flexible-body coupling during proximity operations is a first-order issue, not a detail.
- **rq-0-19 fleet result.** Its "15×150 t optimal" is a model-generated simulation output. It is not evidence and carries no TRL weight.

### What has never been demonstrated, at any TRL

- Autonomous capture of a body above ~20 t.
- Any rendezvous at a Lagrange point.
- Proximity operations by a vehicle carrying MW-class flexible arrays.
- Proximity operations by a vehicle whose only main propulsion is ~10⁻⁴ m/s² EP.
- Navigation within an artificial, continuously generated debris environment, such as a mining site producing 13–47 kt/yr of tailings.

### Not evidence

- Gateway/NRHO rendezvous planning. It has not flown.
- The rq-0-17 and rq-0-19 trade discussions. These are design-study material.

## 2. Current TRL: 2–3

**Lower bound, TRL 2.** The exit criterion is met, though thinly. The concept and its benefit are documented in the round-2 architecture and rq-0-17: autonomous docking, berthing backup, or a dedicated handling craft. Node structure, orbit and traffic remain TBD.

**Upper bound, TRL 3.** I allow this only if the assessor accepts that heritage-validated models already cover the key parameters at scale. Those models are APAS/NDS contact dynamics, ATV/Dragon GNC and small-body navigation, all of whose flight data agreed with prediction. That is a defensible but generous reading.

**Unmet for TRL 3.** No project document gives analytical or experimental results validating predictions of the key parameters at stack scale:
- capture envelope;
- contact momentum and loads;
- passive-safety and abort Δv under ~10⁻⁴ m/s² authority;
- array–controller interaction;
- proximity-operations propellant;
- collision probability in the site debris field.

**Blocked for TRL 4.** The exit criterion requires a documented definition of the relevant environment. That cannot exist while node structure, traffic rate, SEL1 orbit family and the site debris environment are undefined. The debris environment depends on D-03.

### Sub-function view

## 3. Target and difficulty

**Is it project-ending?** I classify T25 as architecture-change, not project-ending, because delivery can be redesigned around it (see §5). Under D-02 that gives:

| Milestone | Target TRL | What it requires |
|---|---|---|
| 0A mid-term review | 3, plus a documented relevant-environment definition | Analysis and simulation complete; environment written down |
| KDP-0B | 5 | The tug design and interface standard are frozen here. Assumed date ~2031–2034, my assumption; KDP-0B is unscheduled. |
| Tug CDR | 6 | Before the first operational arrival, which is 1.5–5 yr after first launch |

If the panel removes all fallbacks (a payload-transfer-only architecture), T25 becomes project-ending and the KDP-0B target rises to TRL 6 with no waivers.

**AD² = 5** for the step from 2–3 to 5. Similar developments have been done: autonomous docking, ~100 t piloted docking, autonomous small-body operations. The combination required here has not: autonomous, low-thrust, MW flexible arrays, 200–450 t, deep-space latency and self-generated debris. The path is nonetheless clear:

1. scaling analysis;
2. heritage-calibrated contact models;
3. hardware-in-the-loop (HIL) tests;
4. small-mass flight demonstration on 0A assets.

Sub-element difficulty varies:

| Sub-element | AD² | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| SEL1 operations | 3 | Well within the experience base |
| Contact at mass | 4–5 | Similar work done at lower mass |
| Flexible-array GNC | 5 | Not done at this scale |
| Debris avoidance | 5–6 | Rises to 6 if D-03 leaves the debris source term uncharacterised |

**Interface with D-05 (propellant selection).** Proximity abort cannot rely on the EP main engines. A secondary RCS is needed.
- Iodine cannot double as cold gas.
- Argon could act as a common-propellant cold-gas RCS at low Isp (~50 s, approximate).
- This should be fed into D-05.

## 5. Risk and fallback

**Risk class: architecture-change.** Failing to reach target does not stop delivery, but it reshapes the delivery chain. The fallbacks, in order of preference:

1. **Integral payload.** The product stays on the tug's hardpoints. The node holds no mated hand-off; it accepts slow robotic offload or parks the whole tug-plus-payload.
2. **Small containers.** Use ~10–30 t containers handled by heritage-class mechanisms. This multiplies transfer count by ~5–30×, but at ~2–17 arrivals/yr the traffic is tolerable.
3. **Dedicated handling craft.** Gemini's option of a dedicated cargo-handling spacecraft at the node.
4. **No node.** Drop the SEL1 node and deliver directly to the Phase 1 consumer.

The most severe credible failure is a 300 t mis-capture at the node, which risks the tug (stated in rq-0-17 at $150–250M each) and the node. This argues for a passive-safe approach design whatever the TRL.

## 6. Where others might disagree

- **"It is all TRL 9 heritage, so rate it 4–6."** The strongest counter-case. Every sub-function has flown and ~100 t docking exists. My objection: that 100 t docking was crew-piloted in LEO, and the integration of autonomy, low thrust, MW flexible arrays, 200–450 t mass and a self-made debris field is unvalidated. Requirements are also undefined, so the TRL 4 exit is formally unmet.
- **Project-ending versus architecture-change.** If the panel treats payload transfer as essential to 0B economics, the target becomes TRL 6 at KDP-0B. That would likely force the step-6 demonstration before KDP-0B and add several years.
- **Scope.** If the architecture adopts integral-payload delivery, contact dynamics leaves T25. The remaining function (navigation, hovering, stationkeeping) rates ~4–5 today.
- **Debris avoidance.** Whether this is a T25 GNC problem or a D-03 licensing and environment problem. Its TRL cannot rise until the escaped-mass source term is characterised.
- **Assumed stack mass.** If tug dry mass is much higher than my 10–75 t estimate, all ratios worsen.
