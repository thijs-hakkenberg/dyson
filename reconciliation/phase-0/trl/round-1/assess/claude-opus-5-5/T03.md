# T03: MW-class waste-heat rejection (plant and tug)

## 0. Assumptions and split

**How T03 is split.** I rate four elements separately. Plant and tug differ by more than an order of magnitude in heat load and in heritage. The tug range of 50–800 kWth also mixes two different problems:

- **T03-P: plant heat rejection.** Rated per metallurgy route: P-NM (no-melt, carbonyl or H₂), P-RS (rotating smelter) and P-NA (narrow native-metal).
- **T03-T1: tug PPU and avionics pumped loop.** This loop removes about 4–6 % of electrical power, which is 50–150 kWth for a 1.0–2.5 MWe tug, at 320–370 K.
- **T03-T2: thruster self-radiation.** This is 25–40 % of electrical power, which is 250–1,000 kWth, radiated from the thruster bodies at roughly 500–800 K.

The 800 kWth upper bound only makes sense if it includes thruster losses. Those losses are radiated passively by the thrusters. They do not pass through a radiator loop, so they belong to the propulsion rating, not to T03 proper. **I recommend splitting T03 into T03-P and T03-T in the register.** The single YAML block at the end carries the plant, which is the governing, lowest-TRL element. The tug ratings are stated in the text and in the evidence entries.

**Radiator sizing.** Two-sided panel, ε = 0.85, effective sink 200 K. The panel must not view the sunlit NEA surface, which is 300–390 K at 1 AU. At 300 K that view would erase most of the net rejection.

| Radiator T | Net flux (two-sided) | Area for 1 MWth | Area for 9 MWth |
|---|---|---|---|
| 300 K | ~630 W/m² | ~1,600 m² | ~14,000 m² |
| 350 K | ~1,290 W/m² | ~770 m² | ~7,000 m² |
| 400 K | ~2,300 W/m² | ~430 m² | ~3,900 m² |
| 600 K | ~12,300 W/m² | ~80 m² | ~730 m² |

At an assumed 3–10 kg/m² for the radiator plus its share of loop and structure, a 9 MWth, 300 K plant radiator weighs 40–140 t. That mass feeds directly into D-02's economics gate. **The rejection temperature, which the route sets, matters more than the MW figure.**

**Plant heat cap (Claude's point).** Plant heat equals site electrical power (2.5–8 MW) plus any solar-thermal input to furnaces. A solar-thermal smelter could therefore exceed 9 MWth. The 9 MWth ceiling is valid only for all-electric routes. A sanity check supports the range: heating and melting 15–50 kt/yr of ore at about 1.5 MJ/kg gives 0.7–2.4 MW average for sensible and fusion heat alone, before losses.

**Other assumptions.**
- KDP-0B falls about 2032–2035. This is my assumption; no date is given.
- Plant life is 5–15 yr uncrewed with no EVA repair.
- Tug life covers multiple 1.5–5 yr round trips, about 10–15 yr in total.

**Plant overall: 3–4.** This is the governing element.

I do not credit modular scaling, meaning "a MW radiator is just N panels", above TRL 4. The panel-scale physics scales linearly. Deployment, structure, manifolding, leak and micrometeoroid isolation over 10⁴ m², and dust exposure do not.

## 3. Target and difficulty

**Targets.**
- **Plant: TRL 6 at KDP-0B, for the selected route only, with an internal 0A milestone of TRL 4–5 at the 0A mid-term review.**
  - Strictly, failure is an architecture change: the plant power is downsized. But heat-rejection mass and power cap throughput.
  - A plant that cannot reject 1–9 MWth at the route's temperature cannot pass D-02's economics gate.
  - So I treat it as project-ending for 0B, which under D-02 means no waiver.
  - Needed by about 2032–2035, my assumed KDP-0B date.
- **Tug T1: TRL 6 at KDP-0B** (AD² 3), because tugs deliver all 0B product.
- **Tug T2: TRL 6 at KDP-0B,** carried jointly with the EP thruster technology.

**AD² for the plant: 5, range 4–6.**
- The path is clear: modular heat-pipe or pumped panels, known coolants, existing large vacuum chambers.
- But nothing similar has been done at 10–100× scale, uncrewed for 10+ yr, beside a regolith-processing plant.
- By route:
  - P-NM and P-NA: AD² 5. The difficulty is low temperature, hence large area and mass, and condensing heat exchangers in microgravity.
  - P-RS: AD² 6. It depends on either a novel high-cycle rotary fluid joint or co-rotating radiators under spin load.
- Tug: T1 is AD² 3–4. T2 is AD² 4–5.

**Coolant and temperature by band.**

| Band | Coolant / transport | Leak isolation |
|---|---|---|
| 250–350 K | Ammonia pumped loop (ISS heritage) | Pumped loops need segmentation valves |
| 350–500 K | Water heat pipes, titanium or Monel, or an organic pumped loop | Heat pipes give per-pipe isolation (preferred for micrometeoroid and debris tolerance) |
| 500–800 K | NaK or Na/K heat pipes (SNAP and TOPAZ heritage) | Heat pipes give per-pipe isolation |

## 5. Risk and fallback

- **Plant:** project-ending for 0B through the economics gate. The technical failure mode is architecture change.
  - **Fallback:** cap plant thermal power at what a demonstrated segment design rejects within the mass budget.
  - Prefer routes and unit operations that reject heat at ≥500 K, or radiate hot slag and product directly.
  - If delivered $/kg then fails, fall back per D-02 to "0A only, Earth supply".
- **Tug T1:** schedule-delay or cost-increase. The fallback is ISS-derived heavier ammonia loops, at a specific-mass penalty.
- **Tug T2:** architecture change. The fallback is more thrusters at lower power each, or higher-efficiency thrusters to cut waste heat.

## 6. Where others might disagree

1. **Modular scaling.** Gemini or GPT may argue that MW plant radiators are "N × proven panels" and rate them 4–5. I hold 3–4 because deployment, manifold and leak-isolation behaviour over 10⁴ m², and dust next to the plant, are untested.
2. **ISS heritage for the tug.** One could rate T1 at 6–7 on ISS flight heritage. I cap it at 6 because ISS depended on EVA repair (E2), and the tug has a lower mass budget.
3. **Thermal ranking of routes.** Plausibly, the rotating smelter has the *easier* plant-level heat problem, since high-temperature rejection needs about 1/10–1/20 the area. Its rotary joint or spin structure is the harder part. The no-melt route has the hardest area and mass problem but no rotation. Which risk dominates is contestable.
4. **Risk class.** Calling the plant project-ending, rather than architecture-change, is a judgement that the economics gate binds. If Phase 1 demand tolerates a smaller plant, it reverts to architecture-change and a TRL 5 target.
5. **Unverified evidence.** E9 and E11 are unverified. The TOPAZ and SNAP-10A thermal figures are approximate.
