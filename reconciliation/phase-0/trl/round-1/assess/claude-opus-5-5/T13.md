# TRL assessment: T13, asteroid water extraction pilot (`isru-water-extraction`)

## 0. Scope and what changed since March 2026

**What is rated.** The rating covers the integrated chain that turns C-type regolith into stored, purified liquid water at an NEA:

feed acquisition → containment → thermal dehydration → vapour capture → purification → storage

The required output is 10–100 t/yr of water.

**What is not rated.** Electrolysis, LOX/LH₂ and water-EP thrusters are outside this technology. The revised architecture rules out production propellant in Phase 0. Rq-0-33 (microgravity electrolysis) therefore does not bear on this rating. It matters only if rq-0-55 later selects a water-EP route that needs electrolysis, and most water-EP concepts (resistojet, electrothermal, RF/plasma) do not.

**Three corrections to the March entry.**
1. **Target TRL 7 and "project-ending" no longer apply.** The 0B product is Fe-Ni, and the tugs are iodine or argon (D-05). Nothing in the 0B baseline consumes water.
2. **The lunar-poles fallback contradicts the architecture.** There is no production propellant in Phase 0, so there is nothing for lunar water to substitute for.
3. **Two items in the evidence list are not technology evidence.** The Paper 05 Monte Carlo is a design study, so it cannot support TRL ≥ 3. The Bennu sample is evidence about the *resource* and the feed mineralogy, not about *extraction*.

**Site implication (Claude's condition).** CI and CM chondrites carry almost no native Fe-Ni. A C-type pilot is therefore almost certainly a separate site, with its own:
- power (estimated 5–200 kWth, below);
- feed handling and containment;
- debris licence under D-03.

The only plausible co-location case is a CR-like body, which is both metal-bearing and hydrated, but carries less water. I assign a low prior to finding one among accessible NEAs. This is my judgement, not a survey result.

**Working assumptions.** The inputs do not state these, so they are mine:

| Quantity | Assumed value | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Recoverable water yield | 4–12 wt% of feed | Heating to ~400–800 °C. The total H₂O-equivalent in CI material is higher, but not all of it is recoverable at practical temperatures without excessive SO₂/CO₂ co-release. |
| Feed | ~80–2,500 t/yr (≈0.2–7 t/day averaged) | Follows from the yield and the 10–100 t/yr output |
| Thermal energy | ~10–20 MJ/kg water before losses; 15–60 MJ/kg with losses | Sensible heat ≈0.5–0.7 MJ/kg ore, plus dehydroxylation of the order of a few MJ/kg water |
| Average thermal power | ~5–200 kWth | Peak is higher at a 9–25% duty cycle |
| Duration | ≥1 yr continuous campaign within a 3–5 yr site life | Not specified in the requirement |

**Purpose (Claude) and purity specification (GPT).** Neither is defined. This matters directly for the rating, because the TRL 4 exit criterion requires a "documented definition of the relevant environment". In practice that includes the feed specification and the product specification. Neither exists.

### What the evidence does and does not show

**Not demonstrated anywhere, to my knowledge:**
- continuous processing of hydrated regolith at the required rate;
- operation under vacuum *and* in microgravity;
- vapour capture from an open or partially sealed granular bed in microgravity;
- fines-laden condensate purification to a stated specification;
- runs on feed with representative organic, sulfur and salt chemistry, rather than only on simulant.

**On the related research questions:**
- **Rq-0-40.** Its literature review frames volatile loss as ice sublimation. That is largely misdirected for an NEA at ~1 AU, where the water is in phyllosilicates and is stable to several hundred °C. Frictional excavation heating is a second-order loss for bound water, though loosely adsorbed water may be lost early. The Interamnia ice result it cites concerns a main-belt body and does not apply to NEAs.
- **Rq-0-33.** Its "500–750 kW" figure is from the abandoned architecture.

## 2. Current TRL: 3–4

The rating splits by element.

| Element | TRL | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Dehydration chemistry and yield prediction | 3, approaching 4 | E1 and E7 provide experimental results that validate the key parameter (water release versus temperature) |
| Integrated feed-handling, capture and purification chain | 3 at best | No breadboard has been tested against predictions on a defined feed |

**Lower bound, TRL 3: met.** The TRL 3 exit criterion is "documented experimental results validating predictions of key parameters". E1 meets it on water release versus temperature for real meteorites, supported by simulant work (E7) and small vacuum demos (E8, E9).

**Unmet for TRL 4 (at the lower bound). Three gaps:**
1. **No breadboard test on a defined feed.** There is no documented breadboard extractor, run on feed of defined mineralogy, whose yield and condensate composition agree with prediction. The simulants used likely lack Bennu-like organic, NH₃ and evaporite content.
2. **No product specification.** There is no documented definition of the relevant environment *including* the purity specification. This cannot exist until the pilot's purpose is stated.
3. **No purification breadboard.** None has been validated on CI-derived condensate.

**Upper bound, TRL 4.** This is credible only if E8 and E9 are accepted as breadboard validation of the extraction step alone. Even then, two criteria remain unmet:
- **For TRL 5:** a component or breadboard validated in the relevant environment. Nothing has been tested combining vacuum, microgravity granular handling and containment, thermal cycling at 0.9–1.3 AU, and charged abrasive fines over meaningful duration. Parabolic and drop-tower time (seconds) is too short for a heat-up, dehydration and capture cycle.
- **For TRL 5:** a documented definition of scaling requirements.

**Scale gap.**
- The best demonstrated rates I can confirm are grams to perhaps kg per test.
- The required rate is ~0.2–7 t/day of feed.
- The gap is therefore roughly **10²–10⁴ on rate**, with no continuous-duration demonstration at all.

## 3. Target and difficulty

**Target TRL at KDP-0B: 5.** The reasoning under D-02:
- The pilot is not project-ending for 0B, so TRL 6 without waivers is not required.
- The internal-milestone band of 4–5 applies.
- Because the KDP-0B decision is to *commit a flight pilot*, I set the top of that band.
- The pilot then functions as the TRL 6–7 demonstration at reduced scale.

**Escalation clause.** If rq-0-55 selects water EP as a Phase 1 transport route, water becomes project-critical and the target rises to **TRL 6 at KDP-0B**.

**Needed by.** KDP-0B. The inputs give no date; I assume roughly 2031–2034. Two conditions precede it:
- a C-type target sampled under the D-02 target gate;
- a written purpose and purity specification, which should be in hand by the 0A mid-term review.

**AD² = 5** (new development, not done before, path clear, ~70% success).
- The chemistry is understood and the process steps (oven, cold trap, staged purification) are conventional.
- Microgravity granular containment and vapour capture from a fines-laden bed have never been done, so AD² 4 overstates heritage.
- **For a step to TRL 6 the rating would be AD² 6.** Three routes compete with no clear winner: excavate-and-oven, optical mining, and in-place bagged or capped heating. Each has a different failure mode: seal and abrasion, optics fouling, and capture efficiency respectively.

## 5. Risk and fallback

**Risk if it fails to reach target: cost-increase.**
- No 0B baseline element depends on water. The Fe-Ni product, the iodine/argon tugs and the uncrewed operations all proceed without it.
- What is lost is the option value of in-space water, plus any sunk R&D.
- This becomes **architecture-change** only if rq-0-55 makes water EP part of the Phase 1 transport plan.

**Fallback.** Drop or defer the pilot:
- keep the iodine/argon tug reference (D-05);
- hold water extraction at ground TRL 4 as a Phase 1 option;
- if water is ever needed in Phase 0, supply it from Earth in tonne quantities.

Lunar polar water is not a meaningful fallback under the revised architecture.

## 6. Where others might disagree

1. **Should the pilot be in Phase 0 at all?** I tag it 0B (conditional) because the architecture names it. A stricter reading of Claude's "purpose to be stated" and GPT's analysis in rq-0-55 would tag it **deferred**: with no customer, a separate site and its licence are hard to justify. I would accept "deferred" if no purpose is written by the 0A mid-term review.
2. **The TRL 4 upper bound.**
   - *Towards higher:* proponents of the Honeybee and TransAstra demos could argue TRL 4–5, treating a vacuum chamber as relevant for dehydration chemistry. That is defensible for the chemistry alone, because gravity matters little to dehydroxylation.
   - *Why I hold 3–4:* the binding risks are microgravity feed containment, capture and purification, none of which have been tested.
   - *Towards lower:* a stricter assessor would put the upper bound at 3, because the extraction demos are not documented against predictions (E8 and E9 are marked not confident).
3. **Risk class.**
   - Claude's view that water EP is the only route to an ISPP customer, if adopted, makes this architecture-change.
   - Gemini's 1,500 t/yr industrial water would also make it architecture-change, and that rate is 15–150× the pilot. At that scale this rating does not apply. The step from pilot to 1,500 t/yr would carry its own AD² of at least 5.
4. **Yield.** The 4–12 wt% recoverable figure is my assumption. Using total H₂O-equivalent (higher) halves the feed estimate. Excluding high-temperature release (to avoid SO₂/CO₂) could push it below 4%. GPT's demand for a defined yield is unmet, and the feed mass, power and tailings estimates move by ~3× on this alone.
5. **Bennu as reference feed.** An extraction pilot target is not necessarily Bennu-like. CM-like or more thermally altered C-complex NEAs would have less water and different contaminants. The rating assumes the KDP-0B target gate supplies sampled mineralogy.
