# TRL assessment: T15 Site power generation and transfer

**Summary.** T15 is **TRL 2–3 at the required scale**. Solar cells and blankets are flight-proven, but nobody has generated or distributed 2.5–8 MW in space, on or near a small body, or at kV over km-scale links. The surface vs free-flyer vs beamed decision is still open, which itself caps the rating.

**Scope.** T15 covers generation, power management and distribution (PMAD) at the NEA processing site, and transfer from generation to loads. Transfer includes the trunk feeding the 1–3 kV robot tethers. Thermal rejection (1–9 MWth) and storage (0.5–15 MWh) are interfaces, not rated here. The 1–2.5 MWe tug arrays are a separate technology, but they share the array module, and I treat that as a lever.

**The related RQs are stale.** RQ-0-21, RQ-0-22 and RQ-0-25 assume 100 MW at L4/L5 for the central plant that D-01 and the revised architecture abandoned. Their L4/L5 radiation framing no longer applies. The site is at an NEA in heliocentric orbit at 0.9–1.3 AU. That is the ordinary interplanetary environment, for which deep-space III-V heritage exists. Errors in those reviews are listed at the end of Section 6.

## 0. Assumptions used for scaling

These are my calculations, not evidence.

**Flux and array area**
- Flux swings 1,680–805 W/m² across 0.9–1.3 AU, a 2.1× ratio.
- Sizing for aphelion at 8 MW requires the following, with all factors assumed:
  - triple-junction (TJ) efficiency about 30% at operating temperature;
  - end-of-life (EOL) factor about 0.8;
  - packing and PMAD factor about 0.85.
- That gives about 165 W/m² delivered, so roughly **15,000 m² at 2.5 MW and 48,000 m² at 8 MW** for sun-tracking free-flyers.
- This is about **5–17 MW of beginning-of-life (BOL) 1-AU-equivalent rating**. At an assumed 100–200 W/kg array-level specific power, the array alone is **about 25–170 t**.

**Surface arrays on a rotating NEA**
- Typical periods are 2–10+ h; many rubble piles sit above the ~2.2 h spin barrier.
- Arrays are dark for about half of each rotation.
- Continuous load needs about L·P/2 of storage. At 8 MW and P = 8 h that is about 32 MWh, which exceeds the 15 MWh ceiling in the revised architecture.
- So **the storage specification implicitly favours free-flyers, fast rotators, or night-side curtailment of processing.** This is an unrecognised coupling worth stating in the trade.

**Free-flyer dynamics** (worked example: 500 m body, density ~2 g/cm³, array 100 t and 40,000 m² at 1 km)
- Solar radiation pressure is about 0.4 N.
- Asteroid gravity is about 0.9 N.
- These are the same order. Array area-to-mass (~0.4 m²/kg) is about 10× or more that of OSIRIS-REx-class spacecraft.

Consequences:
- Terminator-orbit techniques do not transfer directly to arrays this light for their area.
- The free-flyer must hover on electric propulsion or stand off. A 0.5 N hover for 15 yr at Isp 2,500 s needs about 10 t of propellant.
- A passive tether to the surface cannot easily be kept in tension:
  - on the sunward side, radiation pressure and gravity both push the array toward the body;
  - on the anti-sun side, the umbra extends about 50 km for a 500 m body.

**Cable mass** (8 MW, 2 km round trip, 2% resistive loss, aluminium)
- At 3 kV the conductor is about 14 t.
- At 10 kV it is about 1.2 t.
- Transfer voltage is therefore a first-order design variable, and kV-class in a regolith plasma sheath has no heritage.

**Beaming**
- Laser end-to-end efficiency DC→DC is plausibly 15–25%, meaning 30–50 MW of generation to deliver 8 MW, plus the matching waste heat.
- Microwave at 5.8 GHz over 20 km needs a transmitter-receiver aperture product of order 10³ m².
- Both are bad trades against a cable for a co-located site.

## 2. Current TRL

**T15 as a system: TRL 2–3.** Sub-elements are rated in the table below.

| Element / option | TRL now | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| PV cells and blankets in interplanetary 1 AU radiation | 8–9 (component) | E3–E5 |
| MW-class array, any configuration | 3–4 | Wing-level 20–30 kW flown; MW scaling not validated |
| kV-class MW DC PMAD and trunk (incl. 1–3 kV robot feed) in NEA plasma and dust | 2–3 | Terrestrial MVDC exists; E13 shows a known space gap |
| Option A: surface-mounted arrays on NEA | 2–3 | E9 dust data; no rubble-pile anchoring or rotation-cycle data |
| Option B: free-flying arrays, cabled | 2 | E7 not representative at high area-to-mass; no tethered-array analysis validated by data |
| Option C: free-flying arrays, beamed | 2 | Concept documented; critical function shown only at ~10⁹× below scale (E11, E12) |

**Exit met at the lower bound (TRL 2).** The concept and application are documented with feasibility and benefit. That is the architecture text plus the analyses in Section 0.

**Partially met (TRL 3).** Some key parameters are experimentally validated, but for other environments:
- dust on PV (E9);
- interplanetary cell degradation (E5);
- small-body navigation (E7).

**Unmet for TRL 3.** Key parameters for the selected configuration in the NEA environment have not been predicted and validated, either analytically or experimentally:
- (a) dust and ejecta flux on arrays near excavation;
- (b) radiation pressure versus gravity station-keeping for a high area-to-mass array;
- (c) arcing and insulation lifetime of kV conductors in a photoelectron sheath on abrasive regolith;
- (d) day/night storage versus load for the target's rotation.

**Unmet for TRL 4.** There is no documented definition of the relevant environment. That cannot be written until a target is sampled (D-02 target gate) and the scouts return dust, plasma and rotation data.

## 3. Target and difficulty

**Target.** TRL 6 at KDP-0B, no waivers. Site power is a necessary condition for any 0B production at a site, so it falls under D-02's top stringency tier.

**Internal 0A milestones**
- Option down-select at the 0A mid-term review.
- TRL 4 for array, PMAD and transfer breadboards at the 0A mid-term review.
- TRL 5 before the pathfinder's critical design review.

**When it is needed.** KDP-0B, whose date is not fixed. I assume 6–10 years after KDP-0A, about 2032–2036.

**What TRL 6 can mean here.** A full 2.5–8 MW system cannot be ground-tested. TRL 6 must therefore be claimed on a representative module plus validated scaling, as below.

- **Proposed module:** 0.25–1 MW repeating unit. This aligns with the 1–2.5 MWe tug array, so one development serves both.
- **Proposed flight subsystem:** 50–250 kW array, kV trunk and robot-tether load on the pathfinder.
- **Scaling requirements:** must be documented at TRL 5, as that level's exit criterion requires.
- **Scale ratio of the flight demonstration:** 10–160× below required, depending on combination:
  - 50 kW flight against 8 MW is about 160×;
  - 250 kW against 2.5 MW is about 10×.
- **Whether that counts as TRL 6:** contestable; see Section 6.

**AD² = 6 now.** Several competing approaches remain and the path is not clear, which is the literal definition. The approaches are surface arrays, cabled free-flyers and beamed free-flyers, crossed with transfer voltage and storage strategy.
- After a down-select to surface or cabled free-flyer, I would expect **AD² 5**. That is new development; similar has not been done at this scale or environment, but the path is clear because the parts exist.
- **Beamed** remains AD² 7. End-to-end efficiency and waste heat at MW are a fundamental engineering problem.

## 5. Risk and fallback

**Risk class: architecture-change.**

Generating MW from PV is not physically in doubt; any configuration fails into a redesign rather than an impossibility. Likely failure modes:
- kV transfer in regolith fails, so voltage drops and the site is redesigned with heavy cables;
- free-flyer dynamics prove unworkable, so the site moves to surface arrays with night curtailment;
- MW cost or mass is unaffordable, so power drops toward 2.5 MW and throughput falls.

**Escalation to project-ending for 0B.** If cost per watt kills the economics gate, the outcome becomes project-ending for 0B. At an assumed $100–500/W (unsure; space-array cost data is sparse), 5–17 MW BOL-equivalent is about **$0.5–8.5 B**. RQ-0-21's $5/W has no heritage support.

**Fallbacks, in order**
1. Run site loads from docked tug-class arrays. These are the same module, so this relies on the tug development.
2. Curtail to the 2.5 MW floor and batch the metallurgy to sunlit periods.
3. Fall back to "0A only, Earth supply" per D-02.

## 6. Where others might disagree

1. **The lower bound.** One can argue that arrays are modular and cells are TRL 9, so T15 is TRL 5–6 by aggregation. I reject this. The novel parts are system-level:
   - kV MW PMAD in plasma and dust;
   - the generation-to-load link;
   - the high area-to-mass dynamics or regolith anchoring.

   None of these is demonstrated, and the configuration is not chosen.
2. **What counts as TRL 6.** Some would accept a 50–250 kW flight subsystem as TRL 6 for 8 MW; others would require ≥1 MW in relevant conditions. At a 10–160× ratio, I think that call should be explicit at KDP-0B, not assumed.
3. **Risk class.** Some panel members may call this project-ending, since no power means no 0B. I hold that a configuration failure leads to redesign, and only the cost failure ends 0B.
4. **Dropping beaming.** Advocates will cite rapid laser-beaming progress (E12). At co-location distances, cable mass is 1–14 t against a 3–6× generation penalty for beaming. I see no Phase 0 case, but that conclusion is analysis, not test.
5. **Metallurgy coupling.** This should stay visible because it changes the requirement:
   - Smelting (Gemini) pushes toward 8 MW and steady load, and may need MW across rotating joints (E14 gap).
   - No-melt carbonyl or H₂ reduction (Claude) may sit near the low end and tolerate batching.
   - GPT's narrow native-metal route probably sits lowest.
6. **Errors in the inherited RQs** (sources I have not independently verified, including the arXiv IDs, are treated as unconfirmed):
   - The claim that STEREO used silicon cells is, I believe, wrong, though I am not certain. STEREO also drifted past L4/L5 rather than stationing there.
   - "No III-V data outside the magnetosphere" is wrong (E5).
   - $100–300/cm² cell cost is about an order of magnitude too high on a per-watt basis.
   - The 350 W/cm² waste heat at 500× should be about 45 W/cm².
   - The two reviews contradict each other on Lang et al. annealing temperatures (60 °C vs 100–200 °C).
   - Artemis and Gateway are not L4/L5 flight opportunities.
