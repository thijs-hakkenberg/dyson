# TRL assessment: T17, H₂ reduction of the reducible feed fraction with H₂ recycle

**Scope.** This assessment replaces the March 2026 `microgravity-metallurgy` entry for this technology only. That entry rated a melt-based route at a central station, which no longer exists. Its evidence (EML levitation, UMG-Si, slag work) and the attached rq-0-13 slag literature review do not bear on a no-melt gas–solid route, and I have not used them.

Two notes on the rq-0-13 review:
- Some of its statements should not be carried forward. Molten silicate slags are ionic conductors, not insulators. Its "natural density separation via Marangoni effects" claim is unsupported.
- Its main relevance here is indirect. A no-melt route avoids slag–metal separation but still has to handle 13–47 kt/yr of solid tailings.

## Requirement as I interpret it

The rating is pinned to these quantities:

| Quantity | Value | Basis / assumption |
|---|---|---|
| Fe(-Ni) reduced via the H₂ route | **0.1–2.5 kt/yr** | The share of the 0.6–2.5 kt/yr product that comes from reduction, rather than from native metal recovered by magnetic beneficiation alone, is unallocated. The upper bound assumes the whole product. |
| Product water to electrolyse | **~30–1,200 t/yr** | Stoichiometry gives 0.32 t H₂O per t Fe (FeO feed) to 0.48 t H₂O per t Fe (Fe₂O₃/Fe₃O₄ feed). Claude's "tens to hundreds of t/yr" sits inside this range. |
| H₂ circulated (consumed and regenerated) | ~36–54 kg per t Fe; ~4–135 t/yr | Same stoichiometry. |
| Electrolysis power | ~2–3 MWh per t Fe; ~25–850 kWe average | Assumes ~50–55 kWh/kg H₂. Reactor heating to 600–900 °C comes on top. This is a material but feasible share of the 2.5–8 MW site. |
| Environment | Microgravity at the NEA, vacuum outside the reactor, abrasive/charged fines, 0.9–1.3 AU thermal cycling, uncrewed | Gas–solid contact must not rely on gravity for bed formation, solids flow or gas/liquid separation. |
| Duration | ≥5 yr continuous operation | Assumed. The architecture gives no plant life for 0B. |
| Make-up H₂ | Must be imported, unless the target is C-type with the 10–100 t/yr water pilot | Loss channels: H₂S from troilite, HCl/HF, moisture in tailings, purge, leaks. Loss rate is unknown; my working assumption is 1–10 kg H₂ per t Fe, giving roughly 0.1–25 t/yr import. This is an assumption, not data. |

**Feed caveat that dominates everything.** Which fraction is "reducible" depends on the target class:
- **Ordinary-chondrite-like S-type.** Oxide rims on metal grains reduce easily. FeO bound in olivine and pyroxene reduces slowly and incompletely below ~1,000 °C. FeS is thermodynamically unfavourable to H₂ and yields H₂S.
- **CI/CM-like C-type.** Magnetite reduces readily. Phyllosilicates dehydrate and release volatiles.

The reducible fraction could therefore be anything from a few percent "polishing" to the main yield lever. This cannot be pinned down until the D-02 target gate (sampled grade) is passed.

**What does not exist, to my knowledge:**
- Any H₂ reduction reactor operated in microgravity or sustained partial gravity at any scale.
- Any reduction of asteroid meteorite or NEA-representative simulant feed with characterisation of H₂S/HCl/HF in the recycle loop.
- Any closed H₂ loop electrolysing dirty process water over more than ~1,000 h.

I may be missing small academic microgravity granular-flow experiments. None I know of involved reduction chemistry.

## 2. Current TRL: 3 (min) – 4 (max)

**Met at TRL 3.** E4 and E5 provide documented experimental results validating predictions of key parameters:
- reduction kinetics versus temperature and H₂ partial pressure;
- the equilibrium H₂O/H₂ limits;
- yield versus FeO content.

The chemistry itself is gravity-indifferent at particle scale.

**Case for TRL 4 (max).** E6 is a breadboard-to-prototype integration of reactor, water capture and electrolysis, and its performance was broadly as predicted. If the chemistry and recycle loop are credited separately from the contact mode, TRL 4 is defensible.

**Why I hold the floor at 3.** The TRL 4 exit criterion requires both of the following, and neither is met:
- Test performance agreeing with prediction for the component as it will be built. No breadboard of a *gravity-independent* contactor, such as a compacted packed bed, centrifugal bed or screw reactor, has been run with representative feed.
- A *documented definition of the relevant environment*. This cannot be written yet because the feed (mineralogy, S/Cl content, grain size after beneficiation) is unsampled.

**Unmet for next level (4).** A lab breadboard of the selected microgravity-compatible contactor plus water removal plus electrolysis, run on NEA-class feed (meteorite concentrate or validated simulant), with yield, sticking/sintering, contaminant evolution and H₂ loss matching prediction. Plus a written relevant-environment specification.

**Scale ratio.** The best integrated demonstration (E6) is roughly 10²–10⁴ below the required rate. The only flight heritage (E7) covers one subsystem at roughly 10–500× below required, and on clean water. Terrestrial plants exceed the required scale but depend on gravity for solids flow and fluidization.

## 3. Target and difficulty

**Target under D-02:**
- **TRL 4 by 0A mid-term** (internal milestone).
- **TRL 5 before the pathfinder flight-experiment build.**
- **TRL 6 at KDP-0B** if the H₂ route carries any baselined share of 0B product.

On the TRL 6 target, a caveat. D-02 mandates TRL 6 strictly only for project-ending items, and I classify this one as architecture-change (section 5). I still recommend TRL 6 if the route is baselined, because discovering a contactor failure after the 0B commitment forces a plant redesign at the NEA. If the route is only a yield enhancer, TRL 5 at KDP-0B is acceptable.

**When it is needed.** The architecture does not date KDP-0B. Assuming 0A runs 6–9 years from October 2026, the decision falls around 2032–2035, and that is when TRL 6 is needed.

**AD² = 6** (several competing approaches; path not clear; ≈60%). The reasons:
- **Contactor not chosen.** Candidates are a mechanically compacted batch packed bed, a rotating/centrifugal fluidized bed, an auger/screw reactor, or vibration-assisted beds. Each has different failure modes: channelling, sticking of fresh sponge Fe, and unloading of sintered product.
- **Recycle loop must tolerate S, Cl and F.** H₂S from troilite poisons PEM and SOEC electrolysers and cannot be removed by simple condensation. A cleanup stage (sorbent or scrubber), with its own consumables, is required.
- **Water removal has two candidate routes.** A microgravity condenser/separator (with ISS heritage, but there it is a known maintenance item) or direct steam SOEC (no direct flight heritage for steam).
- **Uncrewed ≥5 yr operation** with abrasive fines in the gas loop.

If the feed turns out to be magnetite-rich and the packed-bed option works on first breadboards, this would drop to AD² 5.

**Years to TRL 6:** 6–10, gated by a flight opportunity.

## 5. Risk and fallback

**Risk class: architecture-change.** The architecture's product is native or reduced Fe-Ni. Excavation of 13–47 kt/yr of material for 0.6–2.5 kt/yr of product implies a feed with a few percent recoverable metal, so most product could come from native metal via magnetic beneficiation without reduction.

If H₂ reduction fails, the consequences are:
- lower yield per tonne mined;
- more excavation and tailings, which presses on the D-03 debris ceiling;
- or adoption of the carbonyl or rotating-furnace route.

This rises to project-ending only if sampling shows the native-metal fraction alone cannot meet the D-02 demand and economics gates.

**Fallback, in order of preference:**
1. Narrow product to native metal recovered by magnetic separation, accepting lower yield.
2. Carbonyl route, if separately qualified.
3. Rotating-furnace route (rq-0-11), with its +20–60 t and $0.5–2B module.
4. D-02 pre-committed fallback: 0A only, Earth supply.

## 6. Where others might disagree

- **Claude-type view: "TRL 4–5."** The argument is that H₂-oxide chemistry is TRL 9 terrestrially, that gravity does not enter a forced-flow packed bed, and that Mauna Kea demonstrated the loop. My response is that bed integrity, solids handling of sticky sponge iron, water separation and contaminant tolerance are where gravity and feed bite. None has been tested in the required configuration.
- **GPT-type view: "TRL 2–3."** "Gas/solid metallurgy is not automatically gravity-independent," and no contactor even exists on paper as a selected design. I keep 3 because the critical chemistry predictions are experimentally validated.
- **Electrolysis credited separately at TRL 7+ on ISS OGS heritage.** I agree for the component on clean water at ISS scale. I do not agree for 10–500× scale on dirty process water, uncrewed.
- **Significance of the route.** If the target is an LL-type S-type, the reducible fraction may be so small that this is a cost-increase item, not architecture-change. If it is a magnetite-rich C-type, it may be central. That disagreement cannot be settled before target sampling.
- **AD² 5 vs 6.** This turns on whether one regards the contactor choice as "path clear" (packed bed) or genuinely open.
