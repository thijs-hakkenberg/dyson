# TRL assessment: T18 Subsurface sampling and in-situ bulk grade assay

## 0. What is being rated, and the assumptions it depends on

**The March 2026 entry rated a different technology.** It rated remote spectroscopic prospecting at TRL 5–6. Remote spectra can classify a surface, but they cannot certify bulk grade at depth. The round-2 panel agreed on this, and rq-0-50 records it. T18 should therefore be a separate entry. The remote-prospecting rating can stand for what it covers, but it is not evidence for T18.

**Requirement as I interpret it.** On each of at least 2 candidate NEAs (rq-0-50), T18 must deliver all of the following:

- **Sites and depths.** Samples at 6–10 sites, with 3 depths per site: 0–5 cm, about 30 cm, and at least 1 m.
- **Measurements.** Bulk native Fe-Ni wt%, the Fe⁰/Fe-total ratio, and a metal liberation-size proxy. At a C-type target, water wt% as well.
- **Gate output.** A target-mean recoverable grade with a stated confidence interval, for the D-02 target gate.
- **Conditions.** All of this in microgravity (about 10⁻⁵–10⁻⁴ g), in abrasive cohesive regolith, under thermal cycling at 0.9–1.3 AU, with a surface campaign of about 3–12 months.

**Accuracy is not yet stated in the architecture. This is my proposal:**

- Per sample: native-metal wt% to ±1 wt% absolute (1σ).
- Per target: the 90% confidence interval half-width on mean recoverable grade should be at most the larger of ±25% relative or ±1.5 wt% absolute.
- Water (C-type only): ±1 wt%.

The basis is that the gate threshold is about 5% recoverable Fe-Ni, so the campaign must separate roughly 3% from roughly 7% with confidence.

**Why at least 6–10 sites.** Assume the site-to-site coefficient of variation in grade is 30–50%. That is plausible for rubble piles with exogenous material; Bennu carries pyroxene-rich boulders from another parent body (DellaGiustina et al. 2021, *Nature Astronomy*; I am fairly confident of this citation). The required number of sites is then n ≥ (1.645·CV/0.25)², which gives about 4–11. This is an analysis on assumed variance. The variance itself is unknown, and the first sites must measure it.

**Why at least 1 m depth. The panel should check this justification:**

1. **Mining consumes the top metre quickly.**
   - Ore throughput is about 14–50 kt/yr (product 0.6–2.5 kt/yr plus tailings 13–47 kt/yr).
   - At a bulk density of 1.2–1.9 t/m³, that is about 7,000–42,000 m³/yr.
   - Mined to 1 m depth, this is 0.7–4 ha/yr.
   - A 300–500 m body has about 30–80 ha of surface.
   - So the first several years of feed come mostly from the top 1–3 m. One metre is the minimum depth that represents early feed, not a generous margin.
2. **The surface is not representative.** Space weathering, size segregation and the seasonal thermal skin depth (tens of cm; this is a model estimate) all mean the top centimetres differ from the material below.
3. **Orbital measurements do not reach deep enough.** Gamma-ray and neutron spectrometry integrates only over about the top tens of cm, so it cannot substitute for sampling at 1 m.
4. **Bennu showed the near subsurface behaves unexpectedly.** The TAG event behaved differently from pre-mission predictions.

One metre does not represent decade-scale feed. Covering that requires radar (structure only) or the pathfinder's own excavation.

**Bulk grade versus recoverable grade.** T18 measures bulk grade, the Fe⁰ fraction and liberation. Converting these to *recoverable* grade needs a beneficiation recovery curve, which the separate beneficiation technology must supply. The gate needs both. A small in-situ magnetic-separation test (described in the path below) narrows the gap but does not close it.

**Corrections to the rq-0-39 literature review.** These affect T18's environment definition and should be fixed before that review is used as a source:

- **TAG citation.** The TAG results are attributed to "Ballouz et al. 2020". The TAG analyses are Lauretta et al. 2022 and Walsh et al. 2022. Ballouz et al. 2020 (*Nature*) concerns boulder craters and Bennu's exposure age.
- **SCI citation.** "Michikami et al. 2019" for the SCI crater should be Arakawa et al. 2020.
- **Bennu's interior.** The claimed "denser core" conflicts with Scheeres et al. 2020 (*Sci. Adv.*), which inferred an *underdense* interior or centre. I am fairly confident of this.
- **"No subsurface samples."** This is too strong. Hayabusa2 TD2 targeted SCI ejecta, and TAGSAM penetrated to decimetres.
- **Unverified items.** I have not verified the arXiv identifiers, Nallapu et al. as an "optimized bucket wheel" source, the "~1 N contact force" figure, or the 0.2–20 Pa bearing figure as stated. Treat them as unverified.

## 2. Current TRL: 3–4 (integrated function)

The rating applies to the integrated system that must fly: anchor, access ≥1 m, collect, relocate, assay, and repeat at 6–10 sites, in microgravity.

| Sub-function | Current TRL in the required environment |
|---|---|
| Fe⁰ and bulk assay (Mössbauer, magnetic susceptibility, XRF or LIBS) | 4–5 |
| Surface grab sampling | Effectively 7–9 (TAG, Hayabusa2) |
| Access to ≥1 m in microgravity | 2–3 |
| Multi-site relocation and repeated anchoring | 3 |

The system is limited by its weakest critical function.

- **Lower bound, TRL 3: exit criterion met.** Documented experimental results validate the key parameters taken separately:
  - drill penetration and sampling at ≥1 m (lunar);
  - Fe⁰ quantification by Mössbauer (MER) and by susceptibility (Rochette 2003);
  - the sample-mass requirement through Gy sampling theory.

  My Gy estimate, for 5 wt% metal and fully liberated grains, gives these masses for ±10% relative fundamental error:

  | Top size of sampled fragments | Sample mass needed |
  |---|---|
  | 3 mm | about 50 g |
  | 1 cm | about 2 kg |

  This is an estimate, not test data.
- **Upper bound, TRL 4: claimed only if component breadboards count** (asteroid drills and anchors in lab simulant, a Mössbauer instrument on meteorites).
- **Exit criterion unmet for TRL 4, and partly for the lower bound.** Two things are missing:
  1. No integrated breadboard has documented test performance agreeing with predictions in lab simulant across the property envelope.
  2. There is no **documented definition of the relevant environment**. Subsurface cohesion at 1 m is uncertain by 1–2 orders of magnitude and has never been measured. Philae and Bennu show that predictions have failed in both directions.

## 3. Target and difficulty

T18 is the instrument that produces the D-02 target-gate evidence. It must therefore be **TRL 6 before scout CDR**, which falls inside 0A, not at KDP-0B. Its successful use at the first target then gives TRL 7 before KDP-0B.

- **Date assumption (not stated in the architecture).** Scouts launch around 2029–2031, with 1.5–3 yr of electric-propulsion cruise. TRL 6 is then needed by about 2029–2030, and TRL 7 data by KDP-0B, around 2033–2035.
- **Target: TRL 6 at scout CDR; TRL 7 by KDP-0B.** This follows D-02's no-waiver rule, because the gate cannot be passed without valid grade data.
- **AD² for the step 3–4 → 6: AD² 6.** Several competing approaches exist and the path is not clear:
  - anchored drill;
  - self-penetrating mole (HP3 failed on Mars);
  - impactor excavation plus ejecta or crater-wall sampling (Hayabusa2 heritage, but one shot per impactor);
  - a small excavator that is a scaled precursor of the mining robot.

  Microgravity reaction and anchoring have no successful precedent. Philae is the only attempt.
- The assay part alone would be AD² 4.
- AD² 5 is defensible if the panel accepts impactor excavation plus surface sampling as sufficient. I do not accept that, because it gives few sites per mission.

## 5. Risk and fallback

**Risk class: project-ending for 0B.** Under D-02 there are no waivers. Without valid sampled grade, the target gate cannot pass, and the pre-committed outcome is "0A only, Earth supply."

**Fallback.** Convert the problem into an architecture change. The 0A pathfinder lands a small excavator, a scaled prototype of the mining robot. It digs a test pit of a few cubic metres to 1–3 m depth and runs bulk magnetic beneficiation on tonne-scale feed. This would give a recoverable grade measured directly rather than inferred. The costs are 2–4 years and one fewer target sampled.

The weaker fallback is impactor excavation plus surface sampling plus GRNS. It gives fewer sites, and its confidence intervals would likely fail the ±25% specification.

## 6. Where others might disagree

1. **Can a ground test reach TRL 6 at all?** The cohesion-to-gravity ratio cannot be reproduced at 1 g, and offloading rigs do not reproduce granular dynamics. A strict reading caps ground testing at TRL 5 and makes the first scout the TRL 6–7 demonstration. That would leave the gate dependent on an unproven first flight. I lean towards this strict view, but I rated step E as reaching TRL 6 because the instrument can be validated in an emulated reaction environment. This is the most contestable call in the assessment.
2. **Is 1 m enough, or too much?**
   - A GPT-style position might accept surface sampling plus GRNS and radar.
   - Mining-volume arithmetic argues that ≥1 m is a floor and that 2–3 m profiles should be required.
   - I keep ≥1 m as the minimum and recommend at least one ≥2 m profile per target where feasible.
3. **Whether recoverable-grade conversion belongs in T18.** I placed the recovery curve in beneficiation. Others may want T18 rated on recoverable grade, which would lower it to TRL 2–3.
4. **The target-class debate feeds the accuracy specification.** CI and CM meteorites carry essentially no native metal (moderately confident: CM below about 1%, CI with metal largely replaced by magnetite). CR chondrites carry about 5–8% metal together with water.

   Gemini's claim of 8–12 wt% metal plus 5–10 wt% water "from the same feed" fits CR-like material, not CI or CM. This matters for T18 because a 2–8% grade range sits right on the gate threshold, so accuracy, not just detection, decides the gate. That should be resolved under rq-0-50; I have not averaged the positions.
5. **The AD² rating of 6 versus 5** depends on whether impactor-based access is judged sufficient (see §3).
