# TRL assessment: T12 Autonomous handling, docking and transfer of 150–300 t payloads

## Scope and assumptions

T12 covers every handling step the payload passes through:

1. **Plant to container.** Filling and closing a container at the NEA processing plant, on the surface or free-flying. This is Claude's extension.
2. **Container to tug.** Capture, mate and secure at the site.
3. **Tug in transit.** Load retention, centre-of-mass control and stack attitude control over 1.5–5 yr.
4. **Tug to SEL1 node.** Release or handover, then parking or station-keeping at SEL1.

GPT's additions are rated as cross-cutting drivers: payload form, inertia, capture velocity and contact loads.

**Assumptions.** Neither the brief nor the architecture states these, so each is an assumption:

- **Tug mass.** Tug mass at capture is 30–100 t, made up of the array, the bus and the remaining propellant.
- **Tug array area.** At an array-level 250–350 W/m², a 1.0–2.5 MWe array is roughly 3,000–10,000 m². The RQ background states 300–500 m², which is about 10× too small.
- **Communication delay.** One-way light time is about 5 s at SEL1 and 3–17 min at a 0.9–1.3 AU NEA, depending on geometry. Ground supervision is practical at SEL1. Control loops at the site must be fully autonomous.
- **Transfer cadence.** 10–18 tugs × 150–300 t ÷ 1.5–5 yr gives about 0.3–3.6 kt/yr, consistent with 0.6–2.5 kt/yr delivered. That is only about 2–17 site transfers and the same number of SEL1 transfers per year.
- **Mechanism life.** Each tug sees about 6–20 mate/demate cycles over 15 yr, with dormancy of 1.5–5 yr between uses. Cycle count is not the driver. Dormancy is: seals, lubricants, cold welding and dust on the interfaces.

**The physics changes the problem, and four RQ-0-17 background statements are wrong:**

- **Thrust and acceleration.** Tug thrust is T ≈ 2ηP/(g₀Isp). At η ≈ 0.6 this gives about 40–150 N. On a 180–400 t stack, acceleration is about 1×10⁻⁴ to 8×10⁻⁴ m/s². That is not 0.01–0.1 m/s². The sustained attachment load is 40–150 N, not 2–20 kN. Sustained thrust load is a non-issue. The design loads come from capture transients, attitude control and cargo shift.
- **Contact energy.** Capture contact energy uses the reduced mass, μ = m_tug·m_payload/(m_tug + m_payload) ≈ 25–75 t.
  - At 0.03 m/s this gives KE ≈ 11–34 J. At 0.10 m/s it gives 125–375 J.
  - For comparison, Shuttle–ISS docking had μ ≈ 85–90 t (orbiter about 110 t, ISS about 420 t). At the nominal Shuttle closing rate of about 0.1 ft/s (0.03 m/s; nominal figure, verify) this gives about 40 J.
  - So contact energy alone is inside crewed flight heritage. The novelty is elsewhere: autonomy at this mass, post-capture dynamics of a stack with a 3,000–10,000 m² flexible array, payload form, and the plant-side transfer.
- **Transfer duration.** "48 h vs 24 h halves fleet capacity" is false here. Transfer time is under 1% of a 1.5–5 yr round trip.
- **Other background errors.** Phase 0 is uncrewed, so crew rating is out of scope. Xenon is superseded by D-05 (iodine reference, argon alternate). The fleet is 10–18 tugs, not 10.

## 2. Current TRL

The required scale is 150–300 t payload with μ ≈ 25–75 t, in deep space at 0.9–1.3 AU and at SEL1. The required environment adds NEA dust and charging, thermal cycling, radiation and dormancy, over about 15 yr of operations. On that basis, sub-element ratings are:

| Sub-element | TRL | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Autonomous rendezvous and proximity GN&C to a cooperative container | 4–5 | Algorithms are largely mass-agnostic (E1–E3, E10). Very low control authority on a 300 t stack with a huge array is not demonstrated. |
| Capture/soft-dock mechanism and contact loads | 4–5 | Contact energy is within crewed heritage (E4). Autonomous capture at this μ has not flown. Dust-tolerant and dormancy-tolerant mechanisms have not been shown. |
| Post-capture stack control with 3,000–10,000 m² flexible arrays | 3–4 | Mode excitation and fault cases (E8, E9) are not analysed for this configuration. |
| Payload containment: rigid product (cast or pressed ingot, pallet) | 2–3 | Structurally easy at about 10⁻⁴ m/s². No project analysis exists yet. |
| Payload containment: contained granular product (powder, pellets, beneficiated grains) | 2–3 | Vacuum cold sintering, electrostatic charging and centre-of-mass shift over years are uncharacterised. |
| Payload containment: loose or uncontained bulk | 1–2 | No path consistent with the D-03 debris limit (≤1 t/yr escaped). Recommend excluding it. |
| Plant to container to tug at the NEA (surface or free-flyer) | 2 | No heritage beyond gram-scale sampling (E10, E11). The concept of operations is not defined: surface stockpile vs hovering fill vs tethered loading. |
| SEL1 handover and parking | 2–3 | The receiving node does not exist (D-04 is proposed). Passive containers drift (E12). |

**System current TRL: 2–3.** The lowest critical element sets the system rating.

- **Met at the lower bound (TRL 2 exit).** The application is documented in RQ-0-17 and the architecture, with feasibility and benefit framed. I rate this exit as met only loosely, because three competing transfer architectures (a, b, c) remain open and none is selected.
- **Upper bound of 3.** This applies only if one credits analysis validated by flight heritage for the containerized rigid branch.
- **Unmet for TRL 3.** There are no documented analytical or experimental results that validate predictions of the key parameters for this concept:
  - capture contact loads at μ = 25–75 t under autonomous control;
  - post-capture attitude excursion and array-mode response of the stack;
  - centre-of-mass migration and containment of the selected Fe-Ni product form;
  - the plant-to-tug transfer sequence.

**Coupling to the metallurgy routes.** Payload form follows the contested metallurgy route:

- **Rotating smelting (Gemini).** Yields cast ingots, which are the easiest form to handle. This route itself has the lowest metallurgy TRL.
- **No-melt carbonyl or H₂ reduction (Claude).** Yields powder or sponge, unless carbonyl deposits near-net shapes or pellets. Needs compaction or containment.
- **Native metal (GPT).** Yields granular metal grains. Needs containment.

T12 sits at TRL 2–3 under all three routes. The plant-to-container step is about one level harder for the granular routes.

## 3. Target and difficulty

**Target: TRL 6 at KDP-0B for the selected transfer architecture.** This is backed by an internal 0A milestone of TRL 4 at the 0A mid-term review, which is also when the architecture is selected.

- I classify failure as architecture-change, not project-ending (see §5). Strictly, D-02 then mandates only TRL 4–5.
- I still recommend TRL 6 because of D-02's reversibility principle. The container standard and the tug's capture interface, structure and attitude control are frozen when tugs are ordered at KDP-0B, and are expensive to reverse.
- **Needed by:** KDP-0B. Its date is not fixed in the brief. I assume a pathfinder flight 6–10 yr after KDP-0A.

**AD² = 6** (several competing approaches; the path is not clear; ≈60%):

- Transfer architectures (a), (b) and (c) are unselected.
- Payload form depends on an unresolved metallurgy route.
- No plant-side transfer has ever been done.

If the architecture is constrained to rigid containerized product with vehicle-integrated docking, AD² improves to **4–5**. Similar developments have been done (E1–E4), at 7–15× less autonomous mass.

## 4. Path

Each step gives scale, cost range, duration and the TRL it reaches.

1. **Heritage data review (literature).**
   - Content: ISS, Mir and Tiangong docking and berthing data; contact rates and loads; the anomaly catalogue (E8, E9, E14); light-time-delay operations.
   - Scale: n/a. Cost: $0.2–0.5M. Duration: 0.5–1 yr.
   - Reaches: TRL 2, firmly.
2. **Transfer architecture trade and concept of operations (analysis).**
   - Options: (a) vehicle-integrated docking, (b) docking with berthing backup, (c) dedicated handling craft. Also monolithic 150–300 t vs modular 10–30 t containers on a tug truss.
   - Site options: surface stockpile vs hovering fill. SEL1 options: node vs self-station-keeping containers.
   - Output: payload form recommendation per metallurgy route.
   - Cost: $0.5–1.5M. Duration: ~1 yr.
   - Reaches: TRL 2 exit fully met.
3. **Contact-dynamics and post-capture multibody simulation (simulation).**
   - Parameters: μ = 25–75 t; closing rate 0.01–0.1 m/s; misalignment; flexible MW-class arrays.
   - Fault cases: a Nauka-type thruster fault and cargo centre-of-mass shift.
   - Validation: against published APAS/Shuttle and ATV flight data.
   - Cost: $1–3M. Duration: 1–2 yr.
   - Reaches: TRL 3.
4. **Ground hardware-in-the-loop capture-mechanism breadboard (experiment).**
   - Method: 6-DOF robotic emulation of the 150–300 t virtual mass (EPOS-class facility), plus air-bearing tests of a scaled planar mass.
   - Cost: $5–15M. Duration: 2–3 yr.
   - Reaches: TRL 4.
5. **Product containment and fill/discharge tests (experiment).**
   - Material: actual product from the metallurgy breadboards.
   - Conditions: vacuum and thermal cycling for cold sintering and charging, over a multi-month dwell; parabolic flight or drop tower; ideally a suborbital or ISS-hosted fill/discharge experiment.
   - Cost: $3–10M. Duration: 2–4 yr.
   - Reaches: TRL 4, and TRL 5 if microgravity-hosted.
6. **Relevant-environment component qualification (experiment).**
   - Content: thermal-vacuum tests of the capture mechanism, contaminated with charged abrasive simulant, with radiation-aged seals and lubricants.
   - Simulates: dormancy cycles of 1.5–5 yr.
   - Output: documented scaling requirements.
   - Cost: $10–30M. Duration: 2–3 yr.
   - Reaches: TRL 5.
7. **Pathfinder flight demonstration (experiment).**
   - Content: autonomous capture, hard-mate and post-capture powered manoeuvre of a mass-loaded subscale container; product fill and transfer at the site.
   - Scale: an assumed 2–20 t, a scale gap of about 10–150×.
   - Cost: $50–300M increment on the pathfinder. Duration: 3–6 yr.
   - Reaches: TRL 6 only if steps 3–6 have validated the scaling, otherwise TRL 5.

## 5. Risk and fallback

**Risk class: architecture-change.** A monolithic 150–300 t autonomous transfer is not essential to 0B. The fallback below breaks each capture into near-heritage events.

It would become **project-ending** only if no plant-to-tug transfer of the product works at all. Under the fallback, that is unlikely.

**Fallback:** carry the payload as a cluster of rigid 10–30 t standardized containers on a tug truss, each captured at near-heritage scale (E1–E3). Accept more capture events (about 5–30 per transfer) in exchange for that heritage. Exclude loose cargo. Use ground-supervised berthing at SEL1, where the delay is about 5 s.

## 6. Where others might disagree

- **Credit for heritage.** I credit E4 (crewed Shuttle docking at μ ≈ 85–90 t) as covering contact energy, but not autonomy. Some will argue this lifts the mechanism sub-element to TRL 6 or above. Others will argue crewed heritage should not count at all.
- **Subscale flight and TRL 6.** It is contestable whether a 2–20 t pathfinder can ever certify TRL 6 for 150–300 t. Array-mode coupling and centre-of-mass migration of granular cargo do not scale cleanly. A strict assessor would cap the outcome at TRL 5 and leave a gap at KDP-0B.
- **Target 6 vs 5.** If failure is architecture-change, D-02 read literally needs only TRL 4–5. I raised the target to 6 on reversibility grounds.
- **Architecture preferences.**
  - Gemini's dedicated handling craft and GPT's berthing backup move risk from the tug onto the node and the arm. That changes the binding demonstration to arresting 150–300 t with an arm, which is about 1.3–2.6× the SSRMS rating.
  - Choosing the modular fallback up front would make most of T12 an operations problem, rated about TRL 4–5 now.
- **Product form.** Proponents of the granular routes will contest a payload-form ranking that favours the smelting route on handling grounds.
- **Background figures.** The RQ background figures for acceleration, array area and transfer time should not be carried into requirements (see §Scope).
