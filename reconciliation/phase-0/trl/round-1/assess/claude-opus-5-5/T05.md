# TRL assessment: T05 Microgravity beneficiation of native Fe-Ni

## 0. Requirement restated, and a check on the balance

T05 covers the on-site step that turns excavated NEA regolith into a metal-rich concentrate. That means comminution to liberation, low-intensity magnetic separation (possibly with electrostatic or size pre-concentration), and grain transport and containment in microgravity. It does not cover the downstream metallurgy (carbonyl, H₂ reduction or rotating smelting), which feeds from T05.

The architecture's ranges only close if they are paired. They do not close if read independently:

| Case | Feed (kt/yr) | Concentrate (kt/yr) | Tailings (kt/yr) | Ratio | Delivered product (kt/yr) | Downstream yield implied |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low | 15 | 2 | 13 | 7.5:1 | 0.6 | 30% of concentrate |
| High | 55 | 8 | 47 | 6.9:1 | 2.5 | 31% of concentrate |
| Unpaired extremes | 15–55 | 2–8 | — | 1.9–27.5:1 | — | — |

The tailings figure of 13–47 kt/yr is consistent only with the paired cases. So the reconciled balance actually implies **about 7:1 at both ends**, not "anywhere in 4–15:1". GPT's 4:1 (25% mass yield) and Claude's 15:1 bound lie outside the balance the architecture adopted. I keep the 4–15:1 envelope as the requirement but flag that 7:1 is the design point the other numbers assume.

**Feed-grade check.** These are approximate bulk metal contents, which I hold with medium confidence. The basis is the compilation by Jarosewich (1990, *Meteoritics* 25) and standard ordinary-chondrite classification:

| Type | Fe-Ni metal (wt%) |
|---|---|
| H | ~15–19 |
| L | ~5–9 |
| LL | ~1–3 |

- **L feed (~8%), high case.** 55 kt feed contains ~4.4 kt metal. An 8 kt concentrate at 50% grade then requires ~90% recovery. That is plausible.
- **LL feed (~2%), high case.** 55 kt feed contains ~1.1 kt metal. This cannot support 2.5 kt/yr delivered at any recovery. LL feed closes only near the low case, and only with high recovery.
- **Itokawa.** The only sampled S-type is LL-like (see Evidence). The best-characterised analogue is therefore the one that breaks the high end of the balance.
- **H feed (~18%), low case.** 15 kt feed contains ~2.7 kt metal. A 2 kt concentrate at 70% grade needs only ~50% recovery. Ratio is easy, and grade is the binding constraint.
- **M-type branch.** Feed is metal-dominated. The meaningful operation is silicate rejection, and the ratio will be about 1.2–2:1. **Claude's "<3:1 breaks co-location" falsifier applies to chondritic feed only.** It must not be applied to an M-type target, where a low ratio is the expected and benign outcome.

**Ratio alone is an incomplete specification.** It must be paired with grade, recovery and liberation size. I rate against all four.

**Required throughput.** 15–55 kt/yr is 1.7–6.3 t/h continuous. At 50% plant availability it is 3.4–12.6 t/h. Duration is multi-year operation, assumed at 5–10 yr.

**Liberation.** In ordinary chondrites much of the metal occurs as grains of tens of µm up to ~1 mm. It is often intergrown with troilite or bonded in silicate. Liberation adequate for >80% recovery probably requires grinding to roughly 100–300 µm. That is my estimate from chondrite petrography, not a measured liberation curve on representative feed. No such curve exists, and that gap is the core of rq-0-51.

- No test of continuous magnetic separation of native metal in microgravity.
- No grade/recovery/throughput curve on representative chondritic feed with realistic metal attachment.

Concept literature does not count toward TRL ≥3: Lewis, *Mining the Sky* (1996); Kargel 1994 (*JGR*, metal asteroids); commercial asteroid-mining design studies; and the panel's own chains.

## 2. Current TRL: 2–3

- **TRL 2, met (lower bound).** The application is documented in panel material and older concept literature: magnetic concentration of native Fe-Ni at the NEA with a stated ratio and feed envelope. The underlying separation physics also has peer-reviewed grounding (E3/E4 for separation, E6/E7 for the regime).
- **TRL 3, partially met (upper bound).** The critical separation function (native metal being strongly ferromagnetic and separable with LIMS) is experimentally proven in 1 g at gram scale. That is the reason for the upper bound of 3.
- **Why the range does not collapse to 3.** The exit criterion is "documented analytical or experimental results validating predictions of key parameters". No document I can cite predicts grade and recovery as a function of liberation size for chondritic feed and then measures them. The second critical function, controlled grain transport and feed presentation in microgravity, has only physics-level evidence (E6). There is no separator-level evidence at all.

**Unmet exit criterion for TRL 3:** documented experimental results validating predicted grade, recovery and liberation for representative feed (LL/L/H, realistic metal attachment) at a stated throughput.

**Unmet exit criterion for TRL 4:** a breadboard separator, comminution plus feed plus magnetic stage, tested in the laboratory with performance agreeing with predictions, together with a documented definition of the relevant environment. That definition covers microgravity feed dynamics, vacuum, triboelectric charging, thermal range and abrasion.

**Rated by downstream route.** The TRL is the same (2–3) for all routes. The required concentrate specification differs, so difficulty differs. The grade figures below are my assumptions and need confirmation from the metallurgy assessments.

| Route | Concentrate need (assumed) | Effect on T05 |
|---|---|---|
| Claude, no-melt (magnetic → carbonyl / H₂) | Moderate–high grade (~50–80% metal); exposed, unoxidised metal surfaces; troilite and space-weathered rims matter (sulfur interaction with carbonylation is chemistry I am unsure of) | Hardest on liberation and surface condition |
| Gemini, rotating 0.05–0.15 g smelting | Tolerates lower grade (~30–50%); silicate becomes slag | Easiest on T05; shifts burden to the furnace (separate technology) |
| GPT, narrow native-metal product | Highest grade (≥80–90%) if the concentrate is near-product | Hardest on grade; may need multi-stage cleaning |
| M-type branch | Silicate rejection at ~1.2–2:1; metal comminution difficult (ductile) | Different machine; untested; no sampled M-type until Psyche (arrival ~2029) |

## 3. Target and difficulty

**Target.** T05 is project-ending for 0B, because co-location depends on it and central processing has been abandoned (see §5). Under D-02 it therefore needs **TRL 6 at KDP-0B**: a subsystem prototype in a relevant environment, with no waivers.

**Internal 0A milestone.** TRL 4 at the 0A mid-term review and TRL 5 before pathfinder design freeze.

**Dates.** No KDP dates are given. I assume a 0A mid-term around 2029–2030 and KDP-0B around 2033–2036. Treat these as assumptions.

**Practical constraint on reaching TRL 6.** It almost certainly needs the 0A pathfinder at an NEA, or a long-duration free-flyer in vacuum with validated simulant. Parabolic flight (~20 s) and drop towers (~5–9 s) cannot demonstrate continuous separation. If the pathfinder slips, T05 slips with it.

**AD²: 6** (several competing approaches; path not clear; ≈60%). The reasons:
- The magnetic step is low risk because native metal is about the easiest magnetic separation there is. On its own it would rate AD² 3–4.
- The difficulty is the microgravity system. Feed presentation could be mechanical, centrifugal, vibratory or rotating-drum. A pre-concentration stage, or none, is also open. Comminution of a mix of ductile metal and brittle silicate is unsolved for this setting. Containment of fines must meet a debris ceiling of ≤1 t/yr escaped against 15–55 kt/yr throughput, i.e. losses ≤ ~2–7×10⁻⁵ of feed. Magnetic flocculation will entrain silicate.
- These approaches compete, and none has been tested.
- I would move this to **AD² 7** for an LL-only target or for the M-type branch, and to **AD² 5** if paired with the smelting route.

## 5. Risk and fallback

**Risk class: project-ending (for 0B).**
- Co-location depends on concentrating at the site.
- Central SEL4/5 ore processing is abandoned, so there is no architectural home for unconcentrated ore.
- A ratio below ~3:1 on chondritic feed, or a grade too low for the selected downstream route, fails the economics gate.
- It is **architecture-change** rather than project-ending if an H-chondrite or M-type target is available. On those targets a low ratio is acceptable, or the requirement inverts.

**Fallback.** In order of preference:
1. Retarget to metal-richer feed (H or M-type), using the D-02 target gate (sampled grade).
2. Pair with the smelting route, which tolerates lower grade.
3. Revert to D-02's pre-committed "0A only, Earth supply".

## 6. Where others might disagree

1. **Upper bound of 3 vs. firm 3.** One could argue that magnetic separation of native Fe-Ni is so well understood (E3, E9) that the critical function is proven and TRL is 3, possibly 4, for the separation step. My counter is that TRL attaches to the function at the required environment. Microgravity feed presentation is a critical function, and nothing at separator level exists for it. Splitting T05 into a "separation" element and a "microgravity handling" element would resolve this. I recommend that split.
2. **The ratio metric itself.** Claude's 3:1 falsifier and GPT's 4:1 are feed-dependent. On M-type feed they are meaningless. On LL feed even 15:1 may not close the balance. I would replace the requirement with grade ≥ X at recovery ≥ Y on the sampled target's feed. The architecture currently specifies a ratio.
3. **The architecture's own balance.** The adopted figures pin ~7:1. The 4–15:1 envelope partly reflects unreconciled model positions. The disagreement is preserved here, not averaged: GPT at 4:1, Gemini at 5–8:1, Claude at 5–15:1.
4. **Is a 170–6,300× scaled-down pathfinder sufficient for TRL 6?** I accept it, provided TRL 5 documented scaling requirements, as NPR 7123.1 and ECSS practice allow. A strict reviewer could hold that grain-transport physics does not scale simply with cohesion-dominated fines. On that view TRL 6 would need ≥ ~100 kg/h, which would raise pathfinder mass and power considerably.
5. **Simulant validity.** Using crushed real meteorites is defensible for mineralogy. However, terrestrial weathering and the absence of space-weathering rims (npFe on silicates, which raises silicate magnetic susceptibility and lowers grade) mean ground results may be optimistic. Only real NEA regolith resolves this, which again puts T05's TRL 6 on the pathfinder's critical path.
