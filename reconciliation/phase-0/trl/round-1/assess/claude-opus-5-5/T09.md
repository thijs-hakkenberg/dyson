# T09: High-voltage power tethers and site distribution on regolith (NEA surface)

## 0. Scope, assumptions and a budget reconciliation

**This is not a lunar problem.** The architecture puts mining at the target NEA. Surface gravity there is roughly 10⁻⁵–10⁻³ m s⁻² (Bennu ≈ 6×10⁻⁵). That changes what dominates the risk:

- **Abrasion is weaker.** Normal forces are tiny.
- **Tether dynamics are much worse.** A 3 t robot on Bennu weighs about 0.2 N, so any tether tension above roughly 0.2 N can pull it off the surface or drag it.
- **Charging and dust are poorly characterised.** Electrostatic dust behaviour, terminator charging and dust adhesion to insulation are not well known for NEAs.

Lunar heritage informs the dust problem but is not the relevant environment.

**Robot power versus site budget.** These are my own estimates, not evidence.

| Quantity | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Excavation specific energy, microgravity | ~1–10 kWh/t (cutting and anchoring, negligible lift) |
| Excavation power at 0.5–5 t/h | ~0.5–50 kW |
| Mobility, avionics, thermal | +1–3 kW |
| Per-robot peak | ~5–50 kW |
| Fleet peak (6–20 robots) | 30 kW – 1 MW |
| Fleet average (9–25 % duty) | ~3–250 kW |
| Share of 2.5–8 MW site power | 0.04–10 % average |
| Worst case: full coincidence at a 2.5 MW site | ~40 % |

Two consequences follow:

- **"Hundreds of kW per circuit" only appears if several robots share a feeder.** It would also appear if robots did onboard processing, which the architecture does not assign them. Per-robot tethers are tens of kW. The site-level distribution trunk (plant ↔ processing ↔ storage) is a separate, higher-power circuit. It should be rated with T09 but is a different engineering object.
- **The kV choice is justified by tether mass, not by power.** Take 30 kW over a 500 m run (1 km loop), aluminium conductor, 3 % loss:

| Voltage | Current | Conductor cross-section | Conductor mass |
|---|---|---|---|
| 600 V | 50 A | ~78 mm² | ~210 kg |
| 3 kV | 10 A | ~3 mm² | ~8 kg (before insulation) |

A 210 kg tether is unacceptable for a 2–3 t reeling robot. Below about 100–200 m, 400–600 V becomes competitive. **The voltage should be a trade output, not a requirement.**

**Dielectric physics.** I am diverging here from the Claude framing:

- **Multipaction is an RF phenomenon.** It does not apply to a DC tether. It matters only inside converter or RF hardware.
- **The DC failure modes are:**
  - surface flashover across insulators, worsened by adhered dust;
  - triple-junction field emission at connectors;
  - Paschen breakdown in local partial pressure (outgassing, volatile release at a C-type target with the water pilot, or gas trapped in regolith pores);
  - arcing to the ambient plasma at insulation flaws;
  - internal (deep-dielectric) charging of thick insulation during solar energetic particle (SEP) events.

**Other parameters assumed:**

- **Return path:** two-conductor or coaxial tether. Regolith in vacuum is not a usable return.
- **Fault isolation:** kV-class DC solid-state breakers.
- **Duration:** ≥2 yr per tether, with replacement allowed, and a 5–10 yr site life.
- **Thermal cycling:** ~150–400 K at NEA rotation periods (hours). That is roughly 10³–10⁴ cycles per year.

**What the evidence does and does not cover:**

- **Strong:** voltage, power and reeling (terrestrial); kV in vacuum (short harnesses); microgravity tether deployment.
- **Absent:**
  - any test combining kV, vacuum, charged abrasive simulant, thermal cycling and reeling;
  - any kV dust-tolerant mate/demate connector;
  - any kV-class DC solid-state breaker in vacuum (I know of none flown; unsure);
  - any tether-robot dynamics data for microgravity.

## 2. Current TRL: 2–3 (integrated tethered distribution on an NEA)

**Lower bound, TRL 2, is met.** The application is documented with a feasibility basis. The mass and voltage arithmetic above can be done from first principles.

**Upper bound, TRL 3, is arguable.** Each critical function has been shown experimentally in isolation:

- kV insulation in vacuum (thruster harnesses, TSS);
- reeling of kV cable under load (mining, ROV);
- multi-year surface cable survival at low voltage (ALSEP).

Against TRL 3 is that there is no validation of the *key parameters for this application*:

- insulation wear rate under charged NEA-like simulant;
- flashover voltage with adhered dust;
- tether tension limits in microgravity.

I hold TRL 3 as the maximum, not the expected value.

**Exit criterion unmet for TRL 4:** documented test performance of a breadboard tether, connector and breaker agreeing with predictions in a laboratory environment, **and** a documented definition of the relevant environment. Neither exists. The NEA surface charging, dust and plasma environment is not yet defined to a test-specification level.

Component sub-ratings, for traceability:

| Component | Earth / relevant environment | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Conductor and insulation | TRL 9 on Earth / 3 for NEA | Mining heritage; no relevant-environment test |
| HV connector (dust-tolerant, vacuum mate/demate) | 2–3 | No known kV breadboard |
| kV DC fault isolation in vacuum | 3 | Terrestrial MVDC breakers and ISS 120 V RPCMs exist; no kV space breadboard known to me |
| Microgravity reeling and tether management | 2–3 | Free-space deployment heritage; no surface or robot case |

## 3. Target and difficulty

**Risk class: architecture-change, not project-ending.** Viable fallbacks exist (Section 5). Under D-02, the strict TRL 6 rule therefore does not automatically apply.

**My targets:**

- **0A mid-term review:** TRL 4 for tether insulation, connector and breaker.
- **KDP-0B:** TRL 5. This is a full-scale tether and connector in a vacuum, dust and thermal-cycling chamber with charging, plus validated microgravity dynamics models.
- **Before 0B robot critical design review:** TRL 6. This requires a flight element, because NEA gravity and long-duration charging cannot be simulated on the ground.

**When the phase needs it:** the date of KDP-0B, which the documents do not fix. I assume roughly 2031–2034.

**AD² = 5** (new development; similar has not been done, but the path is clear; ≈70 %).

- The parts are conventional: insulation materials, reeling mechanisms and breakers all have mature analogues.
- Three sub-problems have no precedent:
  - kV mate/demate in vacuum with charged dust;
  - surface tether management at ~10⁻⁵ g;
  - flaw-tolerant insulation over 10⁴ thermal cycles plus SEP internal charging.
- TSS-1R shows that a single insulation flaw at a few kV in space is catastrophic. The path is clear, but it has not been walked.

**Years to target (TRL 5):** 3–6.

## 5. Risk and fallback

- **Risk class:** architecture-change. If tethers fail to reach target, the robot power system and the duty or throughput model change, but Fe-Ni production is not precluded.
- **Fallback ladder, in order:**
  1. **Lower-voltage (400–600 V) short tethers**, with robots working within about 100–200 m of a mobile power node.
  2. **Battery-only robots with autonomous dock charging.** The 9–25 % duty cycle helps here: at 20 kW and 2–6 h/day of active time, a robot uses 40–120 kWh/day. That exceeds the 50 kWh battery, so this needs 1–3 dock charges per day or larger batteries of 80–150 kWh.
  3. **Beamed or inductive power.** This is low TRL and not recommended.
- **Throughput cost of the fallback:** I estimate a 10–40 % lower effective excavation rate. This is still within the tailings and product range if the fleet is sized toward 20 robots.

## 6. Where others might disagree

1. **TRL 4 rather than 2–3.** Someone could argue that terrestrial mining cables (TRL 9) plus kV in vacuum add up to TRL 4. I reject this because no test combines the environments and the relevant environment is undefined, which is an explicit TRL 4 exit item.
2. **Dominant failure mode.** Gemini emphasises insulation abrasion. I rank it below:
   - tether dynamics at ~10⁻⁵ g;
   - kV connector contamination;
   - arc at a flaw (TSS-1R);
   - Paschen events from local volatiles at C-type targets.

   Low normal force reduces wear. Charged dust adhesion could still make abrasion worse than I expect. Step 3 resolves this.
3. **Multipaction.** I treat it as irrelevant to DC tethers. Others may keep it on the list, for converter interfaces only.
4. **Whether 1–3 kV is a requirement at all.** GPT's cable-length question decides it. Short runs favour about 600 V and remove most of the dielectric risk.
5. **Target at KDP-0B: 5 or 6.** Strict readers of D-02 may treat the mining fleet as mission-critical for 0B and demand TRL 6. That would force the flight element (step 8) into 0A and onto the critical path.
6. **Fallback viability.** If robot peak loads turn out to be about 100 kW (for example, if anchoring or cutting energy in cohesive C-type material is underestimated), battery-only operation degrades sharply. The risk class would then move toward project-ending for 0B throughput.
