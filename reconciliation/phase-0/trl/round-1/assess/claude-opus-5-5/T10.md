# TRL assessment: T10 Long-duration autonomy for mining, processing and proximity operations

## 0. Scope, decomposition and assumptions

T10 is not one technology. It is an autonomy stack that has to close loops at three timescales:

| Tier | Loop time | Function | Light-time relevance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Reflex** | ms–s | Bucket-wheel stall or void response, anchor-slip recovery, tether tension, furnace or reactor interlocks | None. It must be onboard at any distance, because even 1 min one-way is too slow. |
| **Tactical** | min–h | Fault detection, isolation and recovery (FDIR), re-planning around a dead robot, tug approach and berthing, plant state transitions | Partial. With supervision 2–50 min round-trip it could be ground-assisted. |
| **Strategic** | days–weeks | Mine planning, throughput balancing, degraded-mode operation through conjunction | This is what the ≥90-day "no required ground intervention" requirement actually tests. |

**Consequence.** The 1–25 min versus "seconds to ~20 min" light-time disagreement does not change the rating. The reflex tier must be onboard either way, and the strategic tier must be onboard because of conjunction, not light-time.

A geometric note on the upper bound: at 0.9–1.3 AU heliocentric, the Earth–target distance tops out near ~2.3 AU, about 19 min one-way. 25 min is conservative, not wrong.

**Assumptions I am making explicit**

1. **A1. What "no required ground intervention" means.** I read it as no ground command being necessary to keep the site safe and keep throughput at or above an agreed fraction of plan. Autonomous isolation and abandonment of failed units is allowed.
   - The strict alternative is zero interventions with full throughput across ~6–20 robots, 1 plant and visiting tugs. That needs per-agent mean time between required interventions of several years, which no robotic system on Earth or in space has documented.
   - The requirement owner (GPT gate) needs to confirm this reading. It is the single largest driver of difficulty.
2. **A2. Why 90 days may be needed.** For NEAs with orbital periods near 1 yr, the synodic period is long. The Sun–Earth–probe angle then changes slowly, so solar-conjunction blackouts can last weeks to months. This is a qualitative geometric point; the actual duration is target-specific and is not computed here.
3. **A3. KDP-0B date.** No date is given in the architecture. I assume pathfinder arrival around 2032–2034 and KDP-0B around 2034–2037. The 0A mid-term review is assumed around 2029–2030.
4. **A4. Software TRL.** NPR 7123.1 includes software-specific TRL descriptions. I believe these allow high-fidelity simulation to count as the "relevant environment" at TRL 5–6 (moderately confident of the exact wording; check the current revision).
   - I do not accept simulation as relevant environment for the contact-dynamics portions. Microgravity regolith mechanics at 10⁻⁶–10⁻⁴ g has no validated simulator.
   - That model cannot be validated without in-situ data (see §6).

### 1d. What does not exist, to my knowledge

1. Any space or analogue demonstration of a multi-robot excavation fleet operating autonomously for weeks.
2. Multi-robot management of 1–3 kV power tethers (tangle and snag avoidance) for 6–20 tethered robots, in any environment.
3. Unattended operation of a metallurgical process in vacuum or microgravity on any route: H₂ reduction, carbonyl, or rotating furnace.
4. Autonomous berthing and loading of 150–300 t payloads at a microgravity body.

## 2. Current TRL

Elements are rated separately because the integrated number hides the spread.

| Element | TRL | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Safe-state survival through no-contact periods, spacecraft-level FDIR | 6–7, for the *survive* function only | Deep-space and Mars-conjunction heritage. **Not** heritage for productive operation, and not for tethered robots or a hot plant. |
| Small-body proximity navigation and touch-and-go | 5–6 | Flown, but seconds of contact versus continuous contact |
| Autonomous tug berthing and loading at an NEA (150–300 t) | 4–5 | LEO docking heritage; target is cooperative; ~10× mass. Load transfer at an NEA is untested. |
| Excavation reflex and tactical autonomy (void, boulder, transition, anchor slip) in microgravity | 2–3 | 1 g terrestrial excavation autonomy exists; microgravity contact is untested beyond seconds |
| Multi-robot tether management | 2 | Concept only |
| Plant autonomy, **no-melt H₂ reduction / magnetic beneficiation** (Claude) | 2–3 | Terrestrial process control exists. Microgravity solids and gas handling with unattended FDIR is untested. |
| Plant autonomy, **carbonyl route** (Claude) | 2–3 | Terrestrial carbonyl refining is highly automated but staffed. Pressure, toxicity and deposition-fouling FDIR in vacuum is untested. |
| Plant autonomy, **rotating 0.05–0.15 g furnace** (Gemini) | 2 | Autonomy cannot exceed the process TRL. The furnace itself has not been demonstrated. |
| Plant autonomy, **narrow native-metal, gated** (GPT) | 2–3 | Magnetic separation is mature terrestrially; untested in microgravity. Limited process scope eases FDIR. |
| **Integrated T10 (fleet + plant + tug interface, ≥90 d, through conjunction)** | **2–3** | Bounded by the weakest project-critical elements: excavation autonomy, tether management and plant autonomy |

**Lower bound, TRL 2: met.** The concept and application are documented with feasibility arguments (rq-0-42, rq-0-8 and the round-2 architecture). These are design documents. They suffice for TRL 2 and do not count for TRL 3.

**Upper bound, TRL 3: arguable.** Critical-function proofs exist in isolation:
- spacecraft FDIR and planning (RAX, ASE);
- 1 g autonomous excavation and drilling;
- small-body autonomous contact (TAG).

What is missing for a clean TRL 3 is experimental validation of the key T10-specific parameters. Two examples: force/torque detection latency for a void or boulder within one bucket revolution in a cohesive low-gravity analogue, and fault-coverage rates for a plant FDIR model.

**Unmet for TRL 4.** No integrated or breadboard autonomy stack has been tested in a laboratory against analytical predictions. The **"documented definition of the relevant environment"** also does not exist. No published fault catalogue, conjunction-duration analysis per candidate target, or regolith-mechanics envelope defines what the autonomy must handle. That second gap is cheap to close and should come first.

## 3. Target and difficulty

**Classification under D-02.** Two elements are project-ending for 0B:
- the minimum autonomy set that keeps the site safe and productive through conjunction (reflex excavation autonomy, robot and plant safing, degraded-mode fleet operation);
- the requirement that no single fault cascades across tethered robots or into the plant.

**Targets**

| Milestone | Target | Scope | When (assumed) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0A mid-term review | TRL 4–5 | Excavation reflex autonomy and plant FDIR on ground breadboards | ~2029–2030 |
| KDP-0B | **TRL 6, no waiver** | Minimum autonomy set, demonstrated in a relevant environment | ~2034–2037 |
| Phase 1 | TRL 7+ | Full 90-day productive autonomy at full fleet scale | Phase 1 |

For contact operations, the KDP-0B relevant environment can, in my judgment, only be the pathfinder demonstrator at an NEA, possibly aided by scout-borne contact experiments.

**AD²: 6.** Several competing approaches; the path is not clear (≈60%).
- **Why not 5:** the path is not clear. There are competing metallurgy routes, each with different FDIR needs. There are competing control philosophies (model-based diagnosis versus learned policies with fleet learning per rq-0-42). And the contact physics the autonomy must be validated against is itself not characterised. OSIRIS-REx showed predictions of surface resistance can be badly wrong.
- **Why not 7:** no new physics is needed. Planning, FDIR, terrestrial excavation autonomy and small-body navigation all have real heritage.
- **By route:**

| Route | AD² | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| No-melt H₂ reduction | 5–6 | Lower-temperature process, simpler interlocks |
| Narrow native-metal (GPT) | 5–6 | Limited process scope |
| Carbonyl | 6 | Pressure, toxicity and fouling FDIR in vacuum |
| Rotating furnace | 7 | Autonomy for a process that does not yet exist |

Step details:

1. **Requirements and environment definition.**
   - FMECA and fault catalogue for robots, tethers, plant and tug interface.
   - Conjunction-duration analysis per candidate NEA.
   - Allocation of each function to reflex, tactical or strategic tiers.
   - Measurable definition of "unattended" (A1).
2. **Analogue intervention-rate study.** Draw on Mars-rover conjunction plans and RAX/ASE lessons. Seek autonomous-mining intervention data from the vendors and operators named in §1c (likely proprietary).
3. **Excavation reflex breadboard.** A bucket-wheel with high-bandwidth force/torque sensing in CI-type and S-type simulants, with seeded voids, boulders and cohesion transitions. Predictions come from DEM, validated by test. This answers rq-0-42 Research Directions 1–3 at 1 g only.
4. **Reduced-gravity contact campaign.** Parabolic flight, drop tower and gravity-offload rigs.
   - **Limitation:** none of these reaches 10⁻⁶–10⁻⁴ g for long durations; residual accelerations on parabolic flights are orders of magnitude too high.
   - Use these results only to validate DEM, not as end-to-end evidence.
5. **Plant FDIR breadboards, per route.** Continuous operation in a thermal-vacuum chamber with fault injection: leaks, clogging, sintering, heater loss, power transients.
   - Fund the no-melt and native-metal routes first.
   - Gate the furnace autonomy on the furnace reaching TRL 4.
6. **Integrated 90-day testbed.** Scope: ≥2 tethered robots on gravity offload, the plant breadboard and a tug-berthing mock-up. Run with real light-time delay and a full conjunction blackout. A red team injects faults blind. The headline metric is required interventions per 1,000 h against the A1 definition.
7. **Scout-borne demonstrations.** Run the onboard planner through a real conjunction. Perform autonomous touch-and-go and anchoring trials. Fly an instrumented penetrometer or small scoop to measure the regolith mechanics the autonomy must handle.
8. **Pathfinder operation.** Tethered excavator(s) and a beneficiation/reduction module at a real NEA. Operate ≥90 days including one blackout at least as long as the target's conjunction, with no required ground intervention per A1. This is the only step I would accept as TRL 6 for the contact and integrated elements.

**Years to target.** 7–12 years from 2026. The schedule is driven by pathfinder development, cruise and 90 days of operations, not by software.

## 5. Risk and fallback

**Risk class: architecture-change.** It is not project-ending, because a degraded but functional mode exists. The fallback has three parts:

1. **Supervised autonomy outside conjunction.** The reflex tier stays onboard and is mandatory regardless. Tactical and strategic decisions go ground-in-the-loop with 2–40 min round-trip.
2. **Pre-planned production pause during conjunction.** Robots park and tethers de-energise. The plant goes to a warm safe state, which is a survival function with real heritage.
3. **Optional relay off the Sun–Earth line**, to shrink the blackout.

**Cost of the fallback:**
- Lower duty cycle and throughput. Annual yield falls in proportion to blackout length, and the 9–25% duty cycle drops further.
- A permanent ground operations staff.
- Possibly a relay spacecraft.

**Where it escalates to project-ending.** This happens if the economics gate (delivered $/kg) only closes with fully unattended operation. It also happens if the target's conjunction is so long that pausing production destroys the case. Both are checkable at KDP-0B. Step 1 should report the conjunction fraction per candidate target so the economics model can price the fallback early.

**Pre-committed outcome per D-02.** If the minimum autonomy set is not at TRL 6 at KDP-0B, the response is not a waiver. It is either (a) 0B sized for supervised operation with production pauses, if the economics still close, or (b) fallback to "0A only, Earth supply".

## 6. Where others might disagree

1. **Rating by components versus by integration.** A reviewer could cite ASE, conjunction survival, TAG and terrestrial autonomous haul fleets and argue for TRL 4–5. I reject that. None of those demonstrates the required function: productive contact operations plus process control with FDIR, multi-agent, in microgravity, for 90 days. Terrestrial mining autonomy is supervised by design. **This is the most contestable call.** A reasonable assessor could put the upper bound at 3 for the integrated system without strain, and some would argue 4 for excavation autonomy alone.
2. **Whether simulation can supply the relevant environment for TRL 6.** NPR 7123.1's software TRL descriptions (A4) would let a well-built digital twin carry the software stack to TRL 5–6 on the ground. My objection is narrow: the contact-mechanics model has no validation data at NEA gravity. OSIRIS-REx is direct evidence that pre-encounter predictions can be badly wrong. If scouts return in-situ mechanical data and the simulator is calibrated against it, my objection weakens. Ground TRL 6 for the tactical and strategic tiers could then be defensible, leaving only the reflex tier tied to the pathfinder.
3. **The 90-day requirement itself.** It is a GPT-originated gate. If target-specific conjunction analysis shows blackouts of ~2–4 weeks, the requirement may be over-specified. Conversely, if the analysis shows months for a low-relative-motion NEA, it may be under-specified. The A1 definition of "unattended" matters more than the day count. Under a strict zero-intervention reading I would lower the integrated rating to 2 and raise AD² to 7.
4. **Risk class.** Others may call T10 project-ending, on the grounds that the 0B economic case assumes unattended operation. I hold architecture-change because a priced fallback exists. That stance depends on the economics gate tolerating the fallback, which nobody has yet shown.
5. **Route coupling.** Gemini's rotating-furnace route inherits AD² 7 for plant autonomy. A Gemini-aligned reviewer would say autonomy for a furnace is no harder than for a gas-phase reactor once the furnace exists. That is fair as a conditional, but the furnace does not yet exist.
6. **rq-0-8 inherited numbers.** The $1B fleet budget, 20-robot count and 2–3 robot pathfinder in rq-0-8 predate the revised architecture (6–20 robots). They are not evidence and were not used.
