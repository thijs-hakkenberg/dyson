# TRL assessment: T02, MW-scale deployable solar arrays (cargo tug, plus NEA site power per Gemini and GPT)

## Assessment basis and assumptions

**Required, sized at the maximum operating distance.** I follow the Claude/GPT position: size at the maximum heliocentric distance of the operating envelope, not at a fixed 1.2 AU. Neither the target list nor the maximum tug range is fixed yet, so I bracket them.

| Case | EOL requirement | Max distance (assumed) | EOL degradation (assumed) | BOL equivalent at 1 AU | Area at ~330 W/m² (1 AU BOL, III-V, ~85% packing) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tug, low | 1.0 MWe | 1.3 AU | 20% | ~2.1 MW | ~6,400 m² |
| Tug, high | 2.5 MWe | 1.7 AU | 20% | ~9.0 MW | ~27,000 m² |
| Site, low | 2.5 MW | 1.3 AU (NEA aphelion) | 20% | ~5.3 MW | ~16,000 m² |
| Site, high | 8 MW | 1.3 AU | 20% | ~17 MW | ~51,000 m² |

- The 20% degradation over 10–15 yr is an assumption. It covers interplanetary proton fluence, UV, thermal cycling and contamination. It is not a validated figure (see the rq-0-25 caveats below).
- The low-intensity, low-temperature efficiency gain at 1.3–1.7 AU gives perhaps 5–10% credit. I have not taken it.
- Fleet total: 10–18 tugs plus the site comes to roughly **30–180 MW of 1 AU-BOL array**. That is a supply-chain fact as much as a technology fact.

**Thrust load (dynamics under thrust).** F ≈ 2ηP/(g₀·Isp). With η ≈ 0.6:

- 1 MWe at 3,000 s gives ≈ 40 N.
- 2.5 MWe at 2,000 s gives ≈ 150 N.

Quasi-static acceleration is small (~10⁻⁴ m/s² on a 300–500 t stack). The concern is the dynamics, not strength:

- Very low fundamental frequencies in 6,000–27,000 m² flexible structures.
- Coupling with attitude control, gimbal slews and thrust vectoring.
- Thermal snap at eclipse and shadow transitions.
- Throttling transients.

My assumption is wing fundamentals of order 0.01–0.1 Hz at this scale. That is not verified.

**What I am rating.** I rate the integrated array system at MW scale, not the cell or the individual heritage wing. The two elements (tug and site) are rated separately because their environments differ:

- Tug: thrust dynamics and plume contamination.
- Site: charged NEA dust, low gravity or free-flying station-keeping, and 1–3 kV tether distribution.

**Cautions on the related RQ literature.** These RQs are written for the superseded 100 MW L4/L5 architecture. They are partly useful for cells and degradation, but they are not evidence at TRL ≥3 for this system.

- **rq-0-22: cell cost.** It cites III-V cell cost of "$100–300/cm²". That implies ~$1–3 M/m², which looks wrong by orders of magnitude. Do not propagate.
- **rq-0-22: concentrator waste heat.** It cites "~350 W/cm² waste heat at 500×". 500 × 0.136 W/cm² is ~68 W/cm² incident, so waste heat is ~45 W/cm². The number is wrong.
- **rq-0-22: concentrator heritage.** It lists "ISS solar arrays" as concentrator heritage. They are flat-plate Si.
- **rq-0-22 vs rq-0-25: annealing temperature.** They disagree on the Lang et al. temperature: 60 °C under AM0 versus 100–200 °C. Lang et al. is on GaInAsP, not the InGaP/GaAs/Ge baseline. Annealing credit is unvalidated.
- **rq-0-25: degradation rate.** The 1.0–1.7%/yr estimate is a synthesis, not a test result.
- **rq-0-25: STEREO.** The claim that STEREO used silicon arrays is unverified by me.
- **rq-0-25: remaining factor.** The specific value "0.85–0.90 at 10¹⁰ MeV/g" is likewise unverified.
- **Use with care.** The arXiv NIEL/DDD papers (Baur, Campesato et al.) are appropriate underlying physics for cell degradation, i.e. TRL 1–3 evidence at cell level.

## 2. Current TRL

**Tug array: TRL 3, upper bound 4.**

- **Met at TRL 3:** Key parameters are validated experimentally in pieces:
  - Flexible-blanket specific power and deployment at the 20–30 kW wing scale (flight).
  - III-V degradation versus displacement damage dose (ground irradiation plus deep-space flight).
  - SEP array operation in deep space (Psyche, Dawn).
- **Why the upper bound is 4:** TRL 4 is reachable only if one accepts "N × heritage wings" as the breadboard.
- **Unmet for TRL 4:**
  - No documented definition of the relevant environment. Propellant is unselected (iodine vs argon plume), maximum distance is unfixed, and there are no thrust and slew load spectra. This is part of the TRL 4 exit criterion itself.
  - No laboratory breadboard whose performance has been checked against predictions for the MW-scale emergent functions:
    - multi-wing structural dynamics under thrust;
    - harness and high-voltage (≥300 V, plausibly up to kV) plasma interaction and arcing;
    - iodine deposition and corrosion on coverglass, interconnects and slip rings;
    - hinge/mast scaling beyond the heritage ratio.

**Site array: TRL 2, upper bound 3.**

- **Met at TRL 2:** Concept documented (Gemini/GPT). It is static and unthrusted, which is easier than the tug.
- **Unmet for TRL 3:** No experimental data on:
  - charged regolith dust adhesion and obscuration rates for NEA-like conditions near active excavation (tailings 13–47 kt/yr);
  - anchoring or station-keeping of tens of thousands of m² at a rotating milligravity body;
  - 1–3 kV distribution from array to tether.

The governing range is reported as 2–4.

## 3. Target and difficulty

**Target: TRL 6 at KDP-0B.** Tug and site power is the dominant input to delivered $/kg. Under D-02 the economics gate cannot be credibly passed on a TRL 3 power system. I recommend TRL 6 even though I classify failure as architecture-change rather than project-ending (see §5).

- TRL 6 must be met with a subscale prototype (≥100–300 kW-class, multi-wing) in a relevant environment, plus a validated scaling model.
  - A full-scale MW ground test is physically impossible; even the Space Power Facility is far too small.
  - In practice this means a flight demonstration on the 0A pathfinder, or a dedicated flight.
  - Ground vacuum, thermal and plume tests can reach TRL 5 at most.
- **Internal 0A milestone:** TRL 4 by the 0A mid-term review, TRL 5 before pathfinder CDR.
- **Needed by:** KDP-0B. The date is not set; I assume ~2031–2034.
- **Phase tag:** 0B. 0A scouts at 200–600 kg use existing kW arrays and do not need T02.

**AD² = 5 (range 5–6).**

- This is new development. Nothing similar has been done: no autonomously deployed array has exceeded ~0.1 MW.
- The flat-plate flexible-blanket path is reasonably clear: scaling ROSA/MegaFlex-type wings and multi-wing modular masts.
- Arguments for 6:
  - Competing approaches remain unresolved: number and size of wings, robotic in-space assembly vs one-shot deployment, high-voltage direct drive vs low voltage plus PPU, flat-plate vs concentrator (rq-0-22).
  - Iodine compatibility could force design changes.
  - Robotic assembly heritage is thin. OSAM-1 was cancelled, and Caltech SSPD-1/DOLCE (2023) was a small-scale deployment with partial issues.

## 5. Risk and fallback

**Risk class: architecture-change.** It becomes project-ending only if combined with an economics-gate failure. Without MW arrays, 0B can still fall back to:

- more tugs at ~0.3–0.6 MWe, with longer trips and a larger fleet;
- lower site power with reduced throughput, which the 0.6–2.5 kt/yr Fe-Ni product may tolerate;
- or nuclear fission power, which is lower TRL at MW scale and is not a near-term fallback.

These degrade delivered $/kg, so failure can cascade to a D-02 economics failure. That cascade ends in the pre-committed "0A only, Earth supply" outcome.

**Fallback:** Re-baseline tugs to the largest flight-demonstrated array (~0.3–0.5 MWe class modular wings) with fleet count and trip time increased. Site power drops to the demonstrated level, and the economics gate is re-run.

## 6. Where others might disagree

1. **"It is just N heritage wings" (likely Gemini/GPT optimism).** On this view the system is TRL 5–6 because each wing is TRL 9 at 20–30 kW. I reject it. TRL is rated at the required scale, and the critical functions are emergent at MW scale: dynamics under thrust, high-voltage plasma interaction, iodine contamination, harness mass, and 70–450× wing count or size scaling. Integrating 100+ heritage wings on one tug is itself untested.
2. **Risk class.** A reviewer could argue project-ending: without ≥1 MWe tugs the delivered-cost model may never close. I keep architecture-change because degraded fallbacks exist, but the gap to project-ending is narrow.
3. **Site TRL may be underrated.** A static site array without thrust loads is simpler. The counterargument is that dust from 13–47 kt/yr of tailings handling and milligravity anchoring have no data at all.
4. **Sizing distance.** Fixing 1.2 AU instead of the maximum distance would cut tug area by ~2× and make the problem look easier. I follow Claude/GPT, but the factor matters to the scale ratio.
5. **Degradation and annealing credit.** The RQ literature implies 0.8–1.7%/yr with annealing credit. I treat this as unvalidated. Annealing evidence is on GaInAsP, and the two RQs give inconsistent temperatures.
6. **Concentrators.** rq-0-22's concentrator case rests partly on erroneous numbers. SCARLET is the only relevant space heritage, at ~2.5 kW. I do not rate a CPV branch.
