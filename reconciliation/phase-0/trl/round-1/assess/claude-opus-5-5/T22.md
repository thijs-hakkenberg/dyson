# TRL assessment: T22 Product consolidation, packaging and qualification

**Re-scoping note.** The March 2026 entry `microgravity-metallurgy` rated something different: smelting, slag separation and silicon zone refining at 50 kt/yr. The revised architecture defers silicon and narrows product to Fe-Ni at 0.6–2.5 kt/yr. It also moves smelting and reduction into the contested metallurgy routes covered by rq-0-11 and rq-0-56.

T22 is the downstream step. It takes reduced or native Fe-Ni and turns it into a form a tug can carry and Phase 1 will accept. I recommend splitting the old entry three ways:

- **Smelting and slag separation** stay with rq-0-11.
- **Zone refining** goes to *deferred*.
- **This entry** covers consolidation, packaging and lot qualification.

Several claims in the old evidence field should not carry over:

- "UMG-Si 4N–5N" is irrelevant now that silicon is deferred.
- "Magnetic electrolysis, Nature Chemistry 2025" is not something I can verify, and it is not relevant to Fe-Ni consolidation.
- The rq-0-11 deliberation conclusion is not TRL evidence.

**Requirement as rated**

| Parameter | Value | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Annual product | 0.6–2.5 kt/yr | Revised architecture |
| Mean rate at 100% duty | 70–290 kg/h | Derived from annual product |
| Rate at 40–60% availability | 120–700 kg/h | My availability assumption |
| Environment | Vacuum; microgravity, or 0.05–0.15 g if the rotating module exists; NEA thermal cycling at 0.9–1.3 AU; abrasive charged fines | Revised architecture |
| Product form | Dust-tight and tug-compatible (Claude) | Requirement |
| Acceptance | Phase 1-accepted composition, contamination and properties (GPT) | Requirement |
| Hold time in package | 1.5–5 yr tug transit, plus storage at SEL1 | Revised architecture |

Two constraints link this entry to others:

- **Debris licence.** Fines shed during handling count against the proposed D-03 ceiling of ≤1 t/yr escaped per site. Dust-tightness is therefore a licence issue, not only a cargo issue.
- **Tug propellant.** If iodine is selected (D-05), leaks or plume contamination would attack high-surface-area Fe-Ni powder. Dense product or sealed packaging reduces this risk. This is my inference, not a tested result.

**What is absent from the evidence base**

- Consolidation of any **asteroid-derived or asteroid-representative** Fe-Ni product, at any scale. Representative means carrying troilite, P, Co, C or O residues and silicate attachment.
- Bulk powder handling for longer than parabolic-flight durations at **0–0.15 g**.
- A **Phase 1 product specification**. Without a specification, "accepted" has no test.
- Any **autonomous lot-acceptance** measurement in space for interstitials (O, C, S, N) or for mechanical properties.

## 2. Current TRL

The rating is broken down by route, because the consolidation step inherits its feed from the route.

| Sub-function | Route | Current TRL | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Consolidation of no-melt product | Claude: carbonyl powder, carbonyl vapour deposition, or H₂-reduced sponge, consolidated by cold pressing, HBI-style hot briquetting (~650–750 °C) or vacuum solid-state sintering | **3–4** | TRL 3 met: densification against pressure and temperature is well characterised for terrestrial Fe and Ni powders (E1, E2). TRL 4 claimed only if terrestrial data are accepted as representative. I do not accept them, because residual S, P and C and carbonyl residues change sintering and dusting. |
| Consolidation of native-metal concentrate | GPT | **3** | Native-metal grains with attached silicate and troilite are not a standard powder-metallurgy feed. Compactibility and the strength of the resulting cermet-like compacts are unmeasured. |
| Casting of melt | Gemini: rotating 0.05–0.15 g furnace feeding ingots or bars | **2–3** | Casting itself is TRL 9 at 1 g. At 0.05–0.15 g, mould filling, shrinkage feeding and gas porosity are uncharacterised. E5 is relevant solidification physics, not casting. Casting is also gated on the melt existing (rq-0-11). Ingots would be the best dust-tight form if the route works. |
| Packaging: sealed canisters for loose or consolidated product | All routes | **3–4** | Physics is simple and terrestrial bulk containerisation is mature. Space sealing heritage is grams-scale (E9). There is no test of dust-tightness under years of thermal cycling with abrasive charged fines. |
| Lot qualification: in-situ assay and property acceptance | All routes | **2–3** | Major-element assay heritage exists (E10). No method has flown for O, C, S or N, density, porosity or mechanical acceptance. There is no customer specification to qualify against. |

**Headline: TRL 3, range 2–4.** The bundle is limited by qualification and, for the casting route, by its dependence on the melt.

- **Met at the lower bound (TRL 2).** The concept and application are documented with feasibility and benefit. This covers HBI-style passivation, the canister option and the lot-acceptance concept. Design studies are admissible evidence at TRL 2.
- **Met for the best route (TRL 3).** Experimental results validate the key parameters, densification against temperature and pressure for Fe and Ni powder. This rests on terrestrial PM and HBI data.
- **Unmet for TRL 4.** Two things are missing:
  1. Documented lab breadboard performance on representative asteroid-derived feed, agreeing with prediction.
  2. A documented definition of the relevant environment. This must include the Phase 1 product specification (composition, contamination limits, form, properties), the tug cargo environment and the dust-escape allocation under D-03.

  Neither exists. The second is a programmatic gap as much as a technical one.

## 3. Target and difficulty

**Target: TRL 6 at KDP-0B; TRL 4–5 at the 0A mid-term review.**

Not all of T22 is project-ending:

- **Physical consolidation** has a credible fallback, containerised loose product. That makes it *architecture-change* in isolation.
- **Acceptance** does not have a fallback. If Phase 1 will not accept the product form, the demand gate (signed schedule within 3×) and the economics gate both fail. D-02's pre-committed outcome is then "0A only, Earth supply".

So the qualification element is project-ending for 0B. Under D-02 that requires **TRL 6 in a relevant environment, with no waivers**. I apply that target to the bundle.

**Dates.** The decisions provided do not date KDP-0B. I assume:

- 0A mid-term review around 2029–2030.
- KDP-0B around 2033–2036, constrained by scout transit times of 1.5–5 yr and a pathfinder flight.

TRL 4 for consolidation and packaging should be in hand by the mid-term review. That allows the pathfinder payload to include a consolidation and packaging experiment.

|---|---|---|
| No-melt consolidation, packaging and qualification | **5** | Similar developments exist on the ground (HBI, PM, NVD), but this one has not been done in vacuum and µg with autonomous operation and unknown feed. ## 5. Risk and fallback

**Risk class: project-ending for 0B**, through the acceptance and qualification element. Physical consolidation failing on its own is **architecture-change**.

Fallbacks, in order:

1. **Ship loose product in sealed canisters.** This works if Phase 1 actually wants powder for AM or PM. If so, consolidation may be counterproductive and T22 reduces to packaging plus qualification. The penalty is import or reuse mass and handling-loss exposure against D-03.
2. **Bulk passivation by HBI-style hot briquetting only,** with no sintering. Lower energy: I estimate the sensible heat to ~700 °C at under 0.3 MJ/kg, about 25 kW mean at 2.5 kt/yr before losses.
3. **Full vacuum sintering.** Thermal floor of about 0.55 MJ/kg to ~1,200 °C, roughly 45 kW mean at 2.5 kt/yr. Plausibly 100–200 kW with losses, within the 2.5–8 MW site power. This is my estimate, not a test result.
4. **D-02 fallback: "0A only, Earth supply"** if Phase 1 acceptance cannot be obtained.

## 6. Where others might disagree

- **Whether ground thermal-vacuum testing is enough for TRL 6 for solid-state consolidation.** Pressing and solid-state sintering are largely gravity-insensitive once the die is filled. A reviewer could argue that steps 6 and 7 together justify TRL 6 without the flight in step 8, and that today's rating is already 4–5 because vacuum hot-pressing and HBI are routine. I keep 3–4 and require flight for three reasons: the feed is unrepresentative, autonomous lot qualification is unproven, and powder transport over long durations at 0–0.15 g has no data beyond seconds. This is the main contestable call.
- **Whether qualification belongs in T22 at all.** Some would place acceptance under the D-02 demand gate and rate T22 as pure process technology. On that view the risk class falls to architecture-change and the target to TRL 5. I bundle it because GPT's requirement explicitly says "Phase 1-accepted".
- **Claude's and GPT's requirement statements may conflict.** Claude asks for dust-tight consolidated form. GPT asks for whatever Phase 1 accepts, which could be powder. Until step 1 is complete, it is not known whether consolidation is required, optional or harmful.
- **The casting rating.** Gemini would likely rate casting higher, arguing that 0.05–0.15 g restores terrestrial practice "with parametric adjustments". There are no partial-g casting data, so I hold it at 2–3. The rq-0-11 deliberation that asserts otherwise is not evidence.
- **The NVD route (E3)** could become a combined refining-and-consolidation step that needs no powder handling. That would raise the no-melt route's prospects considerably. My evidence for it is weak, so it is flagged unconfident.
