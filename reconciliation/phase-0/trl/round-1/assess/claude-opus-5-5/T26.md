# TRL assessment: T26 Deep-space communications and resilient command service

**Scope assumed.** T26 is the integrated *service*, not the radios. It covers:
- **Long-haul links** to 4–10 scouts, 10–18 tugs, the NEA site and the SEL1 node.
- **Ground capacity** for 15–29 concurrently active vehicles over a multi-year campaign.
- **Store-and-forward commanding** across 1–25 min one-way light-time and conjunction outages.
- **Authenticated commanding**, with key management that survives safe-mode resets and long outages.
- **Autonomous outage recovery.**

Local links between the site, robots and plant are counted only where the site acts as a relay.

Three things in the required statement weaken the rating before any evidence is considered:
1. **Data rates are TBD.** The TRL 4 exit criterion requires a documented definition of the relevant environment. A link budget is part of that definition, so performance cannot yet be checked against predictions at the required scale.
2. **The requirement comes from one model (GPT)**, not the panel.
3. **The supplied RQs belong to the superseded architecture.**
   - rq-0-1 assumes 50 survey satellites on X-band with 30-day autonomy.
   - rq-0-8 assumes 20 robots, a mesh network and relay orbiters.
   - rq-0-42 assumes months of autonomous excavation.

   They bear on downlink volume and autonomy duration, but their numbers should not be carried over.

The ground-side scale is the real gap. The DSN already supports several dozen missions on about a dozen large apertures and is commonly reported as oversubscribed. That characterisation is widely published; I hold no confident specific figure. Adding 15–29 long-lived vehicles is a capacity and scheduling problem, not a physics one.

## 2. Current TRL: 5–7

The two ends of the range are separate judgements.

- **Lower bound (5): the TRL 5 exit is met.** Every critical function has been demonstrated in a relevant or operational environment:
  - links and light-time (DSN);
  - multi-asset relay (Mars Relay Network);
  - store-and-forward (DINET);
  - conjunction outage operations (Mars).

  The exception is authenticated commanding with key recovery after an outage. I can cite that only as a standard plus ground implementations, which puts that function at about 4–5.

- **Upper bound (7): one view of the evidence.** If the Mars Relay Network plus DSN MSPA is accepted as an operational system prototype, the system sits at 7. That reading ignores the 2–4× node count, the 15–29-vehicle ground load and the authentication gap.

- **Unmet exit for TRL 6:** documented test performance of the integrated service agreeing with analytical predictions in a relevant environment. The integrated service means authenticated DTN commanding plus multi-asset scheduling plus outage recovery. That prediction cannot be written until data rates and outage durations are specified.

## 3. Target and difficulty

- **Phase need: 0A.** Scouts and the pathfinder fly in 0A and need a flight-qualified TT&C subsystem. Practically, that means TRL 6 at scout CDR.
- **KDP-0B target: TRL 6.** This is not strictly required, because T26 is not project-ending (§5). It is set anyway because tug and site operations depend on it, and 0A scouts can deliver TRL 7 almost for free.
- **AD² 3: new development well within the experience base, about 90%.**
  - Every element has close heritage.
  - The hardest element is crypto-state and key management that survives safe-mode resets, single-event upsets in key memory, anti-replay counter loss and multi-month outages without locking out the vehicle. Military and commercial operators solve this, so it would rate AD² 4 if treated alone.
  - Ground capacity is procurement and negotiation, not R&D.

## 5. Risk and fallback

**Risk class: cost-increase.** A capacity shortfall can be bought out with resources, and the failure modes do not stop the programme:
- leasing or building dedicated 34 m-class apertures (order USD tens of millions each; this figure is uncertain);
- using ESA or commercial deep-space ground stations;
- raising onboard autonomy and accepting lower data return.

**Secondary risk: schedule-delay.** This applies if authentication or key management locks out a vehicle during flight demonstration.

**Fallback:** buy dedicated ground apertures and raise vehicle autonomy so that tugs and the site tolerate multi-month silence. If command security cannot satisfy the licensing state, keep high-energy tug manoeuvres near SEL1 and Earth under ground-in-the-loop windows only.

## 6. Where others might disagree

1. **Rating level.**
   - Some will rate this TRL 8–9 because every radio, protocol and antenna has flown.
   - Others will rate it 4 because data rates are TBD and deep-space authenticated commanding has no flight citation I can give.

   I keep 5–7 and make the requirement gap explicit rather than averaging it away.

2. **Conjunction duration is underspecified and may be the real driver.**
   - Low-Δv NEAs tend to have Earth-like orbits with long synodic periods. A target near superior conjunction could stay inside the solar-exclusion zone for months rather than the 2–3 weeks seen at Mars.
   - This is my own geometric inference, not a cited study.
   - If it holds, the site must run for months without commanding, which pushes the autonomy element toward AD² 4–5. That would overlap rq-0-42.

3. **Risk class.**
   - A hijacked or runaway 1–2.5 MWe tug near SEL1 is a debris and licence matter (D-03 and the D-02 licence gate).
   - A licensing state could make command security a licence condition. That would raise T26 to architecture-change, or even to a KDP-0B gate item.
   - I hold it at cost-increase because the mitigations are known, but this is the most contestable call.
