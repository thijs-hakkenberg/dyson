# TRL assessment T06: No-melt carbonyl refining with CO recycle

**Proposed new entry id:** `carbonyl-refining-co-recycle`

This replaces nothing in the March 2026 `microgravity-metallurgy` entry. That entry rated a different route: a rotating smelting furnace with zone refining. None of its evidence applies to carbonyl refining:

- ISS EML containerless melting
- D'Angelo 2021 additive manufacturing
- UMG-Si solar cells
- magnetic electrolysis

That entry also used the rq-0-11 deliberation as evidence, which the rating rules exclude. Its TRL 3–4 and its target TRL 7 should not carry over. The rq-0-13 literature review covers slag from melting routes. A no-melt route makes no slag, but it does leave a carbonylation residue (silicates, troilite, Co, PGM) that adds to the 13–47 kt/yr tailings stream. I have not relied on that review's composition fractions.

**Scope.** This rates the route that takes magnetically beneficiated native Fe-Ni grains, applies an optional H₂ pre-reduction (Gemini's "combined" variant), carbonylates them, recycles the CO and decomposes the carbonyl to Fe-Ni product. The following are separate technologies and are not rated here:

- magnetic beneficiation
- H₂ reduction as a standalone route
- the rotating furnace (rq-0-11)

**Required (from the architecture):**

| Item | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Product | 0.6–2.5 kt/yr Fe-Ni, or an allocated share of it |
| Plant | Uncrewed and autonomous, co-located at an NEA |
| Gravity | ~10⁻⁵–10⁻⁴ g, unless hosted in a spun module |
| Ambient | Vacuum, with thermal cycling at 0.9–1.3 AU |
| Radiation and dust | Radiation over multi-year operation; charged abrasive dust in solids handling |
| Feed | Meteoritic metal: kamacite/taenite with Co, P (schreibersite), S (troilite), C and oxide films |
| Reagents | Make-up imported, with a CO inventory and pressure containment |
| Carbonylation chemistry | Fe(CO)₅ at high pressure; Ni(CO)₄ near ambient |
| Selectivity and safety | Sulfur effects, selectivity and toxic-gas handling |

### 1a. Import-stream estimate (analysis only, not TRL evidence)

**Stoichiometric CO.** Carbonylation ties up 2.51 kg CO per kg Fe and 1.91 kg CO per kg Ni. For 2.5 kt/yr of Fe₉₂Ni₈ product, about 6 kt/yr of CO circulates at minimum, and more at realistic single-pass conversion. The circulating inventory is not the concern. The make-up is.

**Loss mechanisms and my ranges at 2.5 kt/yr product.** These scale roughly linearly with product.

| Loss mechanism | Basis | CO make-up |
|---|---|---|
| Carbon in product | Boudouard reaction 2CO → C + CO₂ consumes 4.67 kg CO per kg C. At 0.1–0.5 wt% C in product, that is 2.5–12.5 t C. | ~12–58 t/yr |
| Oxide films on feed | Reduced by CO at 0.2–1 wt% O. With H₂ pre-reduction this shifts to H₂ loss, which is partly recoverable by electrolysing the water. | ~9–44 t/yr |
| COS from sulfur, plus inert purge | — | 1–20 t/yr |
| Leakage | Uncertain | 1–20 t/yr |
| **Total, no CO₂ regeneration** | ~0.2–6% of product mass | **~5–150 t/yr CO-equivalent** |

That total is roughly 0.03–1 tug payload per year.

**Effect of CO₂ regeneration.** CO₂ → CO regeneration (MOXIE-type electrolysis or reverse water-gas shift) could recover the oxide-film and Boudouard CO₂. It cannot recover carbon lost into the product. It also adds electrical load and a further technology.

**Uncertainty.** The range is an order of magnitude wide. Measuring it is the single most decision-relevant output of the path in §4.

## 2. Current TRL: 2–3

Two ratings sit inside this, and they must not be confused:

- **Terrestrial carbonyl refining of sulfide-matte or laterite-derived feed** is TRL 9 in its own environment.
- **The required system** has a different feed, a different gravity regime, a vacuum ambient and uncrewed autonomy. It also needs a CO loop closed to an import budget. It is TRL 2–3.

The rest of this section rates the required system only.

**Why heritage does not transfer.** The chemistry is gravity-independent. Several heritage unit operations are not:

- **Rotary carbonylation reactors** tumble the bed under gravity.
- **Fluidised beds** balance gas drag against gravity.
- **Pellet decomposers** are gravity-fed moving beds.
- **Free-space powder decomposers** rely on powder settling.
- **Fe(CO)₅/Ni(CO)₄ distillation** relies on gravity-driven liquid–vapour counterflow.
- **Condensing Fe(CO)₅**, a liquid that boils at ~103 °C, needs liquid management.

GPT's point that gas/solid metallurgy is not automatically gravity-independent is correct for the heritage plant layout. Gravity-independent alternatives exist on paper but none has been tested:

- packed beds with mechanical solids feed and discharge
- wall CVD decomposition
- an all-vapour hot loop with no liquid phase
- centrifugal (rotating packed bed or cyclone) separation
- capillary condensers

**Lower bound, TRL 2. Met.** The concept is documented with a feasibility rationale (Lewis 1996; this architecture). The underlying principles are published (E1–E4), which satisfies TRL 1.

**Upper bound, TRL 3. Arguable, not demonstrated.** TRL 3 requires documented experimental results validating predictions of key parameters. Some key parameters partly transfer from industrial data:

- the equilibrium and pressure–temperature windows
- sulfur promotion
- the decomposition temperatures

The parameters specific to this application are not validated on representative feed:

- carbonylation rate of mm-scale kamacite/taenite grains with oxide films and low surface area
- Fe/Ni selectivity versus pressure
- the effects of P, Co and troilite S
- carbon pickup during decomposition
- CO loss per kg product

I give TRL 3 as the maximum because terrestrial carbonylation of reduced Fe-Ni alloy feeds may supply partial validation. I cannot cite such a dataset with confidence.

**Unmet exit criterion for TRL 3.** Documented experimental results on representative meteoritic Fe-Ni feed (or a qualified simulant) validating predicted:

- carbonylation rate
- Fe/Ni selectivity
- carbon and oxygen pickup in product
- CO consumption per kg product

**Unmet exit criterion for TRL 4.** Two things are missing:

- a closed-loop breadboard whose measured performance agrees with predictions
- a documented definition of the relevant environment, which does not yet exist for this technology

## 3. Target and difficulty

**Target TRL**

- **TRL 4 at the 0A mid-term review.** This is the internal 0A milestone under D-02, and the route down-select against rq-0-11 and GPT's native-metal route needs it.
- **TRL 6 in a relevant environment at KDP-0B, no waivers**, if this route is selected as the 0B baseline. If it is the baseline, failure ends 0B production unless another route is mature.

KDP-0B has no date in the inputs. **I assume ~2032–2034.**

**What TRL 6 implies for flight.** Carbonylation and decomposition run for hours to days. Drop towers (seconds), parabolic flights (~20 s) and sounding rockets (minutes) cannot test hour-scale bed behaviour, condensation or decomposition. A strict TRL 6 in a relevant environment therefore almost certainly needs an orbital free-flyer or the 0A pathfinder demonstrator. A ground test with orientation sensitivity runs is not enough.

**ISS caveat.** Ni(CO)₄ toxicity is extreme; the OSHA PEL is 0.001 ppm (I am confident of this order of magnitude). Hosting it on ISS is likely to be impractical under crewed-vehicle toxic hazard rules. Plan on uncrewed hosts. The same toxicity makes ground testing expensive. In space, leaked carbonyl decomposes on warm surfaces and deposits metal films on optics and arrays, so containment matters for the plant's own equipment even without crew.

**AD² = 6** (5 is defensible) for the step from TRL 2–3 to TRL 6.

- **Path is clear for:** the chemistry, and pressure containment of Fe(CO)₅. High-pressure vessels are within the experience base, though their mass at kt/yr scale has not been estimated.
- **Path is not clear for:** gravity-independent solids contacting, condensation and separation, where several competing approaches exist (rotating packed bed, packed bed with mechanical discharge, all-vapour loop, wall CVD).
- **Also not clear:** CO-loop closure to a make-up budget nobody has measured, for a feed nobody has characterised for this process.

**Condition for AD² 5.** If the design avoids Fe/Ni separation entirely, AD² 5 becomes reasonable. In that design, co-decomposition gives Fe-Ni alloy directly, with composition set by partial pressures. The product spec (native/reduced Fe-Ni) may allow this.

**Years to target:** 6–10 from now. This puts TRL 6 at about 2032–2036. The early end only fits KDP-0B if the flight demonstration rides on the 0A pathfinder, so expect **schedule tension**.

## 5. Risk and fallback

**Risk class: architecture-change.** Three metallurgy routes are being rated separately. If this route fails to reach TRL 6, 0B reverts to a different route, which is an architecture change. It is project-ending for 0B only if every route fails; that is a programme-level conclusion, not this technology's.

**Fallback chain:**

1. **GPT's narrow native-metal route.** Magnetic beneficiation, then H₂ reduction of oxide films and consolidation of native Fe-Ni without vapour refining. This gives lower purity but needs no CO loop.
2. **Gemini's rotating furnace** (rq-0-11). This adds the +20–60 t, $0.5–2B artificial-gravity module.
3. **D-02 pre-committed fallback:** "0A only, Earth supply".

**Partial-success outcome.** If kinetics are fine but CO losses are high, keep the route and add CO₂ regeneration. This is a **cost-increase**, not an architecture change.

## 6. Where others might disagree

1. **"It's TRL 4–5: the Mond process is TRL 9 and gas/solid chemistry doesn't care about gravity"** (likely Claude's view). I reject this. The heritage plant layout depends on gravity in at least five unit operations, the feed is uncharacterised for this process, and the relevant environment has not even been defined. These are the TRL 3 and TRL 4 exit criteria.

2. **"It's TRL 1–2."** If no representative-feed data exists at all, the key parameters are only extrapolated, and a strict assessor would cap the rating at 2. My upper bound of 3 depends on terrestrial alloy-feed data I cannot cite.

3. **CO make-up rate.** An optimist could argue for ~1 t/yr with good decomposition control and CO₂ regeneration. A pessimist could argue for >150 t/yr if mm-scale kamacite needs repeated passes and carbon pickup is high. My 5–150 t/yr is an analysis with an order-of-magnitude spread, not evidence. Others may average it into a single number, which hides the spread.

4. **Can TRL 6 be shown on the ground?** If P4 shows the configuration is genuinely orientation-insensitive, some will accept ground tests plus P5a as a relevant environment and skip the flight test. Under D-02's no-waiver rule I do not accept this for hour-scale multiphase processes.

5. **Whether Fe/Ni separation is required.** This decides whether AD² is 5 or 6. It also decides whether high-pressure Fe(CO)₅ synthesis is the baseline or whether a near-ambient Ni-only extraction suffices, with Fe recovered another way. The product specification should settle it, not the technologists.

6. **Risk class.** A panel member who baselines this route may class it as project-ending. I class it as architecture-change because D-02 rates the competing routes separately.
