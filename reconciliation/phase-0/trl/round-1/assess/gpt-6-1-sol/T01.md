## T01 assessment: MW-class SEP thruster and PPU cluster

**Recommended replacement rating: TRL 2–3 for the iodine-reference system, not TRL 6–7.** Xenon provides substantially stronger **100-kW-class thruster** heritage, but not a demonstrated MW-class propulsion cluster with the required lifetime. Argon flight heritage does not establish readiness at this scale either.

This assessment uses the supplied revised architecture and decisions as requirements. It does **not** assume that programme milestones scheduled after the cited tests have been achieved.

### 1. Assessment boundary and electrical interface

T01 comprises the thrusters, cathodes, PPUs, propellant feed equipment, cluster controller, protection equipment, and their mechanical, thermal and electrical interfaces. Solar arrays are an adjacent technology, not evidence that T01 is ready.

I recommend defining the requirement as follows:

- **1.0–2.5 MWe available at the propulsion PPU input terminals**, after array distribution losses and allocation to spacecraft housekeeping.
- That power must be available at the specified operating point, including **end-of-life conditions at 1.3 AU**. A rating at beginning of life and 1 AU is not equivalent.
- **100–250 kWe input per operating propulsion module**, giving approximately **4–25 simultaneously operating modules**, plus whatever redundancy the reliability analysis requires.
- A **1.5-kV nominal distribution bus** is a provisional engineering assumption, not a selected or qualified interface. This implies approximately **0.67–1.67 kA** aggregate current. Input-voltage range, grounding, isolation, fault interruption, allowable ripple, electromagnetic compatibility and restart behaviour remain to be specified.
- **Isp 2,000–3,000 s must be measured for each propellant branch**, using total propellant consumption, including cathode flow.
- **20,000–40,000 powered hours** is provisionally treated as a requirement on each continuously assigned operating module. Rotating spare modules can reduce individual hours, but only through an explicit fleet/module allocation and reliability model.

Assuming **50–65% efficiency from PPU input power to useful jet kinetic power**, purely for sizing:

\[
T=\frac{2\eta P}{g_0 I_{sp}}, \qquad
m_p=\frac{2\eta Pt}{(g_0 I_{sp})^2}.
\]

The requirement implies approximately:

| Quantity | Envelope |
|---|---:|
| Cluster thrust | 34–166 N |
| Cluster lifetime impulse | 2.4–23.9 GN·s |
| Propellant processed per 100–250 kW module | 8–122 t |
| Propellant processed per tug | 83–1,217 t |

These are combinations of requirement extremes, **not a trajectory or annual-demand prediction**. Nevertheless, they show why a few-thousand-hour wear test or a sub-tonne throughput test cannot close this requirement.

**Thermal boundary:** neither “all non-jet power becomes spacecraft radiator load” nor “only PPU losses matter” is acceptable without measurement. Some energy leaves with the plume; some heats thruster surfaces, spacecraft structures and intercepted surfaces. Conducted heat, radiation view factors and plume interception must be measured separately.

### 3. Current TRL, target and difficulty

#### Separate propellant branches

| Branch | Current T01 TRL | Lower-bound exit met; next unmet exit | AD² to TRL 6 |
|---|---:|---|---:|

| **Argon alternate** | **2–3** | Application formulated; flight evidence supports basic propulsion feasibility. Publicly available evidence does not validate the required high-power module, PPU/cathode combination or lifetime scaling. No representative integrated cluster breadboard establishes TRL 4. | **5–6** |
| **Xenon comparator** | **3–3** | TRL 3: physical high-power Hall tests validate a critical propulsion function near the proposed module-power range. TRL 4 for T01 remains unmet: no cited representative **MW cluster plus PPUs/feed/control** laboratory validation. | **4–5** |

The iodine upper bound is **not** a claim that MW iodine scaling has been demonstrated. It reflects uncertainty over whether low-power physical tests adequately validate this application’s critical functions.

Individual xenon thrusters or PPUs can have higher TRLs. Those ratings cannot simply be inherited by the cluster, and especially cannot be transferred to iodine or argon.

**Target under D-02:**

- **Internal 0A:** TRL **4–5**, with milestone requirements tied to the actual breadboard and relevant-environment test scope.
- **KDP-0B:** TRL **6**, before authorising a production architecture dependent on these tugs.
- **Production deployment:** subsequently needs system qualification and flight evidence; TRL 6 is a decision gate, not permission to skip qualification.

No calendar date for KDP-0B is supplied. The appropriate needed-by date is therefore **“before KDP-0B authorisation.”**

For the iodine reference, **8–12 years to TRL 6** is a planning allowance, not a demonstrated schedule. Xenon could plausibly take less time; argon remains intermediate. AD² 6 reflects competing high-power iodine approaches and unresolved cathode/material compatibility—not uncertain basic propulsion physics. The project’s approximate AD² success percentages should not be treated as calibrated programme probabilities.

### 4. Advancement path

Costs are **rough-order-of-magnitude 2026 USD development costs**, not quotations. Activities overlap; ranges should not be summed without a programme work breakdown. Flight demonstrations exclude a dedicated MW solar-power spacecraft unless expressly included.

| Step | Scale and method | Cost | Duration | TRL contribution |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| **Close interface, mission and evidence baseline** | Literature and analysis: branch-specific throttle maps, electrical boundary, burn histories, module-hour allocation, throughput, fault cases, supplier evidence and test-facility requirements. | $2–6M | 0.5–1 yr | Supports TRL 2; does not alone establish TRL 3 |
| **Branch-specific critical-function tests** | Experiments: approximately 10–50 kW iodine/argon hardware, representative discharge conditions, cathode current density, feed dynamics and material temperatures; roughly 500–2,000 h screening. | $15–40M | 1–2.5 yr | TRL 3 when predictions are experimentally validated |
| **Full-power module breadboard** | Experiment: one 100–250 kW thruster with representative PPU, cathode, feed and controller in vacuum; measured thrust/Isp, conducted heat, electrical stability and wear. | $40–100M | 2–4 yr | TRL 4 |
| **Relevant-environment module and interaction testing** | Experiment: full-power modules and a 2–4-module arrangement; thermal cycling, representative plume geometry, contamination coupons, restart/fault testing, radiation testing of electronics and representative feed behaviour. | $70–180M | 2–4 yr | TRL 5 if scaling requirements and environment effects are documented |
| **Lifetime and throughput validation** | Experiment plus test-validated analysis: full-power endurance articles covering the duty envelope, cathode/feed/PPU life, destructive inspections and independently validated wear models. | $150–400M | 4–7 yr | Supports TRL 5–6; does not establish integrated TRL 6 alone |
| **Integrated MW prototype demonstration** | Experiment: 1 MW cluster, extending toward 2.5 MW or validating that extension with physical data; flight-representative PPUs, distribution, protection, feed and controls; relevant thermal/plume geometry and fault injection. | $100–300M | 3–5 yr | TRL 6, conditional on credible life/throughput closure |
| **Operational-space pathfinder** | Experiment: preferably a representative 100–250 kW module with its actual PPU/feed, spacecraft surfaces and plume geometry. A much smaller iodine flight does not validate high-power scaling. | $100–300M | 3–5 yr | Module TRL 7; not automatically MW-cluster TRL 7 |

Important qualification conditions:

- **5,000 or 10,000 h is not the required lifetime.** Such tests are intermediate evidence.
- A 40,000 h continuous test alone takes **4.6 years**. Parallel articles improve statistical coverage but do not accumulate ageing time on one article.
- Lifetime predictions can support TRL 6 only where the failure mechanisms and acceleration factors are physically validated. Unvalidated linear erosion extrapolation is insufficient.
- Ground vacuum facilities must establish background-pressure and charge-exchange corrections. Large pumping capacity does not automatically create a representative space plume.
- Microgravity-sensitive iodine feed behaviour needs suitable flight or other physical evidence. Hall discharge physics need not all be re-demonstrated in microgravity.
- Include plume exposure of arrays, radiators, insulation and electrical interfaces. The test must cover deposition, corrosion, charging and performance degradation for the selected propellant.

### 5. Risk and fallback

**Primary classification: project-ending for the revised production-dependent 0B architecture.** It is not project-ending for 0A research and scouts.

Branch failures have different consequences:

- **Iodine failure with qualified argon available:** architecture-change, with likely cost and schedule consequences.
- **Failure of all affordable, supply-supported branches:** the product transport chain fails; retain **0A only, Earth supply**, as specified by D-02.
- **Xenon:** retain as a technical comparator or limited demonstration propellant. Its technological maturity does not close bulk supply feasibility.

Smaller tugs using lower-power modules are an **architecture-change candidate**, not an established fallback. They require new trajectory, fleet-throughput, lifetime, power, supply and delivered-cost closure. Multiplying vehicle count does not remove total impulse or total propellant demand.

**Needed:** 0A for development and demonstration; **0B as the gating application**; continued qualification for phase-1 operations.

### 6. Where assessors might disagree

1. **TRL 2 versus 3 for iodine:** small iodine propulsion has been physically demonstrated, but whether it validates a critical function of this particular high-power application is contestable. I would carry **TRL 2 conservatively** until scale-relevant critical-function data exist.
2. **Xenon module versus cluster rating:** X3 justifies substantially stronger module readiness. An assessor treating the thruster alone as T01 could assign a higher rating; that would omit the required PPUs, feed, interactions and lifetime.
3. **TRL 6 lifetime sufficiency:** NASA’s criterion does not prescribe a universal number of test hours. A rigorous, test-validated life model may suffice; a short test plus design-life assertion does not.
4. **Propellant selection:** iodine’s storage/supply attractions, argon’s abundance and xenon’s stronger propulsion heritage are different advantages. None resolves the others’ qualification gaps.
5. **Schedule and cost:** these are low-confidence planning estimates. Availability of a suitably pumped MW test facility could dominate both.
