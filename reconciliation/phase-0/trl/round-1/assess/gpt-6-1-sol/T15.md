## T15 — Site power generation and transfer

**Assessment: integrated T15 is TRL 2–3 today; the preferred flat-plate photovoltaic architecture needs TRL 6 before KDP-0B. AD²: 5 for the modular, short-tether baseline; higher for free-flying tethered or beamed alternatives.**

This is **not** a finding that photovoltaic cells are immature. Space photovoltaics are flight-proven. The gap is an autonomously deployed, maintainable **2.5–8 MW NEA power system**, including its supports, distribution, fault protection and 10–15-year service-life qualification.

**Recommendation:** retain **surface-supported, independently sun-tracking flat-plate arrays with sectionalised wired distribution** as the reference development path. Keep free-flying arrays with tethered transfer as an alternative until target rotation, illumination and anchoring conditions are measured. Do not baseline beamed transfer or high-concentration photovoltaics for 0B.

### Scope and assumptions

- The requirement is **2.5–8 MW delivered to site loads**, not merely array nameplate power. Whether it is continuous, peak or production-window power remains unresolved.
- I assume Earth-manufactured cells and power electronics, modular deployment, replaceable sections, and no requirement for asteroid-manufactured photovoltaic cells.
- I interpret “surface arrays” as supported structures with clearance from the surface—not panels laid directly on abrasive regolith.
- The supplied **100 MW SEL4/5 studies, 600 V bus and $500 million budget are superseded background**, not current T15 requirements or evidence.
- Tug power is outside this assessment except as potentially shared component development.
- The three metallurgy routes need separate load specifications. Their metallurgical readiness belongs elsewhere; their power interfaces are distinguished below.

If 2.5–8 MW must remain available at **1.3 AU at year 15**, an illustrative 1.5% annual degradation assumption gives:

\[
P_{\mathrm{BOL},1AU}\geq P_{\mathrm{load}}\frac{1.3^2}{0.985^{15}}
\approx 2.12P_{\mathrm{load}}
\]

That means **approximately 5.3–17 MW at 1 AU**, before conversion, wiring, pointing, dust, availability and reserve margins. **The degradation assumption is not validated here.**

At 30% conversion efficiency, the collecting area is approximately **6,100–19,600 m²** for the unmarginated 1-AU requirement, or **13,000–42,000 m²** for that illustrative worst-distance/end-of-life sizing.

### Evidence that is missing

I know of **no demonstrated integrated MW-class NEA surface power installation**, no autonomous deployment of the required collecting area on a small body, and no MW-class free-flying array delivering power to a rotating asteroid through a tether or beam.

Long-lived GEO arrays establish a substantial component lifetime experience base. They do not, by themselves, qualify the proposed NEA deployment, dust exposure, anchoring, wiring and maintenance architecture.

**Why not TRL 6–9 for flat-plate T15?** Those levels can describe particular flown arrays or cells. They cannot be transferred wholesale to an installation hundreds of times larger, with new deployment, support, distribution and maintenance interfaces.

## 3. Target and difficulty

- **Internal 0A milestone:** TRL **4–5**, with documented laboratory performance and selected relevant-environment testing.
- **Before KDP-0B:** TRL **6** for the selected integrated power architecture because loss of dependable site power is project-ending for 0B.
- **Calendar date:** none is specified. A plausible baseline development allowance is **5–8 years from funded start**, with an internal 0A review after approximately **2–3 years**. These are planning estimates, not established programme dates.
- **AD² 5:** the preferred baseline combines familiar conversion technology with unproven NEA deployment, support, high-voltage distribution and maintainability. The path is identifiable, but the analogous integrated development has not been done.
- Free-flying tethered and beamed paths remain **AD² 6–7** because competing approaches and coupled dynamics/thermal/pointing solutions remain unresolved.

TRL 6 does **not** require waiting 15 years. It does require a validated service-life case using accelerated tests, physics-of-failure models and relevant operational evidence. Accelerated irradiation alone cannot demonstrate all mechanical, contamination and maintenance effects.

### Conditions for accepting the final TRL 6 claim

1. **Electrical topology is fixed.** Sending the entire site power through a single 1–3 kV connection implies approximately **0.83–8 kA**. Distributed feeders avoid treating a robot-scale cable as a site-scale trunk.
2. Full rated voltage and representative fault energy are physically tested; low-power tests cannot establish high-voltage arc behaviour.
3. Structural and deployment models reproduce measured behaviour, including microgravity reactions and target rotation.
4. Tests bound contamination, abrasion, thermal cycling and radiation over the service-life envelope.
5. Required availability, safe shutdown, sectional isolation and repair/replacement are demonstrated.
6. The system-level model predicts **delivered end-of-life power**, not just cell efficiency.

A small pathfinder plus an unvalidated extrapolation is **not** a TRL 6 exit.

For the free-flying alternatives, add a coupled tether-dynamics experiment or beam-pointing/receiver experiment. I would allow **6–10 years** for those paths and avoid assigning credible total costs before separation distance and transfer geometry are selected.

## 5. Risk and fallback

**Integrated T15 failure: project-ending for 0B.** It need not end 0A.

- **Surface deployment/anchoring failure:** architecture-change risk; investigate free-flying wired generation only if its coupling and dynamics are demonstrably acceptable.
- **Tethered free-flyer failure:** revert to surface-supported distributed generation or change target.
- **Beamed-transfer or concentrator failure:** architecture-change or cost-increase risk; use flat-plate arrays and physical conductors.
- **Lifetime shortfall:** replacement modules or overbuild are possible only after their logistics, availability and economics are validated.

**Pre-committed ultimate fallback:** retain 0A research and survey capabilities, do not authorise 0B production, and supply Phase 1 from Earth under D-01/D-02.

## 6. Where others might disagree

1. **Assessment boundary is the principal disagreement.** A component assessor can correctly call particular space cells TRL 9. A system assessor cannot therefore call T15 TRL 9.
2. **Modular replication may reduce the demonstration burden.** That is valid only where array dynamics, deployment, protection, thermal behaviour and reliability scale predictably. Module count alone does not prove this.
3. **Surface versus free-flying is target-dependent.** Rapid rotation, severe shadows or poor attachment conditions could overturn the reference choice. Survey evidence—not consensus—must settle it.
4. **Concentrator heritage is real but narrow.** SCARLET supports low concentration, not the supplied 500× concept.
5. **The supplied literature summaries need verification.** I have not independently verified every listed arXiv attribution or numerical inference, and do not use them as established T15 qualification evidence.

Several background assertions also need correction:

- At 500×, incident solar flux at 1 AU is approximately **68 W/cm²**, not 350 W/cm² of waste heat.
- Concentrators reduce **cell area**, not collecting-aperture area by the same factor.
- Active cooling is not automatically required; passive feasibility depends on geometry and allowable temperature.
- Sun–Earth L4/L5 do not undergo routine Earth eclipses. Their radiation and geometry are also not the revised NEA site requirement.
- Annual degradation confidence intervals and thermal-annealing credits require a traceable, test-validated mission model.
