The difficulty is principally engineering rather than uncertain basic science:

- low-g powder/granule transport and confinement;
- consolidation of heterogeneous, contaminated feed;
- rotating/nonrotating transfer and thermal interfaces;
- autonomous recovery from jams, failed batches and equipment wear;
- reproducible product acceptance at the required production cadence.

**Planning estimate: 7–12 years from funded development start to TRL 6**, conditional on access to a sustained partial-gravity test platform and selection of a modular process. This is not an established schedule. A full smelting/slag-separation branch could exceed it.

### Gate conditions

1. **Reject rotation if it adds complexity without measurable process benefit.**
2. Compare rotating and no-melt routes on **accepted product**, not merely extracted metal.
3. For the **Gemini smelting branch**, retain the proposed **≥1 t partial-g slag/metal separation experiment**. Do not impose slag separation on a GPT process that demonstrably avoids a slag-producing melt.
4. Demonstrate the declared module production rate. A collection of successful small batches is insufficient without validated replication, availability and shared-interface scaling.
5. The 1,000–3,000-hour campaign is a proposed development test, **not evidence of annual availability or multi-year life**. Those require measured failure/wear data, life models and a maintainable replacement architecture.
6. Include representative vacuum, dust charging/abrasion, thermal boundary conditions for 0.9–1.3 AU, and radiation-sensitive controls. Individual material tests alone do not establish integrated environmental readiness.

## 5. Risk and fallback

**Primary risk class: architecture-change.**

Failure of this particular rotating route should not automatically terminate all of 0B while an independently qualified no-melt route remains available. Failure of **all** qualified production routes is **project-ending for the production option**, invoking “0A only, Earth supply.”

Fallback order:

1. Select a nonrotating or differently confined solid-state consolidation route, **only after its own qualification**.
2. Use terrestrial-produced feedstock/products and retain 0A survey and technology work.
3. Consider higher artificial gravity only as a separately tested redesign.

Increasing rotation to 0.3–0.5 g is not a proven fallback: it changes loads, gravity gradients, Coriolis effects, transfer systems and containment. Nor does rotation provide an ultimate heat sink; external heat rejection remains necessary.

## 6. Where others might disagree

- **TRL 2 versus 3:** An assessor may credit terrestrial powder-metallurgy experiments as proof of a key consolidation function. I accept that for a defined unit operation, but not as proof of the coupled rotating production subsystem.
- **Mandatory rotation:** Gemini treats low artificial gravity as the baseline remedy; GPT should preserve the possibility that mechanical confinement and solid-state processing make rotation unnecessary.
- **Tonne-scale testing:** A validated, replicated smaller module might support TRL 6 without a 1 t batch. Conversely, a tonne-batch design cannot claim readiness from kilogram tests without convincing scaling evidence.
- **AD² 5 versus 6:** AD² 5 becomes more defensible after route selection and successful coupled-function tests. Today the unresolved route and platform choices support 6.
- **No-melt does not mean gravity-independent:** Hydrogen/carbonyl chemistry can avoid bulk melting while still leaving difficult gas–solid transport, reagent recovery, dust containment and product-consolidation problems.
