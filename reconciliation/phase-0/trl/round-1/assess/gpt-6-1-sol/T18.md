## T18 assessment — Subsurface sampling and in-situ bulk grade assay

**Current TRL: 2–3 for the required integrated capability; target TRL 6 before KDP-0B. AD²: 6.**

The original **TRL 5–6 prospecting-constellation rating does not transfer** to this requirement. Rendezvous, surface spectroscopy and sample collection have substantial heritage. **Repeated, depth-resolved sampling followed by a representative in-situ recoverable-metal grade estimate does not.**

### Assessment assumptions

The required accuracy, campaign duration and mining depth have not been specified. I use the following **provisional acceptance specification**, not an already agreed requirement:

- **At least two candidate NEAs**, with **six spatially separated sampling stations per candidate**, selected using geological and geophysical mapping.
- Three depth intervals per station: approximately **0–0.1 m, 0.3–0.5 m and at least 1 m**. Samples must retain depth provenance rather than indiscriminately mixing drill cuttings.
- **0.1–1 kg per interval**, giving **1.8–18 kg assayed per candidate**. This is a starting test envelope, not proof that this mass is statistically sufficient.
- **Recoverable Fe-Ni yield on a dry, unbeneficiated-feed basis**, including beneficiation and processing losses. Concentrate grade alone is insufficient for the economic gate.
- Near the proposed 5 wt% economic floor, a **95% uncertainty interval with half-width no greater than one percentage point**, including sampling, analytical and recovery-model uncertainty. Pass only if the lower bound exceeds the economic threshold established at KDP-0B.
- Several months of sampling and assay operations per target, following a multiyear cruise.

**Why at least 1 m?** It provides a useful test of differences between surface material and shallow subsurface feed, but it is **not a scientifically universal representative depth**. If planned excavation extends deeper, or measurements reveal layering, sampling must extend accordingly. Six stations also cannot guarantee the stated uncertainty: the campaign must expand if heterogeneity requires it.

I would **not adopt** the supplied review’s statements that asteroid subsurface interaction has never extended below approximately 1 cm, or that returned material is uniformly from the top centimetre. Bennu TAG penetration and Ryugu impact-assisted collection contradict that simplification. Conversely, neither provides controlled depth-resolved assay.

The review’s precise porosity-versus-depth tables and some paper-title/arXiv associations need independent verification. They are not used as TRL evidence here.

## 2. Current TRL and unmet exit criterion

**Integrated capability: TRL 2–3.**

- **TRL 2 is supported:** the application is technically formulated, and heritage establishes plausible sampling, sample preparation and analytical approaches.
- **TRL 3 is the conditional upper bound:** physical tests and flight operations validate several critical functions separately. Crediting those results to this particular integrated application requires a documented applicability and scaling argument.
- **TRL 4 is not established:** no identified test demonstrates a representative breadboard performing repeated, depth-tagged collection, preparation, assay and route-specific yield estimation against known heterogeneous feed, with performance agreeing with predictions.

Flight-proven surface sampling or Mars mineralogy instruments should not pull the integrated rating upward by averaging subsystem TRLs.

### Preserve the metallurgy disagreement

The assay campaign must produce separate recovery estimates, not average the routes:

| Route | Required assay/recovery validation |
|---|---|
| **Claude: magnetic beneficiation → carbonyl or H₂ route** | Measure native-metal fraction and magnetic separation response; distinguish metallic Fe-Ni from oxide-bound iron. Validate the selected downstream chemistry on representative splits. Carbonyl processing and H₂ reduction are not interchangeable recovery factors. |
| **Gemini: rotating smelting at 0.05–0.15 g** | Measure phase composition and impurities, then validate metal yield and partitioning under the furnace’s relevant conditions. Total elemental Fe is not demonstrated smelting yield. |
| **GPT: narrow native-metal production** | Quantify liberated and recoverable native Fe-Ni, losses and product purity. Preserve the approximately 20% recoverable-grade assumption as a separate, stronger candidate gate. |

For **route-conditioned grade prediction within T18**, each remains **TRL 2–3 on the evidence identified here**. This is not a rating of the furnace or chemical-processing technologies themselves.

## 5. Risk and fallback

**Risk class: project-ending for 0B.** Failure leaves the feedstock basis of the investment unverified, even if spacecraft and production hardware are otherwise ready.

Fallback: **retain 0A research and remote reconnaissance, use Earth supply, and reconsider 0B only after deeper sampling or another candidate closes the gate.** Remote spectra are not an acceptable substitute for sampled recoverable grade.

## 6. Where others might disagree

1. **TRL 2 versus 3:** an assessor may credit the separate flight demonstrations as sufficient critical-function proof. I retain TRL 2 as the conservative bound because representative integrated grade estimation is not demonstrated.
2. **Ground versus flight TRL 6:** some may accept vacuum and reduced-gravity tests plus validated modelling; others will require an NEA demonstration to close sustained contact, anchoring and sample-transfer uncertainties.
3. **Depth and sample count:** 1 m, six stations and kilogram-scale aliquots are provisional engineering assumptions. Spatial correlation and coarse metal grains could require substantially more sampling.
4. **Accuracy and economics:** the proposed ±1 percentage-point interval may be inadequate near a marginal economic boundary. The 5% and approximately 20% grade positions remain distinct gate hypotheses, not a consensus value.
