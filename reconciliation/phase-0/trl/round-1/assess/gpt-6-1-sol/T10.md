## T10 — Long-duration autonomy for mining, processing and proximity operations

**Assessment: TRL 3–4 for the integrated capability; use TRL 3 for gate planning. Target: TRL 6 at KDP-0B. AD²: 6.**

Spacecraft have demonstrated autonomous planning, fault responses and proximity operations. That does **not** establish 90 days of unattended asteroid excavation and processing. The principal gap is sustained, productive recovery from interacting mechanical, process, navigation and power faults—not simply operating without real-time commands.

I interpret **“no required ground intervention”** to mean that operators may receive telemetry, but no command, diagnosis, software patch or decision from Earth is necessary to complete the qualifying operating period. Entering safe mode and waiting for Earth does not qualify as successful recovery.

### Assessment boundary

The qualifying system must encompass:

- **6–20 mining robots**, each 2–3.5 t, excavating at 0.5–5 t/h while active, with tether management and 9–25% duty cycle.
- Co-located beneficiation and the selected Fe-Ni production route, including feed variability, product verification and tailings containment.
- Autonomous proximity operations, including navigation uncertainty, collision avoidance and recovery from interrupted transfers.
- Operation with **seconds to 25 minutes one-way light time**, plus conjunction outages.
- At least **90 consecutive calendar days** without required ground intervention.

At the specified mining duty cycle, 90 days represents approximately **194–540 active excavation hours per robot**. Both calendar duration and active workload must be demonstrated; a mostly idle 90-day test is insufficient.

The supplied research-question backgrounds contain older fleet and architecture assumptions. I use the revised architecture above. I also do **not** treat the statement that the bucket-wheel system was “validated in rq-0-26” as evidence without its test report.

### Essential acceptance conditions

1. **Productive recovery:** faults must lead to continued qualified production or autonomous reconfiguration—not indefinite safe-mode waiting.
2. **Blind fault trials:** independently selected faults must test more than pre-scripted nominal recoveries.
3. **Fleet independence:** shared learning must not create common-mode corruption; each robot needs a safe degraded mode.
4. **Measured consequences:** record throughput, product quality, energy use, wear, escaped material, recovery time and operator interventions.
5. **Declared outage envelope:** conjunction duration is presently unspecified. The test must cover the adopted maximum, not merely impose light-time delays.
6. **Reliability limits:** one successful 90-day run demonstrates capability but does not establish a five-year fleet reliability distribution. Subsequent qualification and operational evidence remain necessary.

## 5. Risk and fallback

**Risk class: project-ending for 0B under the specified unattended-operation requirement.** It is not project-ending for 0A research and survey.

Fallbacks, in order:

- Reduce process complexity and qualify a narrower native-metal operation.
- Reduce fleet concurrency, isolate failures, and provide redundant tools and replaceable modules.
- Use ground-assisted operation only if the requirement is formally changed. This is an **architecture change**, not compliance with the present gate.
- If no route reaches TRL 6, apply D-02: **0A only, Earth supply**.

Adding operators cannot solve immediate excavation hazards during a 25-minute one-way delay. Local protective control remains necessary even in a ground-assisted fallback.

## 6. Where others might disagree

- **System boundary:** spacecraft autonomy specialists may argue for TRL 6–9 by citing flown components. Those ratings can be correct for those components, but not for this integrated industrial capability.
- **Whether TRL 3 is already justified:** a stricter assessor could assign integrated TRL 2 because the cited demonstrations validate different applications. My TRL 3 credits transferable critical autonomy functions; route-specific implementations remain at a secure TRL 2.
- **Relevant-environment sufficiency:** some assessors may accept gravity-offloaded terrestrial tests plus validated analysis for TRL 6. I would not accept them without evidence closing granular contact, anchoring and process-gravity coupling.
- **No-melt versus smelting difficulty:** neither route has demonstrated the required unattended recovery. No-melt reduces some thermal/melt hazards but can introduce difficult gas-containment and fouling faults.
- **Meaning of unattended:** a system allowed to stop safely until Earth returns has a substantially easier requirement than the productive, no-required-intervention interpretation used here.
