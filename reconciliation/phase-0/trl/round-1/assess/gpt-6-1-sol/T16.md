## T16 — Anchoring, hold-down and surface mobility on low-cohesion bodies

**Current TRL: 2–3 for the production-scale integrated capability. Target: TRL 6 at KDP-0B. AD²: 7.**

The original entry’s TRL 3 justification is invalid: **model consensus does not demonstrate a critical function.** Physical heritage supports surface interaction, small-scale mobility and anchoring to competent rough surfaces—not sustained reaction of mining loads on a rubble-pile asteroid.

This assessment covers **excavator anchoring, plant and power-tether hold-down, tailings restraint, and relocation/re-anchoring**. It does not rate the bucket-wheel cutter itself.

### Assessment assumptions

- Robots: **2–3.5 t**, excavating **0.5–5 t/h**, at **9–25% duty cycle**.
- Service life: **at least five years**, approximately **3,900–11,000 active excavation hours**, plus continuous exposure and potentially continuous plant/tailings restraint.
- Environment: NEA microgravity, vacuum, thermal cycling at **0.9–1.3 AU**, abrasive and potentially charged regolith, and cumulative radiation.
- Tailings: **13–47 kt/yr**, or **65–235 kt over five years** if retained locally without removal. Tailings containment is a substantial civil/structural function, not merely another robot anchor.
- Excavation loads remain **unverified**. The supplied residual torque range of **0.25–5 kN·m** is a provisional test envelope, not demonstrated performance. At a 1 m effective restraint arm, that corresponds to **0.25–5 kN of differential reaction force**, before adding translational cutting forces, shocks, tether loads or plant loads.
- No absolute target gravity, regolith strength profile, anchor geometry or plant mass is specified. These must be fixed before TRL 5 scaling can be closed.

**Counter-rotation does not eliminate translational excavation forces. Nor does gripping a boulder establish that the boulder itself can react the load without moving.**

## 2. Current TRL and unmet exits

- **Lower bound, TRL 2:** documented application concepts exist, and physical heritage establishes feasible principles for selected functions.
- **Upper bound, TRL 3:** defensible only for a narrowly defined critical function—such as engaging rough competent rock—using physical analogue tests. It is **not a demonstrated TRL 3 production anchoring system**.

### Metallurgy-route implications

All three routes inherit the same basic T16 gap; none receives additional TRL credit from a design study.

- **No-melt beneficiation/carbonyl or H₂ route:** rated **2–3** for its integrated surface restraint. Process vessels, transfer equipment and tailings still require restraint.
- **Rotating 0.05–0.15 g smelting route:** rated **2–3** for surface restraint. Internal centrifugal acceleration is not automatically transmitted as an equivalent static ground load, but spin-up, imbalance, bearing reactions and faults must be included.
- **Narrow native-metal route with experimentally gated rotating modules:** rated **2–3** until its actual equipment and load interfaces are demonstrated.

The rotating routes add qualification cases; they do not establish an independently higher or lower anchoring TRL without hardware evidence.

**AD²: 7**, using the project’s working anchors.

The difficult step is not manufacturing a stronger anchor. It is establishing a reliable load path through poorly confined, heterogeneous material, then maintaining and verifying that load path while mining changes the terrain. Coupled granular mechanics, autonomy and long-duration restraint require fundamental engineering work.

A target proven to expose extensive competent rock could reduce the difficulty to approximately **AD² 5–6**. That is a target-selection contingency, not evidence that the rubble-pile requirement is solved.

### Essential qualification constraints

1. **A supported regolith tray can give misleadingly good results.** Its walls and floor may supply confinement and reaction forces absent on the asteroid.
2. **Reduced-gravity test duration is a limitation.** Parabolic flight and drop towers inform transient mechanics but cannot establish long-term creep or multi-year attachment.
3. **The 200–600 kg scout is not automatically production-representative.** Required robot mass is **3.3–17.5×** greater. Its pathfinder payload must reproduce the critical anchor geometry, forces, moments and substrate failure domains, or additional demonstration is needed.
4. **Lifetime cannot be asserted from a brief flight success.** Accelerated tests need validated degradation and creep models; otherwise longer exposure or redesign remains necessary.
5. **Both variants need evidence.** A successful monolith demonstration does not qualify rubble-pile operations. KDP-0B may instead explicitly restrict production to the demonstrated target class.

## 5. Risk and fallback

**Risk class: project-ending for the selected 0B production architecture.**

Failure to react excavation loads or restrain tailings prevents safe, reliable production. It does not end 0A research or the wider Dyson programme.

Fallback hierarchy:

1. Select a qualified **competent-rock target** and constrain excavation to demonstrated attachment sites.
2. Change to an **enclosed, internally reacted excavation/processing module**, with its own separately demonstrated capture and structural interfaces.
3. If neither passes the gate, use the pre-committed **“0A only, Earth supply”** outcome.

**Thermal volatile extraction is not an equivalent fallback:** the revised product is Fe-Ni, and thermal extraction does not remove plant, tether or tailings restraint requirements.

## 6. Where others might disagree

- **TRL 3 versus 4:** an assessor focused on a particular terrestrial rock gripper may reasonably assign TRL 4 to that component. Applying it to the full T16 requirement would overextend the evidence.
- **AD² 6 versus 7:** the lower rating is plausible if measured target properties show stable competent rock or a readily enclosed small body. The current rubble-pile requirement does not establish that condition.
- **Whether flight is essential for TRL 6:** NASA TRL 6 does not universally require flight. Here, a convincing ground qualification could suffice only with validated scaling of weak-gravity granular load transfer. I would not assume that scaling is already available.
- **Whether both target variants must pass KDP-0B:** qualifying only the selected target class is defensible if the architecture formally drops the unqualified variant. It is not defensible to claim both variants qualified from one demonstration.
