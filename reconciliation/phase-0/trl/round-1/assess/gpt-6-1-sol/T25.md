## T25 — Target-local and SEL1 cargo-node navigation and control

**Assessment: current TRL 2–3 for the architecture-specific integrated capability; target TRL 6 at KDP-0B; AD² 6.**

Individual capabilities have substantial flight heritage: autonomous rendezvous, asteroid-relative navigation, docking, and Sun–Earth L1 stationkeeping. **Their combination for repeated operations with 150–300 t payloads, large flexible electric-propulsion tugs, and an unspecified cargo node has not been demonstrated.** Heritage subsystem TRLs should not be transferred to this integrated capability.

### Assessment assumptions

- “SEL1” means **Sun–Earth L1**, with a controlled halo or Lissajous orbit—not a spacecraft held motionless at the mathematical equilibrium point.
- Target-local operations occur near an NEA, including a processing installation and possible dust, debris, irregular gravity and rotating structures.
- The assessed capability includes relative navigation, approach/departure guidance, collision avoidance, stationkeeping, capture/contact control and safe abort. It does **not** qualify the tug propulsion system or cargo handling hardware independently.
- Payload is 150–300 t; approaching vehicle mass is necessarily higher. Node mass, inertia, structural modes, interface design and total tug mass remain unknown.
- Repeated service is required, but the revised architecture does not specify a navigation-system lifetime or docking-cycle qualification requirement. The older 15-year requirement is treated as **provisional**, not adopted automatically.
- No calendar date for KDP-0B is supplied. Dates below are therefore milestone-relative.

Metallurgy-route selection matters only insofar as it changes the target installation’s rotation, geometry, plume/debris environment and docking interface. The three metallurgy routes do not warrant separate T25 ratings before those interfaces are defined.

**AD²: 6 — competing approaches remain, and the development path is not yet selected.**

The uncertainty is principally engineering and architecture integration, not unknown fundamental science:

- Direct docking versus robotic berthing versus free-flying cargo handling;
- Rigid versus compliant capture and allowable approach speed;
- Coupled tug/node flexible dynamics;
- Safe retreat authority when main electric propulsion is unsuitable for terminal manoeuvres;
- Fault containment without prompt ground intervention;
- Rotation and debris exclusion near the target plant.

A frozen cooperative node with passive targets, separated approach lanes and conventional capture could reduce AD² toward **5**. A rotating, debris-exposed interface or novel non-contact capture could increase it toward **7**.

**Estimated development time:** **5–9 years from funded start**, conditional on interface freeze and flight-demonstrator access. This is a planning estimate, not a committed schedule.

### Conditions on the final step

A 10–30 t flight vehicle leaves a **5–30× payload-mass gap**. A space demonstration does not erase that gap merely by flying.

The evidence must cover:

- Effective contact mass, interface stiffness/damping and structural modes;
- Sensor performance under NEA-like lighting and target geometry;
- Terminal manoeuvre authority, actuator faults and plume constraints;
- Loss of navigation, communications and power during close approach;
- Concurrent departure/arrival conflicts and safe holding;
- Capture/latch wear over the required service cycles;
- Node attitude disturbances and recovery after transfer.

Reduced-scale flight evidence must be tied to full-scale physical tests and validated extrapolation. If that cannot be defended, the demonstrator must become larger or the architecture must change.

A LEO demonstration alone does not reproduce NEA gravity, dust or the SEL1 operational geometry. The test programme must establish which environmental differences matter and demonstrate the affected functions separately. **TRL 6 is a conditional outcome, not a guaranteed reward for completing the listed activities.**

## 5. Risk and fallback

**Risk class: architecture-change.**

Failure would prevent use of the reference docking/berthing architecture, but does not necessarily end all 0B options. Alternatives include:

- Smaller independently controlled cargo parcels;
- A stand-off depot with segregated transfer lanes;
- Dedicated handling craft;
- Capture at a non-rotating, isolated target-local interface.

These alternatives require their own qualification and may impose substantial cost or throughput penalties.

**If no alternative achieves TRL 6 by KDP-0B, dependent 0B production cannot pass D-02.** The fallback is 0A-only research with Earth-supplied Phase 1 material. In that circumstance, this technology becomes project-ending for the proposed 0B delivery architecture.

## 6. Where others might disagree

1. **TRL 2–3 versus 4–5:** an assessor defining T25 narrowly as conventional cooperative rendezvous software could cite much higher heritage maturity. That excludes the coupled mass, flexible structures, contact and traffic functions explicitly required here.
2. **Mass as a scaling measure:** some navigation functions scale weakly with mass. Agreed; the low integrated rating is driven mainly by coupled control/contact dynamics and missing system definition.
3. **Whether space testing is necessary for TRL 6:** a sufficiently representative ground prototype can satisfy TRL 6 for some functions. Here, combined microgravity contact and flexible-body dynamics make space correlation valuable; it is not a universal NASA requirement.
4. **Direct docking versus berthing:** neither is selected by the evidence presented. Magnetic/electrostatic capture is an additional development option, not demonstrated heritage for this load class.
5. **Risk classification:** it becomes project-ending if demand, cost or schedule rules exclude smaller parcels and alternate transfer architectures. Those constraints have not yet been established.
