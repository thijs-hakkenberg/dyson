## T14 — Bulk propellant storage, feed and refuelling

**Assessment: TRL 2–3 for the integrated, hundreds-of-tonnes tug subsystem; target TRL 6 at KDP-0B; AD² 6.** Small iodine propulsion systems and conventional pressure-fed spacecraft systems have substantial heritage. That does **not** establish bulk iodine sublimation, long-lived orbital liquid-argon storage, or refuelling at this scale.

This is a storage-and-feed assessment, **not a re-rating of Hall thrusters or solar-electric propulsion generally**. The original entry’s thruster wear tests do not establish T14 readiness.

### Assumptions and requirement boundary

- I treat **145–720 t as potentially the maximum onboard inventory**, because the requirement explicitly calls for storage at hundreds of tonnes per tug. If it instead means cumulative lifetime consumption supplied through frequent refuelling, specify maximum tank inventory separately: storage scaling could become substantially easier, but transfer and cumulative feed-life requirements remain.
- Required iodine flow is **7–18 kg/h = 1.9–5.0 g/s**. Over 20,000–40,000 operating hours, that corresponds to approximately **140–720 t**, consistent with the stated lifetime consumption.
- At approximate material densities, 145–720 t occupies **29–146 m³ of solid iodine** or **104–514 m³ of liquid argon**, before ullage, packaging, insulation and feed equipment. These are sizing calculations, not demonstrations.
- Relevant conditions include vacuum, microgravity, thermal cycling at 0.9–1.3 AU, radiation, launch loads, repeated starts and refuelling. Tanks may be shielded from regolith; exposed docking interfaces still require a contamination-control specification.
- Calendar storage life could exceed the **1.5–5-year round trip**. The architecture has not specified the number of trips or total calendar life.

## 3. Target and difficulty

- **0A:** TRL **4–5** for representative storage/feed modules and transfer breadboards by the internal 0A review. The scouts can use separately qualified, small propulsion systems; they do not require bulk T14.
- **0B:** TRL **6 at KDP-0B**, without a waiver, if the cargo service is necessary to make 0B viable. No calendar date for KDP-0B is supplied.
- **Estimated development interval:** **7–12 years** from funded start to an adequately evidenced TRL 6 campaign. This is a planning range, not a committed schedule.
- **AD²:** iodine **6**; high-pressure argon **5**; cryogenic argon **7**; integrated subsystem with refuelling **6** using the project’s working anchors.

Iodine has competing bulk-feed and replenishment approaches. High-pressure argon uses familiar physics but may impose prohibitive tank mass. Cryogenic argon adds long-duration heat rejection/refrigeration, microgravity acquisition and transfer; its difficulty is principally fundamental engineering, not uncertain science.

A 100–200 t demonstration is not automatically sufficient for a 720 t design. Distributed tank/feed modules could justify scaling through replication; a single bulk iodine reservoir might require a much larger demonstrator. Accelerated tests must preserve the actual failure mechanisms—especially iodine redistribution and deposition—and cannot simply replace 40,000 hours by assertion.

## 5. Risk and fallback

**Primary classification: project-ending for 0B** if neither propellant route can provide the required cargo service at acceptable mass, reliability and cost. Individual route failures are **architecture-change** risks.

The pre-committed programme fallback is **0A only, Earth supply**. Smaller tank modules, more frequent refuelling or smaller tugs are possible architecture changes, but require a new delivered-cost and mission analysis; they are not automatically equivalent substitutes.

## 6. Where others might disagree

1. **Cumulative consumption versus onboard inventory.** This is the largest unresolved requirement. Modular tanks holding tens rather than hundreds of tonnes could warrant a higher storage rating, while leaving refuelling as the dominant low-TRL function.
2. **How much small-system iodine evidence transfers.** A modular design may preserve demonstrated heat-transfer distances. A large contiguous solid inventory does not: depletion geometry, thermal gradients and vapour transport can change materially.
3. **Whether terrestrial cryogenic storage establishes TRL 4.** It establishes mass-scale storage, but I would not assign TRL 4 to an orbital cryogenic feed/acquisition subsystem without its own laboratory breadboard.
4. **What constitutes a representative TRL 6 prototype.** A smaller orbital demonstrator plus a large ground prototype can be sufficient only with validated scaling. Neither “it flew” nor “the tank was full-size” closes the other environment’s gap.
5. **Refuelling is not one technology.** Solid-iodine cartridge exchange, iodine vapour transfer, high-pressure argon transfer and liquid-argon transfer require separate evidence and should not inherit one another’s ratings.
