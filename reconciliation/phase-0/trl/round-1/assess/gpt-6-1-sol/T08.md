## T08 assessment — Particle containment, escape verification and tailings management

**Current integrated TRL: 2–2. Target: TRL 6 before KDP-0B. AD²: 6.**

Containment, dust detection and surface-cleaning technologies have useful heritage, but **no cited demonstration establishes the required integrated escape performance while managing 13–47 kt/yr of tailings at an asteroid**. The limiting technology is not simply a mining enclosure: it is an independently verifiable, fault-tolerant material-handling system spanning excavation, transfer, processing, storage and docking.

This is **project-ending for 0B**, but not for the survey and research activities of 0A. Agreement on a binding debris limit remains a separate licence gate; TRL cannot establish that agreement.

### Assessment assumptions and required scale

- The **≤1 t/yr ceiling is a design-sizing assumption**, not an accepted licence condition. The supplied architecture identifies D-03 as proposed.
- Until the licence defines otherwise, I would count **material released outside the controlled containment boundary**, including particles that remain gravitationally bound to the asteroid. Restricting “escape” to asteroid-unbound trajectories would overlook local contamination.
- Natural dust and operationally released dust must be distinguished. Re-deposition is not automatically containment, and releasing cleaning dust into space is not mitigation.
- I assume a **five-year production campaign**, drawing on the robot lifetime in rq-0-9. The revised architecture does not explicitly fix the plant or tailings-store lifetime.
- The relevant environment includes vacuum, asteroid gravity, charged abrasive particles, thermal cycling at 0.9–1.3 AU, radiation, and autonomous operation. The furnace route additionally requires its specified **0.05–0.15 g rotating environment**.
- The escape requirement is **site-wide**, not an allowance separately available to each robot or material-handling stage.

Using tailings alone as the denominator:

| Requirement | At 13 kt/yr | At 47 kt/yr |
|---|---:|---:|
| Maximum escaped fraction for 1 t/yr | \(7.69\times10^{-5}\) | \(2.13\times10^{-5}\) |
| Corresponding retention | 99.9923% | 99.9979% |
| Maximum escaped fraction for 10 kg/yr | \(7.69\times10^{-7}\) | \(2.13\times10^{-7}\) |
| Corresponding retention | 99.999923% | 99.999979% |

These are **bookkeeping requirements, not demonstrated efficiencies**. Total handled mass is larger than tailings alone, and repeated handling creates additional opportunities for release.

Other useful sizing quantities:

- Tailings: **0.41–1.49 kg/s calendar-average**.
- Escape ceiling: **31.7 mg/s calendar-average**; aspiration: **0.317 mg/s**.
- Five-year accumulated tailings: **65–235 kt**, absent reuse or an approved alternative disposition.

A guarantee for grains **>5 µm** cannot establish the total mass ceiling or protect optics against smaller grains.

## 2. Current TRL and route-specific assessment

### Integrated system: TRL 2–2

**TRL 2 exit met:** the application and its feasibility questions are documented: enclosed excavation and transfer, controlled processing, retained tailings, escape sensing and protected receivers.

**TRL 3 exit unmet:** no cited physical test or test-validated integrated analysis establishes the key parameters together:

- leakage versus particle size, charge and operating state;
- containment during credible abnormal events;
- observability of total escaped mass;
- tailings stability and inventory growth;
- array/radiator contamination under predicted exposure.

A range of **2–3** would be defensible only after identifying a documented, transferable proof of the architecture’s critical containment function. I have not identified that evidence here.

### Metallurgy routes must remain separate

These ratings concern **T08 implications**, not metallurgy readiness.

There is no evidence basis to average these into a more mature common route. A route-specific escape demonstration cannot qualify a substantially different processing route.

### Essential demonstration conditions

**Escape verification:** input/output weighing alone is unlikely to resolve a \(10^{-5}\)-level loss reliably in a variable multi-stage process. Combine inventory accounting with calibrated particle detectors, optical tracking, deposition witnesses and measurements around likely release paths. Validate detection efficiency with known releases. Report an **upper confidence bound**, not only a best estimate.

**Abnormal events:** test, at minimum:

- excavator housing damage and loss of anchoring;
- blocked transfer and failed valve sequencing;
- loss of power, charging control or communications;
- furnace rotation/cooling loss, or process-gas pressure relief;
- damaged or overfilled tailings containers;
- docking misalignment, interrupted transfer and emergency separation.

Emergency emissions must be included in the annual limit; a “normal-operation efficiency” cannot exclude them.

**Array/radiator protection:** establish separate acceptable deposition and degradation limits for photovoltaic output, optical sensors and radiator thermal performance. Neither a site-wide mass cap nor cleaning heritage alone establishes receiver safety.

**Scale limitation:** the proposed 1–10 t flight handling demonstration is still **1,300–47,000 times below annual tailings mass**. It cannot earn TRL 6 merely by operating in space. The ground and flight programme must validate the scaling parameters, wear mechanisms and storage configuration. If it cannot, a larger or longer demonstration is required.

## 5. Risk and fallback

**Risk class: project-ending for 0B.**

Failure can have narrower consequences—for example, failure of a furnace-specific seal may force an architecture change—but inability to contain and verify the site-wide stream defeats all three production routes.

Preferred mitigation hierarchy:

1. Avoid generating fines where practicable.
2. Capture at source.
3. Use enclosed transfers and passive secondary containment.
4. Retain tailings in closed, inspectable, repairable modules.
5. Protect receivers through layout, shielding and cleaning that itself captures dust.
6. Stop operations when containment or measurement confidence is lost.

Do not credit loose tailings piles, nominally “stable” ejecta trajectories or electrostatic barriers as disposal solutions without validating their long-term stability and fault behaviour.

**Fallback:** continue 0A survey and research, defer asteroid production and use Earth-supplied material; reconsider a smaller production system only if it meets the same licensed site-wide limit.

## 6. Where others might disagree

- **Integrated versus component TRL:** sample-return containers, dust instruments and terrestrial sealed handling equipment can have high heritage TRLs. Those ratings do not transfer to the integrated production system. This is the principal reason for my conservative TRL 2.
- **How much flight mass is sufficient:** some assessors may accept full-rate ground modules plus a small NEA demonstration for TRL 6; others may require much greater accumulated flight handling. The unresolved issue is whether leakage, abrasion and tailings failure scale predictably.
- **Meaning of escape:** asteroid-unbound mass is easier to distinguish conceptually than all uncontrolled release, but it is insufficient as a local contamination requirement.
- **ALARP:** it is an optimization and governance obligation, not a measurable replacement for a ceiling. The licence must define acceptable uncertainty and event accounting.
- **Route difficulty:** native-metal production may genuinely simplify containment. Conversely, rotating modules may add interfaces that erase that advantage. Neither claim is experimentally settled.
