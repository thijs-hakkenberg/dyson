## T20 — Distributed energy storage and microgrid power management

**Current architecture-level TRL: 3–4. Target: TRL 6 before KDP-0B. AD²: 5, rising to 6 if 33,000 substantial-depth cycles are mandatory without replacement.**

The reduced storage requirement makes an Earth-supplied, modular battery architecture plausible. It does **not** establish that an autonomous, megawatt-class NEA power network has been demonstrated. Space battery heritage and terrestrial microgrid heritage support its constituent functions; they do not, by themselves, satisfy the integrated subsystem’s relevant-environment exit criteria.

### Assessment assumptions

- Storage is Earth-manufactured, using qualified rechargeable cells in replaceable, thermally controlled modules. In-space cell manufacture is outside T20.
- Required capacity is **0.5–15 MWh per site**, plus **10–50 kWh per robot**. Whether these are nameplate or end-of-life usable capacities is unresolved.
- Site distribution supplies **2.5–8 MW**; robot tethers operate at **1–3 kV**. Battery strings need not operate directly at tether voltage.
- Vacuum, NEA radiation, microgravity, and the external thermal environment apply to the installed subsystem. Cells may remain within controlled temperature limits; exposing bare cells to asteroid surface temperatures is not a requirement.
- **Service life, discharge power, charging profile, acceptable outage, depth of discharge, and replacement interval remain unspecified.** The proposed **9,000–33,000 cycles** is a contested requirement, not demonstrated performance or an automatic consequence of surface emplacement.
- The obsolete 100 MW, 600 V, SEL4/5 background in rq-0-21/22 is not the assessment baseline.

## 2. Current TRL and missing exits

### Architecture-level rating: **TRL 3–4**

- **TRL 3 is supported:** battery buffering, modular storage, and distributed power-control critical functions have physical experimental evidence.
- **TRL 4 is conditional:** substantial constituent breadboards exist, but a strict TRL 4 claim for *this integrated subsystem* requires documented laboratory performance against predictions and a documented relevant-environment definition. Neither is supplied here.
- **TRL 5 is not supported:** there is no cited integrated component/breadboard test in the relevant environment with documented scaling requirements covering energy, power, fault isolation, thermal rejection, and lifetime.

The upper bound is therefore not a claim that Project Dyson already has a TRL 4 test article.

### Requirements that can materially change the assessment

At full site load, ideal energy-only autonomy spans:

\[
t=E/P=0.0625\text{–}6\ \mathrm{h}
\]

That is **3.75 minutes to 6 hours**, before reserve margins, degradation, conversion losses, or temperature effects. A 0.5 MWh bank serving 8 MW would require a nominal **16C** discharge rate if it carries the entire load. Conversely, 15 MWh serving 2.5 MW is approximately **0.17C**. These are radically different storage designs.

Consequently, T20 cannot be treated as merely a capacity procurement problem. Load shedding and critical-load segregation may determine feasibility.

**Metallurgy routes remain separate load cases, not averaged requirements:**

- **No-melt processing:** establish compressor, pumping, heating, and shutdown loads.
- **Rotating smelting:** establish heater power, controlled cooling, rotation continuity, and consequences of interruption.
- **Native-metal/experimentally gated rotating modules:** qualify the actually selected process and its measured transients.

These distinctions concern T20 sizing and protection; they do not assign metallurgy TRLs.

## 5. Risk and fallback

**Primary risk class: architecture-change.**

Storage failure need not end 0B if mining and processing can operate intermittently and batteries are replaceable. It becomes **project-ending** if the selected process requires uninterrupted power for safe shutdown or if the adopted autonomy requirement cannot be met by any practical backup arrangement.

**Fallback:** use smaller, replaceable Earth-supplied batteries reserved for controls, communications, safe shutdown, and robot recovery; operate production during available solar power, shed noncritical loads, and abandon continuous-operation requirements.

For smelting, this fallback is acceptable only after experimentally demonstrating a safe interrupted-power sequence. If that cannot be demonstrated, change the processing route or invoke D-02’s **0A-only, Earth-supply** outcome.

## 6. Where others might disagree

1. **Component heritage versus integrated readiness.** A rating of 6–9 may be offered because space batteries have flown and terrestrial storage exceeds the required capacity. That is valid heritage attribution, but not the revised subsystem’s demonstrated TRL.
2. **TRL 3 versus 4 today.** This is the strongest legitimate disagreement. I retain 3–4 because no Project Dyson integrated breadboard evidence was supplied.
3. **Cycle life.** Shallow daily buffering and deep, repeated outage bridging are different requirements. Neither the cycle count nor the necessary depth is settled.
4. **What counts as relevant environment.** Thermally controlled modules reduce the cell qualification burden, but do not eliminate vacuum thermal rejection, radiation, exposed-interface charging, or network integration.
5. **Risk classification.** Architecture-change is the baseline; project-ending is appropriate if continuous power is an indispensable process-safety requirement.
