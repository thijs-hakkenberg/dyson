Their combination at **0.05–0.15 g, tonne batch size, with autonomous transfer and repeated refractory service** does not yet have a demonstrated critical-function proof of concept that I can confidently cite.

The revised scope removes silicon zone refining, thin-film deposition and oxide-derived Al/Si from this entry. Those technologies cannot support its rating.

## 2. Current TRL and rotation requirements

### Why not retain TRL 3–4?

**TRL 2 is met:** a documented application and physical operating concept exist.

**TRL 3 is not demonstrated for this integrated application:** the evidence does not validate its controlling parameters—separation time and recovery, entrainment, interface stability, melt containment and discharge—under the required centrifugal field.

**TRL 4 is consequently also unmet:** no qualifying rotating-furnace breadboard performance report, agreeing with predictions, has been identified. A detailed design or consensus conclusion cannot substitute for that report.

### Radius and rpm must be paired

For a microgravity-based rotating module:

\[
a=\omega^2r,\qquad
\frac{a}{g_0}=0.001118\,r_{\rm m}\,n_{\rm rpm}^{\,2}.
\]

| Radius | rpm at 0.05 g | rpm at 0.10 g | rpm at 0.15 g |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 5 m | 2.99 | 4.23 | 5.18 |
| 10 m | 2.11 | 2.99 | 3.66 |
| 20 m | 1.50 | 2.11 | 2.59 |

**Gemini’s stated 5–20 m and 1.5–4 rpm envelope must remain visible:** its extreme combinations produce approximately **0.0126–0.358 g**, not uniformly 0.05–0.15 g. Within that envelope, a 5 m radius cannot reach 0.15 g; at 4 rpm it produces approximately 0.089 g. Controls must enforce permitted **radius–rpm pairs**.

### Gradient, Coriolis and interface limits

These are proposed design constraints, not validated process limits:

- **Radial gravity gradient:** \(\Delta a/a \approx h/r\), where \(h\) is radial melt depth. If a 10% bottom-to-top variation is allowed, require \(h/r\leq0.1\). A 10 m radius therefore permits about 1 m radial depth under that convention.
- **Coriolis screening:** a worst-case acceleration ratio is \(2u/(\omega r)\). At 10 m and 0.10 g, keeping this below 10% requires characteristic relative flow speed below approximately **0.16 m/s**. Charging, stirring and tapping may exceed it; actual vector flows require validated modelling.
- **Spun/despun transfer:** baseline transfer of sealed solid feed and cooled product, with matched-speed docking or cartridge exchange. Do not assume routine molten transfer across the interface. Demonstrate torque transients, dust containment, jam recovery and thermal isolation.
- **Refractory life:** demonstrate chemical attack, wetting, infiltration, thermal fatigue and spallation with representative slag and Fe-Ni. Terrestrial liner experience does not establish autonomous vacuum replacement or service life in this module.

A ground centrifuge cannot reproduce net 0.05–0.15 g simply by rotating a furnace: Earth’s gravity remains. Electromagnetic levitation likewise is not a general substitute for the body-force field acting on **both** metal and slag.

A generic “>95% separation” threshold is inadequate until product demand and economics define what separation must achieve.

## 5. Risk and fallback

**Primary risk class: architecture-change.** T07 failure invalidates Gemini’s melt-dependent baseline, but it need not end all 0B production if another route independently passes its gates.

It becomes **project-ending for the selected melt-dependent 0B branch** if no qualified alternative satisfies demand, economics and licensing. Under D-02, the pre-committed outcome remains **0A only, Earth supply**.

Fallback order:

1. Qualified native-metal beneficiation and no-melt processing.
2. Smaller, parallel batch modules, if economics and autonomy remain acceptable.
3. Higher artificial gravity only as a **newly qualified architecture**, not an automatic rescue.
4. No 0B production commitment.

Increasing gravity may accelerate separation but changes loads, rotation, transfer and flow behaviour. It does not qualify refractory life or eliminate Coriolis effects.

## 6. Where others might disagree

- **TRL 2 versus 3:** an assessor may credit terrestrial separation data plus validated centrifugal-flow analysis as a critical-function proof. I would accept TRL 3 upon seeing an applicable validation report—not merely equations or an unvalidated CFD result.
- **AD² 6 versus 7:** the lower rating assumes conventional metallurgy extrapolates reliably. The higher rating reflects the untested coupled environment and autonomous interfaces.
- **Minimum viable gravity:** 0.05 g is a requirement to test, not a demonstrated threshold for acceptable separation of fine emulsions.
- **One tonne versus ten tonnes at TRL 6:** agreement at one tonne may support scaling, but the exit criterion requires evidence that the chosen prototype represents the production subsystem.
- **Route preference:** no-melt processing avoids this melt-separation risk but substitutes gas/solid transport, reagent closure and feed-specific chemistry risks. It is not automatically gravity-independent.
