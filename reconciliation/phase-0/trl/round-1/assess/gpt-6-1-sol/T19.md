# T19 — Microgravity bulk-solids handling and conditional comminution

**Current TRL: 2–3 for the architecture-required subsystem. Target: TRL 6 before KDP-0B. AD²: 6.**

Small-sample capture and manipulation in asteroid gravity are flight-demonstrated. That does **not** establish continuous enclosed feeding, metering, buffering, tailings transfer, autonomous blockage recovery, or five-year wear performance at industrial throughput. Gravity-independent sub-mm comminution is an additional, less-supported requirement—not something demonstrated by asteroid sample-return missions.

The revised architecture removes bulk ore shipment but **does not remove bulk handling at the asteroid**. Tailings remain the dominant solids stream.

## 1. Assessment boundary and required scale

I assess material handling from the enclosed excavation/processing handoff through feed preparation, beneficiation interfaces, concentrate storage and tailings placement. Excavation, separation performance and metallurgy need their own assessments; T19 must nevertheless demonstrate their material interfaces.

Assumptions:

- Gross feed is **15–55 kt/yr**, with approximately **2–8 kt/yr concentrate and 13–47 kt/yr tailings**. These are correlated balances, not independently selectable endpoints.
- Five years means **75–275 kt cumulative gross feed**, unless KDP-0B explicitly adopts replaceable wear cartridges or shorter-lived modules.
- Sub-mm preparation is mandatory for the Claude variant as stated. It is **not automatically imposed on the other routes** without liberation and feed-acceptance evidence.
- Site handling remains in asteroid gravity unless a rotating enclosure deliberately supplies acceleration. A rotating furnace does not supply gravity to upstream bins or external tailings transfers.

The relevant environment includes vacuum, target asteroid gravity, representative abrasive and cohesive feed, triboelectric charging, credible external plasma/UV exposure at openings, thermal boundary conditions for 0.9–1.3 AU, and accumulated radiation effects on controls and materials. Enclosed material is not necessarily continuously UV-exposed.

Terrestrial screw conveyors, positive-displacement feeders and enclosed mills establish useful engineering principles. I have not identified a documented test combining the required asteroid environment, continuous industrial feed, recovery functions and endurance. Their terrestrial maturity cannot be transferred wholesale to T19.

### Treatment of the supplied literature review

I would not use its unverified arXiv identifiers or categorical conclusions as rating evidence without checking the original publications and test records. In particular:

- Ordinary “vacuum suction” cannot collect material from an ambient vacuum without an introduced gas flow and a pressure differential.
- Vacuum does not itself imply zero mechanical overburden.
- Bennu bulk density is not interchangeable with loose-feed bulk density or individual grain density.
- Cohesion, charge and excavation-power claims cannot be generalized from carbonaceous material to the native-metal target without measurements.

The confidently identified flight programmes above are sufficient to establish relevant physical phenomena—but not industrial subsystem maturity.

### Exit criteria

- **Lower bound, TRL 2:** met by documented concepts for active enclosed handling and the stated application.
- **Upper bound, TRL 3:** defensible for selected critical handling functions using physical sample-acquisition and granular-test evidence. It is **not yet demonstrated for all architecture-critical functions**, especially continuous comminution and autonomous upset recovery.
- **Next unmet exit, TRL 4:** documented performance of a representative integrated laboratory breadboard agreeing with predictions, with the relevant environment defined. Required functions include feed, metering, buffering, bridging detection and clearance, containment, and—where required—comminution.
- **TRL 5–6 remain unmet:** no documented relevant-environment validation with scaling requirements, followed by a representative subsystem demonstration.

Thus **2–3 is an evidence-boundary range**, not an average of model opinions. A conservative review board could reasonably assign **TRL 2** to the complete T19 chain.

### Non-negotiable acceptance measurements

1. **Mass balance:** captured input, concentrate, tailings, retained inventory and escaped material—not merely nominal conveyor capacity.
2. **Containment:** allocating the entire interim **1 t/yr** site ceiling to gross-feed handling would permit only about **18–67 ppm escape by mass**. T19 receives only part of that budget. The **10 kg/yr aspiration** corresponds to approximately **0.18–0.67 ppm**.
3. **Recovery:** deliberate bridging, packing, stall, oversize and power-interruption tests, including recovery without ground intervention.
4. **Comminution where required:** product-size distribution, liberation, energy consumption, fines generation and throughput. A sieve aperture alone is insufficient.
5. **Lifetime:** measured wear laws, bounded extrapolation and demonstrated replacement/redundancy arrangements. Accelerated tests must preserve wear mechanisms; higher speed or harsher abrasive feed is not automatically valid acceleration.

An orbital pilot is valuable, but ISS plasma, thermal conditions and residual acceleration do not exactly reproduce an NEA site. Those differences require explicit justification.

## 6. Risk and fallback

**Risk class: project-ending for 0B if no solids-handling route reaches TRL 6.** This means ending the in-space production option, not ending 0A or Project Dyson.

Failure of **one implementation** can instead be an **architecture-change**:

- Replace zero-gravity transfer sections with enclosed rotating drums or cartridges.
- Reduce throughput or add parallel modules, subject to demand and economics gates.
- Select a target with adequately liberated native metal and avoid unnecessary comminution.
- Use replaceable wear components—but validate robotic replacement and revise logistics.

These are fallback concepts, **not mature substitutes**. If none passes the gate, invoke **“0A only, Earth supply.”**

## 7. Where others might disagree

- **TRL 2 versus 3:** the strongest disagreement is whether sample capture and granular tests validate enough of the production chain’s critical functions to satisfy TRL 3. They support selected functions, not the whole chain.
- **Credit for rotating hardware:** some assessors may give terrestrial enclosed feeders and mills substantial credit once artificial gravity is introduced. That is reasonable only for hardware whose force field and interfaces are genuinely reproduced.
- **Whether sub-mm milling is required:** liberation testing could remove it from the native-metal route. That lowers scope and difficulty; it does not retroactively mature the specified Claude implementation.
- **Lifetime interpretation:** five-year plant service with qualified replacement modules is different from five-year life of every wear surface. KDP-0B must specify which requirement applies.
