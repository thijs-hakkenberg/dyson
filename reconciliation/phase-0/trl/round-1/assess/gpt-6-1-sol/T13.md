## T13 — Asteroid water extraction pilot

**Recommended current TRL: 2–3 for the integrated asteroid-water production system.** Laboratory extraction approaches may individually justify **TRL 3–4**, but that is not yet evidence for an autonomous **10–100 t/yr** plant operating at a C-type NEA.

**Target: TRL 6 before KDP-0B authorises the water pilot; AD² 6.** Failure is an **architecture-change** for the revised programme, not automatically project-ending for Fe-Ni production. It becomes project-ending for a water-dependent 0B option only if a signed customer makes water indispensable.

This assessment uses the revised architecture as the requirement baseline. Its October 2026 decisions are not experimental evidence, and I do not assume tests occurred after the March 2026 evidence baseline.

### 1. Define the product and feed before assigning readiness

The pilot should have an explicit purpose: **demonstrate extraction, capture, purification and storage of asteroid-derived water, and qualify it against a specified prospective customer's acceptance limits.** It is not a production LOX/LH₂ plant, and it does not establish propellant supply for the reference iodine/argon electric-propulsion tugs.

Treat its C-type site as **separate from the Fe-Ni site unless prospecting establishes a suitable shared resource**. Neither metal abundance nor accessible water follows from the other.

For assessment, I assume:

- **10–100 t/yr means net recovered, purified water**, not water initially present in processed feed.
- Feed is hydrated carbonaceous material; **free ice is not assumed**.
- Initial design envelope: **5–15 wt% thermally recoverable water equivalent**, with **50–80% end-to-end recovery**. These are engineering assumptions, not established target grades.
- Reference case: 10 wt% recoverable water and 70% recovery.
- One year of autonomous operation, including approximately **2,190 active processing hours** if processing shares a 25% duty cycle. The robot duty cycle does not necessarily constrain the processor if buffering is provided.
- Relevant environment includes vacuum, asteroid gravity, dust and fines, thermal cycling at 0.9–1.3 AU, and mission-duration radiation.

The reference case requires:

| Quantity | 10 t/yr water | 100 t/yr water |
|---|---:|---:|
| Feed processed annually | 143 t | 1,429 t |
| Water production during active processing | 4.6 kg/h | 45.7 kg/h |
| Feed processing during active processing | 65 kg/h | 652 kg/h |

Across the assumed grade/recovery envelope, annual feed demand is **83–4,000 t/yr**.

Purification must specify allowable particulates, dissolved salts, organics, sulfur-bearing species and non-condensable gases. “Water recovered” is not equivalent to “customer-qualified water.”

**Lower-bound exit met:** TRL 2—documented application and feasible process concepts.

**For TRL 3:** obtain documented tests validating key predictions: release versus temperature/time, energy per kilogram, capture fraction and contaminant loading for representative feed.

**For TRL 4:** demonstrate an integrated laboratory breadboard with documented predictive agreement and a defined relevant environment.

**TRL 5 is not established:** no cited test demonstrates representative feed handling, release and capture together in the relevant low-gravity/vacuum environment with documented scaling requirements.

The mechanical and optical alternatives should remain separate:

- **Mechanical thermal processing:** easier to meter and characterise feed, but excavation, transfer, sealing and dust management remain critical.
- **Optical mining:** potentially reduces mechanical handling, but optical access, plume dynamics, enclosure integrity and vapor capture may dominate performance.
- There is insufficient evidence to select a winner or average their readiness.

### 5. Risk and fallback

**Default failure classification: architecture-change.**

The revised Fe-Ni architecture does not depend on asteroid water or Phase 0 LOX/LH₂ production. Failure should therefore close or defer the water option, not automatically terminate metal production.

**Fallback:** retain 0A prospecting and extraction research; obtain any required water from Earth and operate the selected tug propellant architecture independently.

Lunar water is not an established low-risk fallback. It has its own resource, extraction, transport and qualification gates. The inherited claimed cost advantage/disadvantage is not readiness evidence.

If signed demand later makes water essential to a particular 0B option, failure becomes **project-ending for that option**, with D-02's precommitted “0A only, Earth supply” outcome.

### 6. Where assessors may disagree

1. **Integrated TRL 2–3 versus component TRL 3–4.** An assessor with access to complete optical-mining test reports could justify a higher component rating. That would not automatically raise the entire production chain.
2. **What constitutes relevant gravity.** Short reduced-gravity tests may validate individual mechanisms but not continuous feed, capture and autonomous restart.
3. **Modular scaling.** Ten qualified 10 t/yr modules could support 100 t/yr only after shared power, heat rejection, feed distribution and contamination interactions are validated.
4. **Feed assumptions.** C-type classification and hydration signatures do not establish recoverable grade or processing temperature. Sampled target grade remains a separate D-02 gate.
5. **Purpose and criticality.** Water-EP qualification may create a future customer, but it cannot be assumed to close reference transport economics or justify a water-first architecture now.
