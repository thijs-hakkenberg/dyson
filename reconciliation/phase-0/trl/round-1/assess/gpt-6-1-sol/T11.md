## T11 — Rendezvous scouts with subsurface resource sampling

**Recommended current TRL: 2–3 for the complete grade-certifying scout capability.** This is not a rating of asteroid rendezvous alone: rendezvous, electric propulsion and shallow asteroid sampling have substantial flight heritage. The limiting capability is **obtaining representative subsurface samples and determining recoverable Fe–Ni grade with a validated measurement chain**, within a 200–600 kg scout.

The original **TRL 5–6** is defensible only for a narrower, heritage-derived reconnaissance spacecraft—not for the revised resource-certification requirement.

### Assessment assumptions

- **Mass:** 200–600 kg means wet mass at interplanetary departure. A dry-mass interpretation would change the comparisons.
- **Campaign:** 4–10 independently operated scouts, each visiting one target initially; second and third targets remain conditional on validated trajectories, remaining propellant and sampler reuse.
- **Sampling:** at least two candidate NEAs, with multiple sampling locations and depths. Required depth, sample mass and spatial coverage are **not yet specified**. I use **0.1–1 m below the pre-disturbance surface** as a development envelope, not an adopted requirement.
- **Duration:** assume 3–7 years per spacecraft for planning. The revised architecture does not specify scout lifetime.
- **Environment:** vacuum, NEA microgravity, thermal cycling at 0.9–1.3 AU, radiation, abrasive regolith and potentially charged dust.
- **Grade measurement:** either validated onboard assays plus representative recovery tests, or sample return and terrestrial testing. Sample return is **not assumed to fit automatically** within this mass envelope.
- **Schedule:** the supplied October 2026 decisions are treated as governing requirements. No calendar date for KDP-0B is supplied.

“Recoverable grade” must be defined before instrument selection. For native metal, a useful gate quantity is recovered Fe–Ni mass divided by original feed mass, under a specified beneficiation process. **Concentrate grade is a different quantity.** Neither total elemental iron nor spectral taxonomy establishes recoverable native metal.

**Important corrections to the supplied background:**

- **NEA Scout did not demonstrate asteroid rendezvous.** It launched in 2022, but communications were not established; its intended encounter was a flyby.
- Bennu was **not the first** asteroid with both spacecraft spectra and returned-sample mineralogy: Itokawa and Ryugu preceded it.
- DISCUS is a mission concept, not a flight demonstration.
- The supplied resolution/accuracy percentages and miniaturized-instrument TRLs are not accepted without identifiable test reports.
- Synthetic-population Monte Carlo results do not validate rendezvous accessibility or fleet productivity without flight-dynamics validation. The old 20-targets-per-year survey requirement does not apply to these scouts.

**AD²: 6**, using the project anchors. Competing approaches—impact exposure, anchored coring, touch-and-go collection and sample return versus onboard analysis—have materially different risks. The path to reliable **resource certification** is not selected.

For rendezvous alone, AD² would be nearer **2–3**. For a selected impact-assisted shallow sampler with modest representativeness requirements, **4–5** may be defensible.

A pathfinder can support the **TRL 6 gate** more convincingly than terrestrial testing alone. A flight demonstration earns higher TRL only for functions whose predictions and performance it actually verifies.

The **second sampled candidate** is separately necessary for rq-0-50’s target campaign. It is not a generic TRL exit criterion. Likewise, finding an economically unsuitable target can be a successful scout demonstration while causing the **target gate to fail**.

## 5. Risk and fallback

**Primary classification: project-ending for 0B**, if trustworthy sampled recoverable grade remains unavailable. This does not end 0A’s scientific or capability value.

Secondary failures are less severe:

- Reuse fails: **architecture-change** to one target per scout.
- Compact sampling fails: larger dedicated sampling missions, with **cost and schedule increases**.
- Onboard classification fails: ground processing, retaining raw observations; safety-critical terminal autonomy remains onboard.

**Fallback:** continue 0A, use larger heritage-derived sampling missions where justified, and retain Earth supply rather than authorizing 0B from remote spectra alone.

## 6. Where others might disagree

1. **Assessment boundary:** a reviewer rating “spacecraft rendezvous and characterization” could reasonably assign 5–6 or higher. That excludes the capability driving the revised requirement.
2. **Hayabusa2 transferability:** impact exposure plus collection may deserve higher maturity than a new corer. It still does not establish depth-resolved or statistically representative grade.
3. **What constitutes bulk-grade evidence:** several small samples may demonstrate instrument operation without representing a heterogeneous mining volume. Required confidence and spatial coverage must be agreed.
4. **Metal versus water:** C-, S- or M-type labels do not resolve the panel’s competing resource assumptions. Native Fe–Ni, reducible iron phases and water require separate measurements. Scouts should preserve those distinctions rather than report one “resource-rich” classification.
