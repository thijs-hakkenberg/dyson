## Assessment summary

**T21 current TRL: 2–3 for the integrated NEA maintenance, installation and recovery capability.** Spaceflight heritage demonstrates robotic manipulation and replacement of deliberately serviceable components. It does **not** demonstrate autonomous maintenance of abrasive mining and processing equipment, recovery of damaged high-voltage tethers, or sustained spares logistics at an asteroid.

**Target: TRL 6 at KDP-0B**, because loss of maintenance and recovery capability could end uncrewed production over the required 5–15 years. **AD²: 6**, using the project’s working anchors: several approaches exist, but the servicing architecture, recovery strategy and lifetime-validation path remain unresolved.

This rating assumes:
- Equipment is designed for robotic servicing from inception, using replaceable cartridges, standardized interfaces and accessible attachment points.
- “Uncrewed” permits delayed ground supervision and troubleshooting, but not human intervention at the site. Immediate fault containment must be local.
- A maintenance robot need not dismantle every failed mechanism. It may isolate, replace or abandon a failed module.
- Spares can be delivered periodically; neither delivery cadence nor inventory has yet been established.
- T21 includes handling and repositioning **2–3.5 t mining robots**, not servicing the cargo tugs themselves.

Without these assumptions—particularly modular replacement—the difficulty would increase materially.

## 2. Current TRL and unmet criteria

## 3. Target and difficulty

### Milestones

- **0A internal milestone: TRL 4**, before committing to a servicing pathfinder design.
- **Later 0A milestone: TRL 5** for the selected interfaces and critical recovery functions, before flight-test commitment.
- **KDP-0B: TRL 6**, for the selected integrated maintenance and recovery subsystem, with no waiver if it remains project-ending.
- **Production deployment:** flight qualification and operational commissioning remain necessary beyond TRL 6.

No calendar date for KDP-0B is supplied. My planning estimate is **6–10 years from authorization of a funded development programme**, not a forecast tied to the decision date.

### AD²: **6**

The main difficulties are coupled:

1. Uncertain wear and failure distributions in asteroid material.
2. Maintaining stable reaction forces during contact and extraction.
3. Contaminated electrical, mechanical and chemical interfaces.
4. Autonomous diagnosis of faults outside the nominal task sequence.
5. Recovering the maintenance equipment itself.
6. Demonstrating credible 5–15-year life without waiting 15 years.

For cartridge-only servicing of clean, cooperative interfaces, **AD² 4–5** could be defensible. Requiring in-place refractory rebuilding, cable repair rather than replacement, or recovery of arbitrarily damaged machines could raise it to **7**.

### Important qualification conditions

- A short parabolic-flight demonstration cannot establish long-duration vacuum servicing readiness.
- A small pathfinder does not automatically qualify multi-tonne recovery. It must reproduce critical loads and interfaces, or validate scaling models against full-scale tests.
- An integrated NEA prototype could support **TRL 7**, but merely visiting an asteroid would not.
- Accelerated ageing is useful only where the acceleration mechanism is validated. Increasing temperature or abrasive concentration without checking failure-mode equivalence is not proof of 15-year serviceability.
- Qualification must include **failed maintenance attempts**, not just repeated successful nominal exchanges: stuck fasteners, torn cable, failed isolation, loss of anchoring and an immobilized servicing robot.

For rq-0-42, the test campaign should couple excavation adaptation with maintenance: detecting a jam, retreating safely, deciding whether to retry, and handing off to the servicing system. For rq-0-60, electrical isolation and proof of a safe state must precede cable manipulation.

## 5. Risk and fallback

**Risk class: project-ending for the stated 0B architecture.**

Individual failures may cause downtime or higher spare consumption. Failure to establish a credible fleet-level maintenance strategy, however, removes the basis for 5–15-year uncrewed production.

Fallback hierarchy:

1. Replace difficult in-place repairs with sealed cartridge or whole-module exchanges.
2. Standardize interfaces and restrict fleet variants; use dedicated maintenance robots with redundant recovery capability.
3. Abandon isolated failed equipment rather than recover it, where throughput, debris control and inventory permit.
4. Shorten deployed equipment life and replace complete units more frequently—but reassess delivered cost and transport demand.
5. If the resulting architecture still fails D-02 gates, adopt **0A only, Earth supply**.

Disposable equipment is not a free fallback: it consumes transport capacity, increases inventory and may conflict with debris and site-management obligations.

## 6. Where others might disagree

- **TRL 3 versus 4:** an assessor could credit flight-proven robotic replacement as laboratory validation of generic servicing. I reserve TRL 4 for hardware and procedures representative of these industrial interfaces and failure modes.
- **Ground testing versus orbital testing:** some may accept gravity-offloaded, thermal-vacuum tests plus validated dynamics as sufficient for TRL 6. That depends on whether microgravity anchoring, dust transport and cable behaviour are shown not to invalidate the demonstration.
- **Whole-module replacement versus repair:** GPT’s approach could be substantially easier, but only after demonstrating manageable module masses, clean disconnects, reliable restart and affordable spares.
- **Autonomy requirement:** permitting frequent delayed ground intervention could lower development difficulty. It does not solve time-critical jam, electrical fault or anchoring-loss responses.
- **Lifetime standard:** TRL 6 does not require fifteen years of elapsed testing, but it does require credible test-backed predictions. Neither a design lifetime nor model consensus meets that standard.
