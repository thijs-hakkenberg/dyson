## T17 — H₂ reduction of the reducible feed fraction, with H₂ recycle

**Recommended current TRL: 2–3 for the integrated NEA subsystem. Target: TRL 6 at KDP-0B. AD²: 6.**

The reduction chemistry is established, and water electrolysis has spaceflight heritage. **Neither establishes a gravity-independent, closed-loop asteroid metallurgical plant.** The principal gap is sustained gas–solid contact, solids handling and water recovery with representative feed in microgravity—not whether hydrogen can reduce iron oxide.

This assessment uses the revised architecture as the requirement, not as demonstration evidence. The supplied decisions establish milestones but **do not provide a calendar date for KDP-0B**.

### Requirement interpretation and assumptions

- Treat **0.6–2.5 kt/yr as metal product allocated to T17**, not raw ore. If only a fraction of that product requires reduction, reagent and reactor requirements scale accordingly.
- Assume **8,000 operating hours/year** for sizing: approximately **75–313 kg/h of recovered metal**. This availability is a requirement to validate, not demonstrated performance.
- Feed throughput cannot yet be specified: it depends on sampled reducible grade, beneficiation recovery and conversion.
- Reactor operation would use a **contained, pressurised H₂ atmosphere inside an external-vacuum installation**. Relevant environmental testing must distinguish the reactor's internal process conditions from its space environment.
- No production lifetime is specified in the revised requirement. I do **not** carry forward the original 30-year assumption. Campaign duration, replacement intervals and acceptable hydrogen losses must be fixed before TRL 5 qualification.

**Important mass-balance correction:** product-water electrolysis is not necessarily a tens-to-hundreds-of-tonnes/year water system.

For an iron-product approximation:

| Quantity | FeO-derived product | Fe₂O₃-derived product |
|---|---:|---:|
| H₂ reacted per tonne Fe | 36 kg | 54 kg |
| Water produced per tonne Fe | 323 kg | 484 kg |
| H₂ regenerated at 0.6–2.5 kt Fe/yr | 22–90 t/yr | 32–135 t/yr |
| Water electrolysed at that allocation | 194–806 t/yr | 290–1,210 t/yr |

These are stoichiometric quantities, not gas circulation rates. Excess-H₂ circulation may be substantially larger. At an assumed **50–60 kWh/kg H₂** electrolyser consumption, regeneration alone requires about **1.1–8.1 GWh/yr**, or **0.12–0.93 MW annual-average electrical power**, before reactor heating, compression and other loads.

Thus Claude’s “tens to hundreds” is plausible for **regenerated hydrogen**, but generally understates **water throughput** if the full allocation comes from oxides. The separate 10–100 t/yr water-extraction pilot does not bound internally generated reaction-water processing.

## 2. Current TRL and exit criteria

### Integrated T17: **TRL 2–3**

- **TRL 2 is defensible:** the application, process sequence and intended benefit are documented.
- **TRL 3 is conditional:** existing reduction and electrolysis tests establish important constituent functions. The upper bound is justified only if documented analysis maps those results to the selected mineral fraction and validates key predicted conversion, water-production and recycle parameters.
- **The supplied material does not establish that mapping for a sampled NEA feed.** Consequently, TRL 3 should not be treated as an unconditional project baseline.

**Unmet TRL 4 exit:** documented breadboard performance agreeing with predictions for the coupled process—pretreatment, reduction, solids handling, water capture, H₂ regeneration and recycle—with a documented relevant environment.

The constituent technologies are considerably more mature than this integrated rating. Their heritage must not be transferred wholesale to the NEA subsystem.

**AD² = 6:** several credible contact and retention approaches exist—mechanically confined beds, screw-conveyed reactors, porous retainers, or rotating contactors—but their integrated performance is not established. The difficulty is principally engineering, not uncertain basic chemistry.

Selecting rotation could reduce contact uncertainty, but would change the “without gravity” premise. It must be evaluated as a separate architecture branch.

The last prototype operates at approximately **4–63× below required instantaneous product throughput**. It is not automatically sufficient for TRL 6. A credible exit requires demonstrated scaling of the contact mechanism, heat transfer, filtration and recycle equipment to the production module. If those phenomena do not scale predictably, a larger demonstration is necessary.

Before testing, specify pass/fail requirements for:

- Metal recovery and product impurity limits.
- Residual water concentration and H₂/H₂O ratio needed to maintain reduction.
- Dust carryover, filter loading and pressure drop.
- Hydrogen loss and reagent import allowance.
- Electrolyser contaminant tolerance and purification consumables.
- Wear, precision-spare replacement and autonomous maintenance.
- Residue containment and the plant’s allocation of the proposed site debris ceiling.

## 5. Risk and fallback

**Primary classification: architecture-change.**

T17 is not universally project-ending: sampled native-metal feed could allow it to be omitted, and another metallurgy route could replace it. However, **if KDP-0B selects a feed and production commitment that depend on T17, failure becomes project-ending for that 0B configuration** under D-02.

Fallback order:

1. Restrict production to sufficiently rich native Fe-Ni feed and bypass reduction.
2. Adopt a separately demonstrated rotating reduction or smelting route, with revised mass, power and cost.
3. If neither closes the demand, economics and environmental gates, retain **0A only and Earth supply**.

Open-loop imported hydrogen is a possible pilot workaround, not a demonstrated industrial fallback. For the assumed oxide-derived allocation, it requires approximately **22–135 t/yr of stoichiometric H₂**, before purge and leakage losses.

## 6. Where others might disagree

- **TRL 3 versus TRL 2:** chemistry specialists may credit established reduction and electrolysis as sufficient critical-function proof. A strict assessor can reasonably retain TRL 2 until target-feed predictions and gravity-independent contact have experimental support.
- **“No melt” versus “no gravity”:** avoiding molten-phase separation is a real simplification. It does not eliminate bed restraint, particle transport, filtration, condensate management or discharge problems.
- **AD² 5 versus 6:** choosing a mechanical contactor early could justify AD² 5 after representative-feed testing. With the contact mechanism and feed still unresolved, AD² 6 is more defensible.
- **Scale of the recycle plant:** reduced-product fraction matters enormously. A smaller oxide contribution could fit a much smaller loop; the full 0.6–2.5 kt/yr allocation does not imply a small water-electrolysis subsystem.
