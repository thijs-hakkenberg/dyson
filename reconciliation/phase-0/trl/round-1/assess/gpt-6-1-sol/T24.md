## T24 — Dust-tolerant long-life mechanisms and seals

**Assessment: TRL 3–4; target TRL 6 at KDP-0B; AD² 6.** Long-life space mechanisms and mechanisms exposed briefly to extraterrestrial dust both have substantial heritage. Their combination—**five-year service in abrasive, charged NEA regolith, including production machinery and 1–3 kV connectors—does not have demonstrated heritage identified here.**

This is a technology-family assessment, not certification of a particular bearing, seal or connector design.

### Assessment assumptions

- **Life:** five years installed without crew servicing. Robotic replacement is permissible only if explicitly incorporated into the architecture.
- **Mining duty:** five years is approximately **43,830 elapsed hours**. At 9–25% duty, mining mechanisms accumulate approximately **3,945–10,958 operating hours**. Plant mechanisms may have substantially higher duty; that requirement remains unspecified.
- **Scale:** mechanisms serve 2–3.5 t mining robots excavating 0.5–5 t/h, processing equipment, and 1–3 kV tether interfaces. Robot mass and excavation rate are **not** bearing-load or seal-size specifications.
- **Connectors:** assume de-energized mating and demating, followed by operation at 1–3 kV. Dust-contaminated live mating would be a separate, more difficult requirement.
- **Environment:** vacuum, NEA thermal cycling at 0.9–1.3 AU, radiation, and abrasive charged particles. Dust size distribution, mineralogy, charge distribution, exposure flux and temperature limits are not yet specified.
- **Scope:** processing seals include dust containment, but a dust seal alone does not establish qualification for carbonyl chemistry, hydrogen containment or molten-metal service.

## 2. Current TRL and unmet exit criterion

**Lower bound: TRL 3.** Physical operations validate critical functions: protected mechanisms can operate in dusty extraterrestrial settings; rotary mechanisms can operate in space; sampling mechanisms can contact NEA material.

**Upper bound: TRL 4, conditional on the selected component.** This is defensible where an existing component has documented laboratory performance against predictions and a documented relevant-environment definition. The programme-level heritage above does not, by itself, establish those records for every proposed mechanism.

Two distinct gaps remain:

- **From TRL 3 to 4:** a selected breadboard requires documented laboratory tests agreeing with predictions and an explicit environmental specification.
- **From TRL 4 to 5:** it requires tests in the relevant environment, with documented scaling from test loads, particle exposure, cycles and accelerated ageing to the required installation and life.

I would **not credit TRL 5** merely because an analogous component flew briefly, or TRL 9 because another bearing operated for years in a different environment.

## 3. Target and difficulty

- **0A internal target:** TRL 4–5 before selecting the pathfinder’s mechanism designs. Flight hardware also needs mission-specific qualification; a technology milestone does not replace that.
- **0B target:** **TRL 6 before KDP-0B**, for the mechanism chains whose failure would halt mining or production. No calendar date for KDP-0B is supplied.
- **Planning interval:** approximately **5–8 years from a funded start**, with overlapping development and endurance testing.
- **AD²: 6**, using the supplied project anchors. Competing approaches exist—exclusion seals, labyrinths, protected lubrication, active dust removal, replaceable cartridges and contactless interfaces—but the winning combination is not yet clear.

The main difficulty is not new physics. It is validating life under coupled particle ingress, charging, wear, thermal distortion and intermittent operation. A cold, replaceable native-metal implementation could justify **AD² 5** once requirements are fixed; the rotating hot-process implementation may remain at **6–7** until its interfaces are demonstrated.

**Life-test caution:** increased dust concentration, speed or temperature can change the failure mechanism. Five-year equivalence requires a validated acceleration model, not simply “five years’ dust mass” passed through a seal. Long idle periods, cold starts and connector contamination must remain in the test programme.

## 5. Risk and fallback

**Primary classification: project-ending for the specified five-year, unattended 0B architecture.** Failure of production-critical mechanisms can defeat sustained throughput. An isolated noncritical connector failure may instead create a schedule delay or cost increase.

**Fallback:** redesign around shorter-life, robotically replaceable sealed cartridges, protected cold processing and fewer exposed dynamic interfaces; qualify that replacement architecture independently. If autonomous maintenance and revised delivered cost do not pass KDP-0B, retain **0A only and Earth supply**.

A lower maintenance interval is an architecture change—not evidence that the original five-year requirement was met.

## 6. Where others might disagree

1. **TRL 4 versus 5:** a particular dust-tested bearing or seal may have relevant-environment evidence sufficient for TRL 5. I would accept that component-specific rating after reviewing its test conditions and scaling record, not extend it automatically to the whole mechanism family.
2. **Whether life is a TRL issue:** some assessors separate endurance qualification from maturity. Here, life is an essential required characteristic; an unsupported five-year extrapolation cannot satisfy relevant-environment readiness.
3. **Lunar dust as a surrogate:** lunar material may be more abrasive than some NEA material, but NEA charging, low-gravity transport and composition can change ingress. “Lunar is conservative” needs experimental support.
4. **Replaceability:** accepting frequent robotic cartridge changes can substantially reduce development difficulty, but changes availability, logistics and economics.
5. **Hot versus cold routes:** the cold native-metal route may prove substantially easier. That advantage remains a hypothesis until representative protected mechanisms are tested.
