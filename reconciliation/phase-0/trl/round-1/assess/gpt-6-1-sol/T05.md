## T05 — Microgravity beneficiation of native Fe–Ni

**Assessment: TRL 2–3; AD² 6 to reach TRL 6.** Magnetic separation has a strong terrestrial physical basis, but I cannot substantiate an integrated demonstration of liberation, selective recovery, continuous solids transport and tailings containment for representative asteroid feed in microgravity.

The **lower bound, TRL 2, is supported for the integrated application**. The upper bound, TRL 3, is defensible for magnetic discrimination as a critical function—not for the complete beneficiation train. No panel agreement or mass-flow model raises that rating.

This is **project-ending for the native-metal 0B option if no qualifying target/process combination is demonstrated**. It is not project-ending for 0A, which can finish as a research and reconnaissance programme.

---

## 1. Required performance and reconciled balance

### Definitions and assumptions

I interpret **4–15:1 concentration as feed mass divided by concentrate mass**, not as grade enrichment:

\[
R=F/C,\qquad Cg_c=Fg_f r_b,\qquad D=Cg_c\eta_m\eta_d
\]

where:

- \(F,C,D\): annual feed, concentrate and delivered metal;
- \(g_f,g_c\): recoverable native Fe–Ni feed grade and concentrate grade;
- \(r_b\): beneficiation recovery;
- \(\eta_m,\eta_d\): subsequent metallurgical and delivery yields.

“Recoverable native metal” must exclude iron bound in silicates, sulfides or oxides unless a separately qualified conversion process is included.

### One internally closed reference balance

These are **requirements for testing, not demonstrated performance**.

| Stream or parameter | Reference value |
|---|---:|
| Feed | 30 kt/yr |
| Native Fe–Ni feed grade | 10 wt% |
| Beneficiation recovery | 66.7% |
| Concentrate | 4 kt/yr |
| Concentrate native-metal grade | 50 wt% |
| Feed/concentrate ratio | 7.5:1 |
| Beneficiation tailings | 26 kt/yr |
| Metal entering subsequent processing | 2 kt/yr |
| Combined subsequent processing/delivery yield | 75% |
| Delivered Fe–Ni | 1.5 kt/yr |
| Subsequent processing residue/loss allowance | 2.5 kt/yr |

The last allowance must be divided experimentally into contained residue, rejected impurities, retained inventory and actual loss. It is **not permission to discharge material**.

The requested envelopes—**15–55 kt/yr feed, 2–8 kt/yr concentrate and 4–15:1 mass concentration**—are compatible only as selected combinations, not as independent ranges. The reference balance falls within all three.

For sizing, assuming **4,000–6,000 operating hours/year**, the separator train needs approximately **2.5–13.8 t/h**. I assume a **five-year service life**, or **20,000–30,000 operating hours**; the supplied architecture does not establish a plant lifetime, so this remains a requirement to ratify.

### Feed branches must remain separate

- **LL/L/H ordinary-chondrite envelope:** test actual differences in native-metal abundance, grain size, intergrowth and weathering. Do not substitute total iron abundance for recoverable metal.
- **Low-metal LL-like feed:** may fail the delivered-product requirement despite achieving a high feed/concentrate ratio. At 2 wt% native metal, 30 kt/yr contains only 0.6 kt/yr before any losses.
- **M-type branch:** neither spectral classification nor radar reflectivity establishes sampled native-metal grade or liberation. A genuinely metal-rich target may need sorting rather than strong mass concentration.
- **Mass-conservation constraint:** \(R g_f r_b\leq1\). At 80 wt% native metal and near-total recovery, the maximum feed/concentrate ratio is only about 1.25:1. Requiring 4–15:1 on that branch would mean rejecting useful metal.

“Sub-mm liberation” must mean **measured liberation of metal grains**, not merely crushing everything below 1 mm.

The flight pathfinder would remain **25–1,380 times below required instantaneous throughput**, depending on the compared endpoints. A TRL 6 claim therefore needs a credible modular scale-up argument—not merely successful operation of a small separator.

### Acceptance measurements

Before testing, set quantitative acceptance criteria for:

1. **Delivered metal**, not concentration ratio alone.
2. Native-metal grade and recovery for each qualified feed class.
3. Liberation energy and achievable throughput.
4. Total escaped mass across excavation, transfer, separation and tailings storage.
5. Power, consumables, wear and autonomous fault recovery.
6. Traceable mass balance with uncertainty smaller than the recovery differences being evaluated.

The proposed **≤1 t/yr site-wide escape ceiling** permits only about **21–77 ppm of the 13–47 kt/yr beneficiation tailings stream** if the entire allowance were assigned to tailings. In practice, beneficiation receives less than the whole site allowance. A separator that concentrates well but sheds fines fails the architectural requirement.

## 5. Risk and fallback

**Primary risk class: project-ending for native-metal 0B.**

Failure can mean:

- insufficient sampled native-metal grade;
- unacceptable liberation energy;
- recovery collapse when throughput increases;
- inability to move and discharge solids reliably in microgravity;
- unacceptable dust escape;
- inability to operate autonomously for the required life.

**Fallback hierarchy:**

1. Select a better-characterized target and re-size production against signed demand.
2. Use experimentally validated rotating confinement or enclosed forced transport—an **architecture change**, not a presumed solution.
3. Restrict production to coarse, naturally liberated native metal at a lower rate.
4. If no route meets the target, demand, economics and licence gates, execute **“0A only, Earth supply.”**

A ratio below 3:1 is **not a universal physical falsifier**. It is potentially fatal to a low-grade-feed logistics model, but a genuinely metal-rich target can deliver useful metal at a much lower feed/concentrate ratio. Grade, recovery and downstream yield must decide the gate.

## 6. Where others might disagree

- **TRL 3 versus TRL 2:** assessors may credit magnetic discrimination as sufficient critical-function proof. I retain TRL 2 for the integrated train because transport, liberation and containment remain unvalidated.
- **Artificial gravity as a shortcut:** rotating confinement could make separation more terrestrial-like. It does not remove vacuum, charging, transfer, wear or tailings-disposal requirements.
- **How much scale-up supports TRL 6:** a small flight unit may qualify a modular design if scaling is demonstrated. It cannot qualify an arbitrarily larger monolithic plant.
- **Feed representativeness:** loose metal-in-sand simulants can substantially overstate liberation and recovery.
- **Route ranking:** carbonyl may avoid a furnace but introduce demanding reagent and gas-management systems. The evidence does not yet establish a clear winner.
- **The old rq-0-10 assumptions:** central bulk-ore transport and generic asteroid-type “extractable” percentages are superseded or unverified inputs, not evidence for this assessment.
