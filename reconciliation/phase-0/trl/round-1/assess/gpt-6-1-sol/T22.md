## T22 reassessment — Product consolidation, packaging and qualification

**Recommended current TRL: 2–2 for the integrated, architecture-specific capability. Target: TRL 6 before KDP-0B. AD²: 5–7, depending on the selected route; use 6 while route and product specifications remain unresolved.**

This entry should replace—not simply rename—the original “Hybrid Gravity Metallurgy” entry. T22 concerns turning processed Fe–Ni into an **accepted, contained, transportable product**. Primary extraction and reduction belong upstream. Silicon purification and thin-film deposition are outside the revised T22 scope.

Industrial consolidation and space sample containment provide substantial heritage, but I cannot identify a demonstrated system that integrates representative asteroid-derived Fe–Ni, product qualification, autonomous consolidation, dust containment and tug-compatible packaging in the required environment.

## 1. Assessment boundary and assumptions

T22 includes:

- Consolidation by compaction/sintering, pelletising or casting.
- Verification of composition, contamination and agreed physical properties.
- Lot identification, representative sampling and acceptance/rejection.
- Dust-tight packaging, closure verification and autonomous handling.
- Payload restraint, tug attachment and delivery-node unloading interfaces.

A **dust-tight pellet is not necessarily an accepted product**. Pellets can abrade during transport, and either pellets or cast stock can fail Phase 1 composition or property requirements.

### Required scale

Assuming **8,000 production hours/year**, 0.6–2.5 kt/year requires:

- **75–313 kg/h of accepted output**, excluding rejected or reworked material.
- **150–300 t per tug payload**, corresponding to approximately **2–17 payload-equivalents/year**.
- Individual package and batch sizes are **not specified**. I use 0.1–1 t consolidation batches and modular packages as development assumptions, not adopted requirements.

### Relevant environment and duration

- External vacuum and microgravity; internally controlled atmospheres are permissible.
- **0.05–0.15 g inside rotating equipment**, if selected.
- NEA thermal conditions at 0.9–1.3 AU, charged abrasive dust and mission radiation.
- Uncrewed operation, including fault detection and replacement of wear components.
- Packaging survival for the actual delivery interval. Pending a trajectory-specific requirement, **five years is a conservative qualification assumption**, not a claim that outbound transport lasts the entire stated round trip.
- Plant service life and maintenance intervals remain unspecified. These must be fixed before a defensible endurance qualification is possible.

## 3. Current TRL and separate route ratings

### Integrated T22: **TRL 2–2**

**Met exit:** A documented application exists: produce specified Fe–Ni shipping forms at the NEA, qualify them and deliver them in contained tug-compatible packages.

**Unmet TRL 3 exit:** No cited analytical or experimental results validate the key parameters of an **architecture-specific consolidation–qualification–packaging chain** using representative feed and the proposed product form.

This is not a judgement that sintering, casting or containers are individually TRL 2. It is a judgement about the new capability and its boundary.

### Essential modelling and analysis

These support—not replace—the experiments:

- Calibrated powder-flow and contact models for feeding, compaction and abrasion.
- Casting/solidification models validated against the actual rotation, melt depth and residence time.
- Package structural and thermal models validated by load and thermal-vacuum tests.
- Statistical acceptance plans validated against destructive composition/property measurements.
- Throughput and reliability models including rejected lots, maintenance, consumables and autonomous recovery.

A 10 kg orbital experiment is **not automatically TRL 6**. The scaling case must show why its geometry, residence times, interfaces and failure modes represent the production module.

## 6. Risk and fallback

**Integrated failure class: project-ending for 0B**, if no product form can meet signed Phase 1 acceptance and transport requirements. It is not project-ending for funded 0A.

**Individual route failure: architecture-change**, while another route remains viable.

Fallback order:

1. Accept a simpler, explicitly contracted product—such as compacted native-metal stock—if Phase 1 can use it.
2. Use smaller sealed packages and more parallel consolidation modules, accepting increased tare mass, handling and cost.
3. Switch between powder consolidation and casting **only after the replacement route meets its own gate**.
4. If none reaches TRL 6 and the acceptance/economic gates, invoke **“0A only, Earth supply.”**

Increasing centrifugal acceleration is a possible redesign, not a qualified fallback. It requires new validation and may change mass, power and interface requirements.

## 7. Where assessors might disagree

The largest disagreement is **where to draw the technology boundary**.

An assessor rating an ordinary sintered-metal compact or sealed container could reasonably assign high terrestrial maturity. An assessor treating space sample containment as proof of a critical T22 function might assign **TRL 3 to that function**. Neither establishes TRL 3–4 for the complete architecture-specific chain.

The next-largest uncertainty is **Phase 1 acceptance**. A specification allowing porous native-metal stock could substantially simplify T22. A specification requiring dense, homogeneous, low-contamination structural alloy could force melting, additional refining and more demanding qualification. Until signed specifications exist, those alternatives should remain separate.
