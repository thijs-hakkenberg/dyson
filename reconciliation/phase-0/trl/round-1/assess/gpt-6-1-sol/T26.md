## T26 — Deep-space communications and resilient command service

**Current TRL: 3–4 for the integrated service; much higher for conventional deep-space RF links considered separately. Target: TRL 6 at KDP-0B. AD²: 4.**

The principal gap is **not transmitting across interplanetary distances**. It is demonstrating an integrated, authenticated service that supports the required fleet, preserves command integrity during outages and resets, and recovers without executing stale or duplicated commands.

This assessment assumes conventional X-band or Ka-band communications, scheduled ground contacts, onboard command sequencing, and persistent store-and-forward queues. It does **not** assume continuous connectivity, optical communications, or remote real-time control of mining machinery.

### Scope and assumptions

- **Endpoint count:** 4–10 scouts + 10–18 tugs + one site = **15–29 deep-space endpoints**, if these operate concurrently. Fleet phasing could reduce the peak.
- **Local site network:** the 6–20 mining robots are additional local endpoints, not necessarily independently Earth-addressable spacecraft. Local safety control must continue without Earth connectivity.
- **Propagation:** interpret 1–25 minutes as **one-way** light-time: approximately 18–450 million km. The associated round-trip delay is 2–50 minutes.
- **Outages:** qualification must include conjunction-related degradation and complete contact loss. I propose a **30-day no-contact test envelope**, subject to target-orbit analysis; this is a test assumption, not an established requirement.
- **Duration:** the revised architecture supplies 1.5–5-year tug round trips, but not communications equipment lifetime. Assume qualification for at least one **five-year deployment**, with longer service life to be specified. The older seven-year scout requirement is not automatically carried forward.
- **Rates remain TBD:** no defensible throughput, antenna-size, storage-capacity or ground-network-capacity qualification is possible until traffic and contact budgets are established.

Metallurgy route selection does not change this rating directly. Its telemetry, autonomy and safe-shutdown requirements can change the service load.

## 2. Current TRL and exit criteria

### Integrated T26 service: **TRL 3–4**

- **Lower bound — TRL 3:** physical flight demonstrations validate critical functions: deep-space links, disruption-tolerant transport, and operations through planned communication interruptions. This meets critical-function proof-of-concept criteria for a service assembled from those capabilities.
- **Upper bound — TRL 4:** defensible **only if** a specific implementation is selected and documented laboratory results demonstrate its integrated transport, command-security and recovery behavior against predictions. The evidence supplied does not establish that configuration-controlled integrated breadboard.

Thus **TRL 3 is the evidence-supported working rating**; TRL 4 is a conditional upper bound, not credit for the unanimously adopted architecture. If T26 denotes a wholly new, unspecified implementation rather than adoption of demonstrated components, even its integrated TRL 3 claim would need configuration-specific proof.

**Next unmet exit:** TRL 4 requires documented integrated breadboard performance agreeing with predictions, plus a documented relevant environment. The project currently lacks both an identified tested service configuration and complete traffic/outage/security requirements.

These levels should not be averaged.

## 3. Target and difficulty

- **0A internal milestone:** TRL **4–5** before committing the pathfinder’s communications and command-service baseline. Demonstrate the actual command chain and recovery functions before launch.
- **KDP-0B:** TRL **6**, without waiver, for the integrated service needed to support uncrewed production and cargo operations.
- **Deployment:** actual flight equipment must subsequently complete qualification and acceptance; TRL 6 does not replace that work.
- **Needed date:** **before KDP-0B; calendar year not supplied.** The supplied decision acceptance dates do not determine the technology-need date.
- **AD²: 4** under the project’s anchors: new integration, with similar development already demonstrated. The hard work is assurance and systems integration, not new communications physics.
- **Estimated time to TRL 6:** **3–5 years** after requirements baseline, funding and access to suitable hardware.

AD² could rise to **5–6** if requirements expand to autonomous cross-fleet command authority, unusual key-recovery mechanisms, continuous high-rate connectivity, or a largely novel relay network.

**Indicative package:** approximately **$9–24M** if all steps are undertaken. The pathfinder flight extension is not automatically required before TRL 6, but provides particularly useful evidence for deep-space integration.

A ground test cannot reproduce solar-corona propagation merely by introducing packet loss. TRL 6 requires a documented argument combining flight heritage, measured channel behavior, validated channel models and physical testing. Likewise, accelerated radiation tests must have a justified relationship to the specified lifetime.

### Required acceptance measures

Before testing, set numerical thresholds for:

- command-delivery latency and telemetry age;
- delivered data per contact and maximum queue occupancy;
- backlog-clearance time after the worst outage;
- command expiry, ordering, duplication and replay behavior;
- recovery after loss or rollback of persistent state;
- key rollover and isolation of one compromised endpoint;
- emergency-command access during competing fleet traffic.

**No safety-critical local action may depend on an Earth response within its control deadline.** Communications testing must demonstrate this boundary, not substitute for local autonomy validation.

## 5. Risk and fallback

**Primary risk class: architecture-change.**

Failure of the proposed integrated service need not end asteroid operations: independent point-to-point links, scheduled contacts and prevalidated onboard sequences offer a credible alternative. That fallback can increase staffing, ground-contact cost and operational restrictions.

However, inability to demonstrate **any** trustworthy command/recovery service compatible with safe unattended production is **project-ending for 0B** under D-02. This justifies the TRL 6 gate even though failure of the particular networking implementation is usually an architecture-change.

**Fallback:** use independently scheduled heritage RF links, authenticated command sequences with explicit expiration, conservative autonomous safe states, and a reduced fleet; remain at 0A-only if outage-safe production cannot be demonstrated.

Ground-network access is a separate availability and cost gate. High TRL does not establish that DSN capacity is contracted or adequate.

## 6. Where others might disagree

1. **TRL 6–9 from heritage:** defensible for an unchanged radio or flown DTN configuration, but not for the unspecified integrated authenticated fleet service.
2. **TRL 2 rather than 3:** defensible if the assessed object is an entirely new implementation. My lower bound credits physically demonstrated critical functions, not a tested Project Dyson prototype.
3. **TRL 4 upper bound:** contingent on obtaining documented integrated test results; without them, record TRL 3.
4. **Ground demonstration versus flight:** a well-supported relevant-environment prototype can reach TRL 6. A credible assessor may require additional flight evidence if the integration depends strongly on unvalidated channel or environmental assumptions.
5. **Fleet scaling:** 29 endpoints are modest computationally, but scheduling, long-outage backlog and correlated recovery can be limiting. Data rates TBD prevent resolving that disagreement quantitatively.
