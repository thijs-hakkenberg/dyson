## T03 — MW-class waste-heat rejection

**Assessment:** Established spacecraft thermal-control technologies support development, but they do **not** establish readiness of a multi-megawatt, dust-exposed NEA plant or a megawatt-electric cargo tug.

| Application | Current TRL | Target at KDP-0B | AD² | Principal qualification gap |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Plant, stationary no-melt process | **3–4** | **6** | **5** | MW-scale integration, dust, thermal cycling and fault isolation |
| Plant, rotating smelting process | **2–3** for the rotation-dependent heat-transfer subsystem | **6** | **6** | Heat transfer across or within rotating equipment; process/radiator temperature separation |
| Plant, narrow native-metal route | **3–4** if stationary; **2–3** if rotation-dependent | **6** | **5–6** | Depends on the experimentally selected process |
| Tug, integrated thermal management | **3–4** | **6** | **5** | PPU/thruster heat partition, deployed radiator scale, lifetime and propulsion integration |

These are ratings of **T03 at the required scale and environment**, not ratings of ammonia loops, heat pipes or radiators considered individually. The higher bounds are conditional on obtaining applicable breadboard test reports; they are not established by the architecture description alone.

### Assumptions and thermal definition

- **Plant:** 1–9 MWth is an assessment envelope, not an independently validated heat load. Each route must close an energy balance. Heat rejected cannot exceed electrical power dissipated on site **plus explicitly stated absorbed solar-thermal and other thermal inputs**, less energy leaving through products, exhaust or other pathways. The 9 MWth case therefore needs justification against the 2.5–8 MWe site supply.
- **Plant duration:** not specified. I provisionally assume a **five-year operating campaign**, with replaceable radiator modules. A different requirement changes lifetime qualification.
- **Tug:** 50–800 kWth must be separated into **PPU/electronics cooling**, **thruster structural heat rejected by self-radiation**, and any other spacecraft cooling loads. Energy carried away in the exhaust is not a radiator load.
- **Tug duration:** 20,000–40,000 propulsion operating hours, with 1.5–5-year round trips. Those hours do not establish the complete calendar-life requirement.
- **Environment:** vacuum, microgravity or rotating 0.05–0.15 g hardware, 0.9–1.3 AU illumination and thermal cycling, radiation, and—in the plant’s case—abrasive charged regolith and deposition. Tug dust exposure depends on docking and loading operations.

**Temperature and coolant must remain route-specific:**

| Circuit | Assessment reference | Candidate coolant; limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Plant low-temperature auxiliaries | Radiating surface approximately **300–350 K** | Water or ammonia-based architecture; freezing, containment and materials compatibility require qualification |
| Plant process heat rejection | Approximately **450–600 K**, **only where the process permits** | Pressurised water at suitable temperatures, or qualified liquid-metal/heat-pipe circuits; no coolant selection is established |
| Tug PPU/electronics | Approximately **300–350 K** initially | Water/ammonia or another qualified fluid; actual set point follows electronics limits and thermal resistance |
| Tug thruster structure | Temperature set by selected thruster | Predominantly direct radiation where demonstrated by the thruster design; not automatically a shared pumped loop |

Ammonia is **not** a generic coolant across the entire 300–600 K range. Equally, a hot furnace does not justify assuming that all plant auxiliary heat can be rejected at 600 K.

For scale, using emissivity 0.9 and neglecting absorbed sunlight and mutual irradiation:

\[
A_{\rm emitting}=\frac{Q}{0.9\,\sigma T^4}
\]

- Plant at **300 K:** approximately **2,400–21,800 m²** emitting area.
- Plant at **600 K:** approximately **150–1,360 m²**.
- Tug at **300 K:** approximately **120–1,940 m²**.
- Tug at **350 K:** approximately **65–1,050 m²**.

These are **total emitting areas**, not necessarily deployed planform areas. They are optimistic screening values, not qualified designs. Solar flux is approximately **805–1,680 W/m²** over 1.3–0.9 AU; shielding, orientation, dust-induced absorptivity changes and view factors can materially increase required area.

## 1. Evidence

### ISS external active thermal control

**Sources:** NASA, *Reference Guide to the International Space Station* (2015); NASA’s documented Expedition 24 external ammonia pump-module replacement operations, August 2010.

- **Demonstrated:** operational pumped-ammonia heat transport, external radiators, control and maintainable hardware in orbital vacuum and microgravity.
- **Scale:** two external loops, each with a published **approximately 35 kW heat-rejection capacity**.
- **Important qualification:** 35 kW is a published capacity, not a calorimetrically verified full-load operating result established by the sources cited here. I do not equate installed capacity with demonstrated maximum sustained rejection.
- **Required/rated scale ratio:**
  - Plant versus the approximately 70 kW two-loop system: **14–129×**.
  - Tug versus one approximately 35 kW loop: **1.4–23×**.
- **Mismatch:** relatively low-temperature operation; Earth-orbit rather than NEA illumination; no asteroid regolith environment; servicing history does not demonstrate autonomous leak recovery.

**What it supports:** mature underlying low-temperature thermal-control functions. It does not qualify a scaled NEA implementation.

### SNAP-10A

**Source:** US SNAP-10A flight programme, launched in **1965**.

- **Demonstrated:** an approximately **30 kWth** reactor with liquid-metal heat transport and space heat rejection; approximately **500 We** output; roughly **43 days** of operation in orbit.
- **Scale:** heat rejected was approximately **30 kW**, allowing for the small electrical output.
- **Required/demonstrated scale ratio:**
  - Plant: approximately **33–300×**.
  - Tug total heat-load envelope: approximately **1.7–27×**.
- **Mismatch:** a hotter reactor/converter architecture, not a matched 300–600 K NEA process radiator; no dusty operating site; duration far below the required multi-year service.
- **Uncertainty:** I am not assigning a specific demonstrated radiator surface temperature here without the original thermal-performance report.

**What it supports:** liquid-metal heat transport and space radiation are physically demonstrated, not speculative. It does not establish the proposed module size, deployment, contamination tolerance or lifetime.

### Evidence limitation

I cannot confidently identify, from the evidence available here, a physical demonstration combining:

> **MW-class rejection + the selected coolant and radiator temperature + NEA dust + representative deployment + autonomous isolation + required lifetime.**

I therefore give **no TRL uplift** for Project Dyson design studies, panel agreement or unverified radiator proposals.

## 2. Current TRL and unmet exits

### Stationary plant: TRL 3–4

- **Lower bound, TRL 3:** spacecraft heritage experimentally validates the critical functions of pumped heat transport and radiative rejection.
- **Conditional upper bound, TRL 4:** defensible for a defined implementation built around tested components, provided documented laboratory breadboard results agree with predictions and define the relevant environment.
- **Next unmet exit, TRL 5:** documented breadboard performance in the **relevant environment**, with explicit scaling requirements. The missing combination includes representative temperature, coolant, dust deposition, solar loading and isolation behaviour.

The upper bound is **not** a claim that the full plant has already passed laboratory validation.

### Rotating-smelting plant: TRL 2–3

There are two materially different architectures:

1. **Stationary radiator connected across a rotating interface:** rotary fluid joints, seals, hoses or other transfer hardware become a critical subsystem.
2. **Radiator and loop rotate with the furnace/module:** avoids that interface but introduces rotating deployment, fluid behaviour, structural loading and view-factor constraints.

- **TRL 2 is supported:** a documented application can be formulated from established principles.
- **TRL 3 is conditional:** it requires an applicable experiment validating the selected rotation-dependent critical function. None is identified here.
- **Next unmet exit from the lower bound:** experimental or test-validated analytical proof of that critical function.

This rating concerns **heat rejection associated with the route**, not the metallurgical readiness of the furnace.

### Narrow native-metal production

- **Stationary processing:** **TRL 3–4**, as above.
- **Rotating modules:** **TRL 2–3** for the rotation-dependent subsystem until physically demonstrated.

“Experimentally gated” is an appropriate programme posture, but is not TRL evidence.

### Tug: TRL 3–4

- Existing thermal-control heritage supports the critical physics.
- The integrated tug rating remains limited by the unspecified thruster, propellant, PPU efficiency, heat partition, radiator design and duty profile.
- **Next unmet exit, TRL 5:** a representative PPU/thruster thermal breadboard operating in vacuum with the selected heat-load partition, radiator temperature, scaling definition and fault response.

Thruster self-radiation cannot be credited at the required scale merely because smaller electric thrusters operate in space.

### Required qualification details

- **Dust:** measure changes in solar absorptivity and infrared emissivity against deposited mass and particle distribution. Demonstrate shielding or cleaning without unacceptable plume, abrasion or contamination effects.
- **Leak isolation:** segmented inventory, independently isolatable modules, inventory-loss detection and surviving-capacity tests. Demonstrate a puncture or induced leak rather than relying only on valve-command simulations.
- **Lifetime:** endurance testing and a test-correlated degradation model covering pumps, seals, heat pipes, coatings, radiation and start/stop cycles. A short vacuum test does not establish 20,000–40,000 hours.
- **Gravity:** ground tests alone may not bound two-phase fluid distribution or rotating interfaces. Use relevant-gravity testing or flight evidence where the governing behaviour cannot be adequately reproduced.
- **Scale:** a 1 MW test does not automatically qualify 9 MW. Modular extrapolation must validate common manifolds, hydraulic stability, controls, deployment and correlated failures. Otherwise increase test scale or narrow the operating envelope.

## 5. Risk and fallback

**Primary risk class: project-ending for 0B** if neither a stationary plant nor the required tug fleet can meet heat-rejection requirements. D-02 then implies **0A only, Earth supply**.

More local failures have different consequences:

- **Rotating thermal interface fails:** **architecture-change**—use stationary no-melt/native-metal processing if independently qualified.
- **High-temperature loop fails:** **architecture-change/cost-increase**—use lower-temperature, larger radiators if mass, deployment and power budgets close.
- **Tug thermal envelope fails:** **architecture-change**—reduce propulsion power, use more vehicles or lengthen transfer schedules, subject to demand and economics gates.
- **Qualification overruns:** **schedule-delay**, not justification for a TRL waiver.

Radiator redundancy is not a substitute for qualifying common-mode dust deposition, coolant loss or deployment failure.

## 6. Where others might disagree

1. **TRL 3 versus 4:** component specialists may credit mature loop hardware as TRL 4 or higher. That is reasonable for the component, but not automatically for the integrated architecture. The upper bound requires applicable test documentation.
2. **Whether MW operation is essential for TRL 6:** validated modular scaling may justify a smaller prototype. I would accept that only after demonstrating representative shared interfaces and bounding the maximum-load failure modes.
3. **Rotation-dependent rating:** an existing tested rotary thermal interface could raise it. None has been identified here at the required coolant, temperature, environment and lifetime.
4. **Tug heat partition:** a well-qualified, predominantly self-radiating thruster could substantially reduce radiator requirements. Conversely, assigning the entire 800 kWth to a low-temperature PPU loop makes the tug much harder.
5. **Dust severity:** shielded radiator placement may reduce exposure dramatically. That is an experimentally testable architectural benefit, not grounds to omit contamination qualification.
