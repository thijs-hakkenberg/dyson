## T04 reassessment: asteroid excavation, anchoring and reaction control

**Recommended current TRL: 2–3; use TRL 2 for the specified integrated bucket-wheel system until a documented critical-function test is supplied. Target: TRL 6 before KDP-0B. AD²: 6.**

The original entry’s justification—“validated through multi-model consensus analysis”—does not satisfy TRL 3. Physical heritage exists for excavation mechanisms, asteroid surface interaction and laboratory rock gripping, but it does **not** demonstrate an anchored, contained asteroid excavator at the required throughput and endurance.

### Assessment assumptions

- Rate the complete excavation chain: tool engagement, anchoring, reaction control, containment, transfer to primary processing and autonomous recovery.
- Require operation on **one sampled, qualified target material envelope**, not universal operation across C-, S- and purported metallic asteroid surfaces. Universal compatibility would substantially increase development difficulty.
- Treat **0.5–5 t/h as active excavation throughput**, and 9–25% as total calendar duty, including operational interruptions. If that duty excludes maintenance, relocation or failed anchoring, annual production estimates are optimistic.
- Five years means both calendar environmental survival and the corresponding accumulated working hours. Replaceable wear cartridges are permissible only if replacement resources and autonomous servicing are explicitly included.
- This excavation assessment applies to all three proposed metallurgy routes. **It does not assign readiness to carbonyl/H₂ processing or rotating smelting**; those require separate assessments.

### Evidence corrections

The supplied literature reviews contain claims that should not enter the readiness register without checking their original sources:

- Bennu’s approximately **1.19 g/cm³ bulk asteroid density** is not automatically the excavator-feed bulk density.
- Bulk cohesion inferred from asteroid spin behaviour is not a measured anchor pull-out strength.
- Counter-rotation does not cancel translational cutting forces, nor necessarily transient torques from unequal tool engagement.
- Ordinary vacuum suction cannot collect material from an open asteroid surface without a supplied gas flow and its associated containment and consumables.
- “Millions of anchor engagements” is not derived from the stated duty cycle. Engagement count and load-fatigue count are different requirements.
- I have not independently verified the supplied arXiv identifier/title pairings. They should not be treated as validated experimental evidence merely because they appear in the review.

## 2. Current TRL and scaling gaps

### Current rating: **TRL 2–3**

**Lower bound, TRL 2:** met. The application, candidate mechanism, intended operating environment and potential benefit are documented.

**Upper bound, TRL 3:** defensible for the **broader excavation/reaction-management concept**, drawing on ground excavation prototypes and test-informed asteroid interaction mechanics. It is **not yet established for this particular integrated dual-bucket-wheel/anchor/containment implementation**.

For the exact implementation, the next unmet exit criterion is **TRL 3: documented experimental or test-validated analytical results validating its key parameters**. Required parameters include simultaneous cutting loads, residual torque, anchor stability, captured throughput and particle escape.

Even if TRL 3 is accepted by heritage transfer, **TRL 4 remains unmet**: no cited integrated laboratory breadboard has documented agreement with predictions across a defined target-representative material envelope.

### Required production and endurance

For \(N\) robots, active rate \(q\) and duty \(d\):

\[
\bar Q=Nqd
\]

The architecture’s independent limits produce:

- Minimum: \(6\times0.5\times0.09=\mathbf{0.27\ t/h}\).
- Maximum: \(20\times5\times0.25=\mathbf{25\ t/h}\).

Thus, the proposed fleet envelope does **not inherently guarantee** Claude’s **1.7–6.3 t/h fleet-average requirement**. Feasible operating combinations must be selected and tested; duty is an output of reliability and operations, not simply a multiplier that can be assumed.

The fleet-average requirement corresponds to approximately **14.9–55.2 kt/year**. Five-year active exposure is:

\[
5\times8760\times(0.09\text{–}0.25)
=\mathbf{1,971\text{–}10,950\ working\ hours}
\]

No cited heritage demonstrates that exposure with simultaneous asteroid-like particle behaviour, vacuum, charging, thermal cycling and repetitive anchoring loads.

### Containment requirement

If the proposed **1 t/year escaped-material ceiling** were allocated entirely to excavation, the implied retained-or-recaptured fraction at 14.9–55.2 kt/year would be approximately:

**99.9933–99.9982%.**

This assumes every unretained particle escapes. Actual trajectories and recapture may relax the immediate tool-retention requirement, but processing and tailings also consume the site’s escape budget. **A generic 99% containment target is plainly insufficient.**

## 3. Target and difficulty

- **0A internal milestones:** TRL 4, then TRL 5 for integrated hardware. Needed before committing the pathfinder’s excavation design.
- **KDP-0B:** **TRL 6, no waiver**, because failed excavation stops the selected NEA production option.
- **Needed date:** before KDP-0B; no calendar date is supplied. Planning estimate: **8–15 years from funded development start**, not a demonstrated schedule.
- **AD²: 6**, using the project’s anchors: several competing approaches remain, and the path is not clear.

The difficulty is chiefly engineering rather than uncertain basic physics: sustaining force closure on weak heterogeneous material while capturing throughput, preventing dust escape and surviving thousands of working hours. Requiring operation across unsampled asteroid classes, or prohibiting wear-part replacement, could justify **AD² 7**.

TRL 6 does not require waiting five calendar years in space. It does require a convincing, experimentally supported endurance qualification strategy; accelerated tests must preserve the relevant wear and failure mechanisms.

The pathfinder’s success criterion must be **measured agreement with predictions**, not merely successful surface contact. A lower-rate scout instrument can characterize material but cannot alone demonstrate production excavation.

The flight programme should also derive credible fleet duty from relocation, maintenance, jams and recovery. A single successful excavation run does not validate annual fleet-average production.

## 5. Risk and fallback

**Primary risk class: project-ending for the selected 0B production option.** It does not end 0A or Project Dyson as a whole.

If bucket wheels fail but another mechanically contained excavation method reaches the requirement, that is an **architecture change**. If no excavation approach passes KDP-0B, apply the pre-committed fallback: **0A only, Earth-supplied Fe-Ni**.

Thermal volatile extraction is **not a like-for-like fallback** for native/reduced Fe-Ni production. It changes the product and does not remove all anchoring, intake or containment problems.

## 6. Where others might disagree

1. **TRL 2 versus 3:** reviewers may transfer RASSOR and TAG evidence to grant TRL 3. I would require an explicit, documented transfer argument and a test of this implementation’s coupled critical functions.
2. **Counter-rotation:** Gemini treats it as adopted; Claude wants corrected-load analysis; GPT retains it as a candidate. The evidence supports testing it, not declaring anchoring loads solved.
3. **Relevant environment:** some reviewers may accept orbital simulant testing plus validated scaling for TRL 6. I favour an NEA demonstration unless the target-material and boundary-condition differences are convincingly closed.
4. **Power:** Claude’s low-power case, Gemini’s approximately 30 kW case and GPT’s higher electrical-power case remain separate hypotheses. No cited integrated test selects among them.
5. **Lifetime:** a serviceable excavation head could materially reduce difficulty. An inaccessible, maintenance-free five-year mechanism is a different and harder requirement.
