## T02 — MW-scale deployable solar arrays

**Assessment: current TRL 2–3 for the architecture-required subsystem; target TRL 6 at KDP-0B; AD² 5.** Flight-proven photovoltaic cells and deployable arrays provide substantial heritage, but do **not** establish readiness of a 1–2.5 MWe tug array or a 2.5–8 MW asteroid-site installation with the specified lifetime, contamination exposure and structural dynamics.

The lower rating applies to the **integrated, architecture-specific technology**, not to solar cells or existing commercial array products.

### Assessment boundary and assumptions

- **Tugs:** 1–2.5 MWe **net usable EOL power**, including array-side conversion and distribution losses, at the maximum powered operating distance—or with explicitly accepted propulsion derating.
- **Sites:** 2.5–8 MW net usable EOL power. I assume an Earth-manufactured, modular flat-plate installation, rather than locally manufactured cells or structures.
- **Lifetime:** 10–15 years. Accelerated testing and validated life models can support TRL 6; a literal 15-year prototype flight is not required.
- **Environment:** vacuum, solar-particle radiation, operational temperature changes, deployment in microgravity, and applicable site anchoring or free-flying-array dynamics. Tug arrays must remain stable under thrust and attitude manoeuvres. Site arrays require protection against charged dust and processing contamination.
- **Distance:** 0.9–1.3 AU is the stated environmental envelope, **not evidence that every selected tug trajectory stays inside it**. Actual trajectory maxima must be established before design freeze.
- **Electrical boundary:** array strings, deployment structure, pointing, harnessing and array-side power conditioning are included. Complete site distribution and propulsion-system readiness are separate assessments, although their interfaces must be demonstrated.

The older **100 MW at SEL4/5, $500 million, 600 V** research framing is superseded. Its cost, module-size and degradation assumptions are not requirements or readiness evidence for the revised architecture.

### What the supplied literature does—and does not—establish

I would not adopt the supplied literature reviews’ numerical fluences, annual degradation rates, confidence intervals or cost estimates without checking the underlying papers and calculations. In particular:

- Electron-irradiation results alone do not qualify a cell/coverglass assembly for the mission’s **proton spectrum and fluence**.
- Annealing observations in one semiconductor composition do not establish a recoverable power allowance for the selected flight cell.
- Sun–Earth L4/L5 do **not** experience routine Earth eclipses as described in the supplied review.
- ISS iROSA is **not concentrator-array heritage**.
- High-concentration systems reduce **cell area**, not the collection aperture needed to intercept sunlight. Their optics, heat spreading, pointing and degradation remain separate scaling problems.
- Flat-plate cell heat is largely rejected directly from the array surfaces; it should not automatically be assigned to the plant’s process-radiator budget.

The supplied arXiv references may contain useful component evidence, but I have not independently verified their bibliographic details and quoted results here. They receive no additional integrated-system TRL credit in this assessment.

## 2. Current TRL and unmet criteria

### Integrated flat-plate subsystem: **TRL 2–3**

**TRL 2 is met:** the application, functional concept and feasibility basis are documented, and closely related physical technologies exist.

**TRL 3 is the conditional upper bound:** existing deployment and structural-response experiments can support critical-function proof of concept **if** a documented, test-correlated scaling analysis shows that they validate key parameters of the proposed modular MW design. No such architecture-specific correlation package is supplied.

**The next exit criterion is unmet:**

- From the conservative **TRL 2** position: documented experimental or test-validated analytical results validating the proposed system’s key parameters.
- From the conditional **TRL 3** position: a component/breadboard laboratory demonstration with performance agreeing with predictions **and a documented relevant environment**.

Neither a design study nor multiplication of flight-proven 20 kW wings satisfies these criteria by itself.

### Distance and EOL sizing are material, not bookkeeping

An illustrative calculation—not qualification evidence—is:

\[
P_{\rm net,EOL}/A =
\frac{1361}{r^2}\,
\eta_{\rm cell}\,f_{\rm packing}\,f_{\rm EOL}\,\eta_{\rm electrical}.
\]

Using explicitly assumed values of 0.30, 0.85, 0.80 and 0.95 respectively gives approximately **156 W/m² at 1.3 AU**:

- Tug active blanket footprint: approximately **6,400–16,000 m²**.
- Site active blanket footprint: approximately **16,000–51,000 m²**.

These exclude access gaps, deployment clearances, structural supports and reserve capacity. The assumed EOL factor is **not yet substantiated**.

Sizing at 1.3 rather than 1.2 AU requires about **17% more area**, all else equal. Beyond 1.3 AU, power must be resized or operational derating explicitly accepted. Radiation degradation should be expressed through mission-specific remaining-power factors and uncertainty bounds, not an unsupported constant annual percentage.

## 5. Risk and fallback

**Primary classification: project-ending for the selected solar-powered 0B architecture.** Failure to substantiate EOL power, deployment reliability or dynamic stability prevents a no-waiver TRL-6 decision.

Distinguish the failure modes:

- Failure of the **1–2.5 MW-per-tug architecture** could require smaller, more numerous tugs and revised delivery schedules: **architecture-change**.
- Failure of a particular large wing or concentrator concept could be addressed with smaller flat-plate modules: **architecture-change**, usually with cost and mass penalties.
- Failure to qualify any economically acceptable site/tug solar configuration by KDP-0B triggers the pre-committed fallback: **0A only, Earth supply**.

Replacement modules and power derating are possible mitigations, not free readiness credit. Their launch burden, maintenance method and effects on signed Phase 1 delivery demand must be included in the delivered-cost gate.

## 6. Where others might disagree

1. **TRL 2–3 versus 4–5.** An assessor may classify this as an enlargement of flight-proven arrays. My lower rating reflects the absence of architecture-specific test-correlated MW scaling, rather than doubt about photovoltaic conversion. A genuine modular breadboard programme could justify 4–5.
2. **What must be demonstrated at full scale.** Total MW power can sometimes be aggregated without a proportionate structural extrapolation. A monolithic wing cannot claim that advantage merely by electrically subdividing its blanket.
3. **Tug versus site difficulty.** Tugs face thrust-coupled dynamics and propulsion contamination; sites face dust, installation and support uncertainty. Neither is universally harder.
4. **AD² 5 versus 6.** This turns chiefly on modularity and requirement closure. The reference rating assumes conventional flat-plate cells and repeatable deployable modules—not 500× concentration.
5. **Lifetime evidence.** Qualification by accelerated exposure is legitimate, but only with validated degradation models, combined-stress testing and stated confidence bounds. It is not equivalent to assuming a heritage annual degradation rate.
