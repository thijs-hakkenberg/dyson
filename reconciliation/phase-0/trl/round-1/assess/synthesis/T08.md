## 2. Where the panel agrees

1. **TRL 3 exit criterion is unmet for the critical function.** All three say no experiment or test-validated analysis has measured containment or escape efficiency for an enclosed handling system in the relevant environment.
2. **Floor is TRL 2.** No model rates any critical containment function above 2.
3. **Target is TRL 6 at KDP-0B.** All three link T08 to the licence gate for 0B.
4. **Risk is project-ending for 0B.** The ultimate fallback in all three is 0A only with Earth supply.
5. **An NEA pathfinder flight experiment is required for TRL 6.**
   - Claude explicitly caps ground testing at TRL 5.
   - Gemini and GPT both place TRL 6 on an in-space demonstrator.
6. **OSIRIS-REx TAGSAM is the key real-world containment analogue.** All three cite the flap-held-open leak. All three put the mass ratio to required throughput at about 10⁸.
7. **The required efficiency is extreme.** All three state that the 1 t/yr ceiling implies retention above 99.99%. They use different denominators (see §3.4).
8. **Abnormal events must count against the annual budget.**
   - Claude and GPT both list catalogues of events (excavation, transfer, processing, storage, docking).
   - Claude credits GPT with this emphasis.
   - Gemini mentions abnormal events in its requirement statement but does not build them into its path.
9. **Mass-balance verification is insufficient on its own** (Claude and GPT; Gemini is silent).
   - Claude calls it "impossible" at the required resolution.
   - GPT calls it "unlikely to resolve reliably".
   - Both call for direct flux measurement, calibrated detectors and witness surfaces.
10. **The ">5 µm" guarantee does not close the mass ceiling.** Claude and GPT say this; Gemini does not address it.
11. **Unverified arXiv items are not used to raise TRL.** Claude and GPT both say so. Gemini does not adopt this caution (see §4).

## 3. Where the panel diverges, and why

### 3.1 TRL upper bound: 3 (Claude, Gemini) versus 2 (GPT)

**Positions:**
- **Claude** credits analysis capability (ejecta modelling validated against SCI, DART and Bennu) and array contamination at TRL 3. It also gives no-melt enclosure and escape verification 2–3, based on instrument heritage (LDEX, GIADA) and Clydach.
- **Gemini** credits isolated lab experiments: Hao et al. 2025 on e-beam cleaning and Shin et al. 2021 on triboelectric charging.
- **GPT** rates only the integrated system. It says 2–3 "would be defensible only after identifying a documented, transferable proof" of the critical containment function, and finds none.

**Nature of the divergence.** It is largely about what the upper bound represents (sub-function versus integrated system). It is not a factual disagreement: all three agree the critical exit criterion is unmet.

**What would settle it:**
- The panel needs a common rule on whether sub-function ratings may set the upper bound.
- Technically, the vacuum seal and interface leakage tests with charged simulant (Claude P3; GPT laboratory breadboards) would settle it. These need to produce size-binned escape fractions per handling step and compare them against a prior prediction.

### 3.2 AD²: 6 (Claude, GPT) versus 7 (Gemini)

**Positions:**
- **Gemini** says the task "requires fundamental engineering research" because terrestrial dust control relies on gravity settling and water suppression.
- **Claude** gives 6, with a range of 5–7. It states that fines-retention physics "may need fundamental work, which pushes toward AD² 7", and that verification on its own would be AD² 5.
- **GPT** gives 6. It lowers this to 5–6 only for native-metal production, and only if that route avoids the furnace and chemical routes.

**What would settle it:**
- First results from the vacuum leakage tests (Claude P3, GPT lab breadboards).
- The tailings fines-liberation measurements (Claude P6).
- Together these would show whether the required capture levels are an engineering scale-up or need new physics.

### 3.3 Years to target: 4–7 (Gemini), 5–9 (GPT), 7–10 (Claude)

**Positions:**
- **Claude** builds in a pathfinder cruise and 6–12 months of operations after ground qualification, against an assumed KDP-0B of 2033–2036.
- **GPT** says "from funded development start", with overlapping work, and "not a committed schedule".
- **Gemini** gives the shortest range while assigning the highest AD². Its text does not reconcile the two. Its path also has the fewest steps (no separate tailings, verification or integrated ground test).

**What would settle it:** a programme decision on the pathfinder schedule and the KDP-0B date. No model has a date in force.

### 3.4 Denominator and definition of "escaped"

**Denominators:**
- **Claude** uses per-handling escape, with ore handled 4–5 times. This gives an allowed escape of about 4×10⁻⁶ to 1.7×10⁻⁵ per handling at 1 t/yr.
- **GPT** uses tailings alone as the denominator. This gives 2.13×10⁻⁵ to 7.69×10⁻⁵, or 99.9923–99.9979% retention. GPT notes that repeated handling adds further release opportunities.
- **Gemini** states ">99.99%" without a denominator breakdown.

**Definitions:**
- **Claude** proposes a fate-based, size-binned definition. It notes that a flat mass limit penalises coarse grains that re-impact and under-weights µm grains. It calls this a licence-gate value choice.
- **GPT** counts all material released outside the containment boundary, including grains still gravitationally bound to the body. It argues that restricting the definition to unbound trajectories would overlook local contamination. It also states that ALARP "is not a measurable replacement for a ceiling".
- **Gemini** gives no definition.

**What would settle it:** this is a licensing and D-03 decision, not a technical test. Claude's P1 and GPT's first path step both propose to document it.

### 3.5 Chronic loss from the tailings pile

**Positions:**
- **Claude** flags this as possibly the largest single uncertainty. A loss fraction of about 10⁻⁵ per year from a 65–235 kt pile would consume the whole budget. Claude cites the documented mechanisms (radiation pressure, electrostatic lofting, thermal cracking, micrometeoroid impact) but knows of no published loss rate. It predicted that GPT and Gemini would treat the pile as benign.
- **GPT** does not quantify this. It does say loose tailings piles should not be credited as disposal without validation of long-term stability.
- **Gemini** does not address it.

**What would settle it:** the fines-liberation-rate measurements under thermal cycling, UV/plasma charging and impact (Claude P6; GPT tailings endurance step).

### 3.6 Is the requirement physically achievable?

- **Gemini** alone raises the possibility that the 1 t/yr limit is physically impossible for open-faced excavation at 47 kt/yr, "unless the entire asteroid is bagged". It attributes this view to unnamed granular-mechanics experts.
- **Claude** and **GPT** treat the limit as hard but test it through their paths.

**What would settle it:** the microgravity release tests (Claude P4, Gemini parabolic step). These would give velocity distributions from bucket strikes and gate events, which bound the open-face source term.

### 3.7 Route-specific containment ranking

- **Claude:** no-melt route at 2–3, rotating furnace at 2, native-metal at 2. Claude ranks no-melt best on containment grounds. It notes that furnace rim speed sets tangential release well above escape speed, and that open separation in the native-metal route is a lofting source. It anticipates the counter-argument that slag is easier to stabilise than fines.
- **GPT:** all three routes at 2. AD² is 6, except native-metal at 5–6. GPT states that native-metal "may genuinely simplify containment" while rotating modules "may add interfaces that erase that advantage". GPT also states that a route-specific escape demonstration cannot qualify a different route.
- **Gemini:** no route breakdown.

Both Claude and GPT say their rankings are untested.

**What would settle it:** route-specific vacuum interface tests (feed locks, rotating seals, separator enclosures) within the integrated ground pilot. Both Claude P8 and GPT's pilot specify route-specific interfaces.

### 3.8 How much flight demonstration is enough

- **Claude:** 10–100 kg/h subscale for 6–12 months. TRL 6 is conditional on validating the scaling analysis from P8. The cost is stated as an increment on the pathfinder: USD 60–200 M.
- **Gemini:** about 100 kg/h at USD 50–150 M.
- **GPT:** 1–10 t cumulative handling over 6–12 months, as a dedicated mission at USD 150–450 M. GPT flags that this is still 1,300–47,000 times below annual tailings. It says operating in space alone does not earn TRL 6, and a larger or longer demonstration may be needed.

The cost difference reflects "increment" versus "dedicated mission" framing. The scales are stated in different units (rate versus cumulative mass).

**What would settle it:** the integrated ground pilot's demonstrated scaling laws for leakage, wear and tailings failure (Claude P8; GPT pilot plus the tailings endurance step).

### 3.9 Risk class nuance

- All three say project-ending for 0B.
- **Claude** records the counter-view that this is an architecture-change risk, because throughput, target size and tailings method are trade knobs. Claude holds to project-ending under D-02's no-waiver rule.
- **GPT** notes that some failures (such as a furnace seal) would force only an architecture change. It reserves project-ending for site-wide containment and verification failure.
- **Gemini's** rationale adds that a "runaway debris cloud would hazard the entire multi-billion-dollar fleet". This rationale is Gemini's alone and is not quantified.
