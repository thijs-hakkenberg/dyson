## 2. Where the models agree

1. **TRL floor is 3.** All three set the lower bound at 3, and all locate it in MW-class integration or microgrid management rather than in battery cells.
2. **Cell and module heritage is strong, but integrated readiness is not.** All three hold that flight-proven Li-ion modules (ISS) do not by themselves establish readiness for an MWh-class NEA microgrid. Each explicitly rejects the "it's just paralleling ISS batteries / Megapacks in a box" argument for TRL 5–6:
   - Claude §6.2
   - Gemini §6
   - GPT §6.1
3. **The gaps are thermal and fault behaviour in vacuum at scale.** All three identify these as the core unmet items: vacuum heat rejection, high-voltage arcing and fault isolation at kV / MW, and integrated testing.
4. **Baseline risk class is architecture-change.** All three agree. Claude and GPT both name conditions under which it escalates to project-ending, specifically continuous-power or furnace process routes.
5. **Phase need is 0B.**
6. **Fallbacks converge.** Each fallback reduces storage to critical or keep-alive loads and accepts intermittent production:
   - Claude: keep-alive ≤1.5 MWh plus nightly curtailment
   - Gemini: accept eclipse downtime
   - GPT: critical loads only, intermittent operation

   Claude and GPT both name D-02's "0A only, Earth supply" as the terminal fallback.
7. **Terrestrial MWh-to-GWh storage is not environment-relevant evidence.** All three agree, whichever example is cited.
8. **rq-0-60 (tether insulation) bears on T20 through the robot power interface.** All three agree.

## 3. Where the models diverge, and why

### D1. TRL ceiling: 4 versus 5

- **Positions:**
  - Claude: headline ceiling 5, driven by its R and S element ratings.
  - Gemini: ceiling 4, stated as unmet.
  - GPT: architecture ceiling 4, described as conditional.
- **Why:** This is largely an **aggregation-method difference**, not an evidence difference. Claude's and GPT's element ratings for site modules and robot packs are identical (4–5). They differ only on whether the headline reflects the best element or the integrated subsystem.
  - GPT states that its TRL 4 is "conditional" because no integrated breadboard or documented relevant-environment definition was supplied.
  - Gemini says TRL 4 is unmet because no MWh bank and MW controller have been tested together in vacuum.
- **Justification of the lower bound differs:**
  - Gemini rests TRL 3 on "analytical models of MW-class space power systems".
  - Claude and GPT rest it on physical test data: terrestrial MVDC/microgrid tests and ISS EPS (Claude); experimental evidence of the critical functions (GPT).
  - GPT separately states that "analysis alone does not establish TRL 4".
- **What would settle it:**
  - A panel decision on whether T20 is rated as one item or as separate elements.
  - Documented existence (or absence) of an integrated laboratory breadboard whose performance matches predictions.

### D2. Target at KDP-0B: 5 versus 6

- **Positions:**
  - Claude: 5 for S/P at KDP-0B, 6 by PDR.
  - Gemini and GPT: 6 at KDP-0B.
- **Why:** The models interpret D-02 differently given the risk class.
  - Claude argues that T20 is not project-ending if a fallback exists, so D-02 does not mandate TRL 6 at KDP-0B. Claude concedes the target should rise to 6 under surface arrays plus Gemini's continuous furnace.
  - GPT ties 6 "without waiver" to the case where autonomous power continuity is project-ending.
  - Gemini states 6 is needed for the 0B site power architecture, without a D-02 argument.
- **What would settle it:**
  - Selection of the reference metallurgy route and array siting.
  - A determination of whether uninterrupted power is a process-safety requirement. GPT proposes an experimental safe interrupted-power sequence for smelting.
  - A KDP-0B calendar date. None was supplied; Claude assumes about 2031–2033.

### D3. AD²: 4 versus 5 (with stated band 4–6)

- **Why:** The models depend on different requirement assumptions.
  - Claude and Gemini rate 4 on the basis that similar development has been done (ISS EPS, terrestrial microgrids).
  - GPT rates 5 because no directly comparable integrated NEA demonstration exists.
  - Both Claude and GPT state that the rating moves (to 5, or to 6) if deep, high-count cycling without replacement becomes the reference.
- **What would settle it:** Requirements closure on cycle count, depth of discharge (DoD) and replacement interval.

### D4. Years to target: 2–4 versus 3–6

- **Why:** The paths differ in content.
  - Gemini's two-step path contains no lifetime or degradation campaign and no requirements-closure step.
  - Claude and GPT both include multi-year cycle-life testing. Claude gives 2–6 years and notes that real-time 33k cycles at a 4 h period takes 15 years; GPT gives 2–5 years.
- **What would settle it:**
  - Agreement on whether TRL 6 requires lifetime evidence, or only TVAC and integration.
  - The cycle requirement itself (see D5).

### D5. The 9,000–33,000-cycle requirement and whether heritage meets it

- **Positions:**
  - **Gemini** treats the range as a requirement and states that ISS ORUs, "designed for ~60,000 LEO cycles over 10 years", exceed it.
  - **Claude** says the figure is conditional on surface-sited arrays and is consistent with a 4–10 h rotation over 10–15 years, but states it has not seen the derivation. Claude notes the binding requirement becomes outage energy and power if arrays are stand-off.
  - **GPT** calls it "contested" and says the required DoD is undefined, so it declines to rate cycle life.
- **Why:** Claude and GPT both stress that cycle capability depends on DoD; Gemini's comparison does not address DoD.
- **What would settle it:**
  - Documentation of the ISS ORU cycle-life specification and the DoD it assumes.
  - Requirements closure on siting, rotation envelope, DoD and the curtail-versus-ride-through choice.

### D6. Storage sizing and mass closure (raised only by Claude, partly by GPT)

- **Claude** gives its own arithmetic and labels it "not data", contestable by roughly ±30–50%:
  - Night length about 1–5 h.
  - Full ride-through needs 2.5–40 MWh.
  - The 15 MWh high end could reach about 600–700 t installed, exceeding one tug payload.
- **GPT** gives energy-to-power autonomy of 3.75 min to 6 h, and C-rates from 16C (0.5 MWh at 8 MW) to 0.17C (15 MWh at 2.5 MW). It concludes these are "radically different storage designs".
- **Gemini** does not address sizing or mass.
- **Not a dispute on evidence.** This is an unaddressed requirement question. It would be settled by requirements closure and a verified space-pack specific-energy figure (Claude's 100–150 Wh/kg is its own unverified estimate).

### D7. Dependence on metallurgy route

- Claude and GPT both treat the three metallurgy routes as separate load cases.
  - Claude maps them to storage size: no-melt goes low, rotating furnace goes high or toward thermal storage.
  - GPT gives each route distinct load and shutdown questions.
- Gemini does not distinguish routes.
- Only Claude proposes a thermal-storage alternative for furnace standby.

### D8. What test reaches TRL 6 for site storage

- **Gemini:** a 0.5 MWh / 1 MW subsystem in TVAC.
- **GPT:** at least 0.5 MWh with 0.5–1 MW interfaces, plus vacuum-tested *representative modules* and a correlated scaling model to 15 MWh / 8 MW. GPT states TRL 6 is earned "only if scaling and environmental equivalence are demonstrated".
- **Claude:** even the pathfinder leaves MWh "extrapolated by ~10–100×". Site storage and microgrid therefore reach only "partial 6" by that route; Claude's own S/P TRL 6 is placed at 0B PDR.
- **What would settle it:** A panel ruling on the acceptable scaling argument for modular site storage, meaning what fraction of full scale must be tested, and in what environment.

### D9. Fallback detail

- **Only Gemini:**
  - oversize the solar arrays;
  - revert to *centralized* power conditioning.
- **Only Claude:**
  - restrict target selection to fast rotators;
  - stand-off or co-orbiting arrays. Claude notes this creates a new transmission technology to rate.
- **Only GPT:** smelting curtailment is acceptable only after a demonstrated safe interrupted-power sequence.

### D10. Confidence

Gemini rates its own assessment high; Claude and GPT rate theirs medium.
