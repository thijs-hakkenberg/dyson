## 2. Where the models agree

1. **The sampling chain sets the rating, not the rendezvous.**
   - All three state that rendezvous, SEP and shallow asteroid sampling have substantial flight heritage.
   - All three state that the limiting capability is representative subsurface sampling plus grade determination within a 200–600 kg scout.
   - Claude and GPT both explicitly decline to average subsystem ratings.
2. **Hayabusa2 SCI/touchdown is not depth-resolved bulk-grade sampling** (all three). Claude and GPT add that the ~5.4 g returned was never assayed for grade in situ. GPT adds that the 5.4 g is not entirely subsurface material.
3. **Phase need is 0A.** All three agree the scouts' output feeds the KDP-0B target gate.
4. **The TRL 4 exit is the unmet criterion.**
   - Claude and GPT state it the same way: no integrated sampler-plus-assay breadboard tested against known-grade material with agreement to prediction.
   - Both add that the relevant environment is also undefined: depth, mass, the definition of "recoverable", and the cohesion envelope.
   - Gemini's wording differs (see §3.1).
5. **Mass closure is a shared concern.** All three flag that 1–3 rendezvous plus sampling hardware may not close within 600 kg, and may force a single target per scout.
6. **"Recoverable grade" must be defined before instrument selection** (Claude, GPT). Both state that total elemental Fe and spectral taxonomy do not establish recoverable native metal.
7. **Corrections to the supplied background** (Claude, GPT):
   - the rq-0-2 and rq-0-3 Monte Carlo results are design studies, not validation;
   - DISCUS is a concept, not flight heritage;
   - the old 20-targets-per-year requirement does not apply.
8. **Philae is negative heritage** for microgravity anchoring and penetration (Claude, Gemini).

## 3. Where the models diverge, and what would settle it

### 3.1 Current TRL: 2–3 (GPT) vs 3–4 (Claude, Gemini)

| Model | Basis |
|---|---|
| Claude | **TRL 3 met:** Hayabusa2 SCI/TD2 flight data and laboratory magnetic-separation practice validate the critical functions *separately*. **Upper bound 4** only if Earth-return assay is credited, which Claude says fails the mass requirement by more than an order of magnitude. |
| Gemini | TRL 4 is "partially met by various terrestrial drill tests"; TRL 5 is unmet. **No specific test reports are cited.** |
| GPT | **Lower bound 2:** no sampler, depth, sample size or assay/recovery method has been selected. **Upper bound 3** only where adopted critical functions have test-backed predictions. GPT also states that heritage does not fill the architectural omissions. |

**What would settle it:**
- The specific terrestrial drill or sampler test reports Gemini alludes to, checked against the TRL 4 exit wording.
- A panel ruling on whether "critical functions validated separately" can earn TRL 3 before the architecture (sampling route and assay method) is selected.
- Requirement closure under rq-0-50 (depth, mass, definition of recoverable grade). This would make the relevant environment documentable.

### 3.2 AD²: 5 (Gemini) vs 6 (Claude, GPT)

- **Gemini:** the path is clear; the main difficulty is the anchoring and reaction-force deficit noted in rq-0-39.
- **Claude and GPT:** several competing routes remain unselected, so the path is not yet clear. The routes are impactor plus touchdown, anchored coring, penetrator, touch-and-go, and sample return vs on-board assay. Both say AD² 5 (Claude) or 4–5 (GPT) becomes defensible after a downselect.
- **What would settle it:** a route downselect. Claude places this at its reduced-gravity test step.

### 3.3 Target TRL and what the KDP-0B number means

The three targets measure different things:

- **Claude (TRL 7 before KDP-0B):** sampled grade only exists after flight, so the function must reach TRL 7 in operation before the gate.
- **GPT (TRL 6 at KDP-0B, TRL 8 before launch):** TRL 6 is the development gate. GPT states that the second sampled candidate is a target-campaign requirement under rq-0-50, not a TRL exit criterion.
- **Gemini (TRL 8 by 0A launch):** the target is flight qualification.

Gemini and GPT agree that flight hardware needs TRL 8 before launch. The disagreement is mainly about which quantity the `kdp_0b` field should hold.

**What would settle it:** a project-level definition of the `kdp_0b` field and of the D-02 target-gate wording. This is a programme decision, not a test.

### 3.4 Risk class: architecture-change (Claude, Gemini) vs project-ending for 0B (GPT)

The difference turns on whether a non-sampled grade estimate is an acceptable gate input:

| Model | Fallback it considers acceptable |
|---|---|
| Claude | Remote-only scouts with a re-worded gate and an explicit uncertainty band, *if* a blind test shows the band is acceptable. Claude states it "sides with GPT" that GRS cannot separate native from oxidised Fe, but treats the question as empirically open. |
| Gemini | Surface sampling plus kinetic impactors or radar tomography, accepting higher uncertainty. Alternatively, scouts of more than 1,000 kg. |
| GPT | Rejects authorising 0B from remote spectra alone. Prefers larger heritage-derived sampling missions or Earth supply. |

**What would settle it:**
- Claude's proposed blind test: predict the grades of Itokawa, Ryugu and Bennu from remote data only, then compare against the returned-sample laboratory values.
- A panel decision on whether the D-02 "sampled grade" wording is fixed.

### 3.5 Years to target: 4–6 (Gemini), 5–9 (GPT), 6–10 (Claude)

- **Claude:** runs to TRL 7 by first-flight sampling, including cruise.

**What would settle it:** a common endpoint definition across the three estimates.
