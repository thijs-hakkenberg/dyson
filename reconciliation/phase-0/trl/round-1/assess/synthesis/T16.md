## 2. Where the models agree

- **The March 2026 TRL 3 basis is invalid.** Claude and GPT both state that "multi-model consensus" is not evidence. Gemini does not discuss the prior entry but reaches its rating on separate grounds.
- **No heritage demonstrates sustained anchoring under load on a small body.** All three cite:
  - Philae as a failed anchoring attempt;
  - OSIRIS-REx TAG as brief contact without hold-down;
  - Hayabusa2 hopping landers as small, unanchored mobility.
- **Target is TRL 6 at KDP-0B.** No model proposes a waiver.
- **AD² is 7 for the rubble-pile requirement as stated.** Claude and GPT both say a competent-rock target would lower it, to 5 and 5–6 respectively.
- **Risk class is project-ending** at the 0B level.
- **An NEA pathfinder demonstration is on the critical path to TRL 6.** All three paths end with it. GPT adds that flight is not universally required for TRL 6, but says it would not assume the needed ground scaling is available.
- **Counter-rotation cancels torque, not net translational cutting force** (Claude and GPT). Gemini's submission does not address this point.
- **The two target variants need separate evidence** (Claude and GPT). A monolith demonstration does not qualify rubble-pile operation. Both say KDP-0B could instead formally restrict production to the demonstrated target class.
- **Scale ratios are mutually consistent where they overlap.** For example, Claude gives OSIRIS-REx contact duration as about 3 × 10⁷× short and GPT gives about 2.6 × 10⁷×.

## 3. Where the models diverge, and why

### 3.1 Current TRL: 2–3 (Claude, GPT) vs 3–4 (Gemini)

**Gemini's basis.** It treats TRL 3 as met by two things:
- microspines on monoliths, in 1 g and parabolic flight, citing JPL ARM / Parness et al., about 2013–2017;
- "augers in dense regolith." No source is given for the auger claim.

Gemini's upper bound of 4 rests on component breadboards. However, Gemini also calls TRL 4 "highly contested for rubble-pile targets," and states its unmet exit criterion at TRL 5. That implies TRL 4 is met. Its rating text and its exit criterion are not fully consistent.

**Claude's basis.**
- TRL 3 is unmet for rubble-pile, mobility and tailings. No pull-out, cyclic-load or retention test has been run against a prior prediction in Pa-cohesion or cohesion-scaled media.
- TRL 4 is unmet even for monolith. Two items are missing: a documented relevant-environment definition (boulder strength is only thermally inferred), and a kN-class, 10⁶-cycle breadboard test.
- Claude treats OSIRIS-REx, Hayabusa2 SCI and low-g penetration data as validating regolith properties, not anchor function.

**GPT's basis.**
- It largely aligns with Gemini on scope for monolith components, rating them 3–4.
- It rates the integrated capability 2–3, because "anchor penetration or boulder gripping does not prove load transfer into a stable body."

**Partly a scope difference.** Gemini's 3–4 is close to GPT's component-level rating for monolith anchoring. The disagreement is sharpest on whether component evidence on competent rock can set the composite rating. Claude says the weaker variant governs while the D-02 target gate is open.

**What would settle it:**
- Retrieval of the original JPL microspine test records: load values, parabolic-flight results, and whether predictions were documented beforehand. Claude marks the Parness load values and any zero-g tests as unsure. Gemini marks its microspine claim as confident but gives no specific paper or test report.
- A source for the "augers in dense regolith" claim.
- A pull-out or cyclic test against prediction in cohesion-scaled granular media. This is Step 2 of the consolidated path below.

### 3.3 Fallback

- **Gemini** proposes contactless thermal/optical extraction.
- **Claude and GPT** both reject thermal extraction as an equivalent fallback. Their reasons: the revised product is native Fe-Ni, and thermal extraction still requires plant, tether and tailings restraint.
- **Shared ground:** Gemini's boulder-bagging option overlaps with Claude's enclosed capture-and-ingest and with GPT's internally reacted enclosed module. Claude rates that option itself at TRL 2.
- **GPT only** proposes restricting production to a qualified competent-rock target as the first fallback.

**What would settle it:** confirmation of the product definition (Fe-Ni) and of whether any contactless route avoids surface restraint. No model presents evidence that one does.

### 3.4 Microspines and boulder grip on rubble piles

- **Gemini** asserts microspines are "ineffective for reacting high loads" on rubble piles.
- **Claude** lists boulder grip as one of three candidate rubble-pile load paths, untested.
- **GPT** says gripping a boulder does not establish that the boulder can react the load without moving.

None cites a test.

**What would settle it:** a boulder-grip pull-out test in a cohesion-scaled matrix that measures boulder displacement, together with in-situ boulder-interlock data from scouts.

### 3.5 Load envelope definition

- **Claude** assumes a peak net cutting force of 0.5–20 kN, derived as P/v_tip. It also gives a single-wheel torque case of 2.5–95 kN·m and assumes a plant mass of 50–500 t.
- **GPT** converts the 0.25–5 kN·m residual torque to 0.25–5 kN at a 1 m arm. It calls all loads unverified and plant mass unspecified.
- **Gemini** states the requirement as reacting "0.5–5 t/h excavation loads." That is a throughput figure, not a force; the other two express loads in kN and kN·m.

**What would settle it:** the rq-0-26 DEM six-axis force and torque spectra. Claude's Step 1 and GPT's Step 1 both call for these.

### 3.6 Rotating-smelter load interface

- **Claude** says the rotating route adds spin-reaction loads to the plant mount, and that castable slag could ease tailings retention.
- **GPT** says internal centrifugal acceleration is not automatically transmitted as an equivalent static ground load, but spin-up, imbalance, bearing and fault loads must be included.
- **Gemini** does not address route interfaces.

These positions differ in emphasis rather than conclusion. Both Claude and GPT require the rotating-route load cases in qualification.
