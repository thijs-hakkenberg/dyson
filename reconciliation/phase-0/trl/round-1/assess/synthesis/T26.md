## 2. Where the models agree

- **Phase need and target.** Phase need is 0A. The target is TRL 6 at KDP-0B.
- **Physics is not the gap.** All three treat interplanetary RF links as mature. The gap is integration, fleet scale, outage recovery and assurance.
- **Common evidence base.** All three cite the same core heritage:
  - DSN or MRO deep-space operations;
  - DINET on EPOXI (2008) for deep-space DTN;
  - ISS DTN for operational store-and-forward in LEO;
  - CCSDS SDLS (355.0-B) as the authentication standard.
- **No integrated precedent.** No precedent exists for the integrated authenticated multi-asset service at this scale.
- **Path shape.** Each path converges on full-fleet ground emulation with injected light-time delay and outages, reaching TRL 6.
- **Optical communications** are not required for this rating. Claude and GPT state this; Gemini does not address it.
- **Data rates are TBD and block quantitative qualification.** Claude and GPT both state that throughput, ground capacity and scaling cannot be checked until traffic budgets exist. Gemini does not raise this.
- **Authentication is the weakest-evidenced element.** Claude and GPT agree, and both mark deep-space flight evidence of the full key-management lifecycle as absent or uncertain. Gemini disagrees (see §3.3).

## 3. Where the models diverge, and why

### 3.1 Current TRL: 3–4, 5–7 or 6–7

The models disagree mainly on what object is being rated and what counts as demonstration.

- **Claude (5–7)** credits the critical functions demonstrated separately in operational environments: DSN links, the Mars Relay Network, DINET and Mars conjunction operations.
  - It considers the TRL 5 exit met.
  - It holds authentication with post-outage key recovery at about 4–5.
  - Its upper bound of 7 applies only if the Mars Relay Network plus DSN MSPA is accepted as an operational system prototype. Claude notes this reading ignores the 2–4× node-count gap and the authentication gap.
- **Gemini (6–7)** treats the subsystem protocols as demonstrated in relevant space environments, so considers TRL 6 met.
- **GPT (3–4)** rates the specific integrated service.
  - TRL 4 needs a configuration-controlled integrated breadboard with documented results against predictions, plus a documented relevant environment.
  - Neither exists, so GPT records TRL 3 as the working rating and TRL 4 as conditional.
  - It keeps component sub-ratings separate and explicitly declines to average them.

**Internal tensions the panel may wish to examine** (noted, not adjudicated):

- **Claude.** Its unmet exit criterion says the TRL 4/5 environment definition is blocked by undefined data rates and outage durations. Yet it also states the TRL 5 exit is met.
- **Gemini.** Its text names "TRL 8" as the next unmet criterion. Its YAML instead gives full-network test performance against predictions, which is a TRL 6 exit criterion, while also asserting TRL 6 is met.

**What would settle it:**
1. A panel ruling on the assessed object: an integrated project-specific service, or adoption of heritage components.
2. Documented integrated breadboard or test results for a defined configuration, if any exist.
3. The requirements analysis that fixes data rates and outage durations, so the relevant environment is documented.

### 3.2 AD²: 3 or 4

- **Claude and Gemini (3)** see every element as within heritage.
- **Claude** nonetheless flags that crypto and key state surviving resets, single-event upsets, counter loss and multi-month outages would be AD² 4 if treated alone.
- **GPT (4)** classes the work as new integration with similar development demonstrated.

**What would settle it:** results of the authenticated-command and key-management fault-injection testbed, and the orbit-specific outage durations (§3.4).

### 3.3 Flight evidence for authenticated commanding

| Model | Position on SDLS |
|---|---|
| Gemini | "Flown on multiple missions (TRL 8)". No mission or source is given, and it does not appear in the YAML evidence list. |
| Claude | Standard exists. Deep-space flight use is "not sure"; no citation can be given. |
| GPT | Standard exists. There is no confidently identified public flight report for the full lifecycle: replay protection across resets, key rollover during disconnection, compromise isolation, queued-command recovery. |

**What would settle it:** a cited mission report or CCSDS/agency documentation of SDLS, or equivalent authenticated commanding with key management, in deep-space flight. Gemini is invited to supply the missions it relies on.

### 3.4 Outage duration

- **Claude** cites Mars conjunction moratoria of about 2–3 weeks. It infers that low-Δv NEA targets could sit in solar exclusion for months (about 1–6× the Mars case), and labels this as its own geometric inference, not a cited study.
- **GPT** cites Mars conjunction in November 2023 at about two weeks. It proposes a 30-day complete-blackout qualification envelope as a test assumption, not an established requirement.
- **Gemini** does not quantify outage duration.

**What would settle it:** orbit-specific conjunction and solar-exclusion analysis for the candidate targets and the SEL1 geometry. This is included in both Claude's and GPT's first path step.

### 3.5 Risk class

- **Claude and Gemini (cost-increase).** Shortfalls can be bought out through more ground apertures, ESA or commercial stations, or more autonomy.
  - Claude calls this "the most contestable call". It notes a licensing state could make command security a licence condition (D-03, D-02 licence gate), which would raise T26 to architecture-change or a KDP-0B gate item.
- **GPT (architecture-change).** Fallback to point-to-point links is credible but changes operations. Inability to demonstrate *any* trustworthy command and recovery service is project-ending for 0B under D-02.

Claude and GPT therefore identify the same escalation mechanism but set the default class differently.

**What would settle it:** a programme ruling on how D-02 treats command security, and the licensing state's position on command-security conditions for high-energy tug operations.

### 3.6 Years to TRL 6

| Model | Years | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Gemini | 1–2 | Its own two path steps total about 2–2.5 years (1 yr + 1.5 yr), which exceeds its stated range. |
| Claude | 2–4 | Path through ground emulation. |
| GPT | 3–5 | Counted after the requirements baseline, funding and hardware access. Adds a relevant-environment component validation step that the others do not have. |

**What would settle it:** a schedule built after the requirements baseline, with an agreed step list.

### 3.7 Scope and scale

- **Scale.** Gemini uses "30+ nodes". Claude and GPT use the 15–29 implied by the requirement statement.
- **Local robots.**
  - GPT adds up to 20 local robot endpoints to the test scope.
  - Claude counts local links only where the site acts as relay.
  - GPT also states that no safety-critical local action may depend on an Earth response.
- **Ground capacity.**
  - Claude treats it as procurement and negotiation, not R&D.
  - GPT treats it as a separate availability and cost gate that high TRL does not establish.
  - Gemini does not address it.
- **Fallback.**
  - Gemini: manual point-to-point DSN.
  - Claude: dedicated or leased apertures, plus autonomy for multi-month silence, plus restricting high-energy tug manoeuvres to ground-in-the-loop windows.
  - GPT: heritage links, plus expiring authenticated sequences, plus conservative safe states, plus a reduced fleet. Remain 0A-only if outage-safe production cannot be shown.

**Acceptance criteria to fix before testing.**

- **From GPT** (numerical thresholds):
  - command latency and telemetry age;
  - data per contact and queue occupancy;
  - backlog-clearance time after the worst outage;
  - command expiry, ordering, duplication and replay behaviour;
  - recovery from loss or rollback of persistent state;
  - key rollover and isolation of a compromised endpoint;
  - emergency-command access during competing fleet traffic.
- **From GPT** (caveats):
