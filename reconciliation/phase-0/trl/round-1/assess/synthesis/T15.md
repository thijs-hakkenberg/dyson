## 2. Where the assessors agree

1. **Integrated readiness is TRL 2–3.** All three say space PV cells are flight-mature and the gap is system-level. Claude and GPT rate the PV and flat-plate generation component or assembly separately and higher:
   - Claude: cells and blankets TRL 8–9, MW-class array 3–4.
   - GPT: flat-plate generation assembly 3–4.
2. **The aggregation argument is rejected.** Each assessor anticipates the claim that "cells are TRL 9, so modular scaling gives TRL 5+" and rejects it. The scale-dependent issues each names are:
   - **Gemini:** packaging, deployment dynamics, thermal behaviour, HV distribution.
   - **Claude:** kV PMAD in plasma and dust, the generation-to-load link, free-flyer dynamics or anchoring.
   - **GPT:** deployment, supports, distribution, maintainability.
3. **The TRL 3 exit criterion is unmet for the same reason.** No predictions of key parameters have been validated for a selected configuration in the NEA environment, and the configuration is not chosen.
4. **TRL 6 is required before KDP-0B**, because 0B production depends on site power.
5. **Demonstrated scale is roughly 10²–10³× short of the 2.5–8 MW requirement** for generation, and further short for transfer.
6. **Sizing estimates are broadly consistent** where both were computed:
   - Claude: about 5–17 MW BOL 1-AU-equivalent, 15,000–48,000 m².
   - GPT: about 5.3–17 MW, 13,000–42,000 m².
   - Both state their assumptions are unvalidated (Claude: EOL factor 0.8; GPT: 1.5%/yr degradation).
7. **Beamed transfer should not be the 0B baseline.**
   - Claude would drop it for Phase 0.
   - GPT would not baseline it.
   - Gemini keeps it in the trade without a recommendation.
8. **High-concentration PV is not the baseline** (Claude, GPT). Both say SCARLET supports low concentration only. Gemini is silent.
9. **The related RQs (0-21, 0-22, 0-25) reflect a superseded 100 MW L4/L5 architecture** (Claude, GPT). Both list errors in them (§4.3).
10. **The ultimate fallback is the same for all three:** 0A only, Earth supply, per D-02.
11. **Metallurgy route changes the power requirement** (Claude, GPT). Both flag rotating-furnace electrical interfaces as a gap. Gemini does not address this.

## 3. Where they diverge, and what would settle it

### 3.1 AD² 5 versus 6
- **Positions:**
  - Claude and Gemini give 6 because several competing approaches remain open.
  - GPT gives 5 by assessing a nominated baseline: surface-supported, sun-tracking flat-plate arrays with sectionalised wired distribution.
  - Claude also expects 5 after a down-select to surface or cabled.
- **Nature of the split:** the assessors disagree on *when* to rate, before or after an architecture choice. They do not disagree on evidence.
- **Would settle it:** the architecture trade and down-select (Claude P1, Gemini step 1, GPT requirements audit), scheduled by Claude for the 0A mid-term review. The panel should also state whether AD² is rated on the open trade or on a reference baseline.

### 3.2 Preferred configuration: surface arrays versus cabled free-flyers
- **Claude:** leans toward cabled free-flyers *if* the 15 MWh storage ceiling holds. This rests on Claude's own rotation calculation, which it labels as calculation and not evidence: about 32 MWh is needed for 8 MW on an 8 h rotator. Claude also argues that free-flyer dynamics are hard:
  - solar radiation pressure is comparable to asteroid gravity (about 0.4 N vs 0.9 N in its worked example);
  - a passive tether is hard to keep in tension;
  - roughly 10 t of hover propellant is needed over 15 yr.
- **GPT:** prefers surface-supported arrays and rates tethered free-flyers at AD² 6. GPT says the choice is target-dependent and that "survey evidence — not consensus — must settle it."
- **Gemini:** no preference.
- **Would settle it:**
  - target rotation period and illumination from scouts (Claude P2);
  - confirmation of the storage specification;
  - a high area-to-mass station-keeping simulation validated against OSIRIS-REx and Hayabusa2 navigation data (Claude P3);
  - anchoring and pull-out tests in rubble-pile simulant (Claude P8; GPT's breadboard supports).

### 3.3 Risk class
- **Positions:**
  - Gemini and GPT: project-ending for 0B.
  - Claude: architecture-change, because any configuration failure leads to a redesign. Claude escalates to project-ending only if cost per watt fails the economics gate.
- **Nature of the split:** definitional, not evidential. The question is whether the risk class rates the technology's failure modes or the programme's dependence on the function.
- **Would settle it:**
  - a panel ruling on the definition of risk class;
  - for Claude's cost-escalation branch, space-array cost-per-watt data. Claude's own estimate is $100–500/W, which it marks as unsure and based on sparse data. Claude also states that RQ-0-21's $5/W has no heritage support.

### 3.4 What counts as TRL 6 at MW scale
This is the largest substantive split. It changes the path.

| Assessor | Step credited with TRL 6 | Condition attached |
|---|---|---|
| Claude | 50–250 kW pathfinder flight subsystem at the NEA (P9) | P7 scaling holds. Claude flags the call as contestable and says it must be made explicitly at KDP-0B. |
| Gemini | 100–250 kW pathfinder, in LEO *or* at the NEA | None stated |
| GPT | 250 kW–1 MW representative module and distribution zone | Full rated voltage, current and fault energy; a validated delivered end-of-life model; demonstrated sectional isolation and repair. GPT credits a 25–100 kW NEA pathfinder with TRL 5 only and states that "a small pathfinder plus an unvalidated extrapolation is not a TRL 6 exit." |

- **Scale ratio:** Claude gives the flight-demo ratio as 10–160× below the requirement.
- **Would settle it:**
  - a panel rule on admissible scaling for modular systems;
  - module-level (0.25–1 MW) test results showing whether the dominant scale-dependent behaviours are preserved (Claude P7, GPT's integrated prototype step).

### 3.5 Whether analysis can reach TRL 3
- **Gemini:** its architecture-selection analysis step reaches TRL 3.
- **Claude:** P1 is explicitly "not evidence for TRL 3."
- **GPT:** an audit supports TRL 3 "only where predictions are validated against physical data."
- **Would settle it:** the panel's reading of the TRL 3 exit criterion, i.e. whether analytical prediction alone or prediction plus physical validation is required.

### 3.6 Schedule basis
- Claude and Gemini give 6–10 yr; Claude anchors this to KDP-0A with an assumed KDP-0B of 2032–2036.
- GPT gives 5–8 yr from funded start for the baseline only.
- The figures may differ mainly because they are measured from different start points.
- **Would settle it:** a programme-fixed KDP-0B date and an agreed start reference.

### 3.7 Transfer topology and voltage
- **Claude:** treats trunk voltage as first-order. Its calculation for 2 km at 2% loss gives about 14 t of aluminium at 3 kV versus about 1.2 t at 10 kV.
- **GPT:** notes that routing all site power through one 1–3 kV link implies about 0.83–8 kA, and recommends distributed feeders.
- **Gemini:** frames transfer as 1–3 kV dynamic tether spools and 600 V+ DC distribution.
- **Nature of the split:** these differ in emphasis and are not directly contradictory.
- **Would settle it:** kV trunk and connector breadboard tests in a plasma chamber with charged regolith simulant (Claude P4, GPT critical-function breadboards, Gemini step 2).

### 3.8 Fallback beyond 0A-only
- **Claude:** run site loads from docked tug-class arrays (same module), then curtail to 2.5 MW with sunlit-period batching.
- **Gemini:** reduce 0B production targets to fit about 100–200 kW of state-of-the-art arrays.
- **GPT:** move directly to 0A-only. GPT accepts replacement or overbuild only after logistics and economics are validated.

### 3.9 Confidence
Gemini states high confidence; Claude and GPT state medium. Gemini's evidence base is the narrowest of the three (three citations; see §4).

### 4.3 Corrections to the background RQs
These are each assessor's own claims and have not been verified by the panel.

**Waste-heat figure.** Both Claude and GPT reject the RQ's "350 W/cm² waste heat at 500×":
- Claude gives waste heat of about 45 W/cm².
- GPT gives *incident* flux of about 68 W/cm².
- These are different quantities. Both reject 350 W/cm².

**Claude only:**
- STEREO did not use silicon cells; Claude believes this but is not certain.
- STEREO drifted past L4/L5 rather than stationing there.
- "No III-V data outside the magnetosphere" is wrong.
- Cell cost is about 10× overstated.
- The two reviews conflict on Lang et al. annealing temperatures.
- Artemis and Gateway are not L4/L5 flight opportunities.

**GPT only:**
- Concentrators reduce cell area, not aperture area.
- Active cooling is not automatically required.
- L4/L5 have no routine Earth eclipses.
- Degradation and annealing credits need a test-validated model.
