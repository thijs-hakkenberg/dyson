## 2. Where the models agree

- **Current TRL band.** All three rate the full chain 2–3, and none claims TRL 3 is met for the complete chain.
  - Claude and Gemini name TRL 3 as the unmet exit criterion.
  - GPT names TRL 4 as the next unmet criterion but states that "complete-chain TRL 3 evidence also remains incomplete."
- **Comminution maturity.** Where comminution is in scope, all three rate it at or near TRL 2. None knows of any in-space comminution or continuous bulk transfer above the gram–kilogram scale.
- **Target and gating.** All three set TRL 6 by KDP-0B and treat failure as project-ending for 0B. All three name "0A only, Earth supply" as the terminal fallback.
- **Calendar-average throughput.** All three use 15–55 kt/yr, equal to 1.7–6.3 t/h. GPT's 0.48–1.74 kg/s is the same range.
- **Terrestrial heritage does not transfer.** All three say terrestrial bulk-handling heritage is gravity-reliant and cannot be carried over wholesale.
- **Tailings drive the problem.** Claude and GPT state that the revised architecture removes ore shipment but not bulk handling at the asteroid. Tailings are the dominant stream, and site handling stays in asteroid gravity even if processing is spun.
- **Containment budget.** Claude and GPT both derive a retention requirement of order 10⁻⁵ escape fraction from the ≤1 t/yr ceiling. The figures agree once their denominators are aligned:
  - Claude: 2–8×10⁻⁵ against tailings.
  - GPT: 18–67 ppm against gross feed. GPT adds that T19 receives only part of that budget, and that the 10 kg/yr aspiration implies 0.18–0.67 ppm.
- **TAGSAM as upset evidence.** Claude and GPT both cite the TAGSAM flap being wedged open by particles as direct evidence of containment upset.
- **Supplied literature reviews.** Claude and GPT both decline to use the unverified arXiv IDs and quantitative claims in the supplied reviews.
- **Path shape.** All three paths run from characterisation and modelling, through subscale testing, to a long-duration microgravity flight pilot.

## 3. Where the models diverge, and why

### D1. AD² is 6 (Claude, GPT) or 7 (Gemini)

- **Gemini** holds that replacing gravity with pneumatic, mechanical or centrifugal forcing, while managing wear and charging over 5 years, "requires fundamental engineering research." It cites the jamming and dust problems of comminuting charged, abrasive clasts.
- **GPT** states the difficulty is "engineering rather than uncertain basic physics."
- **Claude** sits between the two. It gives 6 because competing approaches exist with no basis yet to choose, and it names its own trigger for moving to 7.
- **What would settle it:** Claude's stated trigger. Orientation-independent vacuum breadboards with cohesive, Bennu-like fines (Claude P2; GPT's integrated breadboard) would show whether wall-plating occurs continuously regardless of transport method.

### D2. Years to target: 4–7 (Gemini) against 6–12 and 6–10 (Claude, GPT)

The gap follows from path structure rather than evidence.

- **Gemini** reaches TRL 4 through parabolic-flight breadboards and TRL 6 through a single free-flyer at 0.5 t/h.
- **Claude and GPT** insert ground vacuum breadboards, a separate relevant-environment campaign and wear testing.
  - Claude adds a separate rotating-route flight.
  - GPT adds a production-representative qualification step after the pilot.
- **Claude** argues that parabolic (~20 s) and suborbital (~3 min) flights cannot show steady-state flow, wall build-up or upset recovery. This bears directly on Gemini's TRL 4 step. Gemini does not address the point.
- **Rapporteur arithmetic:** Gemini's own step durations sum to 4–8 years if run sequentially, against its stated 4–7.
- **What would settle it:**
  - A panel ruling on whether short-duration reduced-gravity tests can satisfy TRL 4 or 5 exit criteria for steady-state functions.
  - A programme schedule built against an actual flight manifest. GPT conditions its estimate on "a timely flight opportunity."

### D3. Whether sub-mm comminution is in scope

- **Claude** rates the union of all requirement statements, so sub-mm comminution is included. Claude flags this as unresolved in its own disagreement section, item 5.
- **Gemini** treats comminution as required without qualification.
- **GPT** treats it as conditional: mandatory for the Claude no-melt variant only, and possibly removable for the native-metal route by liberation testing. GPT notes that removing it "does not retroactively mature the specified Claude implementation."
- **What would settle it:** liberation and feed-acceptance tests on target-representative material (GPT's feed-definition step; Claude's P1), tied to target sampling under the D-02 target gate (Claude).

### D4. Scale and environment needed to earn TRL 6

|---|---|---|---|

- **What would settle it:** a KDP-0B ruling on the minimum demonstrated-to-required scale ratio, and on whether a validated scaling model is acceptable under "no waivers." Claude (§6 item 2) and GPT (§7) both raise this as open.

### D5. Whether ground testing can substitute for microgravity

- **Claude** says inverted 1 g tests with high-cohesion simulants cannot reproduce the absence of a preferred body-force direction. In Claude's view they are not sufficient for steady-state storage and wall build-up. Claude anticipates others will disagree.
- **GPT** uses vacuum, thermal and charging ground tests "combined with reduced-gravity trials where necessary." GPT also cautions that ISS plasma, thermal conditions and residual acceleration differ from an NEA site.
- **Gemini** goes from parabolic flight directly to free-flyer.
- **What would settle it:** paired comparison of the same breadboard in inverted-1 g cohesive-simulant tests and in sustained microgravity (the flight pilot). This tests whether ground results predict flight behaviour.

### D6. Treatment of the rotating route

- **Claude** rates the rotating route separately:
  - in-module handling at 2–3, citing ~1/g arching, Coriolis effects and no data below lunar g;
  - the static-to-rotating solids interface at 2, citing no heritage.
  - Claude budgets a separate spun-module flight (P6R).
- **GPT** rates the rotating-furnace handling interfaces at 2–3, AD² 6. GPT says relevance requires matching rotation rate, radius, gradient, Coriolis, residence time and geometry, not just nominal acceleration.
- **Gemini** does not address the 0.05–0.15 g route in its assessment.
- **What would settle it:** a sustained spun-module test that includes the static-to-rotating interface (Claude P6R; GPT "representative rotating tests").

### D7. Interpretation of the duty cycle

- **Claude** applies 70% plant availability to get a design rate of 2.4–9 t/h. Claude treats the robots' 9–25% duty cycle as making buffering mandatory.
- **GPT** says the robot duty cycle "is not necessarily the plant duty cycle." If it were imposed on the plant, gross flow would be 1.9–19 kg/s.
- **What would settle it:** a defined plant availability and buffer-capacity specification. GPT's feed-definition step includes this.

### D8. Whether simulation can promote TRL

- **Gemini** gives DEM first, reaching TRL 3.
- **Claude** places DEM after tests and pairs it with P3 to reach TRL 5.
- **GPT** says there is "no independent promotion from simulation alone."
- **What would settle it:** a panel convention on whether model-only results count toward TRL exit criteria.

### D9. Lifetime definition

- **Claude** requires ≥5-year wear life, shown by accelerated vacuum wear testing plus in-flight trend data. Claude notes others may prefer to carry wear as cost/schedule risk with replaceable liners.
- **GPT** distinguishes five-year plant service with replaceable modules from five-year life of every wear surface. GPT warns that accelerated tests must preserve wear mechanisms.
- **Gemini** folds wear into the flight demonstration.
- **What would settle it:** a KDP-0B specification of which lifetime requirement applies. Claude (§6 item 6) and GPT (§7) both call for this.

### D10. Confidence

- Gemini states **high** confidence while citing one evidence entry, and that entry depends on an arXiv ID of the kind the other two declined to use.
- Claude states **low** and GPT **medium**, with 9 and 5 evidence items respectively.
- The record preserves this spread without adjustment.

### Acceptance measurements

The following are common to Claude and GPT. Gemini names upset recovery, wear and containment in general terms.

- **Mass-balance closure:** input, concentrate, tailings, retained inventory and escaped mass.
- **Containment:** verification against the allocated share of the ≤1 t/yr budget.
- **Upset recovery:** deliberate bridging, plugging, stall, oversize and power-loss tests, with autonomous recovery.
- **Comminution, where required:** product PSD, liberation, energy, fines generation and throughput (GPT).
- **Wear:** measured wear laws with bounded extrapolation.
