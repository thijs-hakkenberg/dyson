## 2. Where the models agree

1. **Target.** All three require TRL 6 at KDP-0B.
2. **Risk class.** All three say failure is project-ending for 0B, and all three end the fallback chain at "0A only, Earth supply".
3. **Terrestrial heritage.** All three say terrestrial dry magnetic separation is mature but does not carry its TRL into microgravity. Claude and GPT tie this to gravity-assisted feeding and discharge. Gemini ties it to tailings clearing.
4. **No microgravity test exists.** None of the three cites a demonstration of continuous magnetic beneficiation of native Fe-Ni in microgravity. Gemini and GPT both state explicitly that they cannot cite one.
5. **The hard part is grain handling.** All three place the critical difficulty in microgravity grain transport, feed presentation, discharge and containment, not in magnetic discrimination itself. Gemini's §6 concedes the "microgravity continuous flow aspect" could be TRL 2.
6. **Simulants.** All three call for representative feed or simulants covering LL, L and H material. Claude and GPT both warn that loose metal-in-sand or CI/CM simulants would overstate performance.
7. **Pathfinder.** All three make the 0A pathfinder at an NEA the step that reaches TRL 6.
8. **Breadboard scale.** All three use 10–100 kg/h for the 1 g lab breadboard.
9. **Throughput.** Claude and Gemini both give about 1.7–6.3 t/h continuous for 15–55 kt/yr. GPT gives 2.5–13.8 t/h, and Claude gives 3.4–12.6 t/h, once availability is accounted for. The two figures differ only by the availability assumption.
10. **Mass balance (Claude and GPT only).**
    - The 15–55 / 2–8 kt/yr / 4–15:1 ranges close only as paired combinations.
    - Both land near 7:1: Claude derives ~7:1 from the architecture's paired cases; GPT's reference balance gives 7.5:1.
    - Ratio alone is an insufficient specification. Grade, recovery and liberation must be specified with it.
    - LL-like feed (~2 wt% metal) may fail to deliver enough product even at a high ratio.
    - On M-type feed, the requirement inverts (Claude gives ~1.2–2:1; GPT gives ≤~1.25:1 at 80 wt%).
    - The 3:1 falsifier is not universal.
11. **Debris ceiling (Claude and GPT only).** Both convert the ≤1 t/yr escape ceiling into a loss fraction: Claude gives ~2–7×10⁻⁵ of feed; GPT gives 21–77 ppm of the tailings stream. The denominators differ but the figures are consistent.

## 3. Where the models diverge, and why

### D1. Current TRL: 2–3 (Claude, GPT) versus 3–4 (Gemini)

- **Gemini's basis:** 1 g proof of concept, plus a possible vacuum/1 g breadboard. Its single evidence item ("Oder et al. 1990s", "General NASA ISRU studies") is marked `confident: false`. No breadboard report is cited. Gemini's own §6 also suggests TRL 2 for the continuous-flow aspect, which sits in tension with its 3–4.
- **Claude and GPT's basis:**
  - The TRL 3 exit criterion requires documented predictions of grade and recovery that have then been validated on representative feed. Neither can cite such a document.
  - Microgravity feed presentation is a critical function with only physics-level evidence (Claude citing Murdoch 2013).
- **What would settle it:**
  - A citable report of a breadboard separator run on representative chondritic feed with realistic metal attachment, in vacuum, that measures grade and recovery against stated predictions.
  - If such a report exists, Gemini's upper bound of 4 has support. If not, the evidence before the panel supports at most 3.
  - A panel ruling on Claude's proposal to split T05 into a "magnetic separation" element and a "microgravity handling" element would also resolve much of this. GPT's boundary table makes a similar separation.

### D2. AD²: 5 (Gemini) versus 6 (Claude, GPT)

- **Gemini:** the path is clear because the magnetic physics is understood.
- **Claude and GPT:** several feed-presentation, comminution and containment approaches compete, and none has been tested. Claude's list includes mechanical, centrifugal, vibratory and rotating-drum feed, plus an optional pre-concentration stage.
- **What would settle it:** a down-select among feed-presentation approaches, backed by the 1 g breadboard and reduced-gravity results (path steps C3 and C5 below).

### D3. Years to target: 3–5 (Gemini) versus 6–10 or 6–12 (GPT, Claude)

The gap comes from path content, not from different evidence.

**Whether parabolic flight reaches TRL 5:**
- Gemini credits it with TRL 5.
- Claude says 5–20 s campaigns "cannot demonstrate continuous separation". Claude adds a long-duration ISS or free-flyer experiment (P7: $15–60M, 3–5 yr) and a vacuum life test of ≥2,000 h.
- GPT credits reduced-gravity tests with TRL 5 "only with documented scaling requirements". GPT adds a 2,000–5,000 h scaled-module endurance campaign.

**Pathfinder duration:**
- Gemini: 2–3 yr.
- Claude: 4–7 yr, including development.
- GPT: 3–6 yr.

**What would settle it:**
- A panel ruling on whether short-duration reduced-gravity tests with documented scaling requirements satisfy TRL 5 for a continuous-flow function.
- Evidence on whether separator flow reaches steady state within a 5–20 s window would inform that ruling.

- **What would settle it:** a DEM model of the separator, calibrated against breadboards at two or more scales, showing whether performance extrapolates. Both Claude and GPT propose this step. A panel scale-up criterion is also needed.

### D5. Risk qualifier and the 3:1 falsifier

- **Gemini** applies "<3:1 breaks co-location" unconditionally.
- **Claude** limits it to chondritic feed and reclassifies the risk as architecture-change for H or M-type targets.
- **GPT** says a ratio below 3:1 "is not a universal physical falsifier", and limits project-ending to the native-metal 0B option.
- **What would settle it:** an architecture decision on whether the requirement is stated as a ratio or as grade ≥ X at recovery ≥ Y on sampled feed. Claude and GPT both recommend the latter. Sampled grade of the selected target, through the D-02 target gate, then determines which branch applies.

### D6. Effect of the downstream route on T05 difficulty

- **Claude** rates T05 TRL the same across routes. It calls rotating smelting the "easiest on T05" because the furnace tolerates ~30–50% grade, and gives AD² 5 for that pairing. Claude states that its concentrate-grade figures are assumptions needing confirmation from the metallurgy assessments.
- **GPT** rates rotating smelting at AD² 7, the hardest route, because it adds molten containment, heat transfer, phase separation and tapping.
- **Why they differ:** the two models draw the boundary differently. Claude rates T05 alone and treats the furnace as a separate technology; GPT rates the combined route.
- **What would settle it:**
  - A panel ruling on the T05 boundary.
  - Concentrate specifications from the downstream metallurgy assessments.

### D7. H₂ reduction route

- **Claude** lists "magnetic → carbonyl / H₂" as one no-melt route without further comment.
- **GPT** argues that H₂ reduction applies to reducible compounds, not to already-native metal. It rates that route at TRL 2 until the feed phases are specified.
- **What would settle it:** a phase specification of the concentrate entering the H₂ step, from the downstream assessment.
