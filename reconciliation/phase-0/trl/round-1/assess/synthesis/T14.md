## 2. Where the models agree

- **Lower bound and integrated range.** All rate the integrated subsystem at TRL 2 minimum, and all ranges include 2–3.
- **Target.** All set TRL 6 at KDP-0B.
- **Evidence gap.** None cites any demonstration of:
  - bulk (≥ tonne-scale) iodine storage;
  - iodine feed at 7–18 kg/h with representative endurance;
  - multi-year orbital cryogenic storage;
  - in-space transfer of iodine or argon at relevant scale.
- **Refuelling is the least mature function.** All three rate transfer at or near the bottom of their ranges.
- **Iodine scaling is not a simple sizing exercise.** Claude, Gemini and GPT all name the same issues: heat delivery through solid, recondensation or deposition in lines, and depletion geometry.
- **Flight evidence is needed for TRL 6.** Claude and GPT both propose a 1–5 t orbital demonstrator. Both caveat that it earns TRL 6 only if scaling laws are validated. Gemini's path has no flight step.
- **Down-select before scale-up.** Claude and Gemini tie difficulty to the unresolved D-05 propellant choice. GPT says unselected propellant prevents assigning TRL 4 to the integrated subsystem.

## 3. Where the models diverge, and why

**A. What "145–720 t" means.** This is the largest driver of difference.
- Claude treats it as **lifetime throughput**. It estimates (its own derivation, not evidence) ~20–100 t per fill, with one refuel per round trip.
- GPT treats it as **potentially maximum onboard inventory**. GPT notes that if it is cumulative, storage becomes easier but transfer and feed-life requirements remain.
- Gemini treats it as **storage** throughout, e.g. "100,000× larger".
- **What settles it:** a requirement statement from the architecture owner giving maximum onboard inventory and transfer batch size. This is Claude's P1 and GPT's first step. It is a requirements action, not a test.

**B. Iodine bulk storage/feed: 2–3 (Claude) vs 3–4 (GPT).**
- Both rely on the same flight evidence (Rafalskyi et al. 2021).
- GPT allows 3–4 for a *modular* feed unit, on the argument that demonstrated heat-transfer distances may be preserved.
- Claude rates the reference solid-sublimation design without that modular credit, and adds unsure ground evidence (E3).
- **What settles it:** a full-flow-rate (7–18 kg/h) iodine breadboard with documented agreement with prediction, tested in both modular and contiguous-charge configurations. This corresponds to Claude P2, Gemini step 1 and GPT's integrated breadboard.

**C. Cryogenic argon: 3–4 / AD² 4 (Claude) vs 2–3 / AD² 7 (GPT); Gemini 3–4.**
- Claude and Gemini credit cryogenic analogues:
  - Claude cites LOX transfer on Starship IFT-3 and NASA GRC zero-boil-off work (Plachta et al.).
  - Gemini cites NASA CFM, eCryo and Tipping Point, without specific citations.
- GPT gives no analogue credit for orbital acquisition, cooling and transfer. Its only large-scale argon evidence is terrestrial (ProtoDUNE). GPT judges that terrestrial storage at mass scale does not establish an orbital subsystem.
- **What settles it:**
  1. Verify the cited analogue sources: IFT-3 transferred quantity, specific GRC papers, and the eCryo/Tipping Point results.
  2. Run an argon-specific zero-boil-off tank with cryocooler, acquisition and feed in thermal vacuum (Claude P5).
  3. Run microgravity acquisition/transfer tests (Claude P7).

**D. High-pressure argon upper bound.**
- Claude and GPT agree at 3–4.
- Claude explicitly rejects a 5–6 rating based on Starlink fleet heritage, because per-vehicle tank size is not public.
- **What settles it:** public or obtained Starlink V2-Mini tank capacity and feed-life data, plus a 1–5 t COPV module qualification (Claude P6).

**E. Risk class.**
- Claude and Gemini classify it as architecture-change.
- GPT classifies it as project-ending for 0B if no route provides cargo service at acceptable mass, reliability and cost.
- Claude acknowledges the same mechanism: failure could push delivered $/kg past the D-02 economics gate.
- The classifications differ in practice: under D-02, project-ending makes TRL 6 mandatory with no waiver. All three nonetheless target TRL 6.
- **What settles it:** delivered-cost analysis of each fallback against the D-02 economics gate, and a panel ruling on classification.

**F. Fallback viability.**
- Claude's fallback uses krypton (or argon) high-pressure modules.
- Gemini proposes HP krypton as its fallback, but also states that reverting to xenon or krypton "destroys the economic model". It gives no source for this.
- GPT says smaller tanks or tugs are not automatically equivalent substitutes and need a new delivered-cost analysis.
- **What settles it:** the same delivered-cost analysis as in E.

**G. Schedule (4–7 vs 5–10 vs 7–12 years).**
- The ranges reflect different path content:
  - Gemini's path stops at TRL 5.
  - GPT adds a 100–200 t ground prototype.
- They also reflect different KDP-0B assumptions: Claude assumes 2032–35, and the others leave it unspecified.
- **What settles it:** the KDP-0B date from the phase brief, and agreement on whether a large ground prototype is required (see path item 8).

**H. Feed-rate sizing.**
- Claude derives a 5–28 kg/h hardware sizing band, using its own arithmetic with an assumed η ≈ 0.6.
- Gemini and GPT use the stated 7–18 kg/h.
