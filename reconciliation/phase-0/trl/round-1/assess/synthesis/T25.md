## 2. Where the models agree

- **Constituent functions are mature; the integrated capability is not.** All three say relative-navigation sensors, autonomous docking and small-body or L1 operations have flight heritage. All three say the integrated function at 150–300 t payload scale has never been demonstrated. All three reject transferring heritage TRL 9 to T25 and name this as the main counter-argument.
- **The gap is physical, not software.** Contact dynamics, high inertia, and interaction with large flexible arrays are the drivers. GPT adds that some navigation functions scale only weakly with mass.
- **No heritage autonomous capture comes near the required mass.** All three cite the ~20 t ATV-class vehicle as the largest relevant autonomous docker.
- **The path has the same shape in all three:**
  1. analysis and 6-DOF simulation;
  2. hardware-in-the-loop (HIL) or air-bearing/robotic contact testing;
  3. flight demonstration at reduced scale.

  The 6-DOF simulation and HIL steps have broadly overlapping cost bands.
- **Fallback:** all three name smaller cargo units (~10–30 t containers or parcels) as a fallback. Claude and GPT also name dedicated handling craft and stand-off or alternative transfer architectures.
- **Definition gaps:** Claude and GPT both state that node structure, orbit and traffic are undefined. Both treat this as blocking the TRL 4 exit (Claude) or a sound TRL 5 scaling assessment (GPT).

## 3. Where they diverge, and why

### 3.1 Current TRL: 3–4 (Gemini) versus 2–3 (Claude, GPT)

- **Gemini's position.** It sets the lower bound at TRL 3 because "analytical proof-of-concept for the required mass scale … exists in studies". No study is named.
- **Claude's position.** No project document gives analytical or experimental results validating the key parameters at stack scale (capture envelope, contact loads, abort Δv, array–controller interaction, debris collision probability). It explicitly excludes the rq-0-17 and rq-0-19 trade discussions as evidence.
- **GPT's position.** It reaches the same conclusion: none of the supplied consensus statements or logistics simulations provides the architecture-specific TRL 3 mapping.
- **What would settle it.** Produce and review the studies Gemini refers to, or an architecture-specific evidence dossier mapping heritage results to these parameters. GPT's step 1 and Claude's steps 2–3 are designed to create that dossier.

### 3.2 KDP-0B target: TRL 5 (Claude) versus TRL 6 (Gemini, GPT)

- **Claude** reads KDP-0B as the tug design and interface freeze, with an assumed date of ~2031–2034. It places TRL 6 at tug CDR. This follows from its architecture-change classification under D-02.
- **GPT** reads KDP-0B as the gate before authorising dependent production, which in its reading requires TRL 6.
- **Gemini** derives TRL 6 from its project-ending classification.
- **What would settle it.** This is a definitional and panel question, not an evidence question. It needs:
  - a ruling on what KDP-0B gates;
  - a KDP-0B date, which none of the models had;
  - a ruling on the risk class (§3.3).
