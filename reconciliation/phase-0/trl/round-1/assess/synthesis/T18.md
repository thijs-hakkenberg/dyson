## 2. Where the models agree

**Rating and difficulty**
- **The prior TRL 5–6 rating does not transfer to T18.** Claude and GPT say this explicitly. Gemini does not discuss the prior rating, but its own 3–4 rating is consistent with it.
- **TRL 4 is not met, for the same core reason.** No integrated breadboard has documented lab performance combining subsurface access, sample handling and assay. Claude and GPT also say the relevant environment is not yet documented.
- **The integrated rating is limited by its weakest function.** Claude and GPT say it should not be raised by averaging flight-proven subsystems.
- **AD² is 6.** All three cite several competing access approaches with no clear path between them: drilling, mole or percussive, pneumatic, impactor excavation, excavator or enclosed preparation.

**Consequences**
- **Risk is project-ending.** Without sampled grade, the D-02 target gate cannot pass. Claude and GPT add that D-02 allows no waivers.
- **Phase need is 0A,** because the scouts must produce the gate evidence.

**Heritage and open questions**
- **Shared heritage anchors:**
  - OSIRIS-REx TAG penetrated about 50 cm, but without controlled depth sampling.
  - The Hayabusa2 SCI crater gave access to subsurface material, but not stratified sampling.
  - Lunar drilling reached 1 m or more under partial gravity, and that reaction heritage does not transfer to NEAs.
- **Whether ground testing can reach TRL 6 is open** (Claude §6.1; GPT §6.2). Claude leans strict but rates its step E at TRL 6. GPT says ground TRL 6 "is an assessment finding to earn, not assume."
- **All three treat the ≥1 m depth requirement as contestable,** though in different directions (see D6).
- **Correction to the rq-0-39 review (Claude and GPT).** Both reject the review's claim that no subsurface material has been sampled below about 1 cm. Both cite TAG penetration and SCI-associated collection as counter-evidence.

### D2. Scope: bulk grade vs recoverable grade

**Positions**
- **Claude:** T18 measures bulk Fe-Ni, the Fe⁰/Fe-total ratio and a liberation proxy. The recovery curve belongs to the beneficiation technology. A mini magnetic-separation test narrows the gap but does not close it.
- **GPT:** T18 must produce a *recoverable* Fe-Ni yield that includes processing losses. This must be done separately for each metallurgy route. GPT preserves three routes:
  - Claude's magnetic beneficiation leading to carbonyl or H₂ processing;
  - Gemini's rotating smelting at 0.05–0.15 g;
  - GPT's narrow native-metal production.

  GPT rates route-conditioned grade prediction within T18 at TRL 2–3.
- **Gemini:** names bulk assay techniques (XRF, XRD, LIBS) but says the economic case rests on recoverable grade. It does not say where the conversion sits.

**What would settle it:** an architecture or panel decision on which technology entry owns the recovery curve. This is a definitional question, not an evidence question.

### D6. Is ≥1 m the right depth?

**Positions**
- **Claude: ≥1 m is a floor.** Its own mining-volume arithmetic says early feed comes from the top 1–3 m. It recommends at least one profile of 2 m or more per target. It also states that GRS/neutron sensing and radar cannot substitute: radar gives structure only, citing CONSERT.
- **GPT: 1 m is useful but "not a scientifically universal representative depth."** Sampling should extend if planned excavation is deeper or if layering is found.
- **Gemini:** "the most contestable part of this rating" is whether a 1 m drill is strictly required. It suggests the drill could be bypassed if kinetic impactors suffice, or if radar tomography shows the target is homogeneous.

**What would settle it**
- The planned mining depth and excavation plan from the architecture.
- Subsurface layering and property data: Hera/Juventas radar and gravimetry, Tianwen-2, and first-scout remote data (Claude step D).
- An analysis of whether radar can establish compositional homogeneity, as opposed to structural homogeneity. Claude and Gemini disagree on this point.

### D7. Fallback

**Positions**
- **Claude:** the pathfinder lands a scaled excavator, digs a 1–3 m test pit and beneficiates tonne-scale feed. This is an architecture change costing +2–4 years. Claude calls impactor excavation plus surface sampling plus GRNS a weaker fallback that would "likely fail the ±25% specification." The final fallback is 0A only, with Earth supply.
- **Gemini:** use kinetic impactors to expose subsurface material for **remote spectral observation**. Gemini itself says this "severely degrades bulk grade accuracy."
- **GPT:** 0A research plus Earth supply; withhold 0B. GPT says "remote spectra are not an acceptable substitute for sampled recoverable grade."

**The conflict:** Gemini's fallback depends on remote observation, which Claude (citing rq-0-50) and GPT reject as gate evidence.

**What would settle it:** a quantitative error budget showing whether remote assay of impactor-exposed material can meet whatever gate accuracy D5 adopts.

### D8. Target-class and grade figures (raised by Claude, preserved by GPT)

- **GPT:** keeps the 5% and about 20% recoverable-grade positions as distinct gate hypotheses, not a consensus.

**What would settle it:** resolution under rq-0-50, using meteorite-class composition data (Claude step A; GPT's 2–20 wt% envelope).
