## 2. Where the models agree

1. **The TRL 2 floor is met.** All three state that the concept and application are documented and the basic principles are established.

2. **No sustained partial-gravity slag/metal data exist at any scale.**
   - Claude calls this "the binding gap".
   - Gemini: "no experimental data for tonnes-scale molten metal and slag behavior in a 0.05–0.15 g rotating frame".
   - GPT "cannot confidently identify" even a kilogram-scale demonstration. GPT notes this is not a claim that none exists.

3. **ISS-EML is real but in the wrong regime.**
   - Its samples are gram-scale, in microgravity, with no slag. It cannot support TRL 3–4 for partial-g separation.
   - Mass ratio to requirement: ~10⁶–10⁷ (Claude, GPT) or ~10⁵–10⁶ (Gemini).

4. **D'Angelo 2021 (powder additive manufacturing) is not applicable.**
   - Claude and GPT discount it.
   - Gemini lists it but states it is "not applicable to bulk liquid melt/slag separation".

5. **Target TRL 6 at KDP-0B** if the melt route is selected. Claude and GPT state explicitly that D-02 allows no waivers.

6. **Risk class is architecture-change.** Failure pushes 0B to the no-melt route (magnetic beneficiation with carbonyl or H₂ reduction). The final fallback is D-02's "0A only, Earth supply".

7. **Flight is required before TRL 6.**
   - All three paths include an orbital partial-g step.
   - Claude adds that if the 0A pathfinder does not carry a partial-g melt experiment, the melt route cannot be baselined at KDP-0B. Claude says this should be decided at KDP-0A.

8. **Refractory life and spun/despun transfer are open, untested interfaces.** All three name them.

9. **The March scope is narrowed.** Claude and GPT state that Si zone refining, thin-film deposition and oxide-derived Al/Si are out of scope for this entry. Gemini is silent on this.

10. **The March gate ("slag separation >95 %") is inadequate.** Claude and GPT both want it replaced with product-driven metrics. Both list the same metric families:
    - metal recovery;
    - inclusions;
    - S and P in metal;
    - refractory life or heat count;
    - comparison against no-melt delivered cost.

11. **Raising gravity to 0.3–0.5 g is a mitigation, not a qualified fallback.** Claude and GPT say this explicitly. Gemini lists it as a fallback but "with severe mass penalties".

## 3. Where the models diverge, and why

### D1. Upper bound of current TRL: 3 (Claude) versus 2 (Gemini, GPT)

**Claude's position.**
- Argument for TRL 3: the critical function (density-driven settling) is characterised at 1 g by terrestrial practice (E1) and at g > 1 by super-gravity work (E3). Stokes and Bond-number scaling is validated physics, and the bulk-pool Bond number at 0.05 g is high.
- Claude also argues against its own TRL 3: coalescence, entrainment and Coriolis-dominated tapping are unmeasured below 1 g. A 1 g extrapolation "runs in the non-conservative direction".
- Note that the E3 citation Claude relies on is marked unsure by Claude itself.

**Gemini's position.** No analytical models have been validated by experiment, and no proof-of-concept test exists, so TRL 3 is unmet.

**GPT's position.** TRL 3 is unmet. GPT would accept TRL 3 "upon seeing an applicable validation report — not merely equations or an unvalidated CFD result".

**What would settle it:** documented experimental results that validate predicted separation time, metal recovery and entrainment against g, slag viscosity and metal grain size, for asteroid-representative melts. Claude's P2 and GPT's ground critical-function campaign are this test.
- If the panel decides that 1 g plus hypergravity data with validated g-scaling counts as TRL 3 validation, those results would close TRL 3 on the ground.
- If it does not count, TRL 3 requires sub-1 g data.
- This is a rule interpretation the panel must make. A secondary input is verifying the USTB super-gravity literature (see §4).

### D2. AD²: 6 (Claude) versus 7 (Gemini, GPT)

**Claude (6).** The physics path is clear and no new science is required. Several architectural approaches compete, though:
- spun arm, whole-vehicle spin or tether;
- refractory lining or cold-wall skull;
- continuous feed or batch transfer.

**Gemini (7).** It is a regime with "almost no existing data". Gemini expects the transition from surface-tension-dominated to gravity-influenced behaviour under strong Coriolis forces to be fundamentally new.

**GPT (7).** Fundamental engineering research is needed on coupled multiphase flow, scaling, transfer and refractory service. AD² 8–9 is not justified.

**Source of divergence.** The models disagree on whether conventional metallurgy extrapolates reliably (GPT's framing of the 6-vs-7 question). This partly depends on D3.

### D3. Which physics dominates at 0.05 g

This is a direct contradiction.

**Gemini.** At 0.05 g, "surface tension and Coriolis forces … will dominate fluid dynamics", and reduced buoyancy "must compete with surface tension for slag separation".

**Claude.** Gemini's surface-tension claim is contested on the following basis:
- Capillary length at 0.05 g is ~2 cm, assuming σ ≈ 1 N/m.
- A ~0.5 m pool therefore has a Bond number of several hundred.
- "Bulk stratification is not the problem; the pool will layer."
- The open questions are kinetics, coalescence and entrainment, with 100 µm droplets needing ~6 h at 0.05 g under stated assumptions.
- Claude agrees that Coriolis dominates tapping and launder flows (30–90 % of g_eff for a 1 m/s stream).
- Claude also says the Rossby number of ~0.03 contradicts the rq-0-11 claim that convection gives thermal homogeneity.

**GPT.** 0.05 g is "a requirement to test, not a demonstrated threshold". GPT proposes a Coriolis screening limit: flow below ~0.16 m/s at 10 m and 0.10 g to keep the Coriolis ratio under 10 %.

**What would settle it:**
- First, the parameters Claude's estimates rest on: measured slag/metal interfacial tension and slag viscosity for the candidate fluxed melts, and metal grain-size distributions in the target feed. These are Claude's P1 inputs; Claude flags viscosity as uncertain by a factor of ~50.
- Second, rotating-frame analogue tests.
- Ultimately, the sustained partial-g flight experiment.

All three positions rest on analysis, not data. Claude says this explicitly.

### D4. Rotation envelope

**Gemini.** States 5–20 m at 1.5–4 rpm.

**Claude and GPT.** Both independently compute that this envelope spans ~0.013–0.36 g. GPT adds:
- 5 m cannot reach 0.15 g within 4 rpm (5.18 rpm is needed);
- 4 rpm at 5 m gives ~0.089 g.

**What each proposes instead.**
- Claude proposes r ≥ ~8 m (reference 10–20 m). It notes a 20 % gravity gradient across a 1 m pool at 5 m, and free-surface sag of ~0.1 m on a 2 m tangential hearth at 5 m.
- GPT proposes enforced radius–rpm pairs and h/r ≤ 0.1.

**What would settle it.** This is a requirements-closure item, not an evidence item. Gemini would need to restate the envelope as paired values or accept a lower g ceiling at 5 m, as Claude notes.

### D5. Scale needed for TRL 6, and therefore years and cost

| TRL 6 demonstration | 10–100 kg orbital pathfinder | 0.1–1 t flight prototype at r ≥ 10 m, ≥50–100 heats over ≥1 yr | ≥1 t, then selected size up to 10 t; ~100-cycle planning target |

**Claude.** Tapping, refractory and transfer behaviour "does not scale down cleanly", so kg-scale flight plus models is insufficient.

**GPT.** One tonne does not automatically qualify a ten-tonne module. GPT requires either a demonstration at the selected batch size or a validated scaling case.

**Rapporteur cross-check of Gemini's own numbers.**
- Gemini's three path steps sum to 6–10 years if run sequentially, against its stated 5–8 years.
- Its pathfinder step is labelled "TRL 5/6" in the prose but 6 in the YAML.

**What would settle it.** A panel decision on the TRL 6 scale-representativeness rule, informed by a validated scaling case. That case would come from the P6 and multiphysics validation work in Claude's and GPT's paths.

### D6. Validity of ground analogues and of CFD as a TRL step

**Gemini.**
- CFD alone reaches TRL 3.
- A terrestrial centrifuge with 1–10 kg low-melting analogues (e.g. Ga/water, Sn/salt) validates "fractional-g separation" and reaches TRL 4.

**GPT.**
- A ground centrifuge "cannot reproduce net 0.05–0.15 g … Earth's gravity remains".
- Electromagnetic levitation is not a substitute for the body force acting on both phases.
- Unvalidated CFD earns no promotion.

**Claude.**
- Uses a rotating table with room-temperature fluids, dimensionally matched on settling time, Rossby, Ekman and Bond numbers.
- Credits this to TRL 4 for the fluid-dynamics element only.
- Uses hot hypergravity tests (1–20 g) to fit g-scaling.

**What would settle it.** A panel ruling on what counts as relevant-environment evidence. A pre-stated scaling-rule document would also help; Claude asks that the scaling rules from P1–P3 be stated before flight.

### D7. Molten-material transfer across the spun/despun interface

- **GPT.** Baseline is sealed solid feed and cooled product, with matched-speed docking or cartridge exchange. "Do not assume routine molten transfer."
- **Claude.** Lists continuous feed versus batch transfer at the hub as an open architecture choice. Notes that radial mass movement (~4,000 t·m² for 10 t at 20 m) requires momentum and balance compensation.
- **Gemini.** Lists spun/despun transfer as a pathfinder validation item without specifying a concept.

**What would settle it.** An architecture decision, followed by a ground transfer rig (Claude's P4 and GPT's integrated breadboard).

### D8. Refractory approach

- **Claude.** Raises specific vacuum and slag failure modes:
  - MgO-C degradation in vacuum;
  - FeO-rich slag attack on oxide linings;
  - no crewed or robotic relining;
  - a cold-wall skull alternative that moves the load into heat rejection.
- **GPT.** Lists the attack modes to demonstrate: chemical attack, wetting, infiltration, thermal fatigue and spallation.
- **Gemini.** Names refractory life only as a pathfinder item.

There is no conflict here, only a difference in depth. Settled by the ≥100-heat ground life test (Claude P4) and the flight life campaign.

### D9. Minor divergences

- **Feed scope.**
  - Claude: Fe-Ni concentrate, "possibly whole-rock chondritic ore with flux".
  - GPT: native or reduced Fe-Ni; melting is not assumed to reduce oxides.
  - The panel should fix the feed definition in requirements closure.
- **Alternative-route ratings.**
  - Only GPT rates the alternatives: all at TRL 2–2, with comparator AD² of 6 (no-melt) and 5–6 (native-metal).
  - Claude defers the no-melt route to rq-0-56.
  - Gemini omits rq-0-56.
- **Confidence.** Gemini's "high" contrasts with its own statement that almost no data exist. Claude and GPT both say medium.
