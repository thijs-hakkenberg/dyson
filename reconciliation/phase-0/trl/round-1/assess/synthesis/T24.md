## 2. Where the models agree

- **Target and need.** TRL 6 is required before KDP-0B. The phase need is 0B.
- **The combination is not demonstrated.** All three say no heritage covers the combination of hard vacuum, charged abrasive regolith and 5-year life.
- **Mars heritage does not transfer.** All three discount it because of the atmosphere, gravity and dust character.
  - Claude cites Opportunity.
  - Gemini cites Curiosity and Perseverance.
  - GPT cites Curiosity.
  - All three anticipate an upward challenge based on Mars heritage and reject it.
- **Apollo shows the hazard.** Apollo is treated as short-duration evidence of the dust hazard, not as qualification.
- **ISS SARJ shows clean-vacuum rotary heritage only.** It is evidence for rotary mechanisms in vacuum without dust.
- **Hayabusa2 shows only brief NEA contact.** All three treat it as evidence of short-duration mechanism contact with NEA material, not endurance.
- **Operating-hour requirement.** Claude (about 4,000–11,000 h) and GPT (3,945–10,958 h) independently derive the same figure for mining mechanisms at 9–25% duty. Gemini does not compute it.
- **Fallback.** All three include short-life, robotically swappable modules or cartridges, and ultimately "0A only, Earth supply".
- **RQ register.** None assigns related research-question IDs.

## 3. Where they diverge and why

### 3.1 Current TRL: 4–5 (Gemini) vs 3–4 (GPT) vs 2–4 (Claude)

The models rely on different evidence.

**Gemini** bases TRL 4 on NASA KSC/GRC component-level dusty thermal-vacuum (TVAC) testing with lunar simulants (JSC-1A, NU-LHT). Its YAML marks this evidence confident, but it cites no specific report; the source field reads "~2010s-2020s". Gemini's upper bound of 5 rests on subsystem validation "partially achieved for near-term lunar applications", again with no citation.

**Claude** refers to the same body of work (KSC/GRC connector and seal tests, RASSOR) but marks it **unsure** and says it cannot cite specific endurance reports. Claude also holds TRL 4 unmet on a second ground: there is no documented relevant-environment definition. In particular, no validated metal-bearing NEA simulant exists, and no charging or excavation-dust data exist. Claude states that if KSC/GRC reports show vacuum endurance of 10³ h or more, the excavation and connector elements might move to TRL 4.

**GPT** says it has not identified such a report with enough confidence to cite. It explicitly notes that absence from its assessment is not proof that none exist. It allows TRL 4 only where a specific component has a documented lab-vs-prediction record and an environment definition.

The models also disagree on simulant relevance:
- Gemini accepts lunar simulants.
- GPT says "lunar is conservative" needs experimental support.
- Claude says no NEA simulant exists to define the environment.

**What would settle it:**
- Retrieve and review the actual KSC/GRC dust-tolerant mechanism, seal and connector test reports. The review should record vacuum level, simulant, charging, load, duration and whether results were compared against predictions.
- Produce a documented NEA relevant-environment specification, as in Claude's path step 1 and GPT's path step 1.
- Run a comparative lunar-vs-NEA simulant test to answer whether lunar dust is a conservative surrogate.

- Claude treats it as a lubrication-breakdown failure.
- GPT treats it as heritage that shows the importance of maintainability, with crew intervention that would be incompatible with an unattended site.

### 3.3 AD²: 5 vs 6

- **Gemini** treats the path as clear: accelerated life testing in dusty TVAC.
- **Claude and GPT** cite several competing approaches with no clear winner: labyrinth or shroud plus electrodynamic dust shield (EDS), bellows or magnetic coupling, non-contact designs, and replaceable cartridges.
- **Claude only** adds that magnetic couplings and ferrofluid seals would attract free Fe-Ni fines.

**What would settle it:** component screening results comparing the competing seal approaches in metal-bearing simulant (Claude step 3, GPT breadboard step).

### 3.4 Risk class: architecture-change (Claude) vs project-ending (Gemini, GPT)

The disagreement turns on whether a fallback to swappable modules stays within the project.
- **Claude** treats it as an architecture change and gives an MTBR escalation threshold.
- **GPT** states that a lower maintenance interval "is an architecture change — not evidence that the original five-year requirement was met." GPT nevertheless classes failure as project-ending for the specified architecture.
- **Gemini** argues that seizure halts operations and destroys the economic case.

**What would settle it:** a sensitivity analysis of delivered $/kg and availability against mechanism MTBR and spares logistics. Claude's 6–12 month threshold is an unvalidated estimate and could be tested by this analysis. The final classification remains a board judgement.

### 3.5 Years to target: 2–4 vs 5–9 / 5–8

The difference comes from path scope and from assumptions about life testing.

**Gemini's path has two ground steps.** It assumes accelerated wear profiles can simulate 5 years within 2–3 years.

**Claude's path is broader:**
- It adds environment specification, a simulant and facility build, milligravity tests, and a flight pathfinder with at least 1 year of operations at an NEA.
- Its arithmetic gives 0.7–1.9 years of continuous testing per article at 100% duty and a 1.5× life factor.
- It states that calendar-driven degradation and milligravity transport cannot be accelerated by duty compression.

**GPT's path is ground-based** but allows 4–6 years for integrated endurance with parallel long-duration articles. GPT cautions that raising dust concentration, speed or temperature can change the failure mechanism, so a validated acceleration model is needed.

**What would settle it:**
- A validated acceleration model showing failure-mode equivalence between accelerated and nominal conditions. Claude step 4 and GPT's life-test caution both call for this.
- A board decision on whether TRL 6 requires flight correlation. Claude asks the KDP-0B board to accept or reject explicitly a basis of ground testing plus flight correlation over at least 20% of life.

### 3.6 Milligravity dust behaviour

- **Claude** calls it genuinely uncertain whether milligravity makes abrasion milder or worse, citing cohesion, electrostatics, ejecta and possible Fe-Ni galling.
- **GPT** says Earth-gravity ingress must not be assumed conservative.
- **Gemini** is silent.

**What would settle it:** reduced-gravity ingress tests plus an exposure package hosted on a scout (Claude step 6), or particle-transport models anchored to reduced-gravity tests (GPT).
