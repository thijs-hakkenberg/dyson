## 2. Where the models agree

1. **The chemistry is not the gap.** All three hold that H₂ reduction of iron oxides is mature terrestrially and validated at laboratory scale. The development gap lies elsewhere: gravity-independent gas–solid contact, solids handling (loading, unloading, sticking or sintering of sponge iron), water recovery, and sustained closed-loop operation on representative feed.
2. **No relevant-environment demonstration exists.**
   - Claude and GPT state explicitly that they know of no H₂ reduction reactor operated in microgravity coupled to water capture and electrolysis. Both frame this as a limit of their knowledge, not proof of absence.
   - Gemini places microgravity reactor dynamics at "conceptual or early breadboard stage".
3. **Electrolysis heritage does not transfer wholesale.** All three credit ISS water electrolysis for microgravity electrolysis and phase separation. All three deny that it covers dirty or contaminated process water, metallurgical recycle, or the required scale.
4. **The unmet TRL 4 criterion is substantively the same.** Each model requires a breadboard of the coupled process, with gravity-independent or microgravity-analog contact, whose performance agrees with prediction.
   - Claude and GPT add a second requirement: a *documented relevant-environment definition*. Claude notes this cannot be written while the feed is unsampled.
5. **Phase need is 0B, and the primary risk class is architecture-change**, in all three YAML records.
6. **Rotating or artificial-gravity contact appears as a fallback in all three.**
   - GPT notes that rotation changes the "without gravity" premise and must be assessed as a separate branch.
   - Claude cites the rotating-furnace route's mass and cost penalty from rq-0-11.
7. **Superseded evidence is set aside.**
   - Claude excludes the March 2026 melt-route entry and the rq-0-13 slag review as non-applicable. Claude also flags two statements in that review as wrong or unsupported: that slags are insulators, and the Marangoni density-separation claim.
   - GPT gives the original entry's silicon, thin-film and magnetic-electrolysis claims no credit.
   - Gemini does not address the prior entry.
8. **Contaminant tolerance of the recycle loop is a development item.**
   - Claude is most specific: H₂S from troilite poisoning PEM and SOEC electrolysers, plus HCl/HF, requiring a cleanup stage with consumables.
   - GPT lists electrolyser contaminant tolerance and purification consumables among its pre-test pass/fail criteria.
   - Gemini refers to "dusty, unpurified product water".

## 3. Where the models diverge, and why

### 3.1 Current TRL: lower bound (2 vs 3) and upper bound (3 vs 4)

| Model | Evidence relied on | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Claude, floor 3 | TGA kinetics (Pineau et al.); lunar H₂ reduction (Gibson & Knudsen; Allen, Morris & McKay) | Key parameters validated experimentally: kinetics vs T and pH₂, H₂O/H₂ equilibrium, yield vs FeO. The chemistry is "gravity-indifferent at particle scale". |
| Claude, max 4 | ROxygen/PILOT Mauna Kea integrated loop (2008, 2010) | Integrated reactor, water capture and electrolysis, with performance broadly as predicted. Claude nonetheless judges the TRL 4 exit unmet for a gravity-independent contactor. |
| Gemini, 3–4 | "Laboratory breadboards of lunar regolith H₂ reduction" (NASA RESOLVE / ISRU analog testing) | Treats lunar breadboards as TRL 4 for lunar partial gravity. |
| GPT, 2–3 | HYBRIT; Allen, Morris & McKay; ISS OGA. Does **not** cite the Mauna Kea tests. | TRL 2 is defensible on documented concept. TRL 3 requires a documented mapping of analogue tests to the selected NEA mineral fraction, which the supplied material does not establish. |

**What drives the split:**
- *Upper bound.* Whether lunar or terrestrial integrated-loop analogs (Mauna Kea, "RESOLVE") count toward TRL 4 for an NEA microgravity subsystem.
- *Lower bound.* Whether generic-feed chemistry validation suffices for TRL 3 without target-feed mapping.

**What would settle it:**
- Retrieve the ROxygen and PILOT test reports: demonstrated throughput, duration, and predicted-vs-measured performance. Claude marks their scale as unsure; neither other model cites them.
- Identify the specific source behind Gemini's RESOLVE attribution (see §4).
- Run representative-feed reduction tests on meteorite concentrates and CI/CM simulants (Claude P2; GPT step 1). This addresses GPT's TRL 3 condition.

### 3.2 AD²: 6 (Claude, GPT) vs 7 (Gemini)

- **Claude and GPT** see several credible contactor candidates whose integrated performance is unproven. Their candidates are compacted packed beds, screw/auger reactors, porous retainers, and centrifugal, rotating or vibration-assisted beds. On this view the difficulty is engineering.
- **Gemini** views continuous high-throughput gas–solid contact without phase settling as fundamental multiphase-flow research. It cites the unpredictability of dust entrainment and channelling in zero-g.
- **What would settle it:** reduced-gravity contact-discrimination tests (Claude P4, GPT step 3, Gemini step 1) and an orientation-independent breadboard (Claude P3). The question is whether at least one candidate contactor behaves predictably. Claude and GPT both name contactor down-selection as the trigger for AD² 5.

### 3.3 Years to TRL 6: 4–7 (Gemini) vs 6–10 (Claude, GPT)

- Gemini's estimate is shorter despite its higher AD². It rests on a three-step path with no sustained microgravity breadboard and no ground scaling or endurance pilot.
- Observation: Gemini's own step durations (1–2, 2–3 and 3–5 yr) total 6–10 yr if run sequentially. Its 4–7 yr estimate therefore implies overlap that it does not state.
- GPT states that its activities overlap and should not be summed. Claude states its result is gated by flight opportunity.
- **What would settle it:** a scheduled, integrated plan. The deciding questions are whether a ground scaling pilot and a sustained microgravity breadboard are required before TRL 6 (Claude and GPT say yes), and when flight access is available.

- **Gemini:** its §3 text says "project-ending … for Claude's no-melt architecture", while its §5 and YAML say architecture-change. This is unreconciled.

**What would settle it:** target sampling, which establishes the native-metal fraction and the reducible fraction (D-02).
