## 2. Where the models agree

- **Risk class.** All three rate this architecture-change, not project-ending. Claude and GPT each name conditions under which it would escalate.
- **Phase need.** All three place the need at 0B and link it to rq-0-60.
- **No integrated evidence exists.** All three say nothing has demonstrated the full combination: kV, vacuum, charged abrasive regolith, thermal cycling and active reeling together.
- **TSS-1R is the key negative precedent.** All three cite it as kV tether operation in space that failed by arcing at an insulation defect.
- **Microgravity reeling under load is unproven.** All three note that heritage from free-space tethers does not cover reel-in or drag across a surface.
- **Fallback.** All three include battery robots with docking or centralised charging.
- **Return path.** Claude and GPT both reject regolith as a return conductor and assume a two-conductor (or, for Claude, coaxial) circuit. Gemini is silent.
- **Mobile and fixed are different objects.**
  - Gemini and GPT both say static or fixed cable results do not qualify a moving tether.
  - Claude makes a parallel split between per-robot tethers and the site trunk.
- **Multipaction.**
  - Claude and GPT both say it is not the governing mechanism for a DC cable and is relevant mainly at converter or RF interfaces.
  - Claude calls it "irrelevant" to the tether itself. GPT says it should still be assessed for converter ripple.
  - Gemini does not address it.
- **Path structure.** All three have an integrated thermal-vacuum, dust and reeling test as the key late step.

## 3. Where the models diverge, and why

### 3.1 Current TRL: 2–3 (Claude, GPT) versus 3–4 (Gemini)

- **What Gemini relies on.** Its upper bound rests on "NASA GRC Artemis FSP/VSAT studies (~2020–present)", described as 1–3 kV cable and dust-tolerant connector testing in dusty vacuum chambers. Gemini marks this `confident: true`.
  - Its prose says "tens of kW" and "Power ~1:10".
  - Its YAML records the demonstrated scale only as "1–3 kV, static".
- **What Claude relies on.** Claude cites FSP Phase 1, Watts on the Moon and LunaGrid. It states "no flown kV surface cable I know of" and gives LunaGrid low confidence. It treats KSC dust-tolerant connector work as low confidence and low voltage.
- **What GPT relies on.** GPT explicitly declines to credit any Artemis or NASA surface-power test without a test report.
- **Exit criteria differ too.**
  - Claude and GPT both require a documented relevant-environment definition for TRL 4, and both say none exists.
  - Gemini's unmet criterion sits one level higher, at TRL 5 (integrated assembly in a relevant environment).
- **What would settle it:**
  - Retrieve the GRC FSP/VSAT test reports. The key facts are voltage, current or power, dust type and charging, vacuum level, thermal cycling, duration, and whether connectors were mated or demated during the test.
  - Determine whether a documented NEA relevant-environment specification is a precondition for TRL 4. This is Claude's step 1 and GPT's audit step.

### 3.2 Per-circuit power: 5–50 kW (Claude) versus 20–300 kW (GPT) and "tens to hundreds" (Gemini)

- **Claude's basis.** Its own estimate of 1–10 kWh/t excavation specific energy in microgravity at 0.5–5 t/h, which Claude labels "not evidence". Claude attributes hundreds of kW only to shared feeders.
- **GPT's basis.** 20–300 kW is an assumed envelope. GPT notes the upper instantaneous fleet demand (up to 6 MW) is incompatible with a 2.5 MW site. It also says duty cycle is not a coincidence factor.
- **Why it matters.** Power sets current, conductor mass, connector rating and test scale.
  - Claude's integrated test is 30–100 kW.
  - GPT's lab breadboard runs up to 300 kW, and its ground demonstration is 1 km at 300 kW.
  - Claude also states that if peaks reach about 100 kW, the battery fallback degrades sharply.
- **What would settle it:**
  - A robot load model based on measured or simulant-validated cutting and anchoring energy in NEA-like (including cohesive C-type) material.
  - A site coincident-load budget. This is Claude's step 2 and GPT's step 1.

### 3.3 Is 1–3 kV a requirement?

- **Claude.** Voltage should be a trade output. Below about 100–200 m, 400–600 V is competitive and "removes most of the dielectric risk". Claude lists short 400–600 V tethers as its first fallback.
- **GPT.** "A lower voltage is not a free fallback": it raises current, conductor mass and losses. GPT illustrates this with a 1 km copper loop example.
- **Gemini.** Treats 1–3 kV as given.
- **What would settle it:** Cable-length requirements from site layout and anchoring studies, followed by the voltage, mass and loss trade. Both Claude and GPT propose this trade.

### 3.4 Target at KDP-0B: TRL 5 (Claude) versus TRL 6 (Gemini, GPT)

- **Claude.** Argues D-02's strict TRL 6 rule does not automatically apply to an architecture-change risk. Claude acknowledges that strict readers would demand TRL 6, which would put the flight element on the 0A critical path.
- **GPT.** TRL 6 with no waiver if the subsystem remains essential to production.
- **Gemini.** TRL 6.
- **What would settle it:** This is a programme interpretation of D-02, not a technical finding. It needs a ruling on whether the tethered mining fleet counts as mission-critical at KDP-0B.

### 3.5 Is a flight element needed for TRL 6?

- **Claude.** Required, because NEA gravity and long-duration charging "cannot be simulated on the ground". Proposed as a 10–50 m kV tether flown as a rider, $20–80 M.
- **GPT.** "TRL 6 does not inherently require flight", but a ground-only claim needs convincing validation of low-gravity reel, anchor and contact dynamics. GPT's path includes a 100–300 m, 1–3 kV, 20–50 kW low-gravity demonstrator, $25–75 M.
- **Gemini.** Reaches TRL 6 with ground TVAC endurance testing after parabolic or drop-tower reeling tests. This drives most of the cost gap (about $8–18 M versus about $30–110 M).
- **What would settle it:**
  - Whether parabolic, drop-tower and air-bearing tests can validate the tether, robot and regolith dynamics models across the relevant gravity range and timescales. This is Claude's step 6 and the subject of Gemini's step 2.
  - Whether long-duration charging can be reproduced in a chamber.

### 3.6 AD²: 5 (Claude, GPT) versus 6 (Gemini)

- **Gemini.** Several competing approaches exist and the path is not clear. It cites handling stiff, heavily insulated cables without tangling, snagging or abrasion, and dust ingress into connectors.
- **Claude.** The parts are conventional and the path is clear but "has not been walked". Claude names three sub-problems without precedent:
  - kV mate/demate in vacuum with charged dust;
  - tether management at about 10⁻⁵ g;
  - flaw-tolerant insulation over 10⁴ thermal cycles plus solar energetic particle (SEP) internal charging.
- **GPT.** 5, with 6 credible if target mechanical properties and the anchoring regime are too poorly known to choose a handling architecture.
- **What would settle it:** Characterising target regolith and anchoring mechanics, plus a validated tether-handling dynamics model that shows whether a single handling architecture can be selected.

### 3.7 Dominant failure mode

- **Gemini.** Insulation abrasion and dust ingress into HV connectors.
- **Claude.** Explicitly ranks abrasion below four other modes, on the grounds that low normal force reduces wear (while allowing that charged-dust adhesion could reverse this):
  1. tether dynamics at about 10⁻⁵ g;
  2. kV connector contamination;
  3. arcing at a flaw (the TSS-1R mode);
  4. Paschen breakdown from local volatiles at C-type targets.
- **GPT.** Lists arcing, multipaction (converter only), reeling, connectors and fault isolation without ranking them. It adds fault energy from cable capacitance, DC-link energy and battery backfeed.
- **What would settle it:**
  - A vacuum abrasion campaign with charged simulant, including seeded flaws and flashover with adhered dust. This is Claude's step 3, GPT's step 2 and Gemini's step 1.
  - A microgravity dynamics study (Claude's step 6).

### 3.8 Fallback severity

- **Claude.** Battery-only robots need 80–150 kWh or 1–3 dock charges per day. Claude estimates a 10–40 % lower effective excavation rate.
- **Gemini.** Duty cycle falls from 9–25 % to below 5 %, or robot mass and fleet size rise sharply.
- **GPT.** Gives no number. Every alternative needs renewed mass, thermal, productivity and delivered-cost assessment. GPT adds two further options: fixed or shielded distribution with short leads, and relocatable power nodes.
- **What would settle it:** A battery and docking productivity trade built on the robot load model in §3.2.

### 3.9 Smaller discrepancies

- **TSS-1R electrical values.** These are reported differently:
  - Claude: "~3.5 kV open-circuit, ~1 A", medium confidence on exact values.
  - GPT: voltage "exceeded 3.5 kV" and current "exceeded 1 A", with the caution that these were not simultaneous continuous delivered power.
  - Gemini: "up to 3.5 kV".
  - The Mission Failure Investigation Board Final Report (1996), which GPT cites, would settle this.
- **Scale-ratio convention.** Claude gives demonstrated-to-required ratios ("voltage ≈1×"), while GPT gives required ÷ demonstrated ("0.29–0.86"). The underlying positions are consistent, but the numbers should not be compared directly.

## 6. Open items that would change the consolidated rating

1. **GRC FSP/VSAT test reports.** These determine whether a TRL 3–4 range has support or whether 2–3 stands.
2. **NEA relevant-environment specification.** Two of the three models treat this as a TRL 4 exit criterion.
3. **Robot load model.** This sets per-circuit power (5–50 kW versus up to 300 kW), test scale and fallback viability.
4. **Programme ruling on D-02.** This decides whether the KDP-0B target is TRL 5 or TRL 6.
5. **Ground-only low-gravity validation.** If ground tests prove sufficient, the flight element can be removed; this is the largest cost driver.
6. **Coupon and dynamics results.** These order the dominant failure modes: abrasion (Gemini) versus dynamics, connectors and flaw arcing (Claude).
