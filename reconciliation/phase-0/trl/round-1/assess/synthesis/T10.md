## 2. Where the models agree

1. **Phase need and target.** T10 is needed for 0B, and the target is **TRL 6 at KDP-0B**. Claude and GPT state explicitly that no waiver applies. Both also set TRL 4–5 at the 0A milestones.
2. **Integrated AD² is 6.** All three describe several competing approaches and an unclear integrated path. Claude and GPT add that no new physics is needed.
3. **Flown components do not transfer to the integrated capability.** All three hold that the heritage cited (DS1, OSIRIS-REx/Hayabusa2, Mars rovers, terrestrial autonomous haulage) does not demonstrate the combined function. That function is productive contact operations plus process control with fault recovery, multi-agent, in microgravity, for 90 days.
   - Each anticipates a "component-heritage" counter-argument for a higher rating and rejects it.
   - GPT adds that the component ratings themselves "can be correct for those components."
4. **No qualifying demonstration is known.** Claude (§1d) and GPT (§1) both state they know of no demonstration of the combined requirement. GPT frames this as an evidence limitation, not proof of absence.
5. **Requirements and environment definition comes first.** Claude and GPT both open the path with an analysis step: fault catalogue, outage or conjunction duration, and a measurable definition of "intervention" or "unattended". Both cost it at $1–3M over 0.5–1 yr.
6. **Conjunction duration is unspecified.** Claude and GPT both flag it as a missing input that any test must cover.
7. **Fast local reactions must be onboard.** Claude calls this the "reflex tier". GPT calls it "local protective control", noting it is needed "even in a ground-assisted fallback".
8. **A pathfinder at an NEA is needed for TRL 6.** All three place TRL 6 at an in-space NEA demonstration.
   - Gemini states this in prose ("reaches TRL 6/7"), but its YAML path omits the pathfinder step.
   - Claude and GPT both doubt that ground tests alone can close the contact or anchoring physics.
9. **Blind fault injection.** Claude (red team) and GPT (independently selected faults) both require faults the autonomy has not been scripted for.
10. **Inherited rq-0-8 numbers are not used.** Claude and GPT both set aside the older fleet assumptions. GPT also declines to credit the statement that rq-0-26 "validated" the bucket-wheel system without its test report.

## 3. Where the models diverge, and why

### 3.1 Current integrated TRL: Claude 2–3 versus Gemini and GPT 3–4

| Model | Lower-bound basis | Upper-bound basis | Unmet criterion stated |
|---|---|---|---|
| Claude | TRL 2 is met by the concept documents (rq-0-42, rq-0-8, round-2 architecture). | TRL 3 is "arguable": critical functions are proven in isolation (RAX, ASE, 1 g excavation, TAG), but T10-specific parameters are not experimentally validated. | **TRL 4**: no documented relevant-environment definition, and no lab-tested integrated or breadboard stack |
| Gemini | TRL 3: "analytical and experimental proof of concept… in isolation" | TRL 4: no basis given | Stated for **TRL 5**: integrated validation in a relevant environment. This implies TRL 4 is treated as met, but no integrated lab test is cited. |
| GPT | TRL 3: transferable critical functions (planning, diagnosis, navigation, execution) | TRL 4 is "defensible only if" an integrated lab report with predicted-versus-measured results exists. It was not supplied. | **TRL 4**: integrated breadboard agreeing with predictions |

**Why they differ.** The evidence base is broadly shared. The difference is how much credit isolated critical-function demonstrations earn.
- Claude withholds a clean TRL 3 until T10-specific parameters are measured. Its example is void or boulder detection latency within one bucket revolution in a cohesive low-gravity analogue.
- GPT grants TRL 3 and leaves TRL 4 conditional.
- Gemini's upper bound of 4 is not tied to a cited test. Gemini's stated unmet criterion sits one level above its own range.

**What would settle it:**
- A documented integrated or breadboard lab test, with predicted-versus-measured performance. GPT names this explicitly. Its absence would cap all three at 3.
- The rq-0-26 bucket-wheel test report, if it exists.
- Measured T10-specific parameters, for example Claude's detection-latency and plant-FDIR fault-coverage examples.

### 3.2 Meaning of "no required ground intervention"

This interacts with the rating, so it is reported separately.

| Model | Reading |
|---|---|
| Claude (A1) | The site stays safe and throughput stays at or above an agreed fraction of plan. Autonomous isolation or abandonment of failed units is allowed. |
| GPT | Telemetry is allowed. No command, diagnosis, patch or decision from Earth may be necessary. Waiting in safe mode for Earth does **not** count as successful recovery. |
| Gemini | "Completely unattended", "zero-maintenance, zero-intervention" |

**Noted tension.** Claude states that under a strict zero-intervention reading it would lower the integrated rating to **2** and raise AD² to **7**. Gemini adopts what reads as the strict interpretation, yet rates **3–4 with AD² 6**. The rapporteur does not resolve this.

**What would settle it:** a ruling by the requirement owner (the GPT gate) on the definition. Claude calls this "the single largest driver of difficulty". GPT notes a safe-stop reading is "substantially easier" than its own.

### 3.3 Risk class: architecture-change (Claude) versus project-ending (Gemini, GPT)

- **Claude** treats the fallback as a priced, degraded mode that still functions. The fallback is supervised autonomy outside conjunction, pre-planned production pauses with warm safe states, and an optional relay off the Sun–Earth line. Claude concedes this "depends on the economics gate tolerating the fallback, which nobody has yet shown".
- **GPT** classes the same kind of ground-assisted operation as an architecture change that requires a formal change to the requirement, so it does not comply with the present gate. GPT therefore rates the risk project-ending under the requirement as written.
- **Gemini** holds that continuous ground-in-the-loop control is "impossible due to light-time delays and conjunction outages, destroying the throughput model".
  - Claude disagrees on feasibility: the tactical tier "could be ground-assisted" at 2–50 min round-trip.
  - GPT allows ground-assisted operation as a fallback, provided local protective control is retained.

Claude and GPT partly differ on labelling, not substance: both see a ground-assisted mode as an architecture change. Gemini differs on whether such a mode is feasible at all.

**What would settle it:**
- A conjunction-duration analysis for each candidate NEA (Claude, path step 1).
- An economics-gate run pricing the supervised-and-pause fallback in delivered $/kg.
- A requirement-owner ruling on whether a ground-assisted mode is an acceptable 0B architecture.

### 3.5 Route-specific ratings

Gemini does not rate routes. Claude and GPT agree on the no-melt and native-metal routes:

| Route | Claude | GPT |
|---|---|---|
| No-melt | TRL 2–3; AD² 5–6 (H₂), 6 (carbonyl) | TRL 2–3; AD² 6, possibly 7 |
| Native-metal | TRL 2–3; AD² 5–6 | TRL 2–3; AD² 5–6 |

They differ on the **rotating furnace**:
- **Claude:** TRL 2, AD² 7. Reason: "autonomy cannot exceed the process TRL", and the furnace itself is not demonstrated.
- **GPT:** TRL 2–3, AD² 6–7. The higher value applies "until melt/rotor fault recovery is physically established".

Claude anticipates a Gemini-aligned objection, that furnace autonomy is no harder once the furnace exists. Claude calls this "fair as a conditional".

**What would settle it:**
- The furnace's own TRL. Claude gates furnace autonomy on the furnace reaching TRL 4.
- Route-specific FDIR breadboard results.

### 3.6 Whether simulation or ground tests can supply the relevant environment

- **Claude** cites NPR 7123.1 software-TRL descriptions as possibly allowing simulation at TRL 5–6, and marks itself "moderately confident" of the wording. Claude rejects simulation for the contact dynamics, because no simulator is validated at 10⁻⁶–10⁻⁴ g. Claude would accept ground TRL 6 for the tactical and strategic tiers if scout in-situ data calibrate the simulator.
- **GPT** would not accept gravity-offloaded tests plus analysis for TRL 6 without evidence closing granular contact, anchoring and process-gravity coupling. GPT adds that a scout with a "token sampler" is not a substitute for the pathfinder.
- **Gemini** uses a software-in-the-loop simulation to reach TRL 4 and a vacuum chamber to reach TRL 5. It does not discuss the limits of simulation.

**What would settle it:**
- In-situ regolith-mechanics measurements from scouts (Claude, path step 7).
- A check of the current NPR 7123.1 software-TRL wording.
