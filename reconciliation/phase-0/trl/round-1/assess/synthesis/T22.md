## 2. Where the assessments agree

- **TRL 2 is met.** The concept and its application are documented. No assessor puts the lower bound below 2.
- **The target is TRL 6 at KDP-0B.** Claude and GPT add that no waiver is allowed.
- **Nothing integrated has been demonstrated.** No system consolidates, packages and qualifies representative asteroid-derived Fe-Ni in vacuum or reduced gravity at any meaningful scale.
- **Space heritage is gram-scale.** The levitators, Metal3D and the sample-return containers are all orders of magnitude below the requirement. The size of the gap is quantified differently (see §4).
- **Casting at 0.05–0.15 g is the hardest route.** All three hold it at TRL 2–3 or lower. Claude and GPT give it AD² 6–7. No assessor in this round rated partial-g casting higher. Claude had anticipated that Gemini might, but Gemini's assessment rates it 2–3.
- **Phase 1 acceptance is the unresolved driver** (Claude and GPT).
  - Claude points out that "dust-tight consolidated form" and "Phase 1-accepted" may conflict if Phase 1 wants powder.
  - GPT points out that "a dust-tight pellet is not necessarily an accepted product."
  - Both make a signed specification the first path step.
- **The ultimate fallback is the D-02 pre-committed "0A only, Earth supply"** (Claude and GPT).
- **Some legacy evidence is rejected** (Claude and GPT). The "Nature Chemistry 2025 magnetic electrolysis" citation is not credited. The rq-0-11 deliberation's assertions are not treated as TRL evidence. Claude also drops the UMG-Si 4N–5N claim as out of scope.

## 3. Where they diverge and why

### 3.1 Current TRL: 2–2 vs 2–3 vs 2–4

**What is in dispute.** Two questions, linked:
- Where the technology boundary is drawn.
- Whether evidence from outside the architecture counts toward TRL 3.

**Each position and its basis.**
- **Claude** gives the no-melt route TRL 3. The basis is terrestrial powder-metallurgy and HBI densification data (E1, E2). Claude withholds TRL 4 because terrestrial feed is unrepresentative: residual S, P and C, and carbonyl residues.
- **Gemini** gives TRL 3. The basis is gram-scale microgravity sintering (German et al.) and powder additive-manufacturing work (D'Angelo 2021). Both citations are marked confident by Gemini, but Claude marks both unsure and GPT declines to credit D'Angelo (see §4).
- **GPT** gives TRL 2. GPT accepts that the individual elements (sintering, containers) are mature, but states that nothing validates the *architecture-specific chain* on representative feed. GPT notes that an assessor rating a single function, such as sample containment, could reasonably give that function TRL 3.

**What would settle it.**
- A panel ruling on the T22 boundary: individual functions or the integrated chain.
- Documented results from representative-feed experiments. These are Claude step 2 and GPT step 2: 1–100 kg of carbonyl, H₂-sponge and native-metal concentrate containing troilite, P, Co and attached silicate.
- For Gemini's TRL 3 basis specifically: verification of what German et al. and D'Angelo et al. actually demonstrated.

### 3.2 AD²: 5 vs 6

**What is in dispute.** Whether the competing consolidation options are an unresolved fork in the path.

**Each position and its basis.**
- **Claude (5).** The options (press, briquette, sinter, nickel vapour deposition, canister) are fallbacks for one another, not a fork. The path is clear provided a no-melt or native route stays open. Claude would move to 6–7 if the programme converges on casting.
- **Gemini and GPT (6).** There are several competing approaches with no down-select, the product definition is open, and the gravity environment is contested. GPT says AD² could drop to 5 if Phase 1 accepts compacted native-metal stock.

**What would settle it.** A signed Phase 1 product specification and a route down-select. Both assessors' positions converge depending on which way those go.

### 3.3 Risk class

**What is in dispute.** Whether lot qualification and acceptance belong inside T22.

**Each position and its basis.**
- **Claude and GPT.** Acceptance has no fallback within the architecture. If no product is accepted, the D-02 demand and economics gates fail. That makes T22 project-ending for 0B.
- **Gemini.** Rates T22 architecture-change, with sealed bladders as the fallback. However, Gemini's own §3 calls packaging and qualification "project-ending for Phase 0B". The conflict is unresolved inside that assessment.
- **Claude's own caveat.** Claude flags the bundling as contestable. If acceptance is moved under the D-02 demand gate, T22 would become architecture-change with a TRL 5 target.

**What would settle it.**
- A panel boundary decision on whether acceptance sits in T22 or the D-02 demand gate.
- Gemini to reconcile its §3 and §5.

### 3.4 Schedule: 4–7 yr and "2030" vs 6–10 yr

**What is in dispute.** How long the path takes and when KDP-0B falls.

**Each position and its basis.**
- **Gemini** gives 4–7 years and a 2030 need date. No source is given for the date, and Claude and GPT both state that KDP-0B is undated in the decisions provided.
- Gemini's path is also shorter: three steps, ending in a 10–100 kg pathfinder.
- **Claude and GPT** include steps Gemini omits: the specification, representative feed, a qualification instrument suite, a packaging campaign and a scaling closure.

**What would settle it.** A programme-dated KDP-0B, and agreement on which path steps are mandatory.

### 3.5 Is a 10–100 kg flight demonstration sufficient for TRL 6?

**What is in dispute.** What flight scale, and what supporting evidence, qualifies for TRL 6.

**Each position and its basis.**
- **Gemini.** A 10–100 kg pathfinder reaches TRL 6.
- **Claude.** Requires a flight of 0.1–1 t of product, at least 6 months of operation, and a Phase 1 witness test. Claude names whether ground thermal-vacuum testing alone could justify TRL 6 for solid-state consolidation as "the main contestable call".
- **GPT.** "A 10 kg orbital experiment is not automatically TRL 6." GPT requires a separate scaling and endurance closure on a representative production module (0.1–1 t batches).

**What would settle it.** A documented scaling argument showing that the demonstrator's geometry, residence times, interfaces and failure modes represent the production module. Also, a panel position on whether solid-state pressing and sintering are gravity-insensitive once the die is filled. Reduced-gravity die-fill and powder-feed tests (all three paths include these) bear directly on that question.

### 4.3 Strongest evidence

- **For containment heritage and its failure mode:** OSIRIS-REx and Hayabusa2. These are flight-proven in a real NEA environment and corroborated by two assessors for OSIRIS-REx.
- **For terrestrial consolidation maturity:** HBI and carbonyl powder production (Claude) and the ASM Vol. 7 compilation (GPT). These carry the most weight on process physics, but all assessors agree they say nothing about feed, environment or autonomy.
- **For gravity effects on metal processing:** EML and Metal3D. These are corroborated, but gram-scale and on crewed hosts.
- **The weakest load-bearing items** are German et al. and D'Angelo. They are Gemini's main basis for TRL 3, and both are flagged by other assessors. They should be verified before any rating relies on them.

### 4.4 Absences noted

Claude and GPT both report that none of the following exists:
- Consolidation of representative asteroid Fe-Ni at any scale.
- Bulk powder handling at 0–0.15 g for longer than parabolic-flight durations.
- A Phase 1 product specification.
- Autonomous in-space acceptance for interstitials (O, C, S, N) or for mechanical properties.

Gemini notes that no bulk casting has been demonstrated in space.
