## 2. Where the models agree

- **No flight evidence exists at the required scale.** No cited demonstration shows autonomous capture, transfer and securement of a 150–300 t package. All three treat existing docking and robotics as heritage for *constituent functions* only.
- **Target:** TRL 6 at KDP-0B.
- **Risk class:** architecture-change. All three give the same fallback direction: split the payload into smaller standardized packages near autonomous-docking heritage.
  - Gemini proposes ~20 t modules.
  - Claude proposes 10–30 t containers on a tug truss.
  - GPT proposes "smaller sealed packages" within a broader hierarchy.
- **Shared evidence:** all three cite ESA ATV (~20 t; ratio ~7.5–15×) and DARPA Orbital Express.
- **Path:** all three start with contact-dynamics/GNC simulation (→ TRL 3), then a ground breadboard or hardware-in-the-loop / air-bearing campaign (→ TRL 4), then relevant-environment thermal-vacuum testing of full-size interfaces (→ TRL 5).

**Agreed by Claude and GPT (Gemini does not address):**

- **Background figures are wrong.**
  - Transit acceleration of 0.01–0.1 m/s² is inconsistent with the reference propulsion. Claude derives ~40–150 N thrust; GPT derives ~40–180 N.
  - Moving from 24 h to 48 h transfer does not halve fleet capacity on 1.5–5 yr round trips. Claude puts transfer time at <1% of a round trip; GPT puts the added time at ~0.05–0.18%.
  - The legacy "10+ cycles in 15 years" figure needs reconciling.
- **Dynamics depend on more than payload mass.** Tug mass, inertia, flexible modes and receiver boundary conditions matter. Both say to use the reduced mass when two vehicles are free-flying.
- **Loose or uncontained product is a separate, lower-rated branch.**
  - Claude rates it TRL 1–2 and recommends excluding it.
  - GPT rates it TRL 2 and says sealed-container demonstrations must not be credited to it.
  - Both tie it to the proposed ≤1 t/yr escaped-material licensing ceiling.
- **Handling ratings depend on the metallurgy route.** Both say T12 is not a metallurgy rating. Product form follows the route, and the plant-to-container step is harder for granular or loose product.

## 3. Where they diverge and why

### 3.1 Current TRL: 2–3 (Claude, GPT) vs 3–4 (Gemini)

- **Gemini** reasons that contact dynamics, GNC and manipulation are flight-proven at smaller scale. It says the specific application "has only been analytically modeled." It places the unmet criterion at TRL 4 (breadboard validation).
- **Claude and GPT** find no documented, architecture-specific analysis or experiment that validates key-parameter predictions. They therefore place the unmet criterion at TRL 3.
  - Claude adds that TRL 2 is met only "loosely," because transfer architectures (a), (b) and (c) are unselected.
  - GPT allows a "scoped" TRL 3 for selected critical functions by analogy to heritage. It favours TRL 2 for gate decisions.

**What would settle it:**
- Produce the analytical model Gemini refers to, and check whether it was correlated to physical test or flight data. If no such document exists, the TRL 3 exit is unmet on all three models' stated criteria.
- Alternatively, complete the shared first path step: contact-dynamics simulation validated against APAS/Shuttle and ATV flight data.

### 3.2 AD²: 5 vs 6

The difference comes from scope, not from conflicting evidence:

- **Claude** scores AD² 6 because the architecture is unselected and payload form depends on the unresolved metallurgy route. It drops to 4–5 if the architecture is constrained to rigid containers.
- **GPT** scores 5 for sealed cargo and 6 for loose or rotating-interface transfer.
- **Gemini** scores 5, treating the work as scaling NDS/berthing mechanisms and SSRMS-class robotics. It notes critics might argue for 7 (non-contact capture).

**What would settle it:** the architecture and ConOps trade. This covers vehicle-integrated vs berthing vs dedicated craft, monolithic vs modular packages, and stationary buffer vs direct rotating transfer. Claude's step 2 and GPT's envelope-freeze step both include it. Once scope is fixed, the AD² positions converge on the corresponding branch.

### 3.3 Is contact energy novel?

- **Claude** assumes tug mass of 30–100 t, giving a reduced mass of ~25–75 t and contact energy of ~11–375 J at 0.03–0.10 m/s. It compares this with Shuttle–ISS docking (μ ≈ 85–90 t, ~40 J). It concludes contact energy is inside crewed heritage, and the novelty lies in autonomy, flexible-array post-capture dynamics, payload form and plant-side transfer.
- **GPT** tabulates 7.5–1,500 J using payload mass against a much heavier receiver. It derives arresting forces up to ~7.5–15 kN over a 0.1 m stroke and says peak loads can be higher. It does not conclude the loads are within heritage.
- **Gemini** frames the kinetic energy and structural loads of a 300 t mass as the central difficulty. It notes the possibility that non-contact capture would be needed.

**What would settle it:**
- Specify tug mass and receiver boundary conditions. Claude's 30–100 t is flagged by Claude as an assumption.
- Check the Shuttle closing rate, which Claude marks "nominal figure, verify."
- Run the contact-dynamics simulation across the closing-rate and reduced-mass range.

### 3.4 Credit for crewed heritage

- **Claude** credits Shuttle docking (E4) for contact energy but not autonomy.
- **Gemini** cites Shuttle (~100 t, piloted) and SSRMS (human-operated) as context only.
- **GPT** does not cite Shuttle docking. It credits STS-117 truss handling as crew-assisted.

**What would settle it:** a panel ruling on whether crewed or human-in-the-loop demonstrations count toward sub-element TRL for an uncrewed autonomous system. This is a criteria question, not an evidence question.

### 3.5 Years to target and the cost of reaching TRL 6

- **Gemini (3–5 yr):** its path ends at TRL 5, about $7.5–16M across three steps. It includes no flight step, yet states a TRL 6 target. The 3–5 yr figure therefore does not correspond to a path that reaches its own target.
- **Claude (6–12 yr):** a 2–20 t pathfinder at $50–300M reaches TRL 6 *only if* the ground steps have validated scaling; otherwise TRL 5. Claude itself raises the possibility that a strict assessor would cap it at 5.
- **GPT (7–12 yr):** a 1–5 t pathfinder at $100–350M reaches only TRL 5. TRL 6 then requires a ~150 t production-scale demonstration at $0.8–3B. GPT allows that a 20–50 t orbital article plus full-scale ground testing *may* suffice "if earned through test-correlated scaling."

**What would settle it:** a dynamic-similarity / scaling analysis, correlated to the hardware-in-the-loop and pathfinder results, that shows whether subscale flight covers the 150–300 t envelope. Claude and GPT both name array-mode coupling and granular centre-of-mass migration as the terms that may not scale. This is the largest open cost and schedule question for T12.

### 3.6 Scope of "handling"

- **Claude** includes plant-to-container filling, transit load retention over 1.5–5 yr, and dormancy-driven mechanism degradation. It names seals, lubricants, cold welding and dust, and says cycle count is not the driver.
- **GPT** includes internal plant handling and rotating-plant interfaces. It cites ~74–441 kN effective weight if the full load is handled at 0.05–0.15 g, and treats direct rotating transfer as TRL 2.
- **Gemini** does not break the scope down.
- **Cycle life:** Gemini uses "10+ mission cycles"; Claude uses 6–20 mate/demate cycles per tug; GPT says the figure is unreconciled.

### 3.7 Does transfer duration drive the economics?

- **Gemini** justifies the TRL 6 target partly by "turnaround time."
- **Claude and GPT** compute transfer time as a negligible fraction of the round trip.
- GPT notes exceptions: berth congestion, thermal limits or another bottleneck could still dominate.

**What would settle it:** a delivered-cost or fleet-cadence model run with the corrected transfer times.

### 3.8 Fallback details

- **Claude** proposes ground-supervised berthing at SEL1, citing ~5 s one-way delay.
- **GPT** says the fallback should not rely on real-time Earth teleoperation. It adds a stationary plant buffer, tug-integrated cargo structures, and a terminal fallback of "0A only, Earth supply."
- **Gemini** notes that its 20 t module fallback increases packaging mass fraction and complexity.
- Whether supervision (Claude) and the no-teleoperation condition (GPT) conflict depends on how much autonomy is required inside the supervised loop. This is not resolved in the inputs.
