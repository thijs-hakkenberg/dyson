## 2. Where the models agree

- **Target and need.** All three set TRL 6 at KDP-0B, for a Phase 0B need.
- **Difficulty.** All three give AD² 6, and all three justify it by an unresolved choice among servicing approaches. Claude and Gemini describe this as several competing approaches with no clear path.
- **Risk class.** All three say project-ending. All reason that the fallback (redundancy or replace-not-repair) puts the D-02 delivered-cost gate at risk, which leads to "0A only, Earth supply".
- **Heritage is the wrong environment.**
  - All three agree that flight heritage (Orbital Express, Dextre/SPDM, Canadarm2) shows servicing of clean, cooperative, purpose-designed interfaces only.
  - None of it involves abrasive charged regolith, industrial wear parts, damaged tethers or multi-year autonomous campaigns.
- **No integrated demonstration exists.**
  - Claude and GPT both state explicitly that no demonstration combines autonomy or latency, regolith-exposed wear parts, vacuum and microgravity, and long duration.
  - Gemini gives the environment and autonomy ratio as "0:1".
- **Mass handling is not the gap.** All three treat orbital large-mass handling as covering the required 2–3.5 t mass, but not surface anchoring or recovery.
- **Terrestrial autonomous mining.** Claude and Gemini both state that its maintenance remains crewed. Claude holds this with moderate confidence.
- **Fallback.** All three give the same core fallback:
  - replace-not-repair with cartridges or modules;
  - an overprovisioned fleet that abandons failed units;
  - "0A only, Earth supply" if the economics gate fails.
  
  Claude and GPT add that abandonment is not free: GPT cites transport, inventory and debris; Claude cites spares mass.
- **A flight step is in every path.** Each path ends in an orbital or NEA demonstration as the step that reaches TRL 6.
- **Related questions.** All three list rq-0-42, rq-0-8 and rq-0-60.
- **Wear and intervention rate is the key unknown.** Claude and GPT both name it.
  - Claude gives about 10–200 interventions per year at low confidence.
  - GPT calls it undefined and declines to compute a lifetime ratio.
  - Their duty-hour figures are consistent: Claude gives 800–2,200 h per robot per year; GPT gives 3,900–32,900 h over 5–15 yr.

## 3. Where they diverge, and why

### 3a. Current TRL: 2–3 versus 3–4
- **Gemini (3–4)** relies on two things:
  - "NASA Dusty Environment Tool/Connector Testing" from the Artemis and Constellation eras, credited as TRL 4 component evidence for dust-tolerant connectors and seals.
  - Flight servicing heritage.
- **Claude (2–3)** holds that TRL 3 is unmet because none of three key parameters has been measured. Claude explicitly rejects crediting heritage, on the grounds that dust, wear, galling, autonomy, latency and duration each affect the critical function itself.
- **GPT (2–3)** treats TRL 3 as conditional on documented applicability analysis. It reserves TRL 4 for hardware representative of these industrial interfaces and failure modes.
- **Nature of the disagreement.** It is partly about evidence: Gemini's lunar connector tests are not cited by the others. It is partly about criteria: how much clean-orbit exchange counts toward TRL 3 or 4.
- **What would settle it:**
  - A specific citation for the dust-tolerant connector and seal tests Gemini relies on, with a check of whether they used charged asteroid-representative simulant, robotic insertion, and long-dwell or galling conditions.
  - Vacuum wear, abrasion and galling data with asteroid simulant (Claude path step 3), which addresses the TRL 3 criterion directly.
  - A panel ruling under the D-02 TRL definitions on how much heritage credit clean ORU exchange earns. This is a criteria question, not an evidence question.

### 3b. Handling and recovery sub-ratings
- **GPT** rates "handling/repositioning 2–3.5 t robots" and "cooperative module installation" at TRL 3–4, crediting orbital handling heritage that exceeds the required mass.
- **Claude** rates F4 (recovery at about 10⁻⁵ g) at 2 and F5 (installation) at 2–3. Claude gives weight to negative surface evidence: MER Spirit and Opportunity, Philae's anchoring failure, and OSIRIS-REx's near-zero-cohesion finding.
- **What would settle it:**
  - A full-mass gravity-offload or air-bearing handling and recovery test (Claude step 6; GPT "integrated full-scale ground demonstration").
  - A flight anchoring experiment.

### 3c. Route ratings
- **Claude** rates the rotating-smelter route lower (1–2) than the other routes (2).
- **GPT** rates all routes 2–3, while noting the furnace route has the hardest burden.
- **Gemini** gives no route breakdown. This is a gap against the architecture's per-route requirement.
- **What would settle it:**
  - A route-by-route FMECA and maintenance-demand model (Claude step 2; GPT step 1).
  - If the smelter route stays open, a refractory-relining breadboard (Claude step 9).

### 3d. Years to target: 4–7 versus 6–10 or 6–12
- **Gemini's** path has three steps; only two are costed, and the flight step has no duration.
- **Claude and GPT** include long-duration wear and logistics campaigns and treat the flight step as schedule-driving. Claude gives 3–5 yr for the flight step; GPT gives 4–7 yr including development.
- **What would settle it:**
  - The 0A pathfinder schedule.
  - A decision on whether a dedicated servicing flight experiment is required (see 3f).

### 3e. Cost of the flight step and of the total path
- **Claude** costs a pathfinder flight-experiment increment at $40–150M, "on the 0A pathfinder".
- **GPT** costs a microgravity demonstration at $150–500M, including development.
- **Gemini** leaves it uncosted.
- **What would settle it:** a scoped definition of the experiment. The key questions are whether it is a ride-along on the 0A pathfinder or standalone, and what masses and interfaces it must reproduce. GPT states that a small pathfinder does not qualify multi-tonne recovery unless it reproduces the critical loads or validates scaling.

### 3f. Can TRL 6 be reached on the ground?
- **Claude** says no for F4 and F5. Its reasons: DEM plus parabolic flight is thin, and Philae and OSIRIS-REx both behaved off-prediction. For F1 and F2, Claude says ground thermal-vacuum testing can give credible TRL 5–6.
- **GPT** says it is possible only if microgravity anchoring, dust transport and cable behaviour are shown not to invalidate ground results. GPT's own path still puts TRL 6 on the flight step.
- **Gemini** reaches TRL 6 at the pathfinder and does not discuss the question.
- **Both Claude and GPT** note that other assessors might accept ground testing.
- **What would settle it:** comparing DEM and gravity-offload predictions against flight contact data. Claude names OSIRIS-REx and Hayabusa2 data. The panel would also need to agree what "relevant environment, no waivers" requires for F4 and F5.

### 3g. Conditionality of the risk class
- **Claude** ties project-ending to its assumption A3. If wear life is ≥10⁴ h, the class drops to architecture-change or cost-increase.
- **GPT** distinguishes individual failures (cost or downtime) from failure of the fleet-level strategy (project-ending).
- **Gemini** is unconditional.
- **What would settle it:** measured wear lives (Claude step 3) fed into the sparing and delivered-cost model (Claude step 2; GPT step 5).

### 3h. KDP-0B date
- Claude assumes about 2032–2036.
- GPT gives 6–10 yr from programme authorization and expressly declines to tie it to a calendar date.
- Gemini gives none.
- D-01 and D-02 fix no date. This needs a programme input, not a technical one.
