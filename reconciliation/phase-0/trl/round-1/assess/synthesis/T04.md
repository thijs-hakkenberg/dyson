## 2. Where the models agree

1. **Current TRL is 2–3.** TRL 2 is met by all accounts. No model finds TRL 3 met for the integrated critical function.
2. **The prior basis is inadmissible.** Claude and GPT state explicitly that "multi-model consensus" validation does not satisfy TRL 3. Gemini reaches the same conclusion through its unmet-criterion statement.
3. **Target is TRL 6 at KDP-0B,** and failure is project-ending for 0B.
4. **TRL 6 effectively requires an NEA pathfinder.** All three paths end with a 0A pathfinder at the target NEA as the step reaching TRL 6. Their framing differs (see §3.6).
5. **Philae (2014) is negative evidence** for small-body anchoring, not positive heritage.
6. **OSIRIS-REx TAG** is the principal real-environment evidence. It was brief, unanchored, and not sustained excavation.
7. **Counter-rotation is not a solution in itself.**
   - Claude and GPT both state that it does not cancel translational cutting force.
   - GPT adds that it may not cancel transient torques from unequal engagement.
   - Both record the same three-way split on its status: Gemini "adopted," Claude "re-run," GPT "candidate."
8. **The fleet envelope does not close the mass balance.** Claude and GPT independently compute the fleet envelope at 0.27–25 t/h. Both note that its low corner falls short of the ~1.6–6.3 t/h fleet-average requirement.

## 3. Where they diverge, and what would settle it

### 3.1 AD²: 7 (Claude) vs 6 (Gemini, GPT)

- **Claude's case for 7.** Anchor capacity in near-cohesionless regolith at low confining stress is estimated to fall 1–3 orders of magnitude below kN-class reaction loads. The only small-body anchoring attempt (Philae) and a reaction-limited regolith tool (InSight HP3) both failed. Claude therefore argues that a new approach may be required. It allows 5–6 if targets are thin regolith over coherent rock, and 8 given TAG-type surprises.
- **GPT's case for 6.** The difficulty is "chiefly engineering rather than uncertain basic physics." GPT would accept 7 under universal-target or no-servicing requirements.
- **Gemini's case for 6.** Several competing approaches exist and the path is unclear. Gemini gives no load analysis.
- **What would settle it:**
  - requirement closure deriving reaction force and torque from cutting power and tip speed (Claude P1);
  - low-confining-stress anchor pull-out and cut-force tests in calibrated simulant (Claude P4; GPT step 1);
  - target-NEA geotechnical data (Claude P8).

### 3.2 Years to target: 5–8 (Gemini) vs 8–14 / 8–15 (Claude, GPT)

- Gemini's path has four steps, with no target-site geotechnical scout, no dedicated wear/endurance programme and no separate integrated vacuum breadboard. Its baseline date is not stated.
- Claude's estimate is bounded by pathfinder development (3–5 yr), cruise (1–3 yr) and surface operations (0.5–1 yr).
- GPT's estimate runs from funded start, and GPT notes it is not a demonstrated schedule.
- **What would settle it:** a pathfinder mission timeline with target selection and cruise duration. The panel also needs to decide whether ground endurance testing and a scout geotechnical package are prerequisites (see §3.6).

- **What would settle it:** a panel ruling on whether simulation alone can satisfy TRL 3, and whether short-duration reduced-gravity tests without an integrated anchor-plus-excavator constitute a TRL 4 "breadboard."

### 3.4 Required scale and reaction loads

- **Per-machine rate.** Claude rates against 0.8–5 t/h because 0.5 t/h does not close the tailings mass balance. GPT keeps 0.5–5 t/h but requires that feasible combinations be selected. Gemini uses 0.5–5 t/h without comment.
- **Power, which sets reaction loads.**
  - Claude records 2–15 kWe vs 40–100 kWe.
  - GPT records three hypotheses: Claude low, Gemini ~30 kW, GPT higher.
  - Gemini's own submission states no power figure.
- **What would settle it:** reconciliation of the mass balance and power by the panel (Claude P1), then measured cut force from the breadboard.

### 3.5 Target material class and simulant

- **Claude.** The likely Fe-Ni targets are S/LL/M-type, for which no NEA geotechnical data exist. Claude calls for metal-bearing ordinary-chondrite simulant.
- **Gemini.** Its tests use CI/CM simulants.
- **GPT.** Requires one sampled, qualified target envelope, not universal compatibility.
- **What would settle it:** architecture-level target selection, followed by a scout geotechnical package at that target (Claude P8).

### 3.6 TRL 6 venue

- **Claude.** Ground and LEO tests cannot react loads against a real body, so TRL 6 requires anchored excavation at the target NEA. Claude notes this is formally a TRL 7-type environment.
- **GPT.** Favours an NEA demonstration, but leaves orbital testing plus validated scaling open if boundary-condition differences are closed.
- **Gemini.** Its pathfinder reaches "TRL 6/7."
- **What would settle it:** a panel ruling on "relevant environment" for anchoring specifically. Per Claude, conceding the ground/LEO route would shorten the schedule by 3–5 years.

### 3.7 Fallback

- **Gemini** proposes thermal extraction/optical mining.
- **Claude and GPT** reject it as irrelevant to, or not like-for-like for, an Fe-Ni product, and note that it still needs intake and containment.
- **Claude and Gemini** share an enclose-and-process / boulder-capture option.
- **GPT** names only the D-02 outcome.
- **What would settle it:** confirmation from the metallurgy routes of whether any of them accepts a thermally extracted feed.

### 3.8 Containment and durability metrics

- **Containment.** Only GPT quantifies it: a 1 t/yr escape ceiling implies 99.9933–99.9982% retention. Neither other model addresses the figure.
- **Contact hours.** Claude gives ~3,900–11,000 h; GPT gives 1,971–10,950 h.
- **Arithmetic check on GPT's figure.** GPT's own formula, 5 × 8760 × 0.09, evaluates to ~3,942 h rather than 1,971 h. GPT's lower cumulative-mass figure (986 t) and its mass ratio (8.1 million) appear to derive from the 1,971 h value. This is a check of the models' stated arithmetic, not an independent technical claim, and GPT should confirm or correct it.
- **Wear-part servicing.** GPT permits replaceable wear cartridges if servicing is qualified. Claude does not address servicing. GPT notes that servicing could lower difficulty.

### 3.9 Confidence
