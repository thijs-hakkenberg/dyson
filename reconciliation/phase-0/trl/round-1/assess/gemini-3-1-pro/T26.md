### 1. Evidence

Deep-space communications is a highly mature field, though the specific requirement of a resilient, highly autonomous 30-node network (4–10 scouts, 10–18 tugs, site infrastructure) utilizing Delay/Disruption Tolerant Networking (DTN) and automated outage recovery exceeds current standard operations. 

- **Deep Space Network (DSN) & Mars Relay Network:** NASA and ESA routinely operate multiple deep-space assets. The Mars Relay Network coordinates communications between multiple orbiters and surface assets (TRL 9).
- **Delay/Disruption Tolerant Networking (DTN):** The Bundle Protocol (RFC 5050/9171) has been demonstrated in space on the EPOXI mission (Deep Impact) in 2008 simulating deep-space delays, and is operational on the ISS (TRL 7–8).
- **Authentication:** CCSDS Space Data Link Security (SDLS) protocol is standardized and flown on multiple missions (TRL 8).

While the individual components (RF/Optical links, DTN, SDLS) are TRL 7–9, an integrated, highly automated command service scaling to 30+ independent deep-space nodes with automated conjunction recovery has not been flown as a unified system, placing the integrated architecture at TRL 6.

### 2. Current TRL

**TRL 6–7.** The lower bound (6) is met because the subsystem models and protocols (DTN, SDLS, DSN scheduling) have been demonstrated in relevant space environments. The unmet criterion for TRL 8 is the actual system completion and flight qualification of the specific 30-node automated management software required for Project Dyson.

### 3. Target and difficulty

**Target:** TRL 6 by KDP-0B (and for internal 0A milestones for the scouts).
**AD²:** 3. New development well within the experience base. The protocols and hardware exist; the effort is in software integration, network management scaling, and rigorous testing of automated outage recovery.

### 4. Path

- **Step 1: Network Simulation.** Software simulation of the 30-node topology, injecting 1–25 minute light-time delays, solar conjunction outages, and node failures to validate DTN routing and command resilience. (Scale: 1:1 nodes, $1M–$2M, 1 year, reaches TRL 5).
- **Step 2: Hardware-in-the-loop Testbed.** Ground validation using flight-like transceivers and DSN emulators running the integrated command service software. (Scale: 1:1 nodes, $3M–$5M, 1.5 years, reaches TRL 6).

### 5. Risk and fallback

**Risk:** Schedule-delay and cost-increase. If the automated resilient command service fails, the project must fall back to traditional, manually scheduled point-to-point DSN contacts, drastically increasing ground operator headcount and reducing operational cadence.
**Fallback:** Traditional scheduled point-to-point DSN operations with manual command sequencing and outage recovery.

### 6. Where others might disagree

Others might argue this is TRL 8 or 9 because "it's just software over standard radios." However, managing a 30-node deep-space swarm with high autonomy and automated conjunction recovery represents a significant operational leap over the highly curated, manually scheduled operations typical of current planetary science missions.
