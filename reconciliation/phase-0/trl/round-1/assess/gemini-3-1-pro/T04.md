### 1. Evidence
The current rating is based on the following heritage and literature:
*   **Regolith properties and interaction:** OSIRIS-REx TAG at Bennu (2020) and Hayabusa2 SCI at Ryugu (2019) provided ground truth on C-type rubble pile surface mechanics, demonstrating extremely low bearing capacity (0.2–20 Pa) and fluid-like behavior under disturbance.
*   **Anchoring:** Philae's harpoon system failed on comet 67P (2014). JPL's microspine grippers (Parness et al., ~2017) have been tested in laboratory and parabolic flight environments on terrestrial rock analogs, but not integrated with high-torque excavation systems.
*   **Excavation mechanism:** The dual counter-rotating bucket-wheel concept for microgravity is supported by analytical design studies and DEM simulations (e.g., Nallapu et al., 2017, *Optimized Bucket Wheel Design for Asteroid Excavation*). No physical microgravity testing of this specific integrated system has occurred.
*   **Granular physics:** Parabolic flight experiments (e.g., D'Angelo et al., 2022; Murdoch et al., 2013) have demonstrated basic microgravity granular mechanics, but not sustained excavation.

### 2. Current TRL
**TRL 2–3.** The basic principles of the environment are known (TRL 1), and the application (dual bucket-wheel with microspine/harpoon anchoring) has been formulated and analyzed in literature (TRL 2). While some sub-components (microspines, granular physics) have experimental data (TRL 3–4), the integrated excavation and torque-canceling critical function lacks experimental proof-of-concept. The exit criterion for TRL 3 (experimental results validating predictions of key parameters for the integrated mechanism) is unmet.

### 3. Target and Difficulty
**Target:** TRL 6 by KDP-0B. This is a project-ending technology for Phase 0B; if the robots cannot anchor and excavate, the in-space production architecture fails.
**AD²:** 6. Several competing approaches exist for anchoring (microspines, harpoons, gecko) and excavation (bucket wheel, pneumatic, thermal), and the path to reliable operation in microgravity charged regolith is not clear.

### 4. Path
1.  **DEM Simulation & Analysis:** Re-run dual-wheel torque cancellation and particle containment simulations using OSIRIS-REx ground-truth cohesive parameters. (Reaches TRL 3).
2.  **Parabolic Flight Testing:** 1/3-scale dual bucket-wheel and anchoring prototypes tested in aircraft parabolas with CI/CM simulants to validate torque cancellation and containment. (Reaches TRL 4).
3.  **ISS External Platform / LEO Free-flyer:** Long-duration testing of the mechanism in vacuum/microgravity on charged simulants to validate wear, containment, and anchoring over hours/days. (Reaches TRL 5).
4.  **Phase 0A Pathfinder:** In-situ demonstration on a target NEA to validate 0.5–5 t/h rates and anchoring stability. (Reaches TRL 6/7).

### 5. Risk and Fallback
**Risk:** Project-ending for Phase 0B.
**Fallback:** Switch to thermal extraction / optical mining (heating regolith in a bag to release volatiles/spallation), which avoids mechanical reaction forces entirely but drastically alters the metallurgy feed.

### 6. Where others might disagree
Proponents of terrestrial mining might argue this is TRL 4 or 5 based on Earth-based bucket wheels and vacuum chamber tests. However, the microgravity granular physics and torque-balancing requirements fundamentally change the system architecture, invalidating terrestrial heritage for the integrated system.
