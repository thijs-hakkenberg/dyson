**1. Evidence**
Microgravity bulk-solids handling and comminution are notoriously difficult due to the dominance of cohesive (van der Waals) and electrostatic forces over gravity, leading to jamming, bridging, and persistent dust clouds. 
- **Granular behavior in microgravity:** Parabolic flight experiments (e.g., D'Angelo et al., 2022) and ISS experiments (e.g., Strata-1, 2016) have investigated granular compaction, force chains, and long-term settling of regolith simulants in microgravity. These operate at the scale of grams to a few kilograms.
- **Bulk conveying and comminution:** There is no physical demonstration of continuous enclosed transfer, metering, buffering, or crushing/milling of abrasive rock to sub-millimeter sizes in microgravity. Terrestrial systems rely heavily on gravity for material feed and clearing.
- **Scale ratio:** Required throughput is 15–55 kt/yr (roughly 1.7–6.3 t/h). Demonstrated microgravity handling is at the batch scale of <1 kg. The scale ratio is >10,000:1.

**2. Current TRL**
Current TRL is **2–3**. The physics of granular materials in microgravity are being actively researched (TRL 3 for basic behavior), but the specific application of continuous bulk conveying, metering, and comminution at scale lacks experimental proof-of-concept for key characteristics (TRL 2). The unmet exit criterion for TRL 3 is documented analytical or experimental results validating predictions of key parameters for continuous flow and crushing in microgravity.

**3. Target and difficulty**
Target TRL for KDP-0B is **6**, as failure to handle and process bulk solids renders the entire in-space production architecture unviable (project-ending). The Advancement Degree of Difficulty (AD²) is **7** (Requires new approaches; fundamental engineering research). Terrestrial bulk handling relies on gravity; replacing it with pneumatic, mechanical, or centrifugal forcing while managing extreme wear and electrostatic charging over 5 years requires fundamental engineering research.

**4. Path**
- **Simulation:** High-fidelity DEM modeling of enclosed conveyors, metering valves, and crushers incorporating electrostatic and cohesive forces (TRL 3).
- **Experiment (Parabolic/Drop Tower):** Sub-scale (10–100 kg/h) breadboard testing of transfer and metering mechanisms in parabolic flights to validate DEM (TRL 4).
- **Experiment (LEO Free-flyer / Pathfinder):** Continuous operation of a scaled-down (0.1–1 t/h) handling and comminution loop in sustained microgravity to prove upset recovery, wear resistance, and dust containment (TRL 5–6).

**5. Risk and fallback**

**6. Where others might disagree**
The AD² rating of 7 might be contested by those who believe pneumatic conveying or simple augers can be easily adapted to microgravity. However, the requirement to handle highly abrasive, irregularly sized, electrostatically charged clasts while performing comminution (which generates immense dust and requires high forces) introduces severe jamming and wear risks that have never been solved in space.
