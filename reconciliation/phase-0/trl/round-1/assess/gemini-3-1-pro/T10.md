### 1. Evidence

The requirement is for $\geq$90 days of completely unattended autonomous operations involving complex physical interactions (mining, beneficiation, material transfer) under significant light-time delays and communication outages. 

- **Spacecraft navigation and proximity ops:** *Deep Space 1* (AutoNav, 1999) and *DART* (SMART Nav, 2022) demonstrated autonomous targeting and navigation, but only for hours to days, and without physical resource extraction. *OSIRIS-REx* (2020) and *Hayabusa2* (2019) performed autonomous surface sampling, but these were single, highly scripted events following weeks of ground-in-the-loop rehearsal, not continuous unattended operations.
- **Surface robotics:** Mars rovers (*Curiosity*, *Perseverance*) utilize autonomous navigation (AutoNav) and target selection (AEGIS), but operate on daily command cycles from Earth. They do not operate unattended for 90 days.
- **Terrestrial mining:** Rio Tinto's AutoHaul and autonomous drilling systems operate continuously, but rely on GPS, high-bandwidth low-latency networks, and frequent human maintenance interventions.

Ratio to required scale: Duration of unattended physical interaction in space is currently demonstrated in minutes/hours, whereas 90 days (130,000 minutes) is required (ratio ~1:10,000).

### 2. Current TRL

**TRL 3–4**
- *Lower bound met:* TRL 3. Analytical and experimental proof of concept exists for autonomous navigation, fault detection, and robotic excavation in isolation.
- *Unmet exit criterion for next level:* TRL 5 requires component/breadboard validation of the *integrated* autonomy software and hardware suite in a relevant environment (vacuum, microgravity, abrasive regolith) operating unattended for long durations.

### 3. Target and difficulty

- **Target:** TRL 6 at KDP-0B.
- **AD²:** 6. Several competing approaches exist (e.g., behavior-based robotics, machine learning, deterministic state machines), but the path to achieving 90 days of zero-maintenance, zero-intervention physical mining in microgravity is not clear.

### 4. Path

1. **Integrated Software-in-the-Loop Simulation:** High-fidelity physics simulation of the mining fleet and processing plant with injected faults. (Scale: 1:1 software, 2-3 years, $10M-$20M, reaches TRL 4).
2. **Terrestrial Vacuum Chamber Autonomy Test:** A breadboard mining robot operating in a vacuum dirt chamber, commanded only by the autonomy engine, with simulated light-time delays, left unattended for 30+ days. (Scale: 1:1 hardware/software breadboard, 2-4 years, $30M-$50M, reaches TRL 5).
3. **Phase 0A Pathfinder:** In-space demonstration of unattended operations on a target NEA. (Scale: 1:1 pathfinder, 3-5 years, $150M+, reaches TRL 6/7).

### 5. Risk and fallback

- **Risk if fail:** project-ending. Without 90-day autonomy, the fleet requires continuous ground-in-the-loop control, which is impossible due to light-time delays and conjunction outages, destroying the throughput model.
- **Fallback:** 0A only, Earth supply.

### 6. Where others might disagree

Software engineers might argue that autonomy is merely a software integration task (TRL 5+), pointing to terrestrial autonomous mining and self-driving cars. However, the inability to physically intervene or repair hardware when the autonomy makes a mistake in space fundamentally changes the stringency of the requirement.
