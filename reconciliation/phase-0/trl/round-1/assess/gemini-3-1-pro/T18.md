An assessment of **T18 Subsurface sampling and in-situ bulk grade assay** against the revised Phase 0 architecture.

### 1. Evidence and Current TRL
Subsurface sampling on small bodies has a mixed heritage. The OSIRIS-REx TAG event (2020) inadvertently penetrated ~50 cm into Bennu due to the surface's extremely low bearing capacity, but this was not a controlled depth sample or in-situ assay. Hayabusa2's Small Carry-on Impactor (SCI, 2019) excavated subsurface material by creating a ~14 m crater, sampling the resulting ejecta rather than drilling. Rosetta's Philae lander carried the SD2 drill designed to reach 23 cm depth on a comet (2014), but it failed to deliver samples to the onboard instruments due to microgravity anchoring and material handling issues. 

While terrestrial and lunar autonomous drills exist (e.g., Luna 24 reaching 2 m, Mars rovers reaching a few cm), drilling to ≥1 m in microgravity while reacting forces, extracting the sample, and performing a bulk grade assay (e.g., XRF, XRD, or LIBS) is a major unresolved challenge. Honeybee Robotics and others have tested microgravity drill components (e.g., for the cancelled Asteroid Redirect Mission) in laboratory and parabolic flight environments, achieving TRL 4 for some mechanisms.

**Current TRL is 3–4.** The exit criterion for TRL 4 (Component/breadboard validation in a laboratory environment) is partially met for the drilling mechanisms, but the integrated system of drilling, sample handling, and bulk grade assay in a microgravity-analog environment lacks breadboard validation.

### 2. Target and Difficulty
To pass the D-02 target gate for KDP-0B, the Phase 0A rendezvous scouts must physically measure the subsurface bulk grade. Therefore, this technology must reach TRL 6 by the scout mission Preliminary Design Review (internal 0A milestone) and TRL 8 to fly on the scouts. 
**AD² is 6.** There are several competing approaches (percussive drilling, pneumatic excavation, kinetic impact cratering) and the path to reliable microgravity sample handling and assay is historically fraught.

### 3. Path
1. **Simulant and Breadboard Testing:** Develop integrated drill/assay breadboards and test in vacuum chambers using calibrated asteroid simulants with varying cohesion (TRL 4).
2. **Microgravity Component Testing:** Parabolic flight or drop-tower testing of the sample extraction and transfer mechanisms to ensure material does not jam or disperse (TRL 5).
3. **System Prototype:** Thermal-vacuum testing of the full sampling and assay payload, including force-reaction (anchoring) simulations (TRL 6).

### 4. Risk and Fallback
**Risk if fail:** Project-ending. The entire Phase 0B economic case relies on discovering a target with sufficient recoverable grade; without subsurface assay, the target gate cannot be passed.
**Fallback:** Abandon controlled drilling in favor of kinetic impactors (like Hayabusa2 SCI) to expose subsurface material for remote spectral observation, though this severely degrades bulk grade accuracy.

### 5. Disagreement
The most contestable part of this rating is the assumption that a 1-meter drill is strictly required. If the scouts can rely on kinetic impactors or if the target is proven homogeneous via radar tomography, the need for a complex mechanical drill could be bypassed.
