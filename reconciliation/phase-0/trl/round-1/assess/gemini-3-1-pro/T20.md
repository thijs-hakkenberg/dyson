### 1. Evidence

- **ISS Li-ion Battery Upgrade (2017–2021):** The ISS replaced its Ni-H2 batteries with 24 Li-ion Orbital Replacement Units (ORUs). Each ORU has a nameplate capacity of ~9.7 kWh and is designed for ~60,000 LEO charge/discharge cycles over 10 years. This perfectly matches the 10–50 kWh per robot requirement and exceeds the 9,000–33,000 cycle requirement.
- **ISS Power Management System:** Manages ~120 kW of distributed solar power and storage. 
- **Terrestrial Microgrids (e.g., Tesla Megapack):** 1–15 MWh storage and MW-class power management are commercial off-the-shelf (COTS) on Earth, but have never been packaged for or tested in a space vacuum/thermal environment at this scale.

Scale ratio: Robot batteries (1:1). Site storage (15 MWh required vs ~0.3 MWh ISS, ratio 50:1). Power management (8 MW required vs 0.12 MW ISS, ratio ~65:1).

### 2. Current TRL

**TRL 3–4.** 
The core battery chemistry and 10–50 kWh packaging are TRL 9 (ISS). However, the 0.5–15 MWh distributed space microgrid and MW-class space power management lack component/breadboard validation in a relevant environment. The lower bound (3) is met by analytical models of MW-class space power systems; the upper bound (4) is unmet because a MWh-class space-rated battery bank and MW-class microgrid controller have not been tested together in a vacuum chamber.

### 3. Target and difficulty

**Target:** TRL 6 by KDP-0B (needed for Phase 0B site power architecture).
**AD²:** 4. New development; similar development has been done (ISS power system, terrestrial MWh microgrids), but scaling space power management by 50x requires significant engineering for thermal dissipation and high-voltage fault management in vacuum.

### 4. Path

1. **MW-scale Microgrid Breadboard:** Hardware-in-the-loop simulation and vacuum testing of a 100 kWh / 100 kW slice of the microgrid architecture to validate fault isolation and thermal management. (Reaches TRL 5).
2. **Subsystem Prototype TVAC:** 0.5 MWh battery bank and 1 MW power conditioning unit tested in a thermal-vacuum chamber under simulated NEA eclipse cycling. (Reaches TRL 6).

### 5. Risk and fallback

**Risk:** Architecture-change.
**Fallback:** Oversize the solar arrays to minimize required storage, accept operational downtime during eclipses, and revert to centralized rather than distributed power conditioning.

### 6. Where others might disagree

Some might argue this is TRL 5 or 6 because it is "just" scaling up ISS batteries or putting terrestrial Megapacks in a vacuum-rated box. This ignores the non-linear thermal management challenges of MWh-class Li-ion storage in a vacuum and the arcing risks of MW-class switching in the space environment.
