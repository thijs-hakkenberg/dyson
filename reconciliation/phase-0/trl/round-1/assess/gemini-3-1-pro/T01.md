### 1. Evidence
The requirement demands 100–250 kW thrusters operating on iodine or argon for 20,000–40,000 hours, clustered to 1.0–2.5 MWe. 
- **High-power Hall (Xenon):** The X3 nested Hall thruster demonstrated >100 kW operation (University of Michigan/NASA, 2017), but on xenon and for short durations (TRL 4 for power, TRL 2 for life). 
- **High-power Argon:** The VASIMR VX-200 demonstrated 200 kW operation on argon (Ad Astra, 2013), but only for short pulses in a laboratory environment. 
- **Iodine:** ThrustMe's NPT30-I2 demonstrated in-space iodine propulsion (2021), but at ~50 W. Busek has tested iodine Hall thrusters at the 200–600 W scale. Scaling iodine to 100+ kW involves severe, unproven challenges regarding feed system corrosion, thermal management of the sublimator at high mass flow rates, and plasma-wall interactions.
- **PPU & Clusters:** MW-class space Power Processing Units (PPUs) and autonomous clustering at this scale do not exist. 

### 2. Current TRL
**TRL 2–3.** 
The lower bound (TRL 2) is met because the application is formulated and basic physics of high-power argon and low-power iodine are known. The upper bound (TRL 3) is met only for argon (analytical/experimental proof of concept at 200 kW), but not for iodine at the 100 kW scale. The unmet exit criterion for TRL 4 is component/breadboard validation in a laboratory environment of a 100+ kW iodine thruster, or validation of 20,000+ hour life on argon at scale.

### 3. Target and Difficulty
- **Target:** TRL 6 by KDP-0B. This is a project-ending technology for Phase 0B; if the tugs cannot move the cargo economically, the in-space production architecture fails.
- **AD²: 6.** Several competing approaches exist (nested Hall, applied-field MPD, VASIMR) and the path is not clear. Managing iodine corrosion at MW-scale mass flows, or achieving 40,000-hour life on argon at 100+ kW, requires significant engineering research.

### 4. Path
1. **100 kW Iodine/Argon Breadboard (TRL 3-4):** Laboratory demonstration of a 100 kW-class thruster on iodine and argon to measure efficiency, thermal loads, and initial erosion rates. (2-3 years, $10M-$20M)
2. **Accelerated Wear Testing & Plume Analysis (TRL 4-5):** 5,000+ hour wear test in a vacuum chamber with validated erosion modeling to extrapolate to 40,000 hours; array coupon plume deposition tests. (3-4 years, $20M-$40M)
3. **MW-Class PPU and Cluster Test (TRL 5-6):** Thermal-vacuum testing of a multi-thruster cluster (e.g., 3x 100 kW) with flight-like PPUs, measuring heat rejected to the bus. (3-5 years, $50M-$100M)

### 5. Risk and Fallback
- **Risk:** Project-ending. Without MW-class, non-xenon SEP, the cargo tugs cannot meet the delta-V, payload, and economic requirements of the Phase 0B transport chain.
- **Fallback:** Revert to Phase 0A only (Earth supply), or attempt a massive architectural change to chemical propulsion (which likely destroys the delivered-cost model).

### 6. Where others might disagree
Advocates of existing high-power SEP programs (like HERMeS/AEPS) might argue this is TRL 5-6 based on xenon heritage. However, changing the propellant to iodine or argon at 10x the power and 4x the required life fundamentally resets the TRL to the component/breadboard phase due to erosion, corrosion, and thermal physics.
