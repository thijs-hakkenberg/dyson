This assessment evaluates the rotating partial-gravity smelting and slag separation architecture (0.05–0.15 g, 1–10 t batches) proposed as a solution to the microgravity metallurgy scaling problem. 

### 1. Evidence
Current space-based metallurgical experience is restricted to pure microgravity or parabolic flight profiles, processing samples measured in grams. Terrestrial smelting operates at kilotonne scales but at 1 g without Coriolis forces. There is no experimental data for tonnes-scale molten metal and slag behavior in a 0.05–0.15 g rotating frame (1.5–4 rpm at 5–20 m radius), where Coriolis forces will induce asymmetric flow patterns, and reduced buoyancy must compete with surface tension for slag separation. 

- **ISS Electromagnetic Levitator (EML) / MSL:** Gram-scale containerless melting in pure microgravity. Scale ratio to requirement: ~10⁶.
- **Parabolic flight powder AM (D'Angelo et al., 2021):** Gram-scale gravity-independent powder deposition. Not applicable to bulk liquid melt/slag separation.
- **Terrestrial centrifugal casting:** Operates at high g-loads (10–100+ g) to force separation or fill molds, not in the fractional-g regime required here.

### 2. Current TRL
**TRL 2 (Min 2, Max 2).** The concept of using a 0.05–0.15 g rotating module to restore buoyancy-driven slag separation while minimizing structural mass has been formulated. However, there are no documented analytical models validated by experiment, nor any physical proof-of-concept tests demonstrating that 0.05 g is sufficient to overcome surface tension and Coriolis-induced mixing in a 1–10 t melt pool. Therefore, it has not met the exit criteria for TRL 3.

### 3. Target and Difficulty
**Target:** TRL 6 at KDP-0B. If this route is selected as the baseline for Phase 0B, it must be demonstrated at prototype scale in a relevant environment before committing to the multi-billion-dollar production phase.
**AD²:** 7 (Requires new approaches; fundamental engineering research). The transition from surface-tension-dominated to gravity-influenced behavior in large melt pools under strong Coriolis forces represents a regime with almost no existing data.

### 4. Path
1. **CFD and Multiphysics Modeling:** Validated computational models for 1–10 t molten metal behavior at 0.05–0.15 g, incorporating Coriolis effects and slag-metal interfacial physics. (TRL 3)
2. **Terrestrial Centrifuge Analog:** 1–10 kg batches of low-melting-point analogs (e.g., gallium/water or tin/salt) in a ground-based centrifuge to validate CFD predictions of Coriolis mixing and fractional-g separation. (TRL 4)
3. **Orbital Partial-Gravity Pathfinder:** A dedicated free-flying or tethered rotating platform (0A pathfinder) processing 10–100 kg batches of asteroid simulant at 0.05–0.15 g to validate refractory life, spun/despun transfer, and slag separation in vacuum. (TRL 5/6)

### 5. Risk and Fallback
**Risk if fail:** Architecture-change. If partial-gravity smelting fails or requires excessive g-loads (driving up station mass), the architecture must pivot entirely to no-melt extraction routes.
**Fallback:** Abandon smelting in favor of magnetic beneficiation followed by carbonyl or hydrogen reduction (the Claude baseline), or increase rotation radius/rate to achieve 0.3–0.5 g, accepting the massive structural and mass penalties.

### 6. Where others might disagree
Advocates of terrestrial metallurgy might argue that since gravity is restored, TRL should be higher (TRL 3 or 4). However, 0.05 g is a fractional regime where surface tension and Coriolis forces (due to the tight 5–20 m radius) will dominate fluid dynamics in ways terrestrial smelting never encounters.
