This assessment evaluates the technology readiness for uncrewed robotic maintenance, repair, spares logistics, and site installation/recovery in the context of a 5–15 year autonomous mining operation on a near-Earth asteroid.

### 1. Evidence
Robotic manipulation and Orbital Replacement Unit (ORU) swapping have extensive heritage in clean, low-Earth orbit environments (e.g., ISS Dextre/SPDM, DARPA Orbital Express). However, the Phase 0 architecture requires autonomous or high-latency teleoperated maintenance of heavy mining equipment (2–3.5 t robots) in an abrasive, electrostatically charged dust environment. 
- **ISS Dextre (SPDM)**: Demonstrated robotic replacement of ORUs up to 600 kg in LEO (teleoperated/scripted). (TRL 9 for clean LEO).
- **DARPA Orbital Express (2007)**: Demonstrated autonomous capture and ORU transfer in LEO. (TRL 7 for clean LEO).
- **NASA Dusty Environment Tool/Connector Testing**: Various component-level tests for lunar surface operations (Artemis/Constellation eras) have validated dust-tolerant connectors and seals in vacuum chambers. (TRL 4).

The ratio of demonstrated scale to required scale is approximately 1:1 for mass (Canadarm2 handles >3t), but 0:1 for the required environment (abrasive dust) and autonomy level for complex mechanical repair (clearing jams, replacing refractories).

### 2. Current TRL
**TRL 3–4**. 
The lower bound (TRL 3) is met because analytical and experimental proof-of-concept exists for dust-tolerant connectors and robotic manipulation. The upper bound (TRL 4) is partially met at the component level for lunar applications.
*Unmet exit criterion for TRL 5*: Documented test performance of a breadboard robotic maintenance system (manipulator, dust-tolerant ORU interfaces, and autonomous fault-recovery logic) in a relevant environment (vacuum, thermal cycling, abrasive charged simulant).

### 3. Target and Difficulty
**Target TRL**: TRL 6 by KDP-0B. 
**AD²**: 6. Several competing approaches exist for dust mitigation (seals, electrostatic clearing, mechanical wiping) and robotic autonomy, but the path to a reliable 5–15 year uncrewed maintenance architecture in this environment is not clear.

### 4. Path
1. **Dusty Vacuum ORU Testing**: Laboratory validation of dust-tolerant mechanical and electrical interfaces under thermal vacuum and charged simulant conditions. (Reaches TRL 4).
2. **Subsystem Autonomy Demo**: Ground-based robotic testbed demonstrating autonomous recovery of jammed mining end-effectors and cable management. (Reaches TRL 5).
3. **Pathfinder Demonstration (Phase 0A)**: In-space demonstration of basic module installation and self-clearing on the target NEA. (Reaches TRL 6).

### 5. Risk and Fallback
**Risk**: Project-ending. If the plant and robots cannot be maintained uncrewed for 5–15 years, the economic model of Phase 0B collapses due to equipment failure.
**Fallback**: Fallback to "0A only, Earth supply" (abandoning 0B in-space production), or redesigning for massive redundancy (deploying 3x the robots and abandoning failed units) which severely impacts the delivered-cost model.

### 6. Where others might disagree
Robotics experts might argue that ISS heritage (Dextre) and terrestrial autonomous mining put this closer to TRL 5. However, terrestrial mining relies heavily on human mechanics for physical repairs and maintenance, and ISS operates in a pristine vacuum without the severe abrasive dust and complex mechanical wear inherent to comminution and excavation.
