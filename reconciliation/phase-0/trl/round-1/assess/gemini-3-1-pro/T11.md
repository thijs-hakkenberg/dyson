**1. Evidence**
Small spacecraft electric propulsion and deep-space navigation have strong heritage (SMART-1 at 367 kg, Dawn, NEA Scout packaging). Asteroid rendezvous and surface sampling are flight-proven by large and medium spacecraft (OSIRIS-REx at ~1,500 kg dry; Hayabusa2 at ~600 kg dry). However, the specific requirement for a 200–600 kg scout to perform *subsurface* sampling for bulk grade across multiple targets is unprecedented. Hayabusa2 used a kinetic impactor (SCI) to expose subsurface material for a subsequent surface grab, which does not yield a continuous depth profile or bulk grade. Philae (~100 kg) attempted drilling (SD2) on a comet, but anchoring failed and sample delivery was unconfirmed. OSIRIS-REx sank 50 cm during TAG due to low surface bearing capacity, but this was an uncontrolled plunge, not a designed deep-sampling mechanism. 

**2. Current TRL**
TRL 3–4. The individual elements (EP smallsats, surface sampling) are TRL 7–9, but a lightweight, multi-target subsurface sampling system (e.g., a coring drill or tethered penetrator that can anchor and extract bulk grade in microgravity) has only been analytically modeled and tested in 1g/laboratory conditions. The exit criterion for TRL 4 (breadboard validation of the deep sampling mechanism in a laboratory environment) is partially met by various terrestrial drill tests, but TRL 5 (relevant environment: vacuum, microgravity, abrasive cryogenic regolith) is unmet.

**3. Target and difficulty**
Target TRL: 8 by the launch of Phase 0A (needed to execute the 0A mission). 
AD²: 5 (New development; similar development has not been done, but the path is clear). Integrating a high-torque/force subsurface sampler onto a low-mass platform in microgravity requires solving severe anchoring and reaction-force deficits (as noted in rq-0-39).

**4. Path**
- **Sampling mechanism breadboard:** 1g and drop-tower testing of anchoring and subsurface extraction in vacuum with calibrated simulants (TRL 3 → 4).
- **Subsystem prototype:** Thermal-vacuum and parabolic flight testing of the sampling head and transfer mechanism (TRL 4 → 5).
- **Integrated scout prototype:** Full-scale engineering model of the 200–600 kg spacecraft, demonstrating EP, autonomy, and sampling dynamics in TVAC (TRL 5 → 6).
- **Flight qualification:** Build and qualification of the Phase 0A flight units (TRL 6 → 8).

**5. Risk and fallback**
Risk if fail: **architecture-change**. If the scouts cannot sample the subsurface, the project cannot certify the bulk recoverable grade (the primary gate for Phase 0B). The fallback is to rely on surface sampling combined with kinetic impactors (Hayabusa2 style) or radar tomography, accepting much higher uncertainty in the 0B economic case, or to increase scout mass to >1,000 kg to accommodate heritage sampling arms.

**6. Where others might disagree**
The assumption that a 200–600 kg spacecraft can carry enough propellant for 1–3 rendezvous *and* the structural mass for a subsurface anchoring/drilling system. Electric propulsion provides the Δv, but the dry mass fraction required for deep sampling may force the spacecraft to the absolute maximum of the 600 kg limit, or restrict it to a single target.
