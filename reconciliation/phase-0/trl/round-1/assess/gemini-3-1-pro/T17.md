### 1. Evidence
Hydrogen reduction of iron oxides is a mature terrestrial technology (Direct Reduced Iron, e.g., the HYBRIT pilot plant in Sweden, 2020, demonstrating fossil-free H₂ reduction at ~1 t/h). However, the space environment introduces severe challenges: gas-solid contacting without gravity. On Earth, gravity settles the bed against the gas flow (fluidized or packed beds). In microgravity, gas flow will entrain the dust, requiring mechanical containment, acoustic settling, or centrifugal forces. NASA has funded extensive research into H₂ reduction of lunar regolith (ilmenite reduction), demonstrating the chemistry at laboratory scales (e.g., NASA RESOLVE payload development, ~grams to kg scale, TRL 4 for lunar partial gravity). However, microgravity gas-solid reactor dynamics for continuous bulk processing remain at the conceptual or early breadboard stage. Water electrolysis is mature terrestrially and on the ISS (Oxygen Generation System), but coupling it with a dusty, unpurified product water stream from an asteroid reactor at tens of tonnes per year requires significant development. Scale ratio: terrestrial DRI is 100x larger than needed; space ISRU demonstrations are 10,000x smaller than needed.

### 2. Current TRL
**TRL 3-4**. The basic chemistry and terrestrial analogs are well understood (TRL 9 on Earth). Laboratory breadboards of lunar regolith H₂ reduction exist (TRL 4). However, for the specific relevant environment (microgravity gas-solid contacting without artificial gravity), only analytical models and short-duration parabolic flight/drop-tower tests of granular mechanics exist. The exit criterion unmet for TRL 4 is component validation of a gas-solid reactor in a microgravity environment demonstrating continuous throughput without clogging or massive dust entrainment.

### 3. Target and Difficulty
**Target TRL 6** at KDP-0B. This is a project-ending technology for Claude's no-melt architecture.
**AD² 7** (Requires new approaches; fundamental engineering research). Designing a continuous, high-throughput gas-solid reactor that achieves high conversion efficiency without gravity to separate the phases or settle the bed is a fundamental multiphase flow challenge.

### 4. Path
1. **Microgravity Gas-Solid Contacting Experiments**: Parabolic flight and drop tower tests of mechanical/acoustic agitation concepts for regolith-gas mixing. (TRL 4)
2. **Integrated Breadboard**: Laboratory demonstration of the full loop (reduction, water condensation, electrolysis, H₂ recycle) using asteroid simulants in a vacuum chamber. (TRL 4-5)
3. **Pathfinder (0A) Demonstration**: A sub-scale (kg/day) flight experiment on a 0A scout to demonstrate the end-to-end process in actual microgravity and radiation. (TRL 6)

### 5. Risk and Fallback
**Risk if fail**: Architecture-change. If zero-g gas-solid reduction fails, the architecture must adopt Gemini's rotating smelting modules (introducing artificial gravity and high temperatures) or pivot to carbonyl extraction.
**Fallback**: Implement a rotating reactor vessel to provide 0.1g artificial gravity to settle the regolith bed, accepting the mass and complexity penalties of high-temperature rotating seals.

### 6. Where others might disagree
Advocates of the no-melt route might argue that mechanical augers or constrained packed beds solve the microgravity contacting problem easily, pushing the current TRL to 4 or 5 based on terrestrial vacuum-furnace heritage. However, dust entrainment and gas channeling in zero-g are notoriously difficult to predict, justifying the lower TRL.
