### 1. Evidence

*   **Tethered Satellite System (TSS-1R, 1996):** Demonstrated high-voltage (up to 3.5 kV) tether operations in LEO. *Scale demonstrated:* 3.5 kV, but in a plasma environment, not abrasive dusty regolith, and failed due to an arc through breached insulation.
*   **Artemis Fission Surface Power (FSP) & VSAT cable studies (NASA GRC, ~2020–present):** Ongoing laboratory testing of 1–3 kV transmission cables and dust-tolerant connectors for lunar surface operations. *Scale demonstrated:* 1–3 kV, tens of kW, in dusty vacuum chambers. *Ratio:* Voltage 1:1, Power ~1:10.
*   **Microgravity reeling:** Tether management in microgravity has flown (e.g., YES2, TSS), but not with heavy, stiff, high-power insulated cables dragged across abrasive terrain.

### 2. Current TRL

**Range: 3–4**
Basic analytical and experimental proofs of concept exist for 1–3 kV space cables (TRL 3 met). Component breadboard validation in laboratory environments (dusty vacuum chambers) is underway for Artemis (TRL 4 met for static cables). 
**Unmet exit criterion for TRL 5:** Documented test performance of the *integrated* assembly (cable, dust-tolerant connectors, and active reeling mechanism) in a relevant environment (dusty vacuum with thermal cycling and simulated microgravity abrasion).

### 3. Target and difficulty

**Target:** TRL 6 by KDP-0B (needed for Phase 0B architecture baseline).
**AD²: 6** (Several competing approaches; the path is not clear). Managing stiff, heavily insulated 1–3 kV cables on a microgravity surface without tangling, snagging, or rapidly abrading the insulation requires novel reeling mechanisms and materials. Dust ingress into high-voltage connectors is a major failure risk.

### 4. Path

1.  **Cable & Connector Dust/Vacuum Testing:** Experiment. Static testing of insulation and connectors in thermal-vacuum with charged simulant. Scale: 3 kV, 100 kW. Cost: $1M–$3M. Duration: 1–2 yr. Reaches TRL 4.
2.  **Micro-g Reeling Breadboard:** Experiment. Parabolic flight or drop-tower testing of reeling mechanisms with stiff cables to validate snag-prevention and spooling dynamics. Scale: 10-50m cable. Cost: $2M–$5M. Duration: 1–2 yr. Reaches TRL 5.
3.  **Integrated Endurance Demo:** Experiment. Long-duration thermal-vacuum testing with active reeling, abrasion over simulant, and live 3 kV power to measure arc thresholds and wear rates. Scale: Full voltage/power, scaled length. Cost: $5M–$10M. Duration: 2–3 yr. Reaches TRL 6.

### 5. Risk and fallback

**Risk if fail:** Architecture-change.
**Fallback:** Abandon tethered power and switch to battery-only mining robots with centralized charging stations, which would drastically reduce the excavation duty cycle (9-25% to <5%) or require a massive increase in robot mass and fleet size.

### 6. Where others might disagree

The assumption that lunar FSP cable research (TRL 4) directly translates to NEA tethers. Lunar cables are largely static point-to-point distribution lines; the Dyson architecture requires *dynamic* tethers continuously reeled and dragged by mobile robots in microgravity, which introduces severe mechanical and abrasion stresses not covered by static lunar surface power studies.
