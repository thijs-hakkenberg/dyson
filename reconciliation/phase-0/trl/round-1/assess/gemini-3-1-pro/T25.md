### 1. Evidence

Autonomous rendezvous, proximity operations, and docking (RPOD) are well-established in LEO and GEO, but the required scale (150–300 t payloads, likely 200–400 t total vehicle mass) and environments (SEL1, target NEA) present novel challenges in contact dynamics, plume impingement, and low-gravity stationkeeping.

- **ISS Resupply (ATV, Dragon, Cygnus, Progress):** Autonomous docking and berthing in LEO with vehicle masses up to ~20 t (ATV). Scale ratio: ~10–20× mass. Environment: LEO (not SEL1/NEA). Confident: true.
- **Mission Extension Vehicle (MEV-1/2, 2020/2021):** Autonomous rendezvous and docking in GEO. Mass: ~2.3 t. Scale ratio: ~100×. Confident: true.
- **DART (2022) / OSIRIS-REx (2020):** Autonomous terminal navigation to a NEA (DART) and touch-and-go sampling (OSIRIS-REx). Scale ratio: ~100× mass. Confident: true.

### 2. Current TRL

**TRL 3–4**
While the basic principles and algorithms of autonomous RPOD are TRL 9 for 20 t vehicles in Earth orbit, the specific application to 150–300 t payloads in SEL1 and NEA environments lacks component/breadboard validation of the contact dynamics and control systems at scale. The lower bound (TRL 3) reflects that analytical proof-of-concept for the required mass scale and multi-vehicle traffic management at SEL1/NEA exists in studies, but the upper bound (TRL 4) is not fully met because laboratory validation of the specific contact dynamics and high-inertia control algorithms has not been documented.

### 3. Target and difficulty

**Target:** TRL 6 by KDP-0B (project-ending for Phase 0B, as failure to safely dock and transfer cargo precludes the logistics chain).
**AD²: 4** (New development; similar development has been done). The algorithms and sensors exist, but scaling the contact dynamics, structural load management, and collision avoidance for 300 t vehicles requires significant engineering.

### 4. Path

1. **High-Fidelity Simulation:** 6-DOF simulation of contact dynamics, plume impingement, and control authority for 300 t vehicles at SEL1 and NEA. (Simulation, $1M–$3M, 1–2 years, reaches TRL 4).
2. **Hardware-in-the-Loop (HWIL) Testing:** Flat-floor or robotic testbed validation of docking sensors, algorithms, and scaled contact mechanisms. (Experiment, $5M–$15M, 2–3 years, reaches TRL 5).
3. **Subscale Space Demonstration:** Pathfinder mission (Phase 0A) demonstrating autonomous RPOD at the target NEA or SEL1 with a smaller vehicle, validating the environment and algorithms. (Experiment, $50M–$150M, 3–5 years, reaches TRL 6).

### 5. Risk and fallback

**Risk if fail:** Project-ending. If 300 t tugs cannot safely navigate and dock, the logistics architecture fails.
**Fallback:** Revert to smaller, heritage-scale cargo vehicles (e.g., 20 t), drastically increasing the fleet size and operational complexity (architecture-change).

### 6. Where others might disagree

Some might argue that RPOD is entirely software and sensor-driven, and therefore TRL 9 based on ISS heritage. However, the physical realities of arresting a 300 t mass without damaging the node or entangling large solar arrays make this a coupled cyber-physical problem that has not been validated at this scale.
