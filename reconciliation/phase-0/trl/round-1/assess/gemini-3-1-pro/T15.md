### 1. Evidence

The site power architecture requires generating 2.5–8 MW at a Near-Earth Asteroid (0.9–1.3 AU) for 10–15 years, and transferring it to 6–20 mobile mining robots via 1–3 kV tethers or wireless beaming. 

- **Space Solar Arrays (Generation):** The largest deployed space solar arrays are on the International Space Station (ISS), which generates approximately 240 kW total. The Roll-Out Solar Array (ROSA) technology, currently being deployed on the ISS (iROSA) and planned for the Lunar Gateway Power and Propulsion Element (PPE), scales to ~50 kW per wing (NASA/Redwire, 2021). The required 2.5–8 MW scale is a 50× to 160× increase over the largest individual space array wings flown.
- **Tethered Power Transfer:** The Tethered Satellite System (TSS-1R, 1996) demonstrated high-voltage electrodynamic tether operation in space, reaching ~3.5 kV and ~480 W before failure. Transferring MW-class power over dynamic tethers to mobile robots in a dusty, microgravity environment has no flight heritage.
- **Wireless Power Transfer (Beamed):** The Caltech Space Solar Power Demonstrator (SSPD-1) MAPLE experiment (2023) demonstrated wireless power transfer in space, but at the milliwatt scale.

### 2. Current TRL

**TRL 2–3**
Basic principles of the subsystems (photovoltaics, tethers, beaming) are understood, and concepts for MW-class space solar power have been formulated. However, analytical and experimental proof-of-concept for a 2.5–8 MW integrated generation and dynamic transfer system in a relevant environment (dusty NEA) is incomplete. The exit criterion for TRL 3 (Documented analytical or experimental results validating predictions of key parameters for the integrated MW-class transfer system) is unmet.

### 3. Target and difficulty

- **Target:** TRL 6 at KDP-0B. Site power is a project-ending requirement for Phase 0B.
- **AD²:** 6. Several competing approaches exist (surface vs. free-flying arrays, tethered vs. beamed transfer), and the path to scaling space power generation by two orders of magnitude while managing dynamic high-voltage transfer in an abrasive regolith environment is not clear.

### 4. Path

1. **Architecture Selection (Analysis):** Trade study to select surface vs. free-flying arrays and tethered vs. beamed transfer based on NEA dust mitigation and thermal constraints. (Scale: 2.5-8 MW, $1M–$3M, 1 yr, reaches TRL 3).
2. **Component Lab Testing (Experiment):** Vacuum chamber testing of 1–3 kV dynamic tether spools or beamed power transmitters with simulated charged regolith to validate insulation and dust mitigation. (Scale: 10-50 kW transfer, $5M–$15M, 2–3 yrs, reaches TRL 4).
3. **Subsystem Breadboard (Experiment):** Thermal-vacuum deployment testing of MW-class array structural hinges and high-voltage distribution nodes. (Scale: 100-500 kW array segments, $20M–$50M, 3 yrs, reaches TRL 5).
4. **Pathfinder Flight Demo (Experiment):** Subscale free-flying array or surface array deployed in LEO or at the NEA during Phase 0A, demonstrating deployment and power transfer to a dummy load or scout. (Scale: 100-250 kW, $100M–$250M, 4 yrs, reaches TRL 6).

### 5. Risk and fallback

- **Risk if fail:** Project-ending. Without MW-class site power, the required 0.5–5 t/h excavation and processing rates cannot be sustained.
- **Fallback:** Fall back to Phase 0A only (Earth supply), or drastically reduce Phase 0B production targets to fit within the ~100-200 kW power limits of current state-of-the-art space arrays.

### 6. Where others might disagree

Others might argue that because solar panels are TRL 9, the generation aspect is simply a matter of modular scaling (TRL 5+). However, scaling space structures by two orders of magnitude introduces fundamental non-linearities in launch packaging, deployment dynamics, thermal management, and high-voltage distribution (600V+ DC) that invalidate heritage at the 20-50 kW scale.
