### 1. Evidence
Terrestrial dry magnetic separation is a mature (TRL 9) technology for mining, but microgravity beneficiation of asteroid regolith lacks large-scale physical validation. Basic principles of magnetic separation for lunar and asteroid simulants have been demonstrated in 1g laboratory environments, and limited electrostatic/magnetic particle behavior has been observed in drop towers and parabolic flights (e.g., early work by Oder on lunar soils, and various NASA NIAC studies on asteroid ISRU). However, I am unsure of any specific, peer-reviewed parabolic flight campaign that successfully demonstrated continuous magnetic beneficiation of Fe-Ni from a silicate matrix at a measurable concentration ratio in microgravity. 

The demonstrated scale in 1g lab tests is typically on the order of grams to kilograms per hour. The required scale is 15–55 kt/yr feed (approx. 1.7–6.2 tonnes per hour). The scale ratio is therefore roughly 1,000:1 to 10,000:1.

### 2. Current TRL
**TRL 3–4.** 
Analytical and experimental critical function proof-of-concept exists in 1g (TRL 3). Some breadboard validation in laboratory environments (vacuum, 1g) may push this to TRL 4. 
*Unmet exit criterion for TRL 5:* Documented test performance of a component/breadboard in a relevant environment (microgravity/vacuum) agreeing with predictions, and documented definition of scaling requirements.

### 3. Target and Difficulty
**Target TRL:** 6 at KDP-0B (project-ending for Phase 0B if the concentration ratio falls below 3:1).
**AD²:** 5. New development; similar development has not been done in microgravity, but the path is clear (terrestrial magnetic separation physics is well understood, though particle flow and clearing without gravity require new engineering).

### 4. Path
1. **Simulant development & 1g baselining:** Create LL/L/H and M-type simulants with realistic sub-mm liberation characteristics. Run through 1g dry magnetic separators to establish baseline grade/recovery. (Scale: 10–100 kg/hr; Cost: $0.5M–$1M; Duration: 1 yr; Reaches TRL 4).
2. **Microgravity parabolic flight campaign:** Test breadboard magnetic/electrostatic separators in parabolic flights to validate particle transport, separation efficiency, and clearing mechanisms without gravity. (Scale: 1–10 kg/hr; Cost: $1M–$3M; Duration: 1–2 yrs; Reaches TRL 5).
3. **Pathfinder demonstrator (0A):** In-space demonstration on the 0A pathfinder vehicle at a target NEA to validate continuous operation, concentration ratio (4-15:1), and throughput in true environment. (Scale: 100–500 kg/hr; Cost: $50M–$150M; Duration: 2–3 yrs; Reaches TRL 6).

### 5. Risk and Fallback
**Risk:** Project-ending. If the concentration ratio falls below 3:1, the co-located processing architecture fails, mass economics break, and the delivered-cost model for Phase 0B cannot close.
**Fallback:** 0A only, Earth supply.

### 6. Where others might disagree
The assumption that dry magnetic separation can be scaled to 6 t/h in microgravity without massive dust mitigation and jamming issues. Terrestrial systems rely heavily on gravity to clear non-magnetic tailings from the drum/belt; replacing this with centrifugal or pneumatic forces in a vacuum may drastically increase power and complexity, potentially lowering the current realistic TRL to 2 for the *microgravity continuous flow* aspect.
