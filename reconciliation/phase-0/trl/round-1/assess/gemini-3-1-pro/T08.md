### 1. Evidence

The requirement is to process 13–47 kt/yr of regolith/tailings with an escape rate of ≤1 t/yr (an efficiency of >99.99%), while managing abnormal events across excavation, transfer, and processing in a microgravity, plasma-charged environment. 

Current space heritage for regolith collection consists of touch-and-go sampling (Hayabusa, Hayabusa2, OSIRIS-REx) and scoop/drill sampling (Apollo, Luna, Chang'e 5). OSIRIS-REx successfully collected an estimated 250g of material in 2020, but a rock jammed the TAGSAM collection flap open, resulting in continuous particle escape until stowage. This demonstrated the extreme difficulty of positive sealing in microgravity granular environments. 

For the physics of dust charging and mitigation, laboratory experiments have demonstrated electron-beam dust removal on flat surfaces in vacuum (Hao et al., 2025, arxiv:2505.24074), and triboelectric charging frameworks exist (Shin et al., 2021). However, as noted in the literature review, no published work addresses the containment of continuous multi-tonne-per-hour flows or the protection of sealed mechanisms in the asteroid surface environment. The scale ratio from OSIRIS-REx (grams) to Phase 0 (47 kt/yr) is ~10⁸.

### 2. Current TRL

**TRL 2–3**
Basic physics of charging and granular mechanics are understood, and concepts for containment (housings, electrostatic barriers) are formulated (TRL 2). Some laboratory experiments exist for isolated phenomena like electron-beam cleaning or triboelectric charging (TRL 3). However, the exit criterion for TRL 3 (analytical or experimental results validating predictions of key parameters for *system-level containment* at high mass-flow rates) is unmet.

### 3. Target and difficulty

**Target:** TRL 6 by KDP-0B. 
**AD²:** 7. Achieving >99.99% containment of abrasive, electrostatically charged granular material in microgravity over thousands of hours requires fundamental engineering research. Earth-based mining relies heavily on gravity for dust settling and water for suppression, neither of which are available.

### 4. Path

1. **Granular-electrostatic simulation:** Coupled DEM (Discrete Element Method) and electrostatic modeling of excavation and transfer to predict steady-state escape rates. Reaches TRL 3.
2. **Parabolic flight containment tests:** Sub-scale bucket-wheel and transfer mechanisms in vacuum chambers on parabolic flights to validate DEM models of particle lofting and seal efficacy. Reaches TRL 4.
3. **Vacuum plasma chamber endurance:** Long-duration testing of seals, electrostatic barriers, and active cleaning (e.g., electron beams) on articulated joints using reactive simulants. Reaches TRL 5.
4. **Pathfinder demonstrator (Phase 0A):** In-space demonstration of excavation, transfer, and tailings management at a sub-scale rate (e.g., 100 kg/h) with in-situ optical escape measurement. Reaches TRL 6.

### 5. Risk and fallback

**Risk if fail:** Project-ending. If the operation cannot meet the licensing state's debris limit (interim ≤1 t/yr), the mission will not be permitted to launch or operate, as a runaway debris cloud would hazard the entire multi-billion-dollar fleet and future missions to the body.
**Fallback:** 0A only, Earth supply.

### 6. Where others might disagree

The assumption that 99.99% containment is achievable at all in microgravity. Some granular mechanics experts may argue that without gravity to bias the random walk of colliding particles, any open-faced excavation will inevitably lose a significant fraction of its working mass, making the ≤1 t/yr limit physically impossible for a 47 kt/yr operation unless the entire asteroid is bagged.
