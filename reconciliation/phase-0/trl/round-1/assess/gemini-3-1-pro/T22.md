### 1. Evidence

The consolidation of bulk metal (Fe-Ni) into transportable, dust-tight forms at kilotonne-per-year scales in vacuum and microgravity (or partial gravity) has no direct space heritage. Terrestrial pelletising, sintering, and casting are mature, but their physics depend heavily on gravity and convective cooling. 

- **Microgravity Sintering:** Liquid-phase sintering experiments have been conducted on the Space Shuttle and ISS (e.g., by R.M. German et al., 1990s; NASA MSFC), demonstrating that pore evolution and densification differ significantly without gravity. Scale: grams.
- **Powder Handling and Consolidation:** D'Angelo et al. (2021) demonstrated gravity-independent powder-based additive manufacturing in parabolic flights. Scale: grams.
- **Bulk Casting in Space:** No bulk casting or continuous casting has been demonstrated in space. Melt containment and controlled solidification of large masses in vacuum/microgravity remain purely theoretical or limited to mm-scale drop tower/ISS experiments.

The required scale is 0.6–2.5 kt/yr (roughly 2–7 tonnes per day). The demonstrated scale is on the order of grams, yielding a scale ratio of approximately $10^6$.

### 2. Current TRL

**Current TRL: 2–3**
- **Lower bound (TRL 2):** Concepts for space-based pelletising, sintering, and casting have been formulated and their benefits for logistics (dust mitigation, tug compatibility) are documented.
- **Upper bound (TRL 3):** Small-scale experimental proof-of-concept for microgravity sintering and powder consolidation exists, validating basic physical parameters.
- **Unmet exit criterion (TRL 4):** Documented test performance of a breadboard consolidation system (kg-scale) agreeing with analytical predictions in a relevant laboratory environment (thermal vacuum).

### 3. Target and Difficulty

- **Target TRL:** 6 at KDP-0B (needed by ~2030). The ability to package and qualify the product is project-ending for Phase 0B, as the economic model and tug design depend on shipping a dense, dust-free, predictable product.
- **AD² Rating:** 6 (Several competing approaches; the path is not clear). The architecture has not down-selected between sintering, pelletising, or casting, and the gravity environment (microgravity vs. 0.05–0.15 g) is contested. Scaling thermal management for bulk consolidation in vacuum is a significant engineering challenge.

### 4. Path

   - *Method:* Experiment / Analysis
   - *Step:* Evaluate sintering vs. pelletising vs. casting of Fe-Ni powder/melt in thermal vacuum at kg-scale to determine density, energy use, and dust shedding.
   - *Scale:* 1–10 kg batches.
   - *Cost:* $5M–$15M | *Duration:* 1–2 years | *Reaches TRL:* 4

   - *Scale:* 0.1–1 kg batches.
   - *Cost:* $10M–$25M | *Duration:* 1–2 years | *Reaches TRL:* 5
3. **Pathfinder Demonstrator (Phase 0A):**
   - *Method:* Experiment

   - *Cost:* $50M–$100M (instrumentation/payload cost) | *Duration:* 2–3 years | *Reaches TRL:* 6

### 5. Risk and Fallback

- **Risk if fail:** Architecture-change.
- **Fallback:** Ship unconsolidated powder or raw chunks in heavy, sealed, reusable cargo bladders, accepting a significant mass penalty for the tug payload and complicating Phase 1 handling.

### 6. Where others might disagree

The degree to which terrestrial powder metallurgy and casting can be assumed to work in 0.05–0.15 g (Gemini's rotating furnace). Some may argue that partial gravity makes this a TRL 4/5 engineering scale-up problem rather than a fundamental physics problem, but the lack of convective cooling in vacuum and the unproven continuous operation of high-temperature seals/moulds in space justify the lower rating.
