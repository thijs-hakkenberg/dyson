### 1. Evidence

The requirement is for bearings, seals, rotary joints, and connectors to operate in hard vacuum and abrasive, electrostatically charged regolith for ≥5 years. 

Heritage systems have demonstrated subsets of these requirements, but not the combination:
- **Mars Rovers (Curiosity, Perseverance, 2012–present):** Have operated for >5–10 years in abrasive dust, utilizing labyrinth seals and specialized lubricants. However, Mars has a partial atmosphere (not hard vacuum), which significantly alters tribology and prevents the vacuum-welding and extreme outgassing seen in space.
- **Apollo Lunar Roving Vehicle (LRV, 1971–1972):** Operated in hard vacuum and highly abrasive lunar dust, but only for a few days. 
- **ISS Solar Alpha Rotary Joints (SARJ, 2007–present):** Continuous operation in hard vacuum for >15 years, but without abrasive dust exposure.
- **OSIRIS-REx / Hayabusa2 (2020 / 2019):** Operated mechanisms in NEA vacuum and dust, but exposure was limited to brief sampling events, not 5 years of continuous mining duty.
- **Ground Testing (NASA KSC / GRC, ongoing):** Component-level testing of bearings, labyrinth seals, and dry-film lubricants in dusty thermal vacuum (TVAC) chambers using lunar simulants (e.g., JSC-1A, NU-LHT) for Artemis and CLPS preparations.

Ratio to required scale: Duration demonstrated in relevant vacuum/dust environment is currently on the order of weeks/months (accelerated testing) or minutes (flight), compared to the required 5 years (ratio ~0.01 to 0.1).

### 2. Current TRL

**Current TRL: 4–5**
- **Min (4):** Component and breadboard validation in a laboratory environment (dusty vacuum chambers) is well documented for lunar simulants.
- **Max (5):** Subsystem validation in a relevant environment has been partially achieved for near-term lunar applications, though not yet validated for 5-year continuous duty cycles.
- **Unmet exit criterion (TRL 6):** Documented test performance of a system or subsystem prototype (e.g., a full mining robot actuator assembly) in a relevant environment (vacuum + charged abrasive dust) demonstrating performance equivalent to a 5-year life.

### 3. Target and difficulty

**Target:** TRL 6 by KDP-0B.
**AD²: 5** (New development; similar development has not been done for this specific combination of extreme duration, vacuum, and charged dust, but the path—accelerated life testing in dusty TVAC—is clear).

### 4. Path

1. **Component Accelerated Life Testing:** Dusty TVAC testing of candidate seals, bearings, and lubricants (dry film and low-outgassing wet) using charged NEA simulants. Scale: 1:1 components. Cost: $2M–$5M. Duration: 1–2 years. Reaches TRL 5.
2. **Subsystem Prototype Demonstration:** Long-duration testing of an integrated rotary joint or wheel drive assembly in dusty TVAC, using accelerated wear profiles to simulate 5 years of operation. Scale: 1:1 subsystem. Cost: $5M–$15M. Duration: 2–3 years. Reaches TRL 6.

### 5. Risk and fallback

**Risk if fail:** Project-ending for 0B. If mechanisms seize, mining and processing operations halt, destroying the economic case.
**Fallback:** 0A only, Earth supply; or architecture change to highly redundant, short-life replaceable robotic modules (drastically increasing Phase 1 mass and cost).

### 6. Where others might disagree

Some might argue TRL is higher (TRL 6+) based on Mars rover heritage. However, the lack of an atmosphere fundamentally changes lubricant behavior and dust adhesion (vacuum welding, static charge without atmospheric dissipation), making Mars heritage insufficient for a TRL 6 rating in a hard vacuum NEA environment.
