### 1. Evidence

The requirement is for 1–2.5 MWe deployable solar arrays per tug, operating under electric propulsion thrust dynamics, exposed to deep-space radiation, potential iodine propellant plume contamination, and mining dust, with a 10–15 year life. 

- **Current flight heritage:** The ISS Roll-Out Solar Array (iROSA) and the Gateway Power and Propulsion Element (PPE) arrays represent the state of the art for high-power deployable arrays. The PPE arrays (Maxar/Deployable Space Systems) are designed for ~50 kW per wing (TRL 6-8 for Gateway). 
- **Scale ratio:** The required 1–2.5 MWe per tug is 20× to 50× larger than the largest currently flown or qualified single array systems.
- **Environment:** Deep space radiation for III-V cells is well understood (TRL 8-9), but the combined effects of MW-scale structural dynamics under continuous low-thrust, iodine plume backflow (iodine is highly reactive with many spacecraft materials), and abrasive NEA dust on array surfaces at this scale have only been modeled or tested at component levels. Iodine plume interaction with solar cells has been tested in small vacuum chambers (e.g., ThrustMe NPT30-I2, 2021), but not at the MW scale where plasma sheath effects differ.

### 2. Current TRL

**TRL 3–4.** 
- **Lower bound met (TRL 3):** Analytical and experimental proof of concept exists for the underlying technologies (flexible blanket arrays, III-V cells, iodine plume interactions at small scale).
- **Unmet exit criterion (TRL 4/5):** Component and/or breadboard validation in a laboratory environment of the deployment mechanisms, structural dynamics under thrust, and iodine/dust mitigation strategies at the MW scale. No documented test performance of a MW-class array structure exists.

### 3. Target and difficulty

- **Target:** TRL 6 by KDP-0B (project-ending for the tug architecture if power cannot be generated). Needed by 2029 for Phase 0B procurement.
- **AD²: 5.** New development; similar development has not been done (MW-scale space arrays), but the path is clear (scaling up ROSA-style or folded-panel architectures, though structural dynamics at this scale are non-trivial).

### 4. Path

1. **Plume & Dust Interaction Testing:** Laboratory vacuum testing of III-V cell coupons and coverglasses exposed to iodine plasma and simulated NEA dust to validate degradation models. (TRL 4, $2M–$5M, 1–2 years).
2. **Sub-scale Structural Dynamics:** Ground testing of a ~100–250 kW class array wing to validate deployment kinematics and structural stiffness models under simulated thrust loads. (TRL 5, $15M–$30M, 2–3 years).
3. **System Prototype Demonstration:** Thermal-vacuum deployment and structural testing of a full-scale (or half-scale, 500 kW) array quadrant, combined with high-fidelity simulations of the full 1–2.5 MW system. (TRL 6, $50M–$100M, 3–4 years).

### 5. Risk and fallback

- **Risk if fail:** Architecture-change. If MW-scale arrays cannot be deployed or stabilized, the architecture must fall back to multiple smaller tugs (e.g., 200–500 kW each), drastically increasing the number of vehicles, or shift to nuclear electric propulsion (which has its own severe TRL and regulatory hurdles).
- **Fallback:** Scale down tugs to 300–500 kWe using multiple Gateway-class arrays, increasing fleet size and mission complexity.

### 6. Where others might disagree

Some might argue that because solar panels are highly modular, scaling from 50 kW to 2.5 MW is merely an engineering exercise (TRL 5). However, the structural dynamics of a multi-megawatt array under continuous thrust, combined with the specific environmental hazards of iodine and dust, introduce fundamental non-linearities that invalidate simple scaling assumptions.
