An assessment of T16: Anchoring, hold-down and surface mobility on low-cohesion bodies, evaluated against the revised Phase 0 architecture.

### 1. Evidence
Anchoring in microgravity to react continuous mechanical loads is an unsolved problem. Flight heritage is limited to brief touch-and-go sampling (OSIRIS-REx, 2020; Hayabusa2, 2019), hopping mobility (MINERVA-II, MASCOT, 2018), and failed anchoring attempts (Philae harpoons and ice screws, 2014). Terrestrial laboratory and parabolic flight testing of microspine grippers (JPL Asteroid Redirect Mission studies, Parness et al., ~2013–2017) demonstrated gripping on monolithic basalt and concrete simulants, but not on low-cohesion rubble piles under continuous multi-kilonewton excavation and tether loads.

### 2. Current TRL
**TRL 3–4**. Analytical and experimental proof of concept exists for specific anchoring mechanisms (microspines on monoliths, augers in dense regolith) in 1g and brief microgravity (TRL 3 met). However, component validation in a relevant environment (TRL 5) is unmet, and even laboratory breadboard validation (TRL 4) is highly contested for rubble-pile targets because 1g terrestrial testbeds cannot accurately simulate the low-cohesion, low-gravity granular mechanics of a rubble-pile asteroid under continuous dynamic loading.

### 3. Target and Difficulty
Target is **TRL 6 by KDP-0B**. This is a project-ending technology for Phase 0B; if the mining robots cannot anchor to react the 0.5–5 t/h excavation loads and tether tension, mechanical mining fails. 
**AD² is 7**: Requires fundamental engineering research. The mechanics of reacting high loads against a microgravity rubble pile without locally disrupting the body and causing the anchor to fail are poorly understood, and several competing approaches (augers, microspines, nets, harpoons) exist without a clear path.

### 4. Path
1. **Simulation**: Coupled DEM/FEM modeling of anchor deployment and load-bearing in low-cohesion granular media.
2. **Experiment**: Parabolic flight and drop-tower testing of sub-scale anchoring mechanisms (augers, microspines) in simulated rubble-pile media.
3. **Spaceflight Demonstration**: Phase 0A pathfinder mission to a NEA to deploy and test anchoring mechanisms in true microgravity and regolith conditions.

### 5. Risk and Fallback
**Risk:** project-ending. 
**Fallback:** Abandon mechanical excavation in favor of contactless extraction (e.g., thermal/optical mining) or free-flying containment bags for small boulders, drastically reducing throughput.

### 6. Where others might disagree
Some may argue that microspines are TRL 5 based on JPL's ARM testing, but those tests assumed a monolithic boulder target. The revised architecture must accommodate rubble-pile variants, where microspines are ineffective for reacting high loads.
