## 1. Design philosophy

### 1.1 Build an accountable institution before building an industrial operator

The principal organizational risk is not insufficient committee capacity. It is committing money and hardware without a legally accountable operator, a controlled technical baseline, or evidence that the next commitment is justified.

The organizational design therefore follows five rules:

1. **One accountable operator for each mission.**  
   Consortium participation must not obscure who holds licences, owns operational responsibilities, or answers to regulators.

2. **Independent scrutiny without duplicate engineering.**  
   bom-0-7 commissions and tracks independent reviews. Detailed systems engineering, mission assurance, qualification, and mission operations remain outside this item.

3. **Reversibility determines approval burden.**  
   A reversible laboratory study needs lightweight authorization. A production fleet commitment requires independently validated evidence and formal board approval.

4. **Openness is the default, not an exemption from law.**  
   Export controls, privacy, cybersecurity, contractual rights, and licensing obligations take precedence over automatic publication.

5. **0B remains a real option.**  
   No standing organization, financing agreement, or supplier commitment should make industrial deployment the default outcome of successful research.

### 1.2 Architecture constraints carried into governance

The configuration-controlled organizational baseline must explicitly recognize:

- Processing at the target NEA, including at least beneficiation and primary processing.
- Native/reduced Fe-Ni as the baseline product.
- No Phase 0 production LOX/LH₂.
- No crew visits.
- MW-class cargo transport, rather than the superseded 100 kW freighter concept.
- A reference Phase 1 receiving location at SEL1, provisional under D-04.
- No selected EP propellant: iodine is the costing reference and argon the alternate under interim D-05.
- Earth-supplied material as a fallback if the space-resource processing chain does not close.

The organization must not quietly reinstate superseded architecture through procurement specifications or funding presentations.

### 3.1 Entity A: public-interest association

**Recommended reference:** A Swiss association, subject to current-law verification and tax/funding advice.

Responsibilities:

- Maintain the public-interest purpose.
- Appoint the operating entity’s governing body through legally enforceable mechanisms.
- Administer independent reviews and public reporting.
- Hold or steward open intellectual property where compatible with grants and contracts.
- Approve entry into 0B and material changes in institutional purpose.

The Swiss structure is provisional. No claim about current Swiss space-law status should be embedded in the baseline without counsel verification.

### 3.2 Entity B: operating entity

The operator must:

- Hold relevant mission authorizations where required.
- Contract with launch, spacecraft, processing, and transport suppliers.
- Maintain accounts, insurance arrangements, and exposure registers.
- Implement export, sanctions, employment, and security controls.
- Provide a single accountable executive to regulators and counterparties.

**Jurisdiction recommendation:** Conduct a mandatory-feasibility screen of at least the United States and Luxembourg before comparing secondary advantages.

Mandatory screens include:

- A credible authorization and continuing-supervision route.
- Compatibility with ownership and control arrangements.
- Access to required technology and personnel.
- Acceptable tax and grant treatment.
- Ability to meet liability and financial-security requirements.

A weighted score must not compensate for failure of a legal prerequisite.

**Decision target:** Select the operator jurisdiction within 6–9 months of organizational authorization, or before the first contract requiring that selection, whichever is earlier.

### 3.3 Separation is not immunity

Separate entities do not automatically eliminate parent exposure or operational responsibility. Counsel must examine:

- Actual control over mission decisions.
- Guarantees and indemnities.
- Commingling of funds and personnel.
- Directors’ duties.
- Insolvency and asset-transfer rules.
- Whether Entity A could be treated as an operator or controlling party.

No special-purpose entity should be created merely to imply that these risks have disappeared.

## 4. Governance and decision rights

### 4.1 Board

**Recommended specification:**

- **7 voting directors.**
- At least **4 independent** of management and material suppliers.
- Staggered **3-year terms**.
- Normally no more than **two consecutive terms**.
- Management attends but does not hold a voting majority.
- Conflicted directors recuse from affected decisions.

Required collective expertise:

- Space mission development.
- Financial control and capital allocation.
- Space authorization and liability.
- Industrial processing or manufacturing.
- Public-interest governance.
- Safety and assurance.
- Phase 1 demand and logistics.

### 4.2 Reserved decisions

The board retains approval of:

- KDP-0B.
- Annual budgets and funding plans.
- Material debt, guarantees, and indemnities.
- Changes to mission purpose or entity control.
- Departure from the approved product or delivery architecture.
- Contracts creating substantial termination exposure.
- Closure or sale of the programme.

For planning, contracts exceeding **$5M or 10% of the approved annual organizational/programme delegation envelope**, whichever threshold is lower, require explicit escalation. These thresholds must be reconciled with the programme’s broader procurement policy before adoption.

### 4.3 Independent safety and cost review

Create two independent functions:

1. **Independent Safety Review Function**
2. **Independent Cost and Schedule Review Function**

Both report directly to a board committee rather than through the executive whose work they review.

They may issue a **hold recommendation**. Unresolved legal noncompliance, licence restrictions, or mandatory gate failures cannot be overridden by an ordinary management decision.

Detailed engineering assurance remains with PM/SE/MA and suppliers. The independent functions assess the adequacy and independence of evidence rather than recreate it.

### 4.4 Charter Trust and sortition

My recommendation is conservative:

- Conduct a Charter Trust design study during 0A.
- Do not transfer programme assets into an untested trust structure.
- Use sortition only for bounded advisory panels, with screening, training, and conflict-of-interest controls.
- Do not use sortition to select the licensed operator’s accountable management.

Long-term institutional persistence is a legitimate research topic. It is not yet a demonstrated substitute for accountable management.

## 7. Autonomy, control, and communications

### 7.1 Organizational automation

Automation may:

- Route approvals.
- Detect missing signatures.
- Track licence conditions and deadlines.
- Check document hashes.
- Flag budget or schedule thresholds.

Automation may not independently:

- Authorize KDP-0B.
- Execute exceptional payments.
- Publish potentially controlled technical material.
- Change mission authority.
- Accept residual safety risk.
- Modify the legal or technical baseline.

AI-generated legal or technical summaries are advisory records until reviewed by a responsible human.

## 8. Legal, safety, resource rights, and publication

### 8.1 Regulatory evidence package

Before flight commitments, maintain a mission-specific authorization matrix covering:

- Operator identity.
- Authorizing and supervising state or states.
- Launch and payload review interfaces.
- Registration responsibilities.
- Spectrum assignments and coordination.
- Resource recovery and product ownership.
- Export controls and sanctions.
- Environmental and contamination obligations.
- Liability, indemnities, and financial-security requirements.

Current law must be verified at entity selection and again before major flight commitments.

COSPAR policy is not itself a universal permit. Planetary-protection obligations require a mission-specific determination through the applicable authorities; spectral class alone is insufficient.

### 8.2 Resource-rights work

Deliverables include:

- Legal opinions on recovered material ownership.
- Sale and delivery terms.
- Treatment of products, tailings, and failed hardware.
- Cross-border enforcement assessment.
- Customer title and acceptance provisions.
- No implication that resource rights confer sovereignty over an asteroid.

Any inherited resource requirement using an undefined production quantity must be clarified or superseded before adoption.

### 8.3 Debris and contamination

Under interim D-03, establish an ALARP assurance process and require a quantified interim ceiling to be developed through modelling, tests, and licensing engagement.

Do not adopt an arbitrary “99% containment” threshold.

bom-0-15 owns technical modelling and mitigation design. bom-0-7 owns:

- Legal obligations.
- Independent review commissioning.
- Acceptance-authority records.
- Reporting commitments.
- Evidence that residual risk was reduced as far as reasonably practicable.

### 8.4 Open licensing

Recommended policy:

- Documents: **CC BY-SA 4.0**, where rights permit.
- Hardware: **CERN OHL v2**, variant selected deliberately for each release package.
- Software: established licence selected by software ownership and dependency policy.
- Controlled or restricted material: separately managed annex.

Every public release requires export classification and rights clearance **before publication**. A promise of openness must include an explicit legal-compliance limitation.

## 9. Phase 1 demand interface

The organizational system must force a material acceptance agreement before 0B investment.

A signed demand specification should state:

- Product form: for example, ingots, feedstock, or another agreed form.
- Fe/Ni composition envelope.
- Permitted oxide, carbon, sulfur, phosphorus, and other relevant contaminants.
- Mechanical or processability requirements.
- Batch size and sampling method.
- Delivery location.
- Required delivery dates.
- Annual quantity and acceptance tolerances.
- Inspection authority.
- Pricing and non-delivery remedies.
- Contingency supply arrangements.

The programme’s order-of-magnitude production ambition is not a substitute for this agreement.

**Recommendation:** Require a side-by-side assessment of Earth-supplied material before KDP-0B. If microgravity metallurgy fails, Project Dyson should still be able to supply Phase 1 through procurement rather than attempt to rescue sunk investment.

### 12.4 Financing, insurance, and reserves

**Liquidity:** At the reference 0A run-rate, 12–24 months of expenditure is approximately **$6.6–13.2M**. Across the cost envelope, the equivalent range is approximately **$4.7–20M**.

This is available cash or committed liquidity, not an additional cost on top of the spending it funds.

**Flight insurance:** Do not put an unsupported premium estimate inside the organizational baseline. Obtain broker indications against defined:

- Mission trajectories.
- Coverage periods.
- Insured entities.
- Limits and exclusions.
- Launch and customer contracts.
- Retentions.

Launch-provider insurance does not automatically eliminate customer exposure. Delivery toward SEL1 also prevents assuming that all relevant risk remains remote from Earth.

**Retained flight risk:** Show separately as financing after exposure analysis. It is neither an insurance premium nor a generic contingency percentage.

### 12.5 Disagreement with round-2 figures

I do **not** recommend adopting **$100–350M as the 0A organizational expenditure baseline**.

The reason is scope and duration, not a belief that governance is inexpensive:

- The proposed six-year 0A scope supports **$31–66M**.
- Six years of 0A plus five illustrative years of 0B supports approximately **$65–135M**, before excluded flight premiums and programme contingency.
- A ten-year continuation at the 0A staffing profile would be approximately **$50–106M**, on the same exclusions.
- Higher totals may be justified by broader scope, expensive insurance, litigation, or longer duration, but those drivers must be identified.

I agree with the reconciliation’s central conclusion: comparing differently scoped totals is not a meaningful cost trade.

I also reject embedding contingency in bom-0-7 while separately budgeting bom-0-17. That would obscure whether uncertainty has been funded twice.
