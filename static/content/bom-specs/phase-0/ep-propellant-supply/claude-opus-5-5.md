# bom-0-16 Electric-Propulsion Propellant Supply: Technical Proposal

**Phase 0, revised architecture. 0A development with gated 0B operations. Reference propellant iodine, alternate argon (D-05, interim).**

---

## 0. Position Summary

1. **The commodity is not the cost.** Round-2 costed this line as iodine at $30–100/kg. On my estimate that is under 5% of the true cost of propellant arriving at a tug.
   - Ground cost at SEL1, excluding launch, is **$180–510/kg**. Most of that is containers, pallets, filling and handling.
   - Delivery to SEL1 (bom-0-8) adds **$1,000–5,000/kg**.
   - The line therefore matters mainly as a **launch-mass generator**, and should be managed that way.

2. **Demand. My planning nominal is 400 t/yr, with a contract range of 150–700 t/yr.**
   - The basis is 1.3 kt/yr delivered, 4 km/s per leg (low-thrust), Isp 2,500 s, 30 t tug dry mass, 200 t payload, and a 1.15 operations factor.
   - This agrees with Claude (150–450) and GPT (160–600).
   - Gemini's 900–1,200 t/yr is reproducible only with ≥6 km/s legs at ≤2,000–2,500 s, or at 2.5 kt/yr. I treat it as the "bad target" corner, which the KDP-0B gate should exclude rather than budget for.

3. **New gate metric: Propellant Import Ratio (PIR).** PIR is the Earth-launched propellant plus packaging mass delivered to SEL1, divided by product mass delivered to SEL1.
   - Proposed values: target ≤ 0.35, hard limit ≤ 0.50 at KDP-0B (the target and economics gates under D-02).
   - At PIR ≈ 0.7, Phase 0 imports 0.7 kg from Earth for every 1 kg of iron it delivers. The Earth-supplied fallback then wins on mass alone, before any other import is counted.

4. **Logistics architecture: no in-space propellant fluid transfer.** Iodine stays solid in sealed **Propellant Cartridge Units (PCU-5, 5 t)**. Three PCUs ride on a **Propellant Pallet (PP-15)**.
   - Pallets are launched, captured at a **Propellant Yard** in the SEL1 halo, and physically swapped onto tugs.
   - The only "wet" connection is a low-pressure (<150 kPa) heated vapour coupling between pallet and tug, made once per pallet.

5. **The pallet standard is propellant-agnostic.** The pallet frame, latch, power/data and coupling envelope accept either iodine PCUs or argon modules. The D-05 decision then changes a module, not the architecture.

6. **Argon is a real alternate, but it carries a storage penalty.**
   - At 300 bar it is roughly 10× less dense than solid iodine, with 20–30% tankage.
   - Cryogenic liquid argon reintroduces cryogenic fluid management, which this phase was restructured to avoid.

7. **Push Isp up.** Every 0.1 reduction in PIR at 1.3 kt/yr saves about 150 t/yr of launch, which is $150–750M/yr. That is worth more tug-time. I recommend bom-0-4 trade towards **3,000–3,500 s**.

8. **Cathode and proximity gas.** Neither may be xenon or krypton. A small argon "service bottle" on each pallet covers tug RCS, final approach near the node, and cathode flow if iodine-tolerant cathodes are not qualified.

## 2. Design Philosophy

1. **Minimise mass to SEL1 per kg of product, not $/kg of commodity.** Every design choice is scored in launched kg.
2. **Solids and swaps over fluids and transfers.** The only wet interface is a single low-pressure, low-flow vapour coupling.
3. **Keep the decision reversible.** Under D-05 no propellant is selected. The pallet standard, yard and servicer are common to both candidates. Only the PCU (iodine) or the argon module is propellant-specific. Before the propellant selection decision, iodine-specific spend is capped (§11).
4. **Contamination is a design driver, not an afterthought.**
   - Iodine attacks silver, which is present in most solar-cell interconnects, and aluminium and copper in wetted paths.
   - The yard stands off from the node.
   - No iodine thrusting is permitted inside a keep-out zone.
   - Proximity operations use argon only.
5. **No dependence on scarce gases.** Programme demand must stay below about 5% of world production of any propellant or auxiliary gas.

## 3. Demand Model

### 3.1 Method

Tugs refuel only at SEL1 and carry propellant for both legs. With dry mass D, payload L, effective exhaust velocity vₑ = g₀·Isp, and f(Δv) = e^(Δv/vₑ) − 1:

```
P_loaded   = (D + L)         · f(Δv_in)     NEA → SEL1, laden
P_outbound = (D + P_loaded)  · f(Δv_out)    SEL1 → NEA, carrying return propellant
R          = (P_loaded + P_outbound) / L    propellant per kg delivered (pre-margin)
Annual     = R × Product × k_ops,  k_ops = 1.15
```

The 1.15 factor covers:
- Δv reserve (~8%);
- residuals (≤3%);
- pallet and PCU dry mass hauled (~2–3%);
- station-keeping at the NEA and SEL1;
- thruster conditioning.

### 3.2 Reference case check

Reference: D = 30 t, L = 200 t, Δv = 4 + 4 km/s, Isp = 2,500 s.

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| f | 0.1774 |
| P_loaded | 40.8 t |
| P_outbound | 12.6 t |
| Total per trip | 53.4 t (61 t with k_ops) |
| R | 0.267 |
| Annual at 1.3 kt/yr | **≈ 400 t/yr** |

Thrust cross-check at 1.5 MWe, η = 0.6:
- T = 2ηP/vₑ ≈ 73 N; ṁ ≈ 3.0 g/s, which is 258 kg/day.
- The laden leg is about 170 days of thrusting.
- This is consistent with roughly one round trip per tug-year and 6–7 trips/yr, within the 10–18 tug fleet.

### 3.3 Sensitivity: R (kg propellant per kg delivered, before k_ops)

D = 30 t, L = 200 t. Δv is per leg, low-thrust, including phasing.

| Δv/leg \ Isp | 2,000 s | 2,500 s | 3,000 s | 3,500 s |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 km/s | 0.25 | 0.19 | 0.15 | 0.13 |
| 4 km/s | 0.35 | **0.27** | 0.21 | 0.18 |
| 5 km/s | 0.48 | 0.35 | 0.28 | 0.23 |
| 6 km/s | 0.61 | 0.45 | 0.35 | 0.29 |

**PIR ≈ R × 1.15 × 1.10**, where 1.10 is the PCU and pallet packaging factor (§6). At the reference case PIR ≈ 0.34, right at the proposed target.

The Isp lesson for bom-0-4:
- Going from 2,500 s to 3,500 s at 4 km/s cuts demand by 33%, about 130 t/yr, worth roughly $130–650M/yr of launch.
- It costs about 40% longer thrust arcs, which means perhaps 3–5 more tug-equivalents.
- At $15–30M/yr amortised per tug, the added tugs are about $60–150M/yr. Higher Isp wins.

| Volume for 400 t/yr | **~80 m³** solid | ~890 m³ (gas) / ~290 m³ (liquid) | — | — | — |
| EP heritage | Flight at ≤100 W (ThrustMe NPT30-I2, 2020); ground Hall/RF to ~10 kW class | Mass-produced flight Hall thrusters at kW class (Starlink V2-mini); ~200 kW lab (VASIMR VX-200) | Extensive | X3 100 kW nested Hall on Kr (ground) | Low TRL at relevant power |
| Contamination | **High concern**: condensable, corrosive, attacks Ag, Al, Cu | Inert, none | None | None | Low (H₂O condensation) |
| Ground handling | Toxic (OSHA ceiling 0.1 ppm), corrosive, UN3495 Class 8; **US DEA List I chemical** (registration, records) | Asphyxiant, high pressure | — | — | — |

**My view.**
- Iodine wins on logistics because of density and low-pressure storage. That advantage is roughly a 3–10× reduction in launch volume and about 15–20 percentage points of tankage versus argon gas.
- Argon wins on contamination, thruster heritage and supply simplicity.
- The decision is fundamentally a bom-0-4 thruster plus bom-0-15 contamination decision. This line's input is the number: **argon at 300 bar raises mass to SEL1 by about 15–20%**, roughly 60–80 t/yr at reference, which is $60–400M/yr.
- If argon is chosen, my recommendation is high-pressure COPV only if the fairing volume available per launch is ample. Otherwise a cryogenic yard with zero-boil-off would be needed, and I would oppose adding that in Phase 0.

**Water.** On a C-type target, water from bom-0-6 could fuel the laden leg. That leg is ~76% of per-trip propellant in the reference case, so imports would fall by about three-quarters. The cost is a dual-propellant tug at low TRL. It stays a $0.5–1.5M desk study until a C-type target is confirmed.

### 6.6 Ground Fill and QA Facility

- Located near the primary launch site to minimise road transport of UN3495 loads.
- Process chain:
  1. Receive crude iodine (≥99.5%).
  2. Resublime to ≥99.99% (Cl, Br, organics and H₂O specified).
  3. Melt at ~120 °C under dry N₂.
  4. Pour into a preheated PCU, then directional solidification to minimise voids.
  5. CT inspection and weighing.
  6. Helium leak test of the sealed PCU.
  7. Pallet integration and argon bottle fill.
- Capacity: 1 PCU per working day (~250/yr, comfortably above the 47/yr maximum).
- Safety: closed-loop scrubbers, toxic monitoring to 0.1 ppm, DEA List I registration, corrosion-resistant facility materials.

## 8. Autonomy, Control and Communications

**Autonomy level.**
- Supervised autonomy for pallet capture, berthing and tug loading. Ground gives go/no-go at the approach-gate, contact and latch steps.
- SEL1 light-time is about 5 s, so teleoperation is possible but is not the baseline. Autonomy is preferred for repeatability and staffing cost.
- During transit to and from the NEA, tug-side propellant management (switching PCUs, thermal control) is fully onboard. Light-time to the NEA is 1–20+ minutes.

**PCU and pallet fault protection firmware** (supplied by this line, hosted on pallet avionics):
- Over-temperature cut-off with independent hardware fuses at 105 °C bulk and 140 °C outlet.
- Pressure-rise detection.
- Iodine-vapour leak detection, triggering automatic isolation of that PCU.
- Gauging estimate and residual prediction.

**Telemetry.**

| Source | Rate |
|---|---|
| Dormant pallet | ~1 sample/min, <100 bps |
| Active pallet on tug | ~1 kbps |
| Yard housekeeping | 10–50 kbps |
| Servicer capture video (bursts, via node relay) | 1–5 Mbps |
| Total yard/servicer average | <1 GB/day |

**Inventory system.** A ground and onboard ledger tracks every PCU from fill to empty: serial number, fill mass, history, leak tests, and mission assignment. It is integrated with bom-0-10 planning.

**Collision safety.** The servicer abort is passively safe: approach trajectories are designed so that a loss of control drifts away from the yard and node.

## 9. Contamination and Safety (inputs to bom-0-15)

- **Silver.** Iodine forms AgI with silver. Many space solar cells use Ag interconnects, so iodine is a direct threat to arrays on the node, yard and tugs.
  - 0A coupon tests at relevant iodine fluence are mandatory.
  - Yard arrays are specified with encapsulated or Ag-free interconnects.
- **Plume keep-out.** No iodine thrusting within an exclusion radius of the node or yard (planning value 50–200 km; final value from deposition modelling). Final approach uses argon.
- **Re-sublimation behaviour.** Iodine has ~40 Pa vapour pressure at 25 °C, so it does not accumulate on warm surfaces but does condense on cold ones (radiators, shaded optics). Cold surfaces near any iodine source need explicit analysis.
- **Ground safety.** Toxic and corrosive handling under standard industrial hygiene; low-pressure storage means low stored energy.
  - Argon COPVs at 300 bar carry stored-energy hazards; heritage design rules apply.
- **Debris.** Empties stay latched to the yard. There is no uncontrolled release.

## 10. Manufacturing Considerations

1. **Rate.** 60–140 PCUs/yr (including 0B ramp) is constellation-like production. Key methods:
   - spun or roll-formed shells;
   - automated orbital welding;
   - brazed sheet fin matrices (or additive manufacture for prototypes only);
   - batch helium leak testing.
2. **Material trade.** All-Ni-alloy shell (robust, ~150 kg) versus Al shell with PFA liner (~90 kg, saving 2–3 t/yr of launch at reference). Decided by 0A liner permeation and adhesion tests.
3. **Casting quality.** Void fraction and fin bonding drive residual and launch survival. CT on 100% of units initially, then sampling.
4. **Valves in iodine service** are the long-lead heritage gap. Qualify one valve family for PCU, pallet and coupling use. Strongly consider dual-sourcing.
5. **Pallet frames** are common to iodine and argon, which keeps the production line propellant-agnostic until the selection decision.
6. **Supply chain.** At least two iodine suppliers (Chile and Japan, plus US as tertiary). Six-month raw-iodine ground stock. Contracts indexed with collars; bom-0-17 holds a price reserve. Commodity risk is small because commodity is ≤15% of line cost.

## 14. Open Engineering Questions

1. What is the real low-thrust Δv per leg between SEL1 and candidate NEAs (bom-0-1), and therefore PIR? This is the single largest demand driver.
2. Where is the Isp optimum once launch $/kg is included, and can iodine RF or gridded-ion or high-voltage Hall thrusters reach 3,000–3,500 s at MW-cluster scale with acceptable life (bom-0-4)?
3. PCU concept: will a sublimation-mode fin matrix achieve ≤3% residual at 5 t scale? Is controlled-melt liquid iodine with propellant management devices better despite the 25% expansion and TRL 2?
4. What is the right PCU material system (all Ni-alloy versus Al plus PFA liner), and what is the permeation and creep behaviour over a 5-year storage life?
5. What iodine fluence is acceptable on node and tug arrays, and what keep-out radius results?
6. Can iodine-tolerant cathodes (LaB₆ or C12A7 electride) remove the argon cathode-gas need? How large is the argon flow otherwise?
7. Should the yard be free-flying (baseline) or hosted on the node (saves $60–150M)?
8. Which pallet size and count per launch is optimal, given bom-0-8's eventual vehicle mix?
9. Should empties go back to the NEA plant as scrap, or does Phase 1 want Ni-alloy at SEL1?
10. For argon: is 300 bar COPV acceptable on volume grounds, or would argon force a cryogenic yard? If the latter, I recommend that weighs heavily against argon.
11. Water EP on a C-type target: is a dual-propellant tug worth the ~75% import reduction? This is D-05's desk study and is decision-relevant only if bom-0-6 is triggered.

## 15. Decisions Requested

1. Adopt **PIR** (target ≤0.35, limit ≤0.50) as a KDP-0B discriminating criterion under the target and economics gates.
2. Adopt the **propellant-agnostic pallet standard** and the **no in-space fluid transfer** principle as the reference logistics architecture.
3. Issue requirements to bom-0-4:
   - no xenon or krypton cathode or RCS gas;
   - Isp trade to include launch cost;
   - 4 pallet berths;
   - iodine thrusting inhibited inside the keep-out zone.
4. Hold a **Propellant Selection Decision at ~T+3**, joint with bom-0-4 and bom-0-15, capping iodine-specific hardware spend at $60M before it.
5. Record that this line's commodity cost is minor. bom-0-8 should carry **~440 t/yr to SEL1 (range 165–770 t/yr)** as the propellant-driven launch load, at an estimated $0.44–2.2B/yr nominal. That makes it one of the largest single drivers of 0B operating cost.
