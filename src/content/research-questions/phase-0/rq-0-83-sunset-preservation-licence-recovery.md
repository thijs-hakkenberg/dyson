## Background

The owner has ruled that the month-180 sunset acts automatically (G-1.6) and that a failed licence gate allows one bounded reapplication (G-1.3). Still open:

- **At M180** (rq-0-69). Claude: authority expires, no extension in place, re-entry only by a new decision. GPT: authority expires, but bounded replacement authority is allowed for preservation work. Gemini: fallback executes, with an owner-level clock pause for catastrophic external shocks.
- **The planning buffer.**
- **The recovery window after licence failure** (rq-0-70): 6, 12 or 18 months.
- **Eligible failures and redesign scope.**

The models disagree mostly on what preserving an option and reapplying for a licence actually cost and take, which no one supplied.

Feeds decision-sheet items 2.9, 2.10 and 2.11 and the buffer item; the buffer also depends on rq-0-63 (integrated schedule).

## Why This Matters

Pre-committed termination protects against sunk-cost drift, but only if keeping the option alive and restarting are priced. A licence window that is shorter than regulators' actual review times just means failing a second time.

## How this gets answered

- **Literature** — Restart and mothballing costs for shut-down capabilities (e.g. Pu-238 production restart, production-line shutdowns with tooling preservation); sunset clauses in programmes and how often they held; review and reapplication timelines for novel-activity licences (space-resources authorisation regimes, launch and re-entry licensing, nuclear and mining permits as analogues).
- **Stakeholder** — Licensing authorities on realistic review cycles and what counts as a remediable deficiency (outreach draft: Luxembourg Space Agency oq-14-1). The US authority for novel in-space activities must first be identified.

## Research Directions

1. Restart cost and time as a fraction of the original programme, with how much was preserved.
2. Licence review and reapplication durations, and the share of reapplications that succeeded.
3. How often sunset clauses were extended in place, and what followed.

**Owner decides afterwards:** whether an extension in place is ever allowed, and the cap on preservation spending.
