## Background

The owner has ruled that any cost above Earth supply needs a separate, explicit owner authorisation (`gate-rulings.yaml` G-1.7). Still open from rq-0-67:

- **Whether an authorised premium can produce a gate pass.** Claude: yes, within pre-approved caps. Gemini and GPT: no; above-parity cost is an economics fail, and a separate decision may proceed anyway.
- **When the premium is authorised.** Claude: in advance, by the 0A mid-term review. Gemini and GPT: at KDP-0B.
- **The cost test.** Claude: P70 against Earth P50 with ±30% estimate readiness. GPT: equivalent expected costs plus a P70 funding envelope.

Feeds decision-sheet items 2.5 and 2.6 and the cost-test item.

## Why This Matters

D-01 calls Phase 0 a capability programme, not a cost saving. The economics gate is where that framing meets a number. A premium set after results are known invites motivated reasoning; one fixed too early may not survive new evidence.

## How this gets answered

- **Literature** — Cost-confidence policy (NASA joint cost-and-schedule confidence, typically budgeted at 70%; GAO Cost Estimating and Assessment Guide); estimate-class accuracy bands (AACE classes); cases where governments paid an above-market premium for a domestic or strategic capability, and how it was authorised and capped; real-options treatment of strategic capability value.
- **Stakeholder** — Cost-estimating and oversight bodies on comparing a project-confidence estimate with a market price, and on pre-committing premium caps (outreach drafts: GAO oq-12-2, ICEAA oq-15-1).

## Research Directions

1. The cost statistic and confidence level used for comparable go/no-go decisions, and why.
2. Precedents for capability premiums: who authorised them, when, with what caps, and how they turned out.
3. Whether a pre-committed premium cap survives in practice or gets renegotiated at the gate.

**Owner decides afterwards:** the size of any premium (the value of the capability), which no evidence can set.
