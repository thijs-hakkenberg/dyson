# Phase 0 Technology Readiness, Re-baselined

In March we rated ten Phase 0 technologies and set five decision gates at fixed months. Both rested on a plan that no longer exists: a central station refining 50,000 tonnes of ore a year, visited by crews, fed by 100 kW-class freighters. The October re-assessment found that plan could not close. This post explains what replaced the readiness ratings and the gates, and why.

## What changed in the plan

All three models in the re-assessment agreed on the main changes:

- **Process at the asteroid, ship product.** Beneficiation and primary processing move to the target near-Earth asteroid. The product narrows to native iron-nickel, of order 1,000 tonnes a year rather than 50,000.
- **No crew, no cryogenic depot.** Phase 0 is uncrewed, and there is no production of liquid hydrogen and oxygen.
- **MW-class tugs.** Cargo moves on electric-propulsion tugs of 1 to 2.5 megawatts each.
- **Split the phase.** Phase 0A (research, scouts and a pathfinder) is funded. Phase 0B (production) is held as an option.

That last change is decision D-01, which the project owner accepted on 12 October. Phase 0 is a capability programme, not a cost saving. Whether asteroid production is worth building is not yet known, and the plan no longer pretends otherwise.

## The new ratings

A three-model panel (Claude Opus 5.5, Gemini 3.1 Pro and GPT-6.1 Sol) rated **26 critical technologies**, each one at the scale, environment and duration the revised plan needs. That condition matters. A 12.5 kW Hall thruster with thousands of hours of wear testing says little about a cluster of 100 to 250 kW modules running for 40,000 hours on iodine.

Each technology got two numbers:

- **TRL**, the NASA readiness scale from 1 (principles observed) to 9 (proven in operation).
- **AD²**, the advancement degree of difficulty from 1 to 9, which says how hard the remaining path is. Two technologies at TRL 3 can be very different problems.

The results:

- **Current readiness runs from TRL 2 to 5.** No model supported the March rating of TRL 6–7 for high-power electric propulsion. The MW-class cluster is now TRL 2–4, with AD² 6.
- **17 technologies are project-ending:** if they fail, there is no production option. Another 9 would force an architecture change.
- **The panel agreed on 24 of 26.** It diverged on one (dust-tolerant mechanisms). The owner ruled on another (deep-space communications), after a deliberation about what exactly its rating should cover.

Every model's rating is kept and shown. The headline is the range across the panel, never an average. Averaging would hide exactly the disagreement a funder needs to see.

Every March rating is accounted for. Five were replaced by one or more of the new technologies. Five were deferred, mostly because the cryogenic depot and solar-grade silicon left Phase 0.

## The new gates

Decision D-02 replaces the five numbered gates. Its rule: **stringency follows reversibility.**

- **KDP-0B**, the one-way decision to fund production, needs every project-ending technology at TRL 6 in a relevant environment, with no waivers.
- **Internal 0A milestones**, which only release money that can still be stopped, need TRL 4–5.
- **Four discriminating gates** test what TRL cannot express: a sampled target with enough recoverable metal, signed demand from Phase 1, delivered cost against shipping material from Earth, and a licence covering debris and tailings.

Gates now have decision windows rather than fixed months. KDP-0B falls between month 72 and month 180 of Phase 0. Month 180 is a sunset: if KDP-0B has not passed by then, the plan falls back automatically to "0A only, Earth supply".

## What is still open

The panel deliberated eleven open gate rules. On a strict test, where both independent syntheses had to agree and the original responses had to bear it out, eight points had converged, and the owner confirmed them. The rest turn on evidence no one has produced yet. Examples:

- How far a test can be extrapolated before it stops counting as TRL 6.
- How much sampling bounds an asteroid's grade.
- Whether a cost premium over Earth supply can ever count as passing the economics gate.

Rather than rule on those by intuition, the owner routed them to eight new research questions (rq-0-76 to rq-0-83). Each is typed by method: literature, simulation, analysis, or outreach to agencies, standards bodies and regulators.
